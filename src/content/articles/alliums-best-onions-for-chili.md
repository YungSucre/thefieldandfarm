The best onions for chili are those that balance sweetness with a sharp bite, and hold up during long simmering. For most chili recipes, you want a yellow onion like 'Yellow Sweet Spanish' or a red onion like 'Red Burgermaster', but the true champion is the 'Walla Walla Sweet' for its mild sweetness, though it has a shorter storage life. If you want a classic pungent kick, go with 'Yellow Globe' or 'Copra'. Here is how to choose and grow them for your chili.

## Why onion choice matters in chili

Chili is a slow-cooked dish, so the onion you add at the start will mellow and sweeten as it cooks. A sweet onion like 'Walla Walla' will almost disappear into the sauce, adding a subtle background note. A pungent onion like 'Copra' will hold its own, giving a sharper edge that some chili heads crave. The key is to match the onion to your chili's heat and spice level. ## Top onion varieties for chili

Here are the best varieties, grouped by flavor profile, with realistic yields and storage notes.

### Sweet and mild
- **Walla Walla Sweet**: A large, mild onion that is perfect for fresh use and for chili where you want sweetness without bite. It stores only 1 to 2 months, so use it within a few weeks of harvest. Yield: 1 to 2 pounds per plant in ideal conditions.
- **Vidalia (Granex)**: A short-day onion grown in the South, but you can grow it in other zones if you plant in fall. It is very sweet, but also short-storage. Yield: 1 to 1.5 pounds per bulb.

### Pungent and bold
- **Yellow Globe**: A classic storage onion with a strong, sharp flavor that mellows when cooked. It is a long-day variety, best for northern zones. Stores 6 to 8 months. Yield: 1 to 2 pounds per bulb.
- **Copra**: A hybrid yellow onion that is excellent for storage and has a strong, spicy bite. It is a long-day variety, reliable in zones 3 to 7. Stores up to 10 months. Yield: 1 to 1.5 pounds per bulb.

### Red onions for color and bite
- **Red Burgermaster**: A red onion with a mild to moderate pungency and a beautiful deep color that adds visual appeal to chili. It stores 3 to 4 months. Yield: 1 to 1.5 pounds per bulb.
- **Red Wethersfield**: An heirloom red with a sharp, spicy flavor that holds up well in cooking. It is a long-day variety, stores 4 to 5 months. Yield: 1 to 1.5 pounds.

## How to grow onions for chili

Onions are cool-season crops. Plant sets or transplants in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked, or in fall for overwintering in mild zones. Here are the steps:

1. **Choose your variety based on your day length**: Long-day onions need 14 to 16 hours of daylight, best for zones 3 to 7. Short-day onions need 10 to 12 hours, best for zones 8 and warmer. Intermediate-day onions work in zones 5 to 7.
2. **Prepare the soil**: Onions prefer loose, well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0 to 7.0. Add compost before planting.
3. **Plant sets or transplants**: Plant sets 1 inch deep, 4 inches apart, in rows 12 inches apart. Transplants go 1 inch deep, 4 to 6 inches apart.
4. **Water consistently**: Onions need about 1 inch of water per week. Mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds.
5. **Fertilize**: Side-dress with nitrogen every 3 weeks until bulbs start to form. Stop fertilizing when the tops begin to yellow.
6. **Harvest**: When tops flop over and dry, pull the onions and cure them in a dry, airy place for 2 to 3 weeks.

## Using your onions in chili

For the best chili, use a combination: start with a sweet onion like 'Walla Walla' for the base, and add a pungent 'Copra' or 'Red Wethersfield' at the end for a final kick. Dice them finely, and sauté in oil until translucent before adding other ingredients. This caramelizes the sugars and deepens the flavor.

If you grow a lot, you can freeze chopped onions for later. Simply chop, spread on a baking sheet, freeze, then bag. They will keep for 6 months.

## FAQ

**Can I use any onion for chili?**
Yes, but the flavor will vary. Sweet onions like 'Walla Walla' will give a milder, sweeter result, while pungent ones like 'Copra' will be more assertive. Choose based on your preference and the chili's spice level.

**What is the best onion for a spicy chili?**
A sweet onion like 'Walla Walla' or 'Vidalia' balances the heat well. The sweetness complements the spice without competing.

**How long do onions store?**
Storage varies: sweet onions last 1 to 2 months, while pungent storage onions like 'Copra' can last 8 to 10 months if cured properly and kept in a cool, dry place.

**Can I grow onions from seed?**
Yes, but it takes longer. Start seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before the last frost, then transplant. Sets are easier for beginners.

## The bottom line

For chili, grow a mix: a sweet onion like 'Walla Walla' for the base, and a pungent 'Copra' or 'Red Wethersfield' for depth. Plant according to your day length, give them consistent water and nitrogen, and harvest when tops flop. With proper curing, you can enjoy your own onions in chili all winter.
