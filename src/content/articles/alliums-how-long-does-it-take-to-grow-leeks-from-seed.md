Growing leeks from seed takes about 120 to 150 days from sowing to harvest, depending on the variety and your growing zone. Most of that time is the slow growth after transplanting, so start seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost date. This guide gives you the exact timeline, step by step.

## The Full Timeline: From Seed to Harvest

Here is the realistic schedule for leeks from seed, assuming you start indoors in late winter or early spring. Times are averages for common varieties like 'King Richard' (fast) and 'American Flag' (slower).

| Stage | Time from sowing | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Germination | 7 to 14 days | Seeds sprout at 65-75°F (18-24°C). |
| Seedling growth | 8 to 10 weeks | Plants grow to pencil thickness, about 6-8 inches tall. |
| Hardening off | 7 to 10 days | Gradually expose to outdoor conditions. |
| Transplanting | 10 to 12 weeks after sowing | Set out after last frost, when soil is workable. |
| Growth to harvest | 60 to 90 days after transplanting | Leeks bulk up; harvest when stems are 1-2 inches thick. |
| Total | 120 to 150 days | From seed to full-size leek. |

## Factors That Affect the Timeline

Your exact harvest date depends on three main things: variety, climate, and how you transplant.

**Variety**
- 'King Richard': Fastest, ready in about 75 days from transplant. Best for early harvest.
- 'American Flag' (also called 'London Flag'): Classic, hardy, takes 90-100 days from transplant.
- 'Giant Musselburgh': Very cold hardy, slow but large, 100+ days. Good for overwintering in zones 5-7.

**Climate**
Leeks are cool-season crops. They grow best when temperatures are between 60-70°F (15-21°C). In hot summer areas (zones 8+), plant early so they mature before heat slows growth. In cold zones (3-4), choose faster varieties or start seeds even earlier.

**Transplanting method**
Leeks grow better if you "puddle" them: make a 6-inch deep hole, drop the seedling in, and water it in. This blanches the stem (keeps it white) and speeds up growth because the plant is not competing with weeds. Do not trim roots or leaves at transplant; that slows them down.

## Step-by-Step: Sowing Leek Seeds Indoors

Start seeds 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost date. For example, if your last frost is April 15 (zone 6), sow seeds in mid-February.

1. **Fill cell trays** with a seed-starting mix. Use 2-inch cells or soil blocks. Leeks have shallow roots, so deep cells are not necessary.
2. **Sow 2-3 seeds per cell**, 1/4 inch deep. Keep the soil moist but not soggy.
3. **Provide bottom heat** if possible. A heat mat (about $20-$30) speeds germination. Leek seeds germinate best at 65-75°F.
4. **Give strong light** once sprouts appear. Use a grow light for 14-16 hours a day, placed 2-3 inches above the plants. A simple shop light with LED tubes works for under $50.
5. **Thin to one seedling** per cell after 2-3 weeks. Snip the weakest at soil level, do not pull, to avoid disturbing roots.
6. **Fertilize weekly** with a half-strength liquid fertilizer (like fish emulsion) after the first true leaves appear.

Your seedlings are ready to transplant when they are about 8 weeks old and as thick as a pencil. If they are still thin, wait another week or two.

## Transplanting: The Critical Step

Leeks are not like onions; they do not bulb. You want a long white shaft, so you must blanch them by planting deep. Here is the method that works best.

1. **Harden off** the seedlings for 7-10 days before transplanting. Put them outside in a sheltered spot for a few hours each day, increasing time.
2. **Prepare the bed**: Loosen soil to 12 inches deep, mix in 2 inches of compost or aged manure. Leeks need fertile, well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0.
3. **Make holes** 6 inches deep and 4-6 inches apart, in rows 12-18 inches apart. Use a dibber or the handle of a trowel.
4. **Drop a seedling into each hole**, roots down. Do not fill the hole with soil. Instead, water it in; the soil will wash around the roots naturally.
5. **Water well** after planting. Keep the soil evenly moist for the first few weeks.

As the leeks grow, you can mound soil around the stems to blanch more, but the deep hole method is usually enough.

## Harvesting: When and How

You can start harvesting leeks when they are at least 1 inch in diameter, but many gardeners wait until 1.5-2 inches for a better yield. Use a garden fork to loosen the soil, then pull the leek by the base. Do not yank, or you will break the stem.

For a continuous harvest, plant a second batch in early summer for fall or winter harvest. In mild zones (7-9), you can sow directly in the garden in late summer for a spring harvest.

**Yield expectations**: A 10-foot row with plants 4 inches apart will give you about 30 leeks. That is enough for a family of four for several meals.

## Common Mistakes That Delay Harvest

- **Starting too late**: If you sow seeds in April, you will not get full-size leeks until September or October, which may be too late in cold zones.
- **Skipping bottom heat**: Leek seeds germinate slowly in cool soil. A heat mat saves 3-5 days.
- **Planting too shallow**: If you do not plant deep, you get short white stems and more green, which is tougher.
- **Letting them dry out**: Leeks need consistent moisture. Mulch with straw to retain water and keep soil cool.

## FAQ

**Can I direct sow leek seeds in the garden?**
Yes, in zones 6 and warmer, you can sow directly in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked. But direct sowing adds 2-3 weeks to the timeline because germination is slower outdoors. For most gardeners, starting indoors is more reliable.

**How long do leeks take to germinate?**
Typically 7-14 days at 65-75°F. At cooler temperatures (50-60°F), it can take up to 3 weeks. Use a heat mat to speed it up.

**Can I grow leeks in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 12 inches deep and 12 inches wide for 3-4 leeks. Fill with potting mix, plant seedlings deep, and water regularly. Harvest will be smaller, but it works for patios.

**Do leeks need full sun?**
Leeks prefer full sun (6+ hours), but they tolerate partial shade, especially in hot climates. In zones 8+, afternoon shade can prevent bolting.

## The Bottom Line

From seed to harvest, plan on 120 to 150 days. Start seeds indoors 8-10 weeks before your last frost, transplant after frost, and harvest 60-90 days later. Choose 'King Richard' for speed, 'American Flag' for reliability, and 'Giant Musselburgh' for winter hardiness. With consistent watering and deep planting, you will have a steady supply of leeks from midsummer into fall. It is a long wait, but leeks are one of the easiest alliums to grow, and the flavor is worth it.
