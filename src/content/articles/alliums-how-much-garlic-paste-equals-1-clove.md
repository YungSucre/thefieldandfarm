If you're substituting garlic paste for fresh cloves, the general rule is that 1 teaspoon of garlic paste equals 1 clove. That's the short answer, but let's get into the details so you can adjust for your own garlic's strength and your recipe's needs.

## Why the Conversion Isn't Exact

Garlic cloves vary in size. A small clove might be 1/2 teaspoon when minced, while a large one can be 1 1/2 teaspoons. Commercial garlic paste is often more concentrated because it's made from fresh garlic with a bit of oil and salt, so it can be stronger than a single clove. For most recipes, 1 teaspoon is a safe starting point, but taste and adjust.

## Measuring Garlic Paste vs. Cloves

Here's a practical breakdown:

- **1 small clove** = about 1/2 teaspoon of paste
- **1 medium clove** = about 1 teaspoon of paste
- **1 large clove** = about 1 1/2 teaspoons of paste

If a recipe calls for 3 cloves, you'll need about 3 teaspoons (1 tablespoon) of paste. For a 5-clove recipe, use 5 teaspoons (1 tablespoon plus 2 teaspoons).

## How to Make Your Own Garlic Paste

Making your own paste is easy and saves money. Here's my method:

1. **Peel** a head of garlic (or more) by crushing the cloves lightly with the flat side of a knife. The skins slip right off.
2. **Blend** the cloves in a food processor with a pinch of salt and enough olive oil to make a smooth paste. Use about 1 tablespoon of oil per cup of garlic cloves.
3. **Store** in a glass jar with a tight lid. It will keep in the fridge for 2 to 3 weeks, or freeze in ice cube trays for up to 3 months. Each cube is about 1 tablespoon, which equals 3 cloves.

A food processor costs $30 to $60 for a good model. If you don't have one, a mortar and pestle works fine, just takes more elbow grease.

## Buying Garlic Paste: What to Look For

Store-bought garlic paste is convenient. In 2026, expect to pay $3 to $5 for a 4-ounce jar. Look for products with minimal ingredients: garlic, oil, salt, maybe citric acid. Avoid pastes with added water or preservatives, they taste weaker. Refrigerate after opening and use within 2 weeks for best flavor.

## Adjusting for Freshness and Strength

Garlic's pungency varies. Freshly harvested garlic (in summer) is milder, so you might need a bit more paste. Older garlic, or paste that's been open a while, can be stronger. Always taste your dish before serving and add a little extra if needed.

## Using Garlic Paste in Cooking

Garlic paste is perfect for marinades, dressings, and sauces where you want even distribution. For stir-fries, add it at the end to avoid burning. For roasting, you can smear it directly on vegetables or meat. Remember, paste is more concentrated than minced garlic, so start with less and adjust.

## Storing Garlic Paste

Homemade paste keeps in the fridge for 2 to 3 weeks if you keep it covered with a thin layer of oil. Freeze in ice cube trays, then pop the cubes into a freezer bag for up to 3 months. Thaw what you need in the fridge or microwave.

## FAQ

**Can I use garlic paste in place of minced garlic in any recipe?**
Yes, but remember the flavor is more concentrated. Use 1 teaspoon of paste for each clove, and adjust to taste.

**How long does homemade garlic paste last?**
In the fridge, 2 to 3 weeks. In the freezer, up to 3 months. Always check for off smells or mold.

**Is garlic paste as healthy as fresh garlic?**
Yes, the health benefits are similar. Just watch the salt content in store-bought versions.

**Can I make garlic paste without oil?**
Yes, you can use water instead, but it won't keep as long. Use it within a few days.

## The Bottom Line

For most recipes, 1 teaspoon of garlic paste equals 1 clove. Adjust for clove size and your taste. Homemade paste is easy to make and store, and it's a great way to use up a bumper crop from your garden. Whether you buy it or make it, garlic paste is a time-saver that delivers real flavor.
