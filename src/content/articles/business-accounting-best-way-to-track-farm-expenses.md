The best way to track farm expenses is to use a simple spreadsheet or accounting app that categorizes every purchase and sale, and to review it monthly. Start with a free tool like Google Sheets or a paid app like QuickBooks, and set up categories that match your farm's actual spending. This guide walks you through the exact steps, tools, and figures you need to get started this week.

![Best way to track farm expenses](/images/articles/best-way-to-track-farm-expenses/section-1.jpg)

## Why tracking matters

If you do not track expenses, you cannot know if your farm is profitable. The IRS requires expense records for tax deductions, and lenders or grant programs may ask for them. Even for a small garden, tracking helps you decide what to grow next year
![Best way to track farm expenses](/images/articles/best-way-to-track-farm-expenses/section-2.jpg)
. For example, if you spend $80 on tomato plants but only sell $60 of tomatoes, you know to adjust.

## Choose your tracking method

You have three main options: paper ledger, spreadsheet, or accounting software. For most homesteaders, a spreadsheet is the best balance of cost and effort. Paper is cheap but hard to summarize. Software like QuickBooks automates reports but costs $30 to $60 per month. Here is a comparison:

| Method | Cost (2026) | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper ledger | $5-$15 for a notebook | No tech skills needed | Hard to sort, prone to errors |
| Spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel) | Free to $10/month | Flexible, easy to customize | Requires manual entry |
| Accounting app (QuickBooks, FreshBooks) | $30-$60/month | Automated reports, tax integration | Learning curve, cost |

For most gardeners and small farms, start with a spreadsheet. It is free, and you can upgrade later if needed.

## Set up your expense categories

Create categories that match your farm. Common ones include:

- Seeds and plants
- Soil and amendments
- Fertilizer and pesticides
- Tools and equipment
- Irrigation and water
- Labor (if you pay help)
- Marketing and packaging
- Utilities (electricity, water)
- Fuel and maintenance
- Insurance and permits

Keep it simple: 10 to 15 categories max. You can always add more later.

## Track every expense, no matter how small

Record every purchase, even a $2 packet of seeds. Use a consistent method: write it down immediately or keep a receipt jar. For cash purchases, note the date, amount, and category on the receipt. For card purchases, you can download transactions from your bank and match them weekly.

Here is a simple weekly routine:

1. Keep a small notebook or a notes app on your phone for on-the-go entries.
2. Every Sunday, enter all expenses into your spreadsheet.
3. Categorize each expense and add a note (e.g., "tomato cages" or "drip tape").
4. Review your totals at the end of each month.

## Use a spreadsheet template

Set up columns: Date, Vendor, Category, Amount, Notes. Here is an example row:

| Date | Vendor | Category | Amount | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-04-15 | Greenway Nursery | Seeds and plants | $45.00 | 20 tomato starts, 10 pepper starts |

You can create this in Google Sheets in 10 minutes. Add a SUM formula to total each category, and use a pivot table to see monthly totals.

## Track income too

Expenses are half the picture. Track sales from farmers markets, CSA shares, or roadside stands. Use separate columns or a separate sheet for income. This lets you calculate profit: income minus expenses. For example, if you sell $500 of vegetables and spend $300, your profit is $200.

## Review monthly and adjust

At the end of each month, look at your totals. Ask: Did I overspend on any category? Are there expenses I forgot? This review helps you catch problems early. For instance, if your water bill spikes in July, you might need to fix a leak.

## Use tools that automate where possible

If you want to save time, consider a paid app. QuickBooks Self-Employed costs about $30 per month and connects to your bank, categorizing transactions automatically. FreshBooks is another option at $19 per month. For a small farm, these are worth it if you have many transactions. Otherwise, a spreadsheet is fine.

## Keep receipts for tax time

The IRS requires receipts for expenses over $75, but keep all receipts for at least three years. Scan or photograph them and store them in a folder on your computer or cloud drive. Apps like Evernote or Google Drive can organize them by category.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Forgetting cash purchases: always record them immediately.
- Mixing personal and farm expenses: use a separate bank account or credit card for farm purchases.
- Not tracking income: you need both sides to know if you are profitable.
- Waiting until tax season: you will miss details and stress out.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need to track expenses if I only garden for myself?**  
A: Not for taxes, but it helps you budget and decide what to grow. You might find that growing your own tomatoes costs more than buying them, which is valuable information.

**Q: What is the cheapest way to track expenses?**  
A: A paper ledger or a free spreadsheet. Both cost under $15 and require no subscription.

**Q: Can I use a mobile app for tracking?**  
A: Yes, apps like Mint or Goodbudget are free and let you log expenses on your phone. They sync with your bank, but you still need to categorize farm items separately.

**Q: How long should I keep expense records?**  
A: Keep receipts and spreadsheets for at least three years, or longer if you have depreciation or carryover losses. Check with your tax advisor.

## The bottom line

The best way to track farm expenses is to start simple with a spreadsheet, record every expense weekly, and review monthly. Use categories that match your farm, and do not forget to track income. This habit takes 30 minutes a week but saves you money and stress at tax time. Start today with a free Google Sheet, and you will have a clear picture of your farm's finances by the end of the season.
