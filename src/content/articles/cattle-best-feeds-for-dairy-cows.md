The best feeds for dairy cows are high-quality forage (like alfalfa or grass hay), balanced grains (corn, barley, or oats), and a proper mineral supplement. For most homesteaders, a total mixed ration (TMR) or a simple forage-plus-grain approach works well. Start with clean, mold-free forage as the base, then add grain based on milk production, and always provide free-choice minerals and fresh water.

![Best feeds for dairy cows](/images/articles/best-feeds-for-dairy-cows/section-1.jpg)

## Forage: The Foundation

Forage should make up 40% to 60% of the dry matter in a dairy cow's diet. Good forage provides fiber for rumen health and milk fat production.

- **Alfalfa hay**: High in protein (17-20% crude protein) and calcium. Ideal for lactating cows. Look for leafy, green hay with fine stems. Price: $150-$250 per ton (2026).
- **Grass hay** (timothy, orchard grass, brome): Lower protein (8-12%) but good fiber. Cheaper: $100-$180 per ton. Often mixed with alfalfa.
- **Corn silage**: High energy, good for milk production. If you grow your own, cost is about $30-$50 per ton standing in the field. Harvest at 30-35% dry matter.
- **Baleage** (wrapped hay): Fermented forage, similar to silage. Convenient but can be more expensive per ton.

For a homestead cow, feed 2-3% of her body weight in forage dry matter daily. A 1,200 lb cow needs about 24-36 lb of dry matter from forage.

 That translates to roughly 30-40 lb of as-fed hay (since hay is about 90% dry matter).

## Grains and Concentrates: Energy for Milk

Grains provide energy (starch) to support milk production. The amount depends on milk yield: about 1 lb of grain per 3-4 lb of milk produced.

- **Corn (cracked or ground)**: Most common energy grain. Price: $6-$8 per bushel (56 lb) in 2026. Feed at 5-10 lb per day for a moderate producer.
- **Barley**: Good energy, slightly lower starch. Price: $7-$9 per bushel (48 lb). Often used in the West.
- **Oats**: Higher fiber, lower energy. Good for heifers or dry cows. Price: $5-$7 per bushel (32 lb).
- **Commercial dairy pellets**: Balanced with protein, minerals, and vitamins. Convenient but costlier: $300-$400 per ton. Look for 16-18% protein pellets.

Start with 2-4 lb of grain per day and increase gradually (over 2 weeks) to the target amount. Sudden changes can cause acidosis.

## Protein Supplements: When Forage Isn't Enough

If your forage is low in protein (like grass hay), you may need to add protein. Common sources:

- **Soybean meal**: 48% protein. Price: $400-$500 per ton. Feed 1-2 lb per day if needed.
- **Canola meal**: 36-38% protein. Price: $350-$450 per ton. Slightly cheaper alternative.
- **Field peas**: 23% protein, homegrown option. Feed 2-3 lb per day.

Test your forage for protein and total digestible nutrients (TDN) to know if you need supplements. A basic forage test costs $20-$40 at a local lab.

## Minerals and Additives: Small but Critical

Dairy cows need specific minerals for milk production and bone health. Provide free-choice loose minerals formulated for dairy cows (not beef).

- **Calcium and phosphorus**: Essential for milk and bone. A 2:1 ratio is typical.
- **Salt**: Provide free-choice or in the mineral mix.
- **Magnesium**: Helps prevent grass tetany, especially on lush pasture.
- **Baking soda** (buffer): If feeding high grain, add 0.5-1% of the grain ration to help maintain rumen pH.

A good dairy mineral mix costs $25-$40 per 50 lb bag. Offer it free-choice in a mineral feeder, and ensure it stays dry.

## Feeding Strategies for Homesteaders

### Option 1: Forage + Grain (Simplest)

Feed high-quality hay free-choice, and give grain at milking time. This works for cows producing under 30 lb of milk per day.

- Morning: 5 lb grain, then milk.
- Evening: 5 lb grain, then milk.
- Always have hay available.

### Option 2: Total Mixed Ration (TMR)

Mix all ingredients (forage, grain, minerals) together in a TMR mixer or by hand. This ensures the cow eats a balanced bite. Ideal for 2+ cows.

- Example TMR (for a 1,200 lb cow producing 40 lb milk):
  - 25 lb corn silage
  - 10 lb alfalfa hay
  - 8 lb cracked corn
  - 2 lb soybean meal
  - 0.5 lb dairy mineral

### Option 3: Pasture-Based

In summer, good pasture can replace some forage. But high-producing cows may need extra grain. Rotate pastures every 1-3 days to keep quality high.

Adjust based on body condition and milk fat test.

## Water and Feeding Management

- **Water**: Cows need 30-50 gallons per day. Provide clean, fresh water at all times.
- **Feed twice daily**: At consistent times. Cows like routine.
- **Clean feeders**: Remove leftover feed daily to prevent spoilage.
- **Gradual changes**: Any feed change should happen over 7-10 days.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Feeding too much grain too quickly: causes acidosis and off-feed.
- Poor quality forage: dusty or moldy hay reduces intake and milk.
- Ignoring minerals: can lead to milk fever or weak calves.
- Not adjusting feed for stage of lactation: early lactation needs more energy.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I feed only hay to a dairy cow?**  
A: Yes, if she's a low producer (under 20 lb milk) and the hay is high quality. But most cows will lose weight and produce less without grain. Add grain for better production.

**Q: How much does it cost to feed a dairy cow per day?**  
A: Expect $3-$6 per day for a moderate producer (40 lb milk) in 2026, depending on local feed prices. That's about $1,100-$2,200 per year.

**Q: Should I feed beet pulp or other byproducts?**  
A: Beet pulp is a good fiber source, but it's not necessary. Use it if it's cheap locally. Other byproducts like distillers grains can replace part of the grain, but watch protein levels.

**Q: How do I know if my cow is getting enough nutrition?**  
A: Monitor body condition (target 3 out of 5), milk production, and manure consistency. A cow that's too thin or too fat needs ration adjustment. Also, check milk fat and protein on your creamery report.

## The Bottom Line

The best feed for your dairy cow is clean, high-quality forage, balanced with grain and minerals based on her milk production. Start with a forage test, then adjust grain in small steps. Keep water fresh, feed at consistent times, and watch your cow's condition. Good feeding is the key to a healthy cow and a full milk pail.
