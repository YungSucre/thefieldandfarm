Garlic curing takes 2 to 4 weeks, depending on your climate and how you hang it. In dry conditions (humidity below 60%), you can cure in as little as 2 weeks; in humid areas, plan for 4 weeks or more. The goal is to dry the outer skins and neck until they are papery, but not so long that cloves dry out or sprout.

![How long does garlic take to cure](/images/articles/how-long-does-garlic-take-to-cure/section-1.jpg)

## Why curing matters

Curing is not optional. Freshly dug garlic has high moisture content and will rot or mold if stored immediately. Curing allows the garlic to enter dormancy,

which preserves it for 6 to 9 months. Skipping curing leads to shriveled cloves and a short shelf life.

## Step-by-step curing process

1. **Harvest at the right time**: Dig when the lower leaves are brown but the upper leaves are still green. In most zones, this is late June to mid-July. Use a garden fork to loosen soil, then lift bulbs by hand. Do not pull by the stems, as they may break.

2. **Shake off soil, do not wash**: Gently remove loose dirt. Washing adds moisture and invites mold. If bulbs are muddy, let them dry in the sun for a few hours, then brush off the soil.

3. **Cure in a dry, dark, ventilated space**: Hang garlic in bundles of 8 to 12 bulbs, or spread it on a wire rack or mesh screen. Ideal temperature is 70°F to 80°F with humidity below 60%. A garage, shed, or covered porch works well. Avoid direct sunlight, as it can cook the bulbs.

4. **Provide airflow**: Use a fan if the space is still. Good airflow prevents mold and speeds drying.

5. **Check progress weekly**: After 2 weeks, squeeze the necks. If they feel firm and the skins are papery, garlic is ready. If the necks are still soft, wait another week.

## How long by climate and variety

Curing time varies with humidity and bulb size. Here are typical ranges:

| Climate | Curing time | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Dry (Arid West) | 2 to 3 weeks | Faster drying, watch for over-drying |
| Humid (Southeast) | 3 to 4 weeks | Use a dehumidifier if needed |
| Temperate (Midwest, Northeast) | 2 to 4 weeks | Typical range |

Hardneck varieties like 'Music' and 'German Red' often cure in 3 to 4 weeks because of their thick necks. Softneck varieties like 'California Early' and 'Inchelium Red' cure faster, about 2 to 3 weeks, and store longer.

## How to tell when curing is done

- The outer skins are dry and papery.
- The neck is tight and no longer green.
- The roots are dry and brittle.
- The bulb feels light for its size.

Cut a test bulb: the cloves should be firm and the skin should peel easily. If the cloves are soft or the neck is moist, continue curing.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Washing bulbs**: Adds moisture, increases mold risk.
- **Curing in direct sun**: Can cause sunscald and cooking.
- **Curing in a plastic bag**: Traps moisture, ruins the crop.
- **Cutting stems too early**: Wait until fully cured before trimming.
- **Curing too long**: Over-dried garlic loses flavor and may sprout.

## After curing: trimming and storing

Once cured, trim the roots to 1/4 inch and cut the stems to 1 to 2 inches for hardneck, or braid softneck. Store in a cool, dark, dry place (50°F to 60°F, humidity 60% to 70%). Use mesh bags or baskets for airflow. Do not store in the refrigerator, as that triggers sprouting.

## Tools and costs

You likely have most tools: a garden fork ($20 to $40), a wire rack or mesh screen ($10 to $20), and string for hanging ($5). If you cure in a humid area, a small dehumidifier costs $50 to $100. A box fan ($20 to $30) improves airflow in still spaces.

## FAQ

**Can I cure garlic in the sun?** No. Direct sunlight heats the bulbs and can cause sunscald, which leads to rot. Always cure in the shade.

**What if my garlic starts to sprout during curing?** Sprouting indicates the garlic is past its dormancy. It is still edible, but the cloves will be less firm. Use it soon or plant it.

**How long does garlic last after curing?** Properly cured and stored garlic lasts 6 to 9 months for hardneck, and up to 12 months for softneck. Check monthly and remove any soft or moldy bulbs.

**Can I cure garlic in my kitchen?** Yes, if it is dry and dark. A pantry or a counter away from the stove works, but avoid high humidity from cooking. Use a fan to keep air moving.

## The bottom line

Curing garlic takes 2 to 4 weeks, and the exact time depends on your climate and variety. The key is to dry the bulbs slowly in a dark, ventilated space until the necks are tight and the skins are papery. Do not rush it: a proper cure is the difference between garlic that stores for months and garlic that rots in weeks. Start with a small batch, check weekly, and you will get the hang of it. Your reward is a pantry full of flavorful, homegrown garlic that lasts through winter.
