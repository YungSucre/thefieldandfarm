If you're new to canning, you need a water bath canner, a set of jars, lids, and a few basic tools. That's it. You don't need a pressure canner until you start canning low-acid foods like vegetables or meats. Here's exactly what to buy, what it costs, and how to use it this week.

## The Essential Supplies

Start with these items, all available at hardware stores or online. Expect to spend $60 to $100 for a complete beginner setup.

### Water Bath Canner

A water bath canner is a large pot with a rack. It's used for high-acid foods like jams, jellies, pickles, and tomatoes (with added acid). Look for an 21-quart capacity, which fits 7 quart jars or 9 pint jars. The Granite Ware 21-quart canner costs about $40 to $50. It's lightweight and heats evenly. Avoid thin aluminum pots; they can scorch.

### Jars and Lids

Use Mason jars made for canning. Ball and Kerr are the most common brands. A case of 12 pint jars costs $12 to $15. Quart jars are slightly more, $15 to $18. You need new lids each time, but bands and jars are reusable. A box of 12 lids costs $3 to $4. Buy extra lids; they're cheap and you'll need them.

### Essential Tools

- **Jar lifter**: $10 to $15. This grips jars safely in boiling water. Don't skip it; tongs will slip.
- **Canning funnel**: $5 to $8. Wide-mouth funnel prevents spills and keeps jar rims clean.
- **Bubble remover and headspace tool**: $5 to $7. Plastic stick with markings to measure headspace and release air bubbles.
- **Magnetic lid lifter**: $5 to $8. Picks lids out of hot water without burning your fingers.
- **Kitchen towels**: Use clean cotton towels to dry jars and handle hot jars.

### Optional but Useful

- **Jar wrench**: $5. Helps tighten bands without over-tightening.
- **Canning rack**: Often included with the canner, but

## Step-by-Step: Your First Batch

This week, make a simple batch of strawberry jam. It's forgiving and teaches the basics.

1. **Prepare jars**: Wash jars in hot soapy water, rinse, and keep warm in a 200°F oven or simmering water. Do not boil empty jars; they can crack.
2. **Prepare lids**: Simmer lids in a small saucepan at 180°F (not boiling) for 10 minutes to soften the sealant.
3. **Make jam**: Use a tested recipe. For strawberry jam, combine 4 cups crushed strawberries with 2 cups sugar and 1 packet of pectin. Bring to a rolling boil, stirring constantly, for 1 minute.
4. **Fill jars**: Place funnel on jar, ladle jam into jars, leaving 1/4 inch headspace. Use the bubble remover to release air bubbles.
5. **Wipe rims**: Damp cloth to clean rims, then place lid and band, tightening fingertip-tight.
6. **Process**: Place jars in canner with warm water (not boiling) covering jars by 1 to 2 inches. Bring to a rolling boil, then process for 10 minutes (adjust for altitude).
7. **Cool**: Turn off heat, remove lid, let jars sit in water 5 minutes, then lift out and place on towel. Let cool 12 to 24 hours. Check seals: lids should be concave and not pop when pressed.

## Choosing the Right Canner for Your Needs

If you plan to can vegetables, soups, or meats, you'll need a pressure canner. A pressure canner costs $80 to $150 for a 23-quart model like the Presto 23-quart. It's a bigger investment, but it's essential for low-acid foods. For beginners, start with water bath canning; you can always add a pressure canner later.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Using non-canning jars**: Mayonnaise jars or old pasta sauce jars are not tempered for canning and can shatter.
- **Overfilling jars**: Headspace is critical for sealing. Too little headspace can cause siphoning or seal failure.
- **Tightening bands too much**: Fingertip-tight is enough. Over-tightening can prevent air from escaping during processing, leading to buckled lids.
- **Skipping altitude adjustments**: At altitudes above 1,000 feet, processing times must be increased. Check a reliable altitude chart.

## FAQ

### Can I reuse lids?
No. Lids are single-use. The sealing compound only works once. Bands and jars are reusable, but always use new lids for a proper seal.

### How long do canned goods last?
Properly canned foods are safe indefinitely, but quality declines after 1 year. For best flavor, use within 12 months. Always check seals before storing and before eating.

### Do I need a pressure canner for tomatoes?
Tomatoes are borderline. If you add lemon juice or citric acid, they can be water bath canned. Otherwise, use a pressure canner. For safety, follow a tested recipe.

### What if my jars don't seal?
If a jar doesn't seal within 24 hours, refrigerate and use within a few days, or reprocess with a new lid within 24 hours. Don't force it; just use it soon.
