The best way to cook tomatillos depends on what you're making. For salsa verde, roasting brings out sweetness and depth; for a brighter, fresher sauce, boil them briefly. For a crisp addition to salads or tacos, use them raw. Here's how to do each method right, with times and temperatures that work.

![Best way to cook tomatillos](/images/articles/best-way-to-cook-tomatillos/section-1.jpg)

## Roasting: The Flavorful Choice

Roasting tomatillos is my go-to for most cooked dishes. It caramelizes their natural sugars and adds a smoky note that raw or boiled versions lack. You can roast on a baking sheet in the oven or directly on a grill.

**Oven method:** Preheat to 400°F. Remove the papery husks, rinse the tomatillos to remove the sticky residue, and pat dry. Place them on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Roast for 15 to 20 minutes, flipping halfway, until they're soft and lightly charred in spots. For extra char, broil for the last 2 to 3 minutes.

**Grill method:** Heat a grill to medium-high. Husk and rinse the tomatillos, then place them directly on the grates. Grill for 8 to 10 minutes, turning occasionally, until blistered and softened. This adds a deeper smoky flavor.

Once roasted, let them cool slightly before blending. Use them in salsa verde, enchilada sauce, or as a base for soups. Roasted tomatillos keep in the fridge for up to 5 days.

## Boiling: The Quick and Bright Option

Boiling is faster and preserves the tomatillo's tart, citrusy brightness. It's perfect when you want a lighter salsa or a base for green rice. The key is not to overcook; 5 to 7 minutes is enough.

**Steps:** Husk and rinse the tomatillos. Place them in a saucepan and cover with water. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer. Cook for 5 to 7 minutes, until they turn olive-green and are tender but not mushy. Drain and cool slightly.

Boiled tomatillos have a thinner consistency than roasted, so you might need to simmer the salsa to thicken it. Add a pinch of salt while boiling to enhance flavor.

**Tip:** Save the cooking water. It's full of flavor and can be used to thin salsas or as a broth for rice.

## Raw: For Fresh Salsa and More

Raw tomatillos are crisp and tangy, ideal for pico de gallo or a quick salsa that doesn't need cooking. They're also great sliced into salads or used as a garnish.

**How to prep:** Husk, rinse, and dice them. For salsa, combine with onion, cilantro, lime juice, and a serrano or jalapeño. Let it sit for 10 minutes to meld flavors.

Raw tomatillos are firmer than cooked, so they hold their shape. Use them within a day or two for the best texture.

## Which Method for Which Dish?

Here's a quick guide:

| Dish | Best Method | Why |
|------|-------------|-----|
| Salsa verde (cooked) | Roasting | Adds depth and smokiness |
| Salsa verde (fresh) | Raw | Bright, tangy, crisp |
| Enchilada sauce | Roasting | Richer flavor |
| Green rice | Boiling | Quick, light, and easy to puree |
| Soups or stews | Roasting | More complex flavor |
| Tacos or salads | Raw | Crunch and acidity |

## Step-by-Step: Roasted Tomatillo Salsa Verde

This is the recipe I make most often. It's simple and works with any of the cooking methods, but roasting gives the best result.

**Ingredients:**
- 1 pound tomatillos, husked and rinsed
- 1/2 white onion, cut into chunks
- 2 garlic cloves, unpeeled
- 1 to 2 serrano peppers, stems removed (adjust to heat preference)
- 1/4 cup chopped cilantro
- 1 tablespoon lime juice
- Salt to taste

**Instructions:**
1. Roast the tomatillos, onion, garlic, and serranos on a baking sheet at 400°F for 15 to 20 minutes, until soft and charred. Let cool slightly.
2. Peel the garlic and remove the stems from the peppers (keep seeds for more heat).
3. Blend everything in a blender or food processor until smooth. Add lime juice and salt.
4. Taste and adjust. If too thick, add a tablespoon of water at a time.

This makes about 2 cups. It keeps in the fridge for up to a week, or freeze for 3 months.

## Tools and Prices

You don't need fancy equipment. A basic baking sheet ($15 to $25) and a blender or food processor ($30 to $100) are enough. If you don't have a blender, a potato masher works for a chunkier texture.

For grilling, any grill works. A charcoal grill adds more smoke, but gas is fine.

## Common Mistakes and Fixes

- **Not removing the husk properly:** The husk is not edible. Always peel it off and rinse the sticky coating.
- **Overcooking:** Boiling too long makes them mushy and bland. Stick to 5 to 7 minutes.
- **Skipping the rinse:** The sticky residue can make your salsa bitter. Rinse well.
- **Using unripe tomatillos:** They should be firm but slightly giving, with a bright green color. Avoid yellow or soft ones.

## FAQ

**Can I eat tomatillos raw?** Yes, raw tomatillos are safe and delicious. They're crisp and tart, great in salsas or salads.

**Do I need to peel tomatillos before cooking?** No, the skin is thin and edible. Just remove the husk and rinse.

**How do I store fresh tomatillos?** Keep them in a paper bag in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. Remove the husks only when ready to use.

**Can I freeze cooked tomatillos?** Yes, puree or chop them and freeze in airtight containers for up to 3 months. Thaw in the fridge.

## The Bottom Line

Roasting is the best all-around method for cooked tomatillos because it adds flavor without much effort. Boiling is quicker and preserves brightness, while raw is best for fresh applications. Try the roasted salsa verde recipe above, and you'll see why tomatillos are a staple in my kitchen. Experiment with each method to find your favorite for different dishes.
