Growing shallots from sets is the easiest way to get a reliable crop. Sets are small bulbs, not seeds, so they skip the slow seedling stage and give you a head start. Plant them in early spring or fall, depending on your zone, and you will harvest full-sized shallots in about 90 days. This guide covers everything from choosing sets to storing your harvest, with real dates and varieties that work.

## Why grow from sets?

Sets are less fussy than seeds. They are less prone to disease, and they mature faster. You can buy them at garden centers or online, and they cost about $8 to $12 per pound, which plants a 10-foot row. For most gardeners, sets are the practical choice.

## Choosing the right varieties

Shallot varieties fall into two types: French (long, slender bulbs) and Dutch (round, larger bulbs). French types store longer, but Dutch types yield more per plant. For 2026, these are reliable choices:

| Variety | Type | Days to maturity | Notes |
|---------|------|------------------|-------|
| 'French Gray' | French | 100-110 | Classic flavor, stores well, but slower |
| 'Jermor' | French | 90-100 | Vigorous, good for northern zones |
| 'Golden Gourmet' | Dutch | 80-90 | Large bulbs, sweet flavor, good storage |
| 'Red Sun' | Dutch | 85-95 | Red-skinned, mild, productive |

If you are in zones 3-5, choose 'Jermor' or 'Golden Gourmet'. In zones 6-9, 'French Gray' and 'Red Sun' do well. Buy from a reputable seed company like Johnny's Selected Seeds or Burpee; avoid grocery-store shallots, which may be treated to prevent sprouting.

## When to plant sets

Timing depends on your climate:

- **Zones 3-5**: Plant in early spring, as soon as the soil can be worked, usually 4-6 weeks before the last frost. Fall planting is risky because the bulbs may not survive hard freezes.
- **Zones 6-7**: Plant in early spring (March-April) or in fall (October) for an early summer harvest. Fall-planted shallots often produce larger bulbs.
- **Zones 8-9**: Plant in fall (October-November) for a spring harvest. Spring planting is possible but yields smaller bulbs.

Soil temperature should be at least 40°F. If you plant too early, the sets may rot; too late, and they may not divide properly.

## How to plant sets

1. **Prepare the soil**: Shallots prefer loose, well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0. Work in 2 inches of compost or aged manure before planting. Avoid fresh manure, which can burn the roots.
2. **Spacing**: Plant sets 6 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart. This gives each bulb room to divide. If you plant too close, you get many small bulbs; too far, you waste space.
3. **Depth**: Plant the set so the tip is just below the soil surface, about 1 inch deep. Press the soil firmly to remove air pockets.
4. **Water**: Water thoroughly after planting, then keep the soil evenly moist but not soggy. Overwatering causes rot.

## Caring for shallots

- **Mulch**: Apply a 2-inch layer of straw or shredded leaves to retain moisture and suppress weeds. This also keeps the soil cool, which shallots like.
- **Fertilizer**: Side-dress with a balanced fertilizer (like 10-10-10) at 3 weeks, and again at 6 weeks. Use about 1 tablespoon per plant. Too much nitrogen leads to lush tops but small bulbs.
- **Watering**: Shallots need about 1 inch of water per week. If it rains, skip watering. Stop watering once the tops start to yellow, about 2 weeks before harvest.
- **Weeding**: Hand-pull weeds; shallow cultivation can damage the shallow roots.

## Harvesting and storing

Shallots are ready when the tops turn yellow and flop over, usually 90-110 days after planting. In spring-planted crops, this is mid to late summer. In fall-planted, it is early summer.

1. **Lift gently**: Use a garden fork to loosen the soil, then pull the shallots by the tops. Do not yank, or you may break the bulbs.
2. **Cure**: Let them dry in a warm, airy place out of direct sun for 2-3 weeks. Spread them on a wire rack or screen. The skins should become papery.
3. **Trim**: Cut off the tops to 1 inch and the roots to 1/4 inch. Do not wash them; just brush off soil.
4. **Store**: Keep in a cool, dry, dark place (50-60°F) with good air circulation. Braid them or hang them in mesh bags. Properly cured shallots store for 6-8 months.

## Common problems

- **Rotting sets**: Usually from overwatering or planting in cold, wet soil. Improve drainage and wait for warmer soil.
- **Bolting (flowering)**: Caused by temperature swings or planting too early. Remove the flower stalk to redirect energy to the bulb.
- **Pests**: Onion maggots and thrips can be issues. Use row covers to prevent egg-laying, and rotate crops yearly.
- **Disease**: Downy mildew and purple blotch are common in humid weather. Space plants well and avoid overhead watering.

## FAQ

**Can I plant shallots from the grocery store?**

Yes, but it is risky. Store-bought shallots may be treated with sprout inhibitors, and they may carry disease. For best results, buy certified disease-free sets from a garden supplier.

**How many shallots do I get from one set?**

One set typically produces a cluster of 5-10 bulbs, depending on variety and spacing. 'Golden Gourmet' yields more, 'French Gray' fewer but larger.

**Can I save my own sets for next year?**

Yes. Select the best-formed bulbs, store them in a cool, dry place, and replant them the next season. Over time, you can develop a strain adapted to your local conditions.

**Do shallots need full sun?**

They need at least 6 hours of direct sun daily. More sun means better growth and flavor. In hot zones, afternoon shade is okay.

## The bottom line

Growing shallots from sets is straightforward if you plant at the right time and give them loose soil, even moisture, and full sun. Choose a variety suited to your zone, space them properly, and harvest when the tops yellow. With minimal care, you will have a flavorful crop that stores for months. Start with a pound of sets, and you will likely have enough for both cooking and next year's planting.
