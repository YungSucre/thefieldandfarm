If you're grilling hot dogs this summer, the right onion can make or break the bite. The best onions for hot dogs are sweet varieties like Walla Walla or Vidalia for a mild crunch, or sharp types like Texas Sweet for a kick. For a classic ballpark taste, pickled onions are a fast, tangy upgrade. Here's how to grow and prep them for your next cookout.

![Best onions for hot dogs](/images/articles/best-onions-for-hot-dogs/section-1.jpg)

## Sweet onions: the classic choice

Sweet onions are the go-to for hot dogs because they're mild, juicy, and don't overpower the dog. They have lower sulfur content, which means less tear-inducing pungency. For fresh slices, choose varieties that store well but still taste sweet.

**Top sweet varieties:**
- **Walla Walla** (zone 4-8): Extra sweet, but stores only 1-2 months. Plant in early spring or fall in mild climates.
- **Vidalia** (zone 8-10): Actually a Granex type, very sweet, short storage. Grow as a short-day onion in the South.
- **Texas Sweet** (zone 7-9): Also called 1015Y Texas SuperSweet, named for its October 15 planting date. Sweet and crisp.
- **Candy** (zone 3-9): A reliable hybrid, sweet and stores up to 3 months. Good for northern gardens.

**Planting and harvest:**
- Plant sets or transplants in early spring as soon as soil can be worked (soil temp above 45°F).
- Space 4-6 inches apart, 1 inch deep, rows 12 inches apart.
- Harvest when tops flop over and start to brown, usually 100-120 days from sets.
- Cure in a dry, shaded spot for 2 weeks before storing.

## Sharp onions: for a bold bite

If you like a stronger onion flavor that stands up to chili or mustard, sharp varieties add punch. They're also great for grilling or caramelizing.

**Top sharp varieties:**
- **Yellow Globe** (zone 3-9): Classic storage onion, pungent, stores 6-8 months. Plant early spring.
- **Red Burgermaster** (zone 3-9): Red, sharp, crisp. Great for grilling or pickling.
- **White Bermuda** (zone 7-10): Mild but still sharper than sweet types. Good for fresh use.

**Planting and harvest:** Similar to sweet onions, but you can plant sets closer (3-4 inches apart) if you harvest some as green onions. For dry bulbs, space 4-6 inches. Harvest when tops die back.

**Tip:** For a milder sharp onion, soak sliced onions in ice water for 10 minutes before serving. That cuts the bite without losing flavor.

## Pickled onions: the ballpark upgrade

Pickled onions are a quick, tangy topping that pairs perfectly with hot dogs. You can make them in 30 minutes, and they last weeks in the fridge. Use any onion, but red or sweet varieties give the best color and flavor.

**Quick pickling recipe:**
- 1 large red or sweet onion, thinly sliced
- 1/2 cup apple cider vinegar
- 1/2 cup water
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1 teaspoon salt
- Optional: 1/2 teaspoon black peppercorns, 1 bay leaf

**Steps:**
1. Bring vinegar, water, sugar, salt, and spices to a boil.
2. Pack onion slices into a jar.
3. Pour hot liquid over onions, let cool, then refrigerate.
4. Ready in 30 minutes, but best after 24 hours.

**Cost:** A jar of pickled onions from the store runs $4-$6, but making your own costs about $1.50 per jar. You can also use leftover pickling liquid from other vegetables.

## Growing onions for hot dogs: practical tips

Onions are easy to grow if you match the variety to your day length. Here's a quick guide:

- **Short-day onions** (for zones 7-10): Plant in fall, harvest in late spring. Varieties: Vidalia, Texas Sweet.
- **Long-day onions** (for zones 3-6): Plant in early spring, harvest in summer. Varieties: Walla Walla, Candy, Yellow Globe.
- **Day-neutral** (for zones 5-7): Plant in spring, harvest in summer. Varieties: Candy, Red Burgermaster.

**Soil and water:** Onions need well-drained soil with pH 6.0-7.0. Add compost before planting. Keep soil consistently moist, but not soggy. Stop watering when tops start to yellow.

**Pests and problems:** Watch for onion maggots and thrips. Use row covers early, and rotate crops yearly. Avoid over-fertilizing with nitrogen, which causes soft bulbs.

## Using your onions on hot dogs

Here are three ways to serve your homegrown onions:

1. **Fresh slices:** Thinly slice sweet onions, soak in cold water for 10 minutes to crisp, then pile on the dog.
2. **Grilled onions:** Slice thick, brush with oil, grill 3-4 minutes per side until charred. Great with peppers.
3. **Caramelized onions:** Cook sliced onions low and slow in butter or oil for 20-30 minutes until brown and sweet. This is a crowd-pleaser.

**Storage:** Fresh onions keep in a cool, dry, dark place (40-50°F) for months if cured properly. Pickled onions last 2-3 weeks in the fridge.

## FAQ

**Can I use green onions (scallions) on hot dogs?**
Yes, sliced green onions add a fresh, mild onion flavor. They're not traditional but work well, especially in a slaw or relish.

**What's the best onion for caramelizing?**
Sweet onions like Vidalia or Walla Walla caramelize faster and sweeter because of higher sugar content. But any onion works; just add a pinch of sugar if needed.

**How long do homegrown onions store?**
Sweet varieties store 1-3 months; sharp storage onions like Yellow Globe can last 6-8 months if cured and kept in a cool, dry spot.

**Can I grow onions in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 8 inches deep and 12 inches wide. Plant sets 3-4 inches apart. Water regularly and feed with a balanced fertilizer every 2 weeks.

## The bottom line

For hot dogs, grow sweet onions like Walla Walla or Candy for fresh slices, sharp types like Yellow Globe for a bold bite, and make quick pickled onions for a tangy twist. Plant according to your zone, harvest at the right time, and you'll have a topping that beats any store-bought version. Start with a small patch this season and experiment with different varieties to find your favorite.
