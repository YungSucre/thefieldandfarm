If you're looking for a garlic supplement to help manage blood pressure, the best choice is aged garlic extract (AGE), specifically Kyolic brand, at a dose of 600 to 1,200 mg per day. Clinical studies show it can lower systolic blood pressure by about 8 to 10 mmHg in people with hypertension, which is comparable to some prescription medications. Other forms like garlic powder or oil have less consistent evidence, so stick with AGE for reliable results.

## Why aged garlic extract works

Aged garlic extract is made by soaking fresh garlic in ethanol for up to 20 months. This process reduces the harsh allicin content and increases beneficial compounds like S-allyl cysteine, which is more stable and better absorbed. The key mechanism: it boosts nitric oxide production, relaxing blood vessels, and it has antioxidant effects that reduce oxidative stress, a contributor to high blood pressure.

Aged garlic extract is the only form with consistent clinical backing. Garlic powder and oil often lose potency during processing, and allicin is highly unstable, so you may not get the same effect. Fresh garlic is great for cooking, but you'd need to eat several cloves daily to match supplement doses, which can upset your stomach.

## How to choose a supplement

1. **Look for standardized AGE**: Choose a brand that specifies the amount of S-allyl cysteine (SAC). Kyolic is the most studied, with a typical SAC content of 1.2 mg per 600 mg capsule. Other reputable brands include NutriFlair and Nature's Bounty, but check the label for SAC content.
2. **Check for third-party testing**: Look for USP, NSF, or ConsumerLab seals to ensure purity and accurate labeling. In 2026, ConsumerLab tests show that many garlic supplements contain less allicin than claimed, so third-party verification matters.
3. **Avoid enteric-coated pills**: They may prevent stomach irritation, but they can reduce absorption of active compounds. Plain capsules work fine.
4. **Start with a lower dose**: Begin with 600 mg per day for a week, then increase to 1,200 mg if tolerated. Split the dose into two capsules, morning and evening, to reduce any stomach upset.

## What to expect and how to track results

Garlic supplements are not a quick fix. You need at least 8 to 12 weeks of consistent use to see a measurable effect. Here's a practical plan:

- **Week 1**: Start 600 mg/day, take your blood pressure daily at the same time (morning, before food). Record it.
- **Week 2-4**: Increase to 1,200 mg/day if no side effects. Continue daily monitoring.
- **Week 8-12**: Evaluate your average systolic and diastolic readings. If you see a drop of 5-10 mmHg, that's a positive response.
- **Ongoing**: Continue if it's working, but always inform your doctor, especially if you're on blood pressure medication. Garlic can enhance the effect of drugs like warfarin or antihypertensives, leading to low blood pressure.

## Potential side effects and interactions

Aged garlic extract is generally safe, but some people experience heartburn, gas, or a garlicky odor. Rarely, it can cause allergic reactions. More importantly, garlic supplements can interact with:

- **Blood thinners**: Garlic inhibits platelet aggregation, so combining with warfarin or aspirin increases bleeding risk.
- **Blood pressure medications**: The additive effect can cause hypotension (too low blood pressure).
- **HIV medications**: Garlic may reduce their effectiveness, so avoid if you take protease inhibitors.

Always talk to your healthcare provider before starting any supplement, especially if you have a medical condition or take prescription drugs.

## FAQ

**Can I just eat more garlic instead of taking a supplement?**

Yes, but it's impractical. To match 600 mg of AGE, you'd need about 2-4 cloves of fresh garlic daily, which can cause digestive issues and bad breath. Plus, fresh garlic's allicin content varies widely, so you can't control the dose. A supplement is more reliable.

**How long does it take to see results?**

Most studies show significant blood pressure reduction after 8 to 12 weeks of daily use. Don't expect changes overnight. Consistency is key.

**Is aged garlic extract better than raw garlic for blood pressure?**

Yes, for supplements. Aged garlic extract has higher levels of S-allyl cysteine, which is more stable and better absorbed than raw garlic's allicin. Raw garlic has benefits, but it's not as practical for daily therapeutic dosing.

**What if I'm already on blood pressure medication?**

Garlic can lower blood pressure further, so you may need a dose adjustment. Always monitor your readings and work with your doctor to avoid hypotension. Never stop or change your medication without medical advice.
