In USDA zone 9, you can plant corn twice a year: once in late winter for an early summer crop, and again in midsummer for a fall harvest. The exact dates depend on your local frost dates and soil temperature, but the general windows are February 15 to March 15 for the spring crop, and July 1 to August 1 for the fall crop. Corn needs warm soil (at least 60°F) and consistent moisture, so timing is critical to avoid heat stress during pollination.

## Understanding Zone 9's Growing Season

Zone 9 has a long growing season, typically 240 to 300 days, with last frost dates ranging from February 1 to March 1, and first frost dates from November 30 to December 30. This allows for two corn crops, but you must work around the intense summer heat. Corn pollinates best when temperatures are between 60°F and 85°F. When daytime highs exceed 95°F, pollen can become sterile, leading to poor kernel set. Therefore, your spring crop should mature before the hottest part of summer, and your fall crop should tassel after the peak heat has passed.

## Spring Planting: February to March

For the spring crop, aim to plant when the soil temperature is consistently above 60°F. In zone 9, this often happens by mid-February in coastal areas, but may be later in inland valleys. Use a soil thermometer to check at 2 inches deep. If you plant too early, seeds may rot in cold, wet soil. If you plant too late, the corn will tassel during the June heat, which can reduce yields.

- **Planting dates**: February 15 to March 15, depending on your microclimate.
- **Soil temperature**: At least 60°F, ideally 65°F to 75°F.
- **Spacing**: Plant seeds 1 to 2 inches deep, 9 to 12 inches apart in rows 30 to 36 inches apart. For block planting, space plants 12 inches apart in all directions.
- **Varieties**: Choose early-maturing varieties (70 to 80 days) to beat the heat. Good options include 'Early Sunglow' (70 days), 'Golden Bantam' (78 days), or 'Silver Queen' (92 days, but may be too late for spring in hot areas). For a reliable spring crop, stick with 70 to 80 day varieties.

## Fall Planting: July to August

Your second crop is planted in midsummer, so the corn matures in the cooler fall weather. This is often more reliable than the spring crop because temperatures are more moderate during pollination. However, you must ensure the corn has enough time to mature before the first frost. Count backward from your first frost date (usually late November to December) using the days to maturity of your chosen variety, then add about 10 days for harvest flexibility.

- **Planting dates**: July 1 to August 1, with the exact date depending on your variety's days to maturity and your first frost date.
- **Soil temperature**: In July, soil is warm, so no issue. Just keep the soil moist for germination.
- **Spacing**: Same as spring: 1 to 2 inches deep, 9 to 12 inches apart, rows 30 to 36 inches apart.
- **Varieties**: Choose varieties with 75 to 90 days to maturity. 'Bodacious' (75 days), 'Peaches and Cream' (80 days), or 'Incredible' (85 days) work well. If you have a shorter window, go with 70 day varieties like 'Early Sunglow'.

## Soil Preparation and Planting Steps

Corn is a heavy feeder and needs rich, well-draining soil with a pH between 6.0 and 6.8. Prepare your bed by incorporating 2 to 3 inches of compost or aged manure, and a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at a rate of 2 pounds per 100 square feet. Work the soil to a depth of 8 to 10 inches.

1. **Test soil temperature**: Use a soil thermometer to confirm it's above 60°F for spring planting.
2. **Plant seeds**: Make a furrow 1 to 2 inches deep, drop seeds every 9 to 12 inches, then cover and firm the soil.
3. **Water thoroughly**: Keep the soil consistently moist until germination, which takes 7 to 10 days at 65°F to 75°F.
4. **Fertilize at knee-high**: When corn is about 12 inches tall, side-dress with nitrogen (e.g., blood meal or 34-0-0) at 1 tablespoon per plant, watered in.
5. **Mulch**: Apply a 2 to 3 inch layer of straw or grass clippings to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

## Watering and Care Through the Season

Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, and more during hot, dry spells. In zone 9, summer heat can quickly dry out the soil, so water deeply and consistently, especially during tasseling and silking (the most critical time for pollination). Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses to keep water off the leaves and reduce disease risk.

- **Weeding**: Keep the area weed-free, especially in the first 4 weeks. Corn roots are shallow, so hoe carefully.
- **Pest control**: Watch for corn earworm and fall armyworm. Use Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) or spinosad sprays when silks appear, applying every 3 to 5 days until silks dry. A drop of mineral oil on the silk tip can also deter earworms.

## Harvesting and Expected Yields

Corn is ready to harvest when the silks turn brown and dry, and the kernels are plump and milky when pierced. This typically occurs about 20 days after silking. For a continuous supply, plant a few varieties with different maturity dates, or stagger plantings every 2 weeks within your planting window.

- **Yield**: Expect about 1 to 2 ears per stalk, depending on variety and conditions. A 10-foot row with 12-inch spacing yields about 10 to 12 ears.
- **Harvest time**: Pick in the morning when temperatures are cool, and refrigerate immediately. Corn loses sweetness quickly, so eat or preserve within a few days.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Planting too early**: Cold soil leads to rot. Wait for 60°F.
- **Planting too late in spring**: Heat during pollination reduces kernel set. Stick to early varieties.
- **Planting a single long row**: Corn is wind-pollinated, so plant in blocks of at least 4 rows, not one long row, to ensure good pollination.
- **Skipping nitrogen**: Corn is a heavy feeder; without enough nitrogen, stalks are stunted and ears are small.

## FAQ

**Can I plant corn in zone 9 in winter?**
No, corn needs warm soil and air temperatures. Winter temperatures in zone 9 are too cold for germination and growth, even if frost is rare. Stick to late winter and midsummer plantings.

**How do I know when soil is warm enough?**
Use a soil thermometer. Insert it 2 inches deep in the soil at mid-morning. If it reads 60°F or higher for three consecutive days, it's safe to plant.

**What if I miss the spring planting window?**
If you miss the spring window, you can still plant in July for a fall crop. Just choose a variety with a short maturity (70 to 80 days) and ensure it will mature before your first frost.

**Can I grow corn in containers in zone 9?**
Yes, but it's challenging. Use a large container (at least 18 inches deep and wide) and plant 3 to 4 plants per container in a block. Choose a dwarf variety like 'Tom Thumb' (60 days) and water daily. Expect lower yields.

## The Bottom Line

In zone 9, timing is everything for corn. Plant in late February to mid-March for a spring crop, and again in July to early August for a fall crop. Always check soil temperature, choose early-maturing varieties, and water consistently. With these dates and practices, you can enjoy two harvests of fresh sweet corn each year. Start by testing your soil temperature this week, and prepare your beds with compost and fertilizer so you're ready to plant at the first opportunity.
