If you are looking for a farm stay in Ireland, the best options combine working farms with comfortable lodging, hands-on activities, and a chance to learn about Irish agriculture. This guide covers top picks across the country, what to expect, and how to book, with realistic prices and practical advice.

## What to expect from an Irish farm stay

Farm stays in Ireland range from a spare room in a working dairy farm to a self-catering cottage on a sheep or cattle farm. Most include a hearty Irish breakfast, access to farm animals, and often a tour or activity. You will not find luxury resorts; you will find genuine hospitality and a chance to see daily farm life.

Expect to pay:

- Bed and breakfast: $80 to $140 per night for two people, depending on region and season.
- Self-catering cottage: $150 to $250 per night for a family-sized unit, with a minimum stay of 2 to 3 nights in peak season.
- Activity packages (e.g., sheep herding, cheese making): $30 to $60 per person, often included in the stay price.

Most farm stays are open from March to October, with some open year-round. Book 2 to

## Top farm stays by region

### 1. Dingle Peninsula, County Kerry: Dingle Farm Stay

This working sheep and cattle farm offers two en-suite rooms in the farmhouse. You can join the farmer for morning feeding, watch sheepdog demonstrations, and hike the coastal trails. The farm is a 10-minute drive from Dingle town.

- Price: $110 per night for two, including breakfast.
- Best for: families, hikers, and those wanting a classic sheep farm experience.
- Booking: direct via their website or through Airbnb.

### 2. Burren, County Clare: Burren Farm Cottage

A 200-year-old stone cottage on a 50-acre farm that raises rare Irish Moiled cattle. The cottage sleeps four, has a full kitchen, and you can join the farmer for evening feeding. The Burren's limestone landscape is ideal for walking.

- Price: $180 per night, 3-night minimum in summer.
- Best for: self-catering families, nature lovers.
- Booking: through their own site or Irish Farm Holidays.

### 3. County Wicklow: Green Valley Farm

This dairy farm is 30 minutes from Dublin, making it an easy first stop. They offer a "milk to cheese" workshop where you make your own soft cheese. The farm has a modern B&B wing with four rooms.

- Price: $95 per night for two, breakfast included; workshop $35 per person.
- Best for: first-timers, cheese enthusiasts.
- Booking: via their website or phone.

### 4. County Cork: Sheep's Head Farm

On the Sheep's Head Peninsula, this farm runs a small flock of pedigree Suffolk sheep. They offer a "shepherd for a day" experience: you help with feeding, checking fences, and if lucky, lambing in spring. Accommodation is a cozy two-bedroom cottage.

- Price: $160 per night, 2-night minimum.
- Best for: hands-on learners, photographers.
- Booking: through their website or via email.

## How to choose the right farm stay for you

Consider what you want to do:

- If you want to work alongside the farmer, choose a B&B on a working farm, not a self-catering cottage. Ask if you can join chores.
- If you prefer privacy, a self-catering cottage is better, but you may have less interaction.
- If you are traveling with children, look for farms with animals they can pet and feed. Many offer pony rides or tractor tours.
- If you are a gardener, ask about the farm's vegetable garden or polytunnel. Some farms will let you help harvest.

Always confirm the level of involvement before booking. Some farms offer guided activities, others just let you wander.

## What to pack and practical tips

- Pack waterproof boots and a rain jacket. Ireland is wet, even in summer.
- Bring layers: temperatures range from 50°F to 70°F (10°C to 21°C) in summer.
- If you plan to help with chores, bring old clothes and work gloves.
- Most farm stays provide towels and bedding, but check for self-catering cottages.
- Driving is essential. Rent a car at the airport; public transport is sparse in rural areas.
- Book directly with the farm when possible to avoid platform fees and to ask specific questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are farm stays in Ireland suitable for children?

Yes, most are family-friendly. Look for farms that list children's activities, such as feeding lambs or collecting eggs. Always supervise children around livestock and machinery.

### Do I need to help with farm work?

No, it is optional. Some guests enjoy joining chores, but it is never required. If you want to help, ask the host when you arrive.

### What is the best time of year to visit?

Spring (April to June) is lambing season, which is a highlight. Summer (July to August) has the best weather and longer days. Autumn (September to October) is quieter and often cheaper.

### Can I bring my dog?

Some farms allow dogs in self-catering cottages, but rarely in the main house. Always ask before booking, and note that farms have livestock, so dogs must be kept on a lead.

## The bottom line

The best farm stays in Ireland are those that match your interests: a working sheep farm in Kerry for hands-on experience, a rare-breed cattle farm in Clare for solitude, a dairy farm near Dublin for convenience, or a Suffolk sheep farm in Cork for a shepherd experience. Prices range from $80 to $250 per night, and booking directly with the farm gives you the most flexibility. Pack for rain, rent a car, and ask questions before you go. You will leave with a better understanding of Irish farming and a full stomach.

For more options, check Irish Farm Holidays (irishfarmholidays.com) or local tourism boards. Book early for summer, and enjoy the green fields.
