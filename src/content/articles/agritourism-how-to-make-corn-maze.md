Making a corn maze is a multi-month project that starts with a design and ends with a field of pathways that thrill visitors. The key is to plan backward from your opening date, choose the right corn variety, and cut paths at the right time. Here is the step-by-step process that works for small farms and homesteads.

## Planning your maze

Start 6 to 8 months before opening day. In January or February, decide on a theme and draw a design. Keep it simple for your first year: a single large shape with one main path and a few dead ends is easier to cut and maintain. Use graph paper or a digital tool like Adobe Illustrator or even PowerPoint to map out the maze on a grid, where each square represents a 3-foot by 3-foot area (the typical row spacing).

Your maze size depends on your land and audience. A 1-acre maze (about 200 by 200 feet) is manageable for a first timer and can host 100 to 200 visitors per day. A 2-acre maze gives more room for multiple paths and longer walk times, but it takes twice the cutting and maintenance. For 2026, expect to pay $150 to $300 per acre for seed corn, depending on variety and whether you buy treated seed.

## Choosing the right corn

Use field corn, not sweet corn. Field corn grows taller (8 to 10 feet), has thicker stalks, and holds up better to foot traffic. Sweet corn is shorter and more fragile. For a maze, you want a variety that reaches at least 8 feet tall by the time you cut paths, which is usually 8 to 10 weeks after planting.

Recommended varieties for mazes:

| Variety | Height | Days to maturity | Best for |
|---------|--------|------------------|----------|
| 'Pioneer 35F44' | 9-10 ft | 110-115 | Northern zones (3-6) |
| 'DeKalb DKC62-08' | 8-9 ft | 105-110 | Midwest and Northeast |
| 'Pioneer 1498' | 9-10 ft | 115-120 | Southern zones (7-9) |

These are standard field corn hybrids available from seed dealers like Pioneer or DeKalb. Order seed by February or March to ensure availability. For a 1-acre maze, you will need about 30,000 seeds (roughly 8 to 10 pounds).

## Planting and fertilizing

Plant corn after the last frost date for your zone, when soil temperature is at least 50°F. In USDA zones 5 and 6, that is typically mid-May. In zones 7 and 8, late April to early May. Use a corn planter or a precision seeder to plant seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep, spaced 6 to 8 inches apart within rows, with rows 30 inches apart. This gives a stalk density of about 30,000 plants per acre, which is thick enough for a solid maze but not so dense that cutting is hard.

Fertilize at planting with a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at 300 pounds per acre, then side-dress with nitrogen (urea or ammonium nitrate) at 100 pounds per acre when corn is knee-high. This costs about $80 to $120 per acre for fertilizer in 2026. Water is critical: corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, so plan for irrigation if you are in a dry area.

## Cutting the maze

Cut paths when the corn is 4 to 5 feet tall, usually 6 to 8 weeks after planting. This timing lets you see the rows clearly but keeps the corn from being too tall to work in. Use a string trimmer with a heavy-duty blade (like a brush cutter) or a walk-behind mower with a mulching blade. For precision, mark the paths with flags or spray paint before cutting. Walk the maze design with a GPS app or printed map to guide you.

Cut paths 3 feet wide to allow two people to pass. For a 1-acre maze, cutting takes 8 to 12 hours with a string trimmer. If you have a tractor with a mower, you can cut wider paths faster, but you will need to be careful not to damage the corn you want to keep.

After cutting, remove the cut stalks from the paths so visitors do not trip. You can compost them or leave them to dry in piles.

## Marketing and pricing

Open your maze in late September or early October, when corn is at full height. Advertise on social media, local event calendars, and with a sign on your road. Price admission at $8 to $12 per person, with discounts for children under 12 and groups. For a 1-acre maze, you can expect 500 to 1,000 visitors over a 4-week season, generating $4,000 to $12,000 in revenue, but this varies widely by location and marketing.

Offer extras like a pumpkin patch, hayrides, or a corn pit to increase time on site and revenue. These add-ons can double your per-visitor income.

## Maintenance during the season

Check the maze daily for fallen stalks or holes in the walls. Corn can lean or fall after heavy rain or wind. Prop up fallen stalks with a stake or replant if early. Keep paths clear of debris. If you have a lot of visitors, consider trimming the walls back slightly to keep paths open.

Also, plan for safety: mark exits clearly, have a map at the entrance, and consider a phone number for lost visitors. For a maze larger than 1 acre, hire staff to monitor entrances and exits.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to grow a corn maze?**
From planting to full height, about 8 to 10 weeks. You need to plant in late spring and cut paths in mid-summer, then wait for fall to open.

**Can I use sweet corn for a maze?**
Technically yes, but sweet corn is shorter and weaker. It will not create the tall, dense walls that make a maze challenging. Field corn is the standard choice.

**What if I do not have a tractor?**
You can cut a maze with a string trimmer or a walk-behind mower. It takes longer, but for a small maze (half an acre or less), it is doable in a weekend.

**How much does it cost to start a corn maze?**
Seed and fertilizer cost $200 to $400 per acre. Cutting equipment, if you need to buy a trimmer, adds $200 to $500. Marketing and insurance (if you charge admission) can add another $500 to $1,000. Total startup for a 1-acre maze: $1,000 to $2,000.

## The bottom line

A corn maze is a profitable agritourism venture if you plan carefully and start small. Choose field corn, plant on time, cut paths when the corn is 4 to 5 feet tall, and market to local families. Expect to invest 20 to 30 hours of labor over the season, but the payoff can be a solid fall income stream. Start with a simple design and expand next year.
