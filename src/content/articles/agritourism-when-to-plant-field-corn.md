Plant field corn when the soil temperature at 2 inches deep reaches 50°F (10°C) and the forecast shows no hard freeze for at least a week. For most zones, that means late April to mid-May, but the exact date varies by your location and microclimate. Here is how to nail the timing for your garden or small farm.

## Why timing matters

Field corn (Zea mays) is warm-season and frost-sensitive. Planting too early in cold, wet soil leads to poor germination and seedling rot. Planting too late shortens the growing season, which can reduce yield and leave immature ears before fall frost. The goal is to get seeds into soil that is warm enough for quick germination, while still giving the crop enough days to mature.

## Soil temperature is the real guide

Air temperature is unreliable; soil temperature is what counts. Corn seeds germinate slowly below 50°F and may rot in cold, wet soil. The ideal range is 50°F to 65°F. Use a soil thermometer (available for $10 to $20 at garden centers or online) to check at 2 inches deep in the morning, before the sun warms the surface.

If you cannot measure, use this rule of thumb: plant when the last frost date has passed and the soil has begun to warm, typically 2 to 3 weeks after the last frost. In most zones, this falls in late April or May.

## Planting dates by USDA zone

Here are typical planting windows for field corn across USDA hardiness zones. These are general guidelines; adjust for your local microclimate and current weather.

In warmer zones (8 and above), you can also plant a second crop in early summer for a fall harvest, but field corn typically needs 90 to 120 days to mature, so plan accordingly.

## Choose the right variety

Field corn varieties are classified by days to maturity. Match the variety to your growing season length.

- **Early season (80 to 90 days):** 'Pioneer 39D85', 'Dekalb DKC39-97', or 'Blue River 48A31'. Good for short-season zones (3 to 5).
- **Mid-season (90 to 110 days):** 'Pioneer P1197', 'Channel 210-79', or 'AgriGold A6659'. Works for zones 5 to 7.
- **Late season (110 to 130 days):** 'Pioneer 33Y74', 'Dekalb DKC61-88', or 'Golden Harvest G12A31'. Best for zones 7 and warmer.

For homestead use, you might also consider open-pollinated varieties like 'Reid's Yellow Dent' (110 days) or 'Bloody Butcher' (100 to 120 days) if you want to save seed. Hybrids offer higher yields and uniformity but require buying fresh seed each year.

## How to plant

1. **Prepare the soil:** Field corn prefers well-drained loam with a pH of 6.0 to 7.0. Test soil and amend with compost or aged manure a few weeks before planting. Avoid fresh manure, which can burn roots.

2. **Plant at the right depth:** Sow seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep. In sandy soil, go 2 inches; in heavy clay, 1.5 inches.

3. **Spacing:** For garden rows, plant seeds 8 to 10 inches apart in rows 30 to 36 inches apart. For a small patch, you can plant in blocks of at least 4 rows to ensure good pollination. Field corn is wind-pollinated, and a single row will have poor kernel set.

4. **Water:** After planting, keep the soil consistently moist but not soggy. Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, more during tasseling and silking.

5. **Fertilize:** Side-dress with nitrogen when plants are about knee-high (12 to 18 inches). Use 1 pound of actual nitrogen per 100 square feet, or follow soil test recommendations. Apply as urea or blood meal, and water it in.

## Tools and costs

For a small plot (under 1/4 acre), hand tools are fine: a hoe, a garden rake, and a soil thermometer. For larger areas, consider a walk-behind planter like the EarthWay Precision Garden Seeder ($50 to $70) or a push planter for multiple rows. Seed costs vary: a 50-pound bag of hybrid field corn costs $40 to $60 and covers about 1 acre at typical seeding rates (20,000 to 30,000 seeds per acre). For a 100-foot row, you need about 1/4 pound of seed, costing $1 to $3.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Planting too early:** Cold, wet soil causes seed rot. Wait for 50°F.
- **Planting too deep or too shallow:** Too deep delays emergence; too shallow dries out.
- **Skipping soil test:** Corn is a heavy feeder; without proper pH and nutrients, yields drop.
- **Planting a single row:** Poor pollination leads to sparse ears. Always plant in blocks.
- **Ignoring frost forecast:** A late frost can kill seedlings. Have row cover or be ready to replant.

## FAQ

**Can I plant field corn in containers?**
No. Field corn grows 6 to 10 feet tall with deep roots; containers restrict growth and yield. Plant in the ground or in a large raised bed at least 12 inches deep.

**What if I miss the planting window?**
If you plant after the recommended window, choose a shorter-season variety (80 to 90 days) and plant as soon as possible. In zones 7 and warmer, you might still get a crop, but yields may be lower.

**How do I know if my soil is warm enough?**
Use a soil thermometer at 2 inches deep in the morning. If it reads 50°F or above for three consecutive days, it is safe to plant. Alternatively, sit on the soil: if it feels warm to your bare skin, it is likely ready.

**Should I soak seeds before planting?**
Soaking is not necessary and can cause seeds to crack if they absorb water too quickly. Plant dry seed directly.

## The bottom line

Plant field corn when soil temperature hits 50°F and frost risk is past, typically late April to mid-May for most zones. Choose a variety that fits your season, plant in blocks, and keep the soil moist. With proper timing and care, you will have a healthy stand of corn for feed, meal, or silage. Monitor your local forecast and soil temperature, and adjust accordingly. Happy planting.
