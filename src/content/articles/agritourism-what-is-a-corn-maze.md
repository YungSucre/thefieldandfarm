A corn maze is a field of maize (corn) that has been mowed, cut, or trampled into a pattern of paths and dead ends, designed for people to walk through and solve. It is a popular agritourism attraction, often open from late summer through fall, and can be a profitable addition to a farm or homestead. This guide explains what a corn maze is, what it takes to build one, and how to decide if it is right for you.

## How a corn maze works

A corn maze is planted just like a regular cornfield, but after the corn reaches a certain height (usually 3 to 6 feet), a design is cut into it using a mower or a specialized cutting tool. The cut paths become the maze corridors, and the standing corn forms the walls. Visitors enter, follow the paths, and try to find their way to designated checkpoints or the exit. Some mazes are simple, with a few turns, while others are elaborate, covering several acres with bridges, tunnels, and interactive games.

The corn itself is typically a field corn variety, not sweet corn, because field corn grows taller and sturdier, and it stays standing longer into the fall. Common varieties include 'Pioneer 1197' or 'DeKalb DKC62-08', but any tall, sturdy field corn works. The maze is usually open from when the corn is tall enough (around August in most zones) until harvest or until the corn dries and becomes brittle, often late October or early November.

## Why build a corn maze?

For a small farm or homestead, a corn maze can be a significant source of revenue. In 2026, admission prices typically range from $8 to $15 per person, with discounts for children and groups. A small maze of 2 to 3 acres can attract 1,000 to 5,000 visitors per season, depending on location and marketing. That means gross revenue of $8,000 to $75,000, but costs and labor must be subtracted. Building a maze requires planning, planting, cutting, and daily maintenance, plus liability insurance and staffing if you sell tickets.

Beyond income, a corn maze draws attention to your farm, which can boost sales of other products like pumpkins, cider, or hayrides. It also builds community goodwill and can be a family-friendly activity that keeps your farm relevant in the fall.

## How to build a corn maze: step by step

If you decide to build a maze, here is a practical sequence, based on what works for many small farms.

### 1. Choose a site and design

Select a field that is relatively flat, well-drained, and visible from the road. The size depends on your goals: a 1-acre maze is a good starting point for a homestead, while 3 to 5 acres is common for a commercial operation. Design the maze on paper or using a simple grid. You can draw a pattern, then transfer it to the field using GPS or by staking out key points. For a first maze, keep the design simple: a rectangle with a few loops and dead ends is enough to be fun.

### 2. Prepare the soil and plant

In most of the US, plant field corn in late April to mid-May, depending on your zone. For zone 5, aim for May 1 to May 15; for zone 7, April 15 to May 1. Use a standard corn planter set for a population of 28,000 to 32,000 seeds per acre, with row spacing of 30 inches. Fertilize according to a soil test, typically 150 to 200 pounds of nitrogen per acre. Irrigate if possible, especially during dry spells, to ensure even growth.

### 3. Cut the maze

The maze is cut when the corn is 3 to 4 feet tall, usually in late June to early July. Use a riding mower or a walk-behind mower with a sharp blade. If you have a complex design, you can use a string line or a GPS-guided mower. Cut paths that are at least 2 to 3 feet wide to allow comfortable walking. The cutting process takes several hours to a few days, depending on size. Be careful not to cut too early: if the corn is too short, the paths may not be visible; if too tall, the mower may struggle.

### 4. Maintain the maze

Once open, check the maze daily for damage, weeds, or broken stalks. Re-cut any paths that are overgrown. Keep the entrance and exit clear. If you have a lot of visitors, consider adding signs or maps at the entrance. Also, ensure that the maze is safe: remove any debris, and have a plan for emergencies.

### 5. Open and market

Open in late August or early September, depending on when the corn is tall enough. Advertise through local social media, farm directories, and word of mouth. Offer special events like flashlight nights or corn maze scavenger hunts to attract more visitors. Charge admission, and consider selling other items like pumpkins or hot cider to increase revenue.

## Costs and equipment

Here are realistic 2026 cost estimates for a small maze (2 acres):

- Seed corn: $150 to $250 per acre, so $300 to $500 total.
- Fertilizer and soil amendments: $100 to $200 per acre, so $200 to $400.
- Mower: if you already have a riding mower, no extra cost; otherwise, a used riding mower costs $1,000 to $3,000. A dedicated maze cutter (like a small tractor-mounted unit) can cost $5,000 to $15,000, but is not necessary for a small maze.
- Liability insurance: $500 to $1,500 per year for a small agritourism operation.
- Marketing and signs: $200 to $500.

Total startup costs, excluding land and labor, are roughly $1,200 to $5,000 for a small maze. Labor is the biggest hidden cost: planting, cutting, and daily supervision can take 100 to 200 hours per season.

## Realistic yields and revenue

A 2-acre maze can host 1,500 to 3,000 visitors per season if you are in a populated area and market well. At an average ticket price of $10, that is $15,000 to $30,000 in gross revenue. Subtract operating costs (insurance, marketing, maintenance) and labor, and you might net $5,000 to $15,000. Larger mazes with more attractions can earn more, but they also require more investment and staff.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Planting too late: if the corn is not tall by August, you lose the early season.
- Cutting too early: paths may disappear as corn grows.
- Overcomplicating the design: a maze that is too hard frustrates visitors.
- Ignoring safety: always have a way for visitors to exit quickly in an emergency.

## FAQ

### How long does a corn maze last?

A maze is typically open from late August through late October, about 8 to 10 weeks. The corn stays standing until it dries and becomes brittle, but most farms close by Halloween.

### Do I need special corn seed?

No, but use a tall field corn variety, not sweet corn. Field corn grows 7 to 10 feet tall and holds up better. Ask your local seed dealer for a variety suited to your area.

### Can I make a corn maze without a GPS?

Yes, for a simple design, you can stake out the pattern by hand. Use a measuring tape and string to mark paths, then mow. For complex designs, GPS is helpful but not required.

### How much land do I need?

A minimum of 1 acre is practical for a small maze. Half an acre can work for a very small maze, but it will be short and may not be worth the effort.

## The bottom line

A corn maze is a field of corn cut into a puzzle for visitors to walk through. It can be a fun and profitable agritourism venture, but it requires planning, labor, and a modest investment. If you have the land and the time, start small, keep the design simple, and focus on safety and marketing. With realistic expectations, a 2-acre maze can bring in thousands of dollars in the fall, while also drawing attention to your farm. It is not a get-rich scheme, but it is a rewarding way to use your cornfield creatively.
