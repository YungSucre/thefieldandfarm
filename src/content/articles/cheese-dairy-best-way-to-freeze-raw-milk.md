Freezing raw milk is a practical way to extend its shelf life, but it must be done correctly to maintain quality and safety. The best method is to freeze in glass jars with headspace, using a two-stage thawing process in the refrigerator. This preserves the flavor, texture, and nutritional value better than plastic containers or rapid freezing.

## Why freeze raw milk?

Raw milk typically lasts 5-7 days in the fridge. Freezing extends that to 3-6 months, letting you enjoy surplus milk from a high-producing cow or goat without waste. Freezing does change the texture slightly: the cream separates and the milk may look grainy afte

## Choose the right container

Glass jars are the best choice for freezing raw milk. They do not leach chemicals, and they withstand cold well if you leave enough headspace. Use wide-mouth Mason jars (quart or half-gallon) or freezer-safe glass bottles. Avoid plastic jugs: they can impart flavors and are more likely to crack. If you must use plastic, choose BPA-free freezer containers, but expect a slight taste change.

## Step-by-step freezing method

1. **Cool the milk quickly.** If the milk is fresh from the animal, chill it to 40°F (4°C) within 2 hours. Use an ice bath or a refrigerator. Do not freeze warm milk; it will warm the freezer and cause ice crystals.

2. **Leave headspace.** Fill each jar leaving 1 to 1.5 inches of space at the top. Milk expands about 9% when frozen. Without headspace, jars will crack or explode.

3. **Seal tightly.** Use new lids for Mason jars to ensure a good seal. For glass bottles, screw caps on firmly but not overly tight.

4. **Freeze quickly.** Place jars in the coldest part of the freezer, away from the door. Do not stack them until fully frozen. Rapid freezing creates smaller ice crystals, which means less texture damage. If your freezer has a "quick freeze" setting, use it.

5. **Label and date.** Write the date and type of milk (cow, goat, etc.) on the lid with a permanent marker. Use within 3 months for best quality, though it is safe up to 6 months.

## Thawing and using frozen raw milk

Thawing is just as important as freezing. The best way is to move a jar from the freezer to the refrigerator and let it thaw slowly for 24-48 hours. This preserves the milk's structure and reduces bacterial growth. Do not thaw at room temperature or in hot water; that can cause spoilage and off-flavors.

Once thawed, shake the jar vigorously to redistribute the cream and protein. The milk may look separated or slightly curdled, but that is normal. Use thawed milk within 3-4 days. Do not refreeze thawed milk.

**Best uses for thawed milk:**

- Baking and cooking (muffins, pancakes, soups)
- Making yogurt or cheese (heat it gently to 180°F first)
- Smoothies and shakes
- Drinking (if you can tolerate the texture change)

Avoid using thawed milk for fresh drinking if you are picky about texture; it is fine for most people, but the mouthfeel is different.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Freezing in glass without headspace:** This is the number one cause of broken jars. Always leave space.
- **Using plastic milk jugs:** They are not designed for freezing and can crack or leach chemicals.
- **Thawing on the counter:** This encourages bacterial growth and spoilage. Always thaw in the fridge.
- **Freezing large blocks:** Freeze in quart or half-gallon portions, not gallon jugs. Smaller portions thaw faster and are more convenient.
- **Forgetting to label:** You will not remember what is in the freezer in 3 months. Label everything.

## Does freezing affect nutritional value?

Freezing raw milk does not significantly reduce its nutritional value. Vitamins A, D, and B12 remain stable. Some vitamin C may degrade slightly, but raw milk is not a major source of vitamin C anyway. The main change is physical: the fat globules break down, causing separation. This does not affect the health benefits.

## FAQ

**Can I freeze raw milk in plastic bags?**

Yes, but use freezer-specific bags designed for liquids. Lay them flat to freeze, then stack. However, bags are more likely to leak and can absorb odors. Glass jars are safer and more eco-friendly.

**How long can I keep frozen raw milk?**

For best quality, use within 3 months. It remains safe to drink up to 6 months if kept at 0°F (-18°C) or below. After that, the taste may deteriorate.

**Can I freeze raw milk in ice cube trays?**

Yes, for small amounts. Freeze in ice cube trays, then transfer cubes to a freezer bag. Each cube is about 1 ounce. This is handy for adding to coffee or recipes. Thaw in the fridge or directly in hot liquid.

**Does freezing kill the beneficial bacteria in raw milk?**

No, freezing does not kill bacteria; it puts them in a dormant state. Once thawed, the bacteria become active again. That is why thawed milk should be used within a few days and kept refrigerated.

## The bottom line

The best way to freeze raw milk is in glass jars with 1 inch of headspace, frozen quickly, and thawed slowly in the refrigerator. This method preserves quality and safety for up to 3 months. Use thawed milk for cooking, baking, or making cultured dairy products. Avoid plastic jugs and counter thawing to prevent off-flavors and spoilage. With these steps, you can enjoy your raw milk year-round without waste.
