Two shallots typically weigh between 1.5 and 3 ounces (42 to 85 grams) total, depending on variety and size. For planting, aim for cloves that weigh about 0.3 to 0.5 ounces (8 to 14 grams) each, so two whole shallots in that range will give you roughly 4 to 8 cloves. For cooking, a recipe calling for "2 shallots" usually expects about 2 to 4 tablespoons of chopped shallot, which matches that weight range.

## Why weight matters for planting

When planting shallots, the size of the bulb directly affects the size of the harvest. Small bulbs (under 0.3 ounces per clove) produce smaller yields, while oversized bulbs (over 0.5 ounces per clove) may bolt or split. Aim for cloves that are uniform and firm, about the size of a walnut. If you buy shallot sets, they are often sold by weight: a 1-pound bag contains about 8 to 12 bulbs, so each bulb averages 1.3 to 2 ounces. That means 2 bulbs from that bag weigh about 2.6 to 4 ounces, which is on the heavier side for cooking but fine for planting if you break them into cloves.

## Average weights by type

Shallot varieties fall into two main types: French (banana-shaped) and Dutch (round). French shallots, like 'Échalion' or 'Jermor', are elongated and often weigh less per bulb, averaging 1 to 1.5 ounces each. Dutch shallots, like 'Ambition' or 'Griselle', are rounder and heavier, averaging 1.5 to 2.5 ounces each. Here is a quick reference table:

| Variety type | Example varieties | Average weight per bulb | Weight of 2 bulbs |
|--------------|-------------------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| French (banana) | Échalion, Jermor | 1.0 - 1.5 oz (28 - 42 g) | 2.0 - 3.0 oz (56 - 84 g) |
| Dutch (round) | Ambition, Griselle | 1.5 - 2.5 oz (42 - 70 g) | 3.0 - 5.0 oz (84 - 140 g) |

Keep in mind that these are averages for mature bulbs. If you harvest early, they will be smaller. If you grow in rich soil with consistent moisture, they may be larger.

## How to weigh shallots at home

You do not need a fancy scale. A basic kitchen scale that reads in grams or ounces works fine, and you can find one for $10 to $20 at any big-box store or online. Here is how to get an accurate weight:

1. Place a small bowl or plate on the scale and zero it out.
2. Add the two shallots, skins on, and record the weight.
3. If you are measuring for a recipe, peel them first, then weigh again. The skins account for about 5% to 10% of total weight, so peeled weight will be slightly less.

If you do not have a scale, use this visual guide: a medium shallot is about the size of a golf ball and weighs about 1 ounce. Two of those are about 2 ounces. A large shallot is closer to a tennis ball and weighs about 2 ounces each, so two would be 4 ounces.

## Why this matters for your garden

Knowing the weight of your shallots helps you plan your planting density. For a 10-foot row, you can plant about 20 to 30 cloves, depending on spacing. If each clove weighs 0.3 to 0.5 ounces, you will need about 6 to 15 ounces of bulbs for that row. That translates to roughly 4 to 10 whole shallots, depending on size. Buy your sets accordingly.

Also, if you are saving your own seed shallots, choose bulbs that are true to type and weigh at least 1 ounce each. Smaller bulbs may not have enough energy to produce a good crop.

## Cooking equivalents

In recipes, "2 shallots" usually means 2 medium bulbs, which yields about 2 to 4 tablespoons of minced shallot. Here is a quick conversion:

- 1 medium shallot (1 oz) = about 2 tablespoons minced
- 2 medium shallots (2 oz) = about 4 tablespoons (1/4 cup) minced
- 1 large shallot (2 oz) = about 4 tablespoons minced

If a recipe calls for "2 shallots" and you only have large ones, use one and a half, or adjust to taste. Shallots are milder than onions, so you can be flexible.

## FAQ

**Q: How many cloves are in a shallot bulb?**
A: Most shallot bulbs contain 2 to 4 cloves, but some varieties like 'Ambition' can have up to 6. The number depends on the variety and growing conditions. For planting, you can break the bulb into individual cloves, each of which will grow into a new bulb.

**Q: Can I use the weight of shallots to determine planting depth?**
A: No, planting depth is based on soil and climate, not weight. In most zones, plant cloves 1 to 2 inches deep, with the tip just below the surface. In cold zones (3-5), plant 2 inches deep; in warm zones (7-10), plant 1 inch deep.

**Q: Why do some shallots weigh more than others?**
A: Weight varies by variety, soil fertility, water, and harvest time. Shallots grown in loose, rich soil with consistent moisture will be larger. Also, French types tend to be lighter than Dutch types. If you want consistent weights, buy from a reputable seed company and follow a regular watering schedule.

**Q: How many shallots do I need for a pound?**
A: On average, about 8 to 12 medium shallots make a pound. If they are large (2 oz each), you will need about 8; if small (1 oz each), about 16. For planting, a pound of sets will cover about 15 to 20 feet of row.

## The bottom line

Two shallots typically weigh 1.5 to 3 ounces, but the exact number depends on variety and size. For planting, focus on clove weight (0.3 to 0.5 ounces) rather than whole bulb weight. For cooking, use the visual or kitchen scale method to match your recipe. Weighing your shallots is a simple habit that improves both your garden planning and your kitchen results.
