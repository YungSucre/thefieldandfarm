If you're growing garlic in Georgia, your best bet is softneck varieties, specifically artichoke and silverskin types. They thrive in our mild winters and long springs, and they store well. Hardneck garlic can grow here, but it's more finicky and often produces smaller bulbs. For the most reliable results, plant softneck garlic like 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red' in October or November.

## Why Softneck Garlic Wins in Georgia

Georgia's climate falls mostly in USDA zones 7 and 8, with some areas in zone 6 in the mountains. Softneck garlic is adapted to these milder winters. It doesn't need a long cold period to form bulbs, and it's more tolerant of our sometimes warm spells in late winter. Hardneck garlic requires a period of cold (below 40°F for several weeks) to trigger bulb formation, and our winters can be too short or inconsistent. Also, so

## When to Plant in Georgia

Garlic is planted in the fall, about 4 to 6 weeks before the first hard frost. In Georgia, that means:

- **North Georgia (zones 6-7):** Plant in late September to mid-October.
- **Central Georgia (zone 7-8):** Plant in October to early November.
- **South Georgia (zone 8):** Plant in November.

Soil temperature should be around 50°F for root growth. If you plant too early, the garlic may sprout and then get damaged by cold; if too late, roots won't establish before winter.

## How to Plant Garlic in Georgia

1. **Prepare the soil:** Garlic needs well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.5. If your soil is heavy clay, add compost or plant in raised beds. Work in a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at a rate of 2 pounds per 100 square feet.
2. **Break apart bulbs:** Separate cloves from the bulb, keeping the papery skin on. Choose the largest cloves for planting; use smaller ones for cooking.
3. **Plant cloves:** Space cloves 4 to 6 inches apart in rows 12 to 18 inches apart. Plant each clove 2 to 3 inches deep, with the pointed end up. In Georgia, a little deeper (3 inches) helps protect from winter temperature swings.
4. **Mulch:** After planting, cover the bed with 3 to 4 inches of straw or shredded leaves. This insulates the soil and suppresses weeds.
5. **Water:** Water thoroughly after planting. Garlic needs about 1 inch of water per week, but reduce watering in late spring to let bulbs cure.

## Caring for Garlic in Georgia

- **Spring:** In early spring, when shoots appear, side-dress with nitrogen fertilizer (like blood meal) at a rate of 1 pound per 100 square feet. Keep the area weed-free.
- **Watering:** Consistent moisture is key, but avoid overwatering, which can cause rot. Use drip irrigation if possible.
- **Scapes:** If you grow hardneck garlic, cut the scapes (curly flower stalks) in late spring to direct energy to bulb growth. They are edible and great in pesto.
- **Pests and diseases:** Watch for onion thrips and white rot. Rotate crops and ensure good air circulation.

## Harvesting and Curing

Garlic is ready to harvest when the lower leaves turn brown, usually in late May to June in Georgia. Gently dig up a bulb to check; it should be full and the skin tight. Harvest before the leaves are completely dry.

Cure garlic by hanging it in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated area for 2 to 4 weeks. Then trim roots and cut stems, leaving an inch. Store in a cool, dry place (50-60°F) with good air circulation.

## FAQ

**Can I grow hardneck garlic in Georgia?**
Yes, but it's less reliable. If you want hardneck, choose a variety like 'Music' and plant in late October. Expect smaller bulbs and shorter storage life. In South Georgia, hardneck may not perform well at all.

**When should I plant garlic in Georgia?**
Plant in October for most of the state. In North Georgia, you can plant in late September; in South Georgia, wait until November. The key is to plant 4-6 weeks before the first hard frost.

**How much garlic can I expect to harvest?**
Typically, one clove produces one bulb. A pound of seed garlic (about 10-12 bulbs) will plant a 10-foot row and yield about 10-15 pounds of garlic, depending on conditions.

**What's the best way to store garlic in Georgia's humidity?**
Store cured garlic in a mesh bag or basket in a cool, dry place. Avoid plastic bags. In humid climates, you may need to use a dehumidifier in your storage area. Softneck garlic stores longer than hardneck.

## The bottom line

For Georgia gardeners, softneck garlic varieties like 'California Early' and 'Inchelium Red' are your best bet for a reliable, bountiful harvest. Plant in October, space cloves 4-6 inches apart, and mulch well. With proper care, you'll have homegrown garlic that stores for months. Start with a few bulbs this fall, and you'll be hooked.
