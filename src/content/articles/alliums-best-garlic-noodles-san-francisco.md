If you're looking for the best garlic noodles in San Francisco, you want the versions that balance savory, buttery, and pungent flavors with a satisfying chew. The city's iconic style originated at Thanh Long in the Outer Sunset, and it's still a benchmark, but several other restaurants and food trucks have perfected their own takes. Here's where to go and what to expect, plus a simple recipe to make them at home.

## The Originals: Thanh Long and Crustacean

Thanh Long (4101 Judah Street) is the birthplace of garlic noodles in San Francisco. The Helms family created the dish in the 1970s, serving it alongside roasted crab. Their version uses fresh egg noodles tossed in a sauce of garlic, butter, oyster sauce, and a touch of fish sauce, finished with a shower of Parmesan. It's rich, garlicky, and slightly sweet. Expect to pay $15 to $20 for a side order, and the crab will set you back market price, often $40 to $60 per pound.

Crustacean (1475 Polk Street), run by the same family, serves a similar but more upscale version. Their garlic noodles are a must with the roasted crab, and the ambiance is fancier. Prices are higher, with noodles around $20 to $25. Both are excellent, but if you want the original experience without the crab, Than Long is more casual and affordable.

## Modern Favorites: Where Locals Go

Beyond the classics, several spots have earned loyal followings:

- **Golden Garlic Noodles at PPQ Dungeness Island** (2332 Noriega Street): This Vietnamese seafood spot serves a version that's less oily but just as flavorful. The noodles are tossed with a garlic butter sauce and topped with green onions. A side order is about $12 to $15.
- **Garlic Noodles at The Slanted Door** (Ferry Building): Chef Charles Phan's modern Vietnamese take uses fresh rice noodles, which gives a softer texture. It's pricier, around $18 to $22, but the view of the Bay makes it worth a splurge.
- **Food Truck: The Chairman** (various locations): Known for their bao, they also do a garlic noodle bowl with a choice of protein. It's a quick, satisfying option for under $15.

For a more casual, budget-friendly option, head to **Sunset's Outerlands** (4001 Judah Street), which occasionally features garlic noodles as a special. Call ahead to check.

## What Makes a Great Garlic Noodle?

The best garlic noodles share a few traits:

- **Noodle texture**: Fresh egg noodles (lo mein style) are ideal. They're chewy and hold the sauce well. Dried noodles can work, but they're less satisfying.
- **Garlic intensity**: The garlic should be prominent but not raw or harsh. It's often minced and cooked briefly in butter to mellow the bite.
- **Sauce balance**: A mix of oyster sauce, soy sauce, and a touch of sugar creates umami and sweetness. Butter adds richness, and a sprinkle of Parmesan adds salty depth.
- **Fresh herbs**: Chopped green onions or chives add a fresh contrast.

## How to Make Them at Home

You can recreate SF-style garlic noodles in 20 minutes. Here's a reliable recipe:

**Ingredients** (serves 2 as a main, 4 as a side):
- 8 ounces fresh egg noodles (look for lo mein or chow mein noodles in the refrigerated section)
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 6 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tablespoons oyster sauce
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 1/4 cup grated Parmesan cheese
- 2 green onions, sliced
- Optional: 1 teaspoon fish sauce for extra depth

**Steps:**
1. Cook the noodles according to package directions (usually 2 to 3 minutes in boiling water). Drain and rinse with cold water to stop cooking, then set aside.
2. In a large skillet over medium heat, melt the butter. Add the garlic and cook for 1 to 2 minutes until fragrant but not browned.
3. Stir in the oyster sauce, soy sauce, sugar, and fish sauce if using. Cook for 1 minute.
4. Add the noodles to the skillet and toss to coat. Cook for 2 to 3 minutes, letting the noodles absorb the sauce.
5. Remove from heat, sprinkle with Parmesan and green onions, and toss again. Serve immediately.

This version is close to the original, though you can adjust the garlic to your taste. For a more authentic touch, use a wok over high heat.

## Where to Buy Ingredients

In San Francisco, you can find fresh egg noodles at Asian markets like **Sunset Super** (26th Avenue and Irving) or **New May Wah** (707 Clement Street). Expect to pay $2 to $4 for a pound. Garlic is cheap, but for the best flavor, use fresh cloves, not pre-minced.

## FAQ

**Q: Are garlic noodles vegetarian?**
A: Traditional versions use oyster sauce, which is not vegetarian. You can substitute mushroom oyster sauce or hoisin sauce, but the flavor will change.

**Q: Can I use dried noodles?**
A: Yes, but they'll be less chewy. Use dried lo mein noodles and cook them al dente. You may need to add a splash of water to the sauce to help it coat.

**Q: What's the best way to reheat leftovers?**
A: Reheat in a skillet over medium heat with a little butter or oil. Avoid the microwave, which makes them mushy.

**Q: How much garlic is too much?**
A: In SF, more is better, but if you're not used to it, start with 4 cloves. The garlic mellows as it cooks, so don't be afraid.

## The Bottom Line

The best garlic noodles in San Francisco are at Than Long and Crustacean, but you can find excellent versions at PPQ and The Slanted Door. If you want to enjoy them at home, the recipe above is simple and authentic. For a true SF experience, pair them with roasted crab, but they're delicious on their own. Go early to avoid lines, and don't skip the Parmesan. Your kitchen will smell like garlic for a day, and that's a good thing.
