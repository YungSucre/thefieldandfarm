Onions take 90 to 365 days from seed to harvest, depending on your method and variety. From sets (small bulbs) you'll wait about 90 to 120 days, from transplants 100 to 130 days, and from seed 120 to 180 days for full-sized bulbs. The exact time hinges on day length (short, intermediate, or long-day varieties) and your USDA zone. Here's how to plan so you harvest at the right time, without guesswork.

## The three ways to start onions

You can grow onions from seed, sets, or transplants. Each has a different timeline and trade-off.

- **Seed**: Cheapest (a packet costs $2 to $4) but slowest. You need to start indoors 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost date. Expect 120 to 180 days from sowing to mature bulbs.
- **Sets**: Small, dormant bulbs (about $8 to $12 per pound). They're the fastest: 90 to 120 days from planting to harvest. Sets are less fussy but more prone to bolting (going to seed early) if exposed to cold snaps.
- **Transplants**: Young plants sold in bundles (about $10 to $15 for 50 to 100 plants). They give you a head start: 100 to 130 days from transplanting to harvest. You can also grow your own transplants from seed, which adds 8 to 10 weeks to the total time.

If you want the quickest harvest, use sets. If you want the widest variety selection, grow from seed.

## Day length: the hidden timer

Onions bulb based on day length, not just calendar days. Choose the right type for your latitude:

- **Short-day**: Bulb when days reach 10 to 12 hours. Best for zones 7 and warmer (roughly south of latitude 35°N). Examples: 'Texas SuperSweet', 'Red Creole'.
- **Intermediate (day-neutral)**: Bulb at 12 to 14 hours. Good for zones 5 to 7 (latitudes 35° to 40°N). Examples: 'Candy', 'Red Candy Apple'.
- **Long-day**: Bulb when days hit 14 to 16 hours. Best for zones 6 and cooler (north of latitude 40°N). Examples: 'Yellow Sweet Spanish', 'Walla Walla'.

If you plant the wrong type, you'll get lots of tops but small bulbs. For example, a long-day onion in Texas will never get enough daylight to bulb properly.

## Growth stages and what to expect

Here's a week-by-week breakdown for a typical long-day onion from sets in zone 6:

- **Week 1-2**: Roots establish. You'll see green shoots within 7 to 10 days if soil is 50°F or warmer.
- **Week 3-4**: Leaves grow rapidly. The plant is building energy for the bulb.
- **Week 5-8**: Bulb starts to swell. The neck begins to soften, and the leaves may flop over.
- **Week 9-12**: Bulb reaches full size. When the tops fall over and start to yellow, stop watering and lift the onions.

From seed, add 8 to 10 weeks for indoor growth. From transplants, subtract 2 to 3 weeks from the set timeline.

## How to know when onions are ready

Don't rely on the calendar alone. Check for these signs:

- The tops (leaves) fall over naturally, usually 50% to 80% of them.
- The neck feels soft, not firm.
- The bulb has a papery skin that's starting to dry.

When you see that, stop watering and wait 2 to 3 days, then lift the onions with a fork. Leave them in the sun for a day, then cure in a dry, shaded spot for 2 to 3 weeks. Curing is essential for storage; skip it and they'll rot.

## Extending the season: overwintering onions

If you live in zones 6 to 9, you can plant short-day or intermediate varieties in fall (Sept to Nov) and harvest in late spring. These onions take 180 to 240 days total, but they use the winter to grow roots and then bulb as days lengthen. 'Yellow Granex' (short-day) works well in the South; 'Red Candy Apple' is a good intermediate for zone 6.

## Common timing mistakes

- **Planting too early**: Cold soil (below 40°F) slows growth and triggers bolting. Wait until soil temps reach 50°F.
- **Planting too late**: Onions need cool weather for leaf growth. If you plant after the last frost in warm zones, you'll get small bulbs.
- **Using the wrong day length**: As noted, this is the #1 cause of failure. Check your latitude before buying.
- **Harvesting too early**: If tops are still green and upright, the bulb is still growing. Be patient.

## FAQ

**Can I grow onions in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 8 inches deep and 12 inches wide. Space sets 2 inches apart. Expect smaller bulbs, but they'll mature in the same time frame.

**Why are my onions taking longer than expected?**
Cool, cloudy weather slows growth. Also, if you planted sets that are too large (over 1 inch diameter), they may bolt instead of bulbing. Stick to dime-sized sets.

**Can I harvest green onions early?**
Absolutely. You can pull scallions at any stage, usually 60 to 90 days from seed or 30 to 45 days from sets. Just harvest before the bulb starts to swell.

**What's the fastest way to grow onions?**
Use sets of a short-day variety in a warm zone (8 or warmer). Plant in early spring and you can harvest in as little as 75 days. 'Red Creole' is a reliable fast maturer.

## The bottom line

Onions take 90 to 180 days from planting to harvest, depending on your method and variety. For the quickest crop, use sets and choose a day-length type suited to your zone. Start seeds 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost if you want more variety. Watch for the tops to fall over, then cure properly. With the right timing, you'll have a full harvest without the guesswork.
