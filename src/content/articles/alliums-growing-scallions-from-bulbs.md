Yes, you can grow scallions from bulbs, and it is one of the easiest ways to get a steady supply of green onions. The trick is to use scallion sets (small bulbs sold for planting) or the rooted base of store-bought scallions. This guide covers both methods, with real planting dates, spacing, and varieties that work in USDA zones 3 through 9.

## Choosing your bulbs

You have two options: scallion sets or kitchen scraps.

**Scallion sets**: These are small, immature onion bulbs sold in spring at garden centers or online. Look for varieties like 'White Spear', 'Red Beard', or 'Nabechan'. A 1-pound bag costs $8 to $12 and contains 50 to 80 sets. Choose firm bulbs, about the size of a marble, with no soft spots.

**Kitchen scraps**: Save the bottom 1 inch of scallions you buy at the store. Use organic ones if possible, as they sprout more reliably. This is free, but the variety is usually a generic bunching onion, and regrowth may be less vigorous after the first harvest.

## When to plant

Plant scallion sets in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked, or in late summer for a fall crop. Here are typical dates by zone:

| Zone | Spring planting | Fall planting |
|------|-----------------|---------------|
| 3-4  | April to May    | July to August |
| 5-6  | March to April  | August to September |
| 7-9  | February to March | September to October |

In zones 8 and 9, you can also plant in winter for a spring harvest. Scallions tolerate light frost, so don't wait for the last frost date.

## How to plant

1. **Prepare the soil**: Choose a spot with full sun (at least 6 hours) and well-drained soil. Loosen the soil to 6 inches deep and mix in 1 inch of compost. If planting in containers, use a pot at least 6 inches deep with drainage holes.

2. **Plant the bulbs**: For sets, plant each bulb pointy side up, 1 inch deep, with the tip just below the soil surface. Space them 2 inches apart in rows 6 inches apart. For kitchen scraps, place the rooted base in a shallow hole, covering the roots but leaving the cut top exposed.

3. **Water well**: After planting, water thoroughly. Keep the soil consistently moist but not soggy. Scallions need about 1 inch of water per week.

## Caring for your scallions

- **Mulch**: Add a thin layer of straw or grass clippings to retain moisture and suppress weeds.
- **Fertilize**: Apply a balanced fertilizer (like 10-10-10) at half strength once a month, or top-dress with compost. Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers, which can make the bulbs rot.
- **Thin if needed**: If you planted sets too close, thin to 2 inches apart when they are 4 inches tall. Use the thinnings as microgreens.

## Harvesting

You can harvest scallions at any size, but they are best when the white part is 1/2 inch thick. Use a trowel to loosen the soil, then pull the whole plant. For cut-and-come-again, snip the green tops 1 inch above the soil, leaving the bulb to regrow. You can get 2 to 3 harvests this way, though each regrowth is slightly thinner.

For a continuous supply, plant a new batch every 2 to 3 weeks. In zones 7-9, you can plant year-round with a break in the hottest summer months.

## Common problems and solutions

- **Bolting (flowering)**: If temperatures fluctuate or the soil dries out, scallions may bolt. Plant at the right time and keep moisture consistent.
- **Pests**: Onion maggots and thrips can be an issue. Use row covers to prevent egg-laying, and rotate crops yearly.
- **Rotting**: Overwatering or poor drainage causes bulbs to rot. Ensure soil drains well and don't let water pool.

## FAQ

**Can I regrow scallions from store-bought ones indefinitely?**
No, each regrowth is weaker. After 2 to 3 cycles, the bulb is depleted. Start fresh with new bulbs or sets.

**Do scallion sets produce the same as seeds?**
Sets give you an earlier harvest, but seed-grown scallions often have larger, more uniform bulbs. For a continuous supply, use both.

**Can I grow scallions indoors?**
Yes, place a pot with sets on a sunny windowsill or under a grow light for 12 hours a day. Harvest in 3 to 4 weeks.

**What is the difference between scallions and green onions?**
In the US, they are the same thing: immature onions harvested before the bulb forms. Some varieties are bred specifically for bunching, like 'Evergreen White'.

## The bottom line

Growing scallions from bulbs is a fast, foolproof way to get fresh greens. Plant sets or kitchen scraps in well-drained soil, keep them watered, and harvest in under a month. With a little planning, you can have scallions from early spring to late fall. Start with a 1-pound bag of sets for $10, and you will have more scallions than you can use.
