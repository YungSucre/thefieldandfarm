## What to Look for in a Compost Tumbler

Before you buy, consider these key features:

- **Capacity**: 7 to 10 cubic feet is ideal for most households. Smaller 3 to 5 cubic foot models work for small gardens but fill up fast. Larger 14 cubic foot models are heavy when full, so make sure you can turn them.
- **Material**: Look for UV-stabilized polypropylene or steel with a rust-resistant coating. Plastic is lighter and won't rust, but steel is more durable. Avoid thin plastic that cracks in cold weather.
- **Turning mechanism**: A crank handle is easier than a push-bar. Some models have a gear system that makes turning smoother. Test the action if you can.
- **Aeration**: Vents or perforations are essential for airflow. Some tumblers have internal aeration tubes that improve oxygen flow.
- **Door design**: A wide, secure door makes loading and unloading easier. Look for a door that won't pop open accidentally.
- **Base stability**: A sturdy base with a wide footprint prevents tipping, especially when the drum is full.

## How to Use a Compost Tumbler Effectively

A tumbler is only as good as your technique. Here's how to get fast, finished compost:

1. **Location**: Place your tumbler in a sunny spot if possible. Heat speeds up decomposition. If you live in a hot climate, partial shade is fine.
2. **Browns and greens**: Aim for a ratio of about 3 parts browns (dry leaves, straw, shredded paper) to 1 part greens (kitchen scraps, grass clippings). Too many greens makes it slimy and smelly.
3. **Chop materials**: Cut up large scraps. Smaller pieces decompose faster. I run a mower over leaves before adding them.
4. **Moisture**: The contents should feel like a wrung-out sponge. If it's too dry, add water; if too wet, add more browns.
5. **Turn regularly**: Turn the tumbler every 2 to 3 days for the first two weeks, then once a week. This aerates the pile and mixes materials.
6. **Patience**: In warm weather (above 60°F), you can have finished compost in 4 to 8 weeks. In cooler weather, it may take 3 to 4 months.

## Troubleshooting Common Tumbler Problems

- **Smelly compost**: Usually too wet or too many greens. Add browns and turn more often.
- **Compost not heating up**: Not enough nitrogen (greens) or too little mass. Add grass clippings or manure, and make sure the tumbler is at least half full.
- **Flies or pests**: Bury kitchen scraps under a layer of browns. Keep the lid closed.
- **Drum won't turn**: Overfilled or too wet. Remove some material and let it dry out a bit.

## FAQ

**Q: How long does it take to get compost from a tumbler?**
A: In warm weather (above 60°F), you can get finished compost in 4 to 8 weeks if you turn it regularly and maintain the right moisture and brown-to-green ratio. In cooler weather, expect 3 to 4 months.

**Q: Can I put weeds in a compost tumbler?**
A: Yes, but avoid weeds that have gone to seed or have persistent roots like bindweed. The heat in a well-managed tumbler can kill many seeds, but not all. To be safe, leave out seed heads and invasive roots.

**Q: How often should I turn my compost tumbler?**
A: Every 2 to 3 days for the first two weeks, then once a week. Turning more often speeds up decomposition but can also dry it out, so check moisture.

**Q: Do I need to add compost accelerator?**
A: No. A balanced mix of browns and greens, moisture, and oxygen is all you need. Accelerators can help in cold weather, but they are not necessary.
