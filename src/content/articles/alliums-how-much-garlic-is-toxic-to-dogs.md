If your dog snatches a clove of garlic from the counter or digs up a bulb in the garden, you need to know the risk. The toxic dose of garlic for dogs is roughly 15 to 30 grams per kilogram of body weight, but even smaller amounts can cause problems, especially with repeated exposure. In practical terms, that means a single clove (about 3 to 7 grams) can be dangerous for a small dog, while a large dog might need several cloves to show signs. If you suspect your dog ate garlic, call your vet immediately, even if you don't see symptoms yet.

## Why garlic is toxic to dogs

Garlic contains thiosulfates, compounds that dogs cannot digest properly. Thiosulfates damage red blood cells, leading to oxidative injury and a condition called Heinz body anemia. This means the red blood cells break down faster than the body can replace them, which can cause weakness, lethargy, and in severe cases, organ damage or death. The toxic effect is dose dependent, but it also accumulates: small amounts over several days can be just as dangerous as one large dose.

## Toxic dose: grams per pound and per kilogram

Most veterinary sources cite a toxic dose of 15 to 30 grams of garlic per kilogram of dog body weight. To put that in perspective:

- A 10-pound (4.5 kg) dog: toxic dose is about 68 to 135 grams, which is roughly 10 to 20 cloves.
- A 30-pound (13.6 kg) dog: toxic dose is about 204 to 408 grams, or 30 to 60 cloves.
- A 60-pound (27.2 kg) dog: toxic dose is about 408 to 816 grams, or 60 to 120 cloves.

However, these numbers are for acute toxicity. Clinical signs have been reported with as little as 5 grams per kilogram, and some dogs are more sensitive than others. Also, garlic powder is more concentrated: 1 teaspoon of garlic powder equals about 3 grams of fresh garlic, so a small amount can be significant.

## Symptoms of garlic poisoning in dogs

Symptoms usually appear within 24 to 48 hours after ingestion, but they can be delayed up to a week if the garlic was eaten in small amounts over time. Watch for:

- Vomiting and diarrhea
- Lethargy or weakness
- Pale gums
- Elevated heart rate
- Collapse or difficulty breathing
- Dark or reddish urine (a sign of red blood cell breakdown)

If you see any of these, especially after known garlic ingestion, get to a vet right away. Do not wait to see if it gets worse.

## What to do if your dog eats garlic

1. **Call your vet or a pet poison hotline immediately.** Have the garlic type (fresh, cooked, powder) and amount ready. The ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) charges a consultation fee, usually around $75, but it can save your dog's life.
2. **Do not induce vomiting unless your vet tells you to.** Inducing vomiting can be dangerous if the dog is already showing neurological signs.
3. **Bring the garlic packaging or a sample** if you can, to help the vet estimate the dose.
4. **Follow your vet's advice.** They may recommend inducing vomiting, giving activated charcoal, or bringing the dog in for blood tests and IV fluids.

## Treatment and prognosis

If treated early, most dogs recover fully. The vet may give your dog antioxidants like N-acetylcysteine or vitamin E to protect red blood cells, and in severe cases, a blood transfusion may be needed. The prognosis is good if treatment starts before anemia becomes severe. However, if left untreated, garlic poisoning can be fatal.

## How to keep your dog safe around garlic

- Store garlic bulbs and powder in sealed containers out of reach, preferably in a pantry or cabinet.
- In the garden, fence off your garlic bed or use a physical barrier. Dogs often dig up bulbs out of curiosity.
- Be careful with compost piles: garlic scraps can attract dogs, so keep compost covered or fenced.
- Teach your dog a solid "leave it" command to prevent counter surfing and garden raids.
- If you cook with garlic, be aware that cooked garlic is still toxic, just slightly less potent than raw.

## FAQ

### Is a single clove of garlic dangerous for a dog?

It depends on the dog's size. A 10-pound dog could be at risk from one clove (3 to 7 grams), while a 60-pound dog would need several cloves to reach a toxic dose. But even if the dose is below the toxic threshold, any garlic ingestion should be reported to your vet, especially if your dog is small or has health issues.

### Can dogs eat garlic in small amounts as a flea repellent?

No. There is no scientific evidence that garlic repels fleas, and the risks outweigh any potential benefit. Garlic is toxic to dogs, and even small amounts can cause gastrointestinal upset and blood cell damage over time. Use vet-approved flea preventatives instead.

### Is garlic powder more toxic than fresh garlic?

Yes, garlic powder is more concentrated. One teaspoon of garlic powder equals about 3 grams of fresh garlic, so a small amount can be significant. Always keep garlic powder out of reach, and be careful with foods that contain it, like seasoned meats or sauces.

### How long after eating garlic will a dog show symptoms?

Symptoms typically appear within 24 to 48 hours, but they can be delayed up to a week if the garlic was eaten in small amounts over several days. If you suspect ingestion, don't wait for symptoms; call your vet immediately.

## The bottom line

Garlic is toxic to dogs, and the toxic dose is roughly 15 to 30 grams per kilogram of body weight, but even less can cause problems in sensitive dogs. If your dog eats garlic, call your vet right away. With prompt treatment, most dogs recover fully, but prevention is always best. Keep garlic out of reach, both in the kitchen and in the garden, and never use garlic as a home remedy for fleas or other issues. Your dog's health is worth more than any bulb.
