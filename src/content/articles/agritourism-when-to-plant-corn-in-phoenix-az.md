In Phoenix, Arizona, you have two distinct windows to plant corn: a spring planting from mid-February to mid-March, and a late summer planting from late July to mid-August. The key is to avoid the extreme heat of June and July, which can sterilize pollen and reduce kernel set. This guide gives you exact dates, varieties, and steps to get a good crop.

## Understanding Phoenix's Growing Seasons

Phoenix sits in USDA hardiness zones 9b and 10a, with hot summers and mild winters. Corn is a warm-season crop that needs soil temperatures of at least 60°F for germination, but it also struggles when temperatures exceed 95°F during pollination. Your two planting windows are:

- **Spring planting**: February 15 to March 15. This allows corn to mature before the worst heat arrives.
- **Late summer planting**: July 25 to August 15. This crop matures as temperatures cool in October and November.

Avoid planting in April and May, as the heat will hit during pollination, leading to poor kernel development. Also avoid June and July, when temperatures are too high for germination.

## Choosing the Right Varieties

Not all corn varieties are suited to Phoenix. Look for short-season types that mature in 70 to 85 days. Here are reliable choices:

| Variety | Days to Maturity | Notes |
|---------|------------------|-------|
| 'Early Sunglow' | 63 | Sweet corn, good for spring |
| 'Golden Bantam' | 75 | Heirloom, reliable |
| 'Silver Queen' | 92 | White sweet corn, best for late summer |
| 'Trucker's Favorite' | 75 | Yellow sweet corn, heat tolerant |
| 'Hopi Blue' | 85 | Flour corn, drought tolerant |

For spring, choose 'Early Sunglow' or 'Golden Bantam'. For late summer, 'Silver Queen' works well because it matures in cooler weather. If you want a drought-tolerant option, 'Hopi Blue' is a native variety that handles heat.

## Soil Preparation and Planting Steps

Corn needs well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. In Phoenix, the soil is often alkaline and compacted, so amend it before planting.

1. **Test your soil**: Use a home test kit (around $15) or send a sample to a lab. Aim for pH 6.5.
2. **Amend the soil**: Mix in 2 to 3 inches of compost and a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at a rate of 2 pounds per 100 square feet. Work it into the top 6 inches.
3. **Plant seeds**: Sow seeds 1 inch deep, spacing them 4 to 6 inches apart in rows that are 30 to 36 inches apart. After germination, thin to 8 to 12 inches apart.
4. **Water deeply**: After planting, water to a depth of 6 inches. Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, but in Phoenix, you may need to water every 2 to 3 days during hot spells.
5. **Mulch**: Apply a 2-inch layer of organic mulch, like straw, to retain moisture and keep soil cool.

## Irrigation and Fertilization

Corn is a heavy feeder and needs consistent moisture, especially during tasseling and silking. In Phoenix, use drip irrigation or soaker hoses to deliver water directly to the roots. Avoid overhead watering, which can promote disease.

- **Fertilize** when the plants are 12 inches tall with a side dressing of nitrogen, such as blood meal or a 21-0-0 fertilizer, at a rate of 1 tablespoon per plant.
- **Repeat** when tassels appear. This ensures good kernel development.

## Managing Heat and Pests

Phoenix heat can stress corn. To reduce heat stress, plant in blocks of at least 4 rows, not single long rows, to improve pollination. Corn is wind-pollinated, so a block helps ensure good kernel set.

Common pests include corn earworm and aphids. Use floating row covers during the early stages to protect seedlings. For earworms, apply a few drops of mineral oil to the silk tip about a week after silks appear. This is a cheap, effective method.

## Harvesting and Expected Yields

Corn is ready to harvest about 20 days after silks appear. The kernels should be plump and milky when pierced. In Phoenix, you can expect 1 to 2 ears per stalk, depending on variety and care. A 10-foot row with 12 plants will yield about 12 to 24 ears.

Harvest in the morning when temperatures are cool, and refrigerate immediately. Corn loses sweetness quickly, so eat or process it within a day or two.

## FAQ

**Can I plant corn in Phoenix in June?**
No. June temperatures often exceed 105°F, which prevents germination and kills pollen. Stick to the spring and late summer windows.

**How much water does corn need in Phoenix?**
Corn needs about 1 to 2 inches of water per week. In the hottest months, you may need to water every other day. Use a moisture meter to check soil at 4 inches deep.

**What is the best fertilizer for corn in the desert?**
Use a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at planting, then side-dress with nitrogen (blood meal or 21-0-0) when plants are 12 inches tall and again at tasseling.

**Do I need to hand-pollinate corn in Phoenix?**
Usually not if you plant in a block. However, if you have a small patch, you can hand-pollinate by shaking the tassels over the silks on calm mornings.

## The Bottom Line

Plant corn in Phoenix from mid-February to mid-March for a spring crop, or from late July to mid-August for a fall crop. Choose short-season varieties, prepare your soil with compost, and water consistently. With these dates and steps, you can enjoy fresh corn even in the desert. Start your spring planting now if the soil is workable, and you will be harvesting by May or June.
