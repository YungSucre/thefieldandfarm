In Arizona, the answer to when to plant corn depends on your elevation and your local frost dates. In the low deserts (Phoenix, Tucson, Yuma, zones 9b-10a), plant from late February through early April for a spring crop, and again from late July through mid-August for a fall crop. In higher elevations (Flagstaff, Prescott, zones 6a-7b), plant from late April through early June, once soil temperatures reach at least 60°F. The key is to avoid frost at planting and extreme heat during pollination, which can ruin your ears. Let's break down the exact timing and steps for your area.

## Know Your Zone and Frost Dates

Arizona's climate ranges from scorching desert to cool mountain forests. Your planting window is dictated by your last spring frost and first fall frost, plus the heat of summer. Here are the typical dates:

- **Zone 6a (Flagstaff area)**: Last frost around May 15, first frost around September 30. Short season, so choose fast-maturing varieties.
- **Zone 7b (Prescott, Payson)**: Last frost around April 15, first frost around October 15. A bit more flexibility.
- **Zone 9b (Phoenix, Yuma)**: Last frost around February 15, first frost around December 15. You can grow two crops, but summer heat is brutal.
- **Zone 10a (Tucson, lower desert)**: Similar to 9b, with even milder winters.

Check your exact frost dates with your local extension office or an online tool like the National Gardening Association's frost date calculator. In the desert, you can often plant earlier than the last frost date if you use row covers or plant in a protected spot, but for corn, it's safer to wait until soil warms.

## Soil Temperature: The Real Trigger

Corn seeds germinate poorly in cold soil. The ideal soil temperature is 60°F to 85°F. Below 55°F, seeds may rot before sprouting. Use a soil thermometer (about $10 at garden centers) to check at 2 inches deep, in the morning. If it's below 60°F, wait a few days. In the low desert, soil can reach 60°F by late February, but in higher elevations, it may not until late April.

To warm soil faster, consider planting in raised beds or black plastic mulch. These can raise soil temperature by 5-10°F, giving you a head start. But don't rush: cold stress can cause stunted growth and poor pollination.

## Planting Windows by Region

### Low Desert (Zones 9b-10a)

- **Spring crop**: Plant from February 20 to April 1. Aim for a soil temperature of at least 60°F. In Phoenix, this often happens by early March. If you plant too early and a frost threatens, cover young plants with a frost cloth.
- **Fall crop**: Plant from July 20 to August 15. This crop matures in the milder fall weather. Avoid planting in June or early July, as heat stress during pollination can cause poor kernel set. For fall, choose varieties that mature in 70-80 days, so they finish before the first frost (which is usually not until December).

### Intermediate Elevations (Zones 7b-8a, e.g., Prescott, Sedona)

- **Spring crop**: Plant from April 15 to May 15, after the last frost. Soil should be 60°F or warmer. In Prescott, this is typically late April. You can plant again in early July for a fall crop, but be cautious of heat in August; choose heat-tolerant varieties.
- **Fall crop**: Plant from June 20 to July 10 for a September-October harvest. This works if you have a long enough growing season (at least 100 days).

### High Elevation (Zones 6a-7a, e.g., Flagstaff)

- **Spring crop**: Plant from May 20 to June 10, after the last frost. Soil may not reach 60°F until late May. Use a fast-maturing variety (60-70 days) to ensure maturity before the first frost in late September.
- **Fall crop**: Not recommended, as the season is too short. Stick with one crop.

## Planting Steps: From Seed to Harvest

1. **Prepare the soil**: Corn is a heavy feeder. Work in 2-3 inches of compost or aged manure before planting. A soil test (about $15 from a lab) can tell you if you need nitrogen. Corn needs nitrogen for growth; side-dress with a balanced fertilizer (like 10-10-10) when plants are 12 inches tall.

2. **Plant seeds**: Sow seeds 1-2 inches deep, spacing them 9-12 inches apart in rows. Rows should be 30-36 inches apart. For better pollination, plant in blocks of at least 4 rows, not a single long row. This ensures wind can pollinate all silks.

3. **Water immediately**: Corn needs consistent moisture. Water deeply at planting, then keep the soil evenly moist. In the desert, this means watering every 2-3 days, especially in hot weather. Drip irrigation is ideal; a simple soaker hose works too. Expect to water about 1-2 inches per week.

4. **Thin seedlings**: When seedlings are 4 inches tall, thin to 12 inches apart. If you leave them crowded, ears will be small.

5. **Fertilize**: Side-dress with nitrogen when plants are knee-high (about 18 inches). Use 1 tablespoon of 21-0-0 per plant, scratched into the soil and watered in.

6. **Watch for pests**: In Arizona, watch for corn earworm and cutworms. Use row covers early, or spray with Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) if you see damage. For earworm, apply a few drops of mineral oil into the silk tip after pollination.

7. **Harvest**: Sweet corn is ready when the silks turn brown and the kernels are plump and milky when punctured. This is usually 20-25 days after silking. Pick in the morning and eat immediately for best sweetness.

## FAQ

**Can I plant corn in the desert in summer?**

In the low desert, summer planting (June) is risky because temperatures above 95°F during pollination can cause poor kernel set. If you must, choose heat-tolerant varieties like 'Trucker's Favorite' and provide shade cloth, but expect lower yields. Better to plant in late July for fall.

**How much corn should I plant for a family?**

For fresh eating, plan on 10-15 plants per person. A 25-foot row with 12-inch spacing yields about 25 ears. For freezing or canning, double that. A pound of seed costs about $10-15 and covers 100 feet of row.

**Do I need to hand-pollinate corn?**

Usually not, if you plant in blocks. But in a small garden with a single row, you can hand-pollinate by shaking the tassels over the silks on a calm morning. This helps ensure full ears.

**What about growing corn in containers?**

It's possible with dwarf varieties like 'Tom Thumb' (3 feet tall) in a 5-gallon pot, but yields are small. For a real harvest, plant in the ground or a raised bed.

## The Bottom Line

Plant corn in Arizona when the soil is at least 60°F and after the last frost. In the low desert, that's late February to April for spring, and late July to August for fall. In higher elevations, wait until May or June. Choose varieties suited to your zone, plant in blocks, water consistently, and you'll have sweet, fresh corn. Start by checking your soil temperature today, and mark your calendar for the next planting window.

Remember, corn is a warm-season crop that thrives on heat, but not extreme heat. Timing is everything. With these dates and steps, you'll avoid the common mistakes of planting too early or too late, and you'll be rewarded with a bountiful harvest.
