Yes, a maze is a type of corn, but not in the way you might think. A maze is a labyrinth cut into a field of growing corn, typically for agritourism. The corn itself is a specific field corn variety, not sweet corn. This guide explains what corn to plant, how to design and cut a maze, and what it costs to start one in 2026.

## What is a maze?

A maze is a path network cut into a standing corn crop. It is also called a corn maze. The corn is planted in a block, then paths are mowed or cut once the stalks reach 4 to 6 feet tall. The goal is to create a challenging, fun walk for visitors, often with a theme or design. The corn is n

## Is maze corn the same as sweet corn?

No. Sweet corn is bred for eating, with high sugar content and tender kernels. Maze corn is a field corn, also called dent corn, which is harder and starchier. Field corn grows taller (8 to 10 feet), has stronger stalks, and holds up better in wind and rain. Sweet corn is shorter, weaker, and would fall over easily in a maze. For a maze, you want a tall, sturdy field corn variety.

## How to plant a corn maze

Plant in late spring after the last frost, when soil temperature is at least 50°F. In USDA zones 5-6, that is typically mid-May. In zones 7-8, early April to early May. Use a grain drill or plant by hand in rows spaced 30 inches apart, with seeds 6 to 8 inches apart in the row. Plant depth is 1.5 to 2 inches. For a 1-acre maze, you need about 30,000 seeds, or roughly 8 to 10 pounds of seed corn.

Fertilize with a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at planting, following soil test recommendations. Corn is a heavy feeder; expect to apply 150 to 200 pounds of nitrogen per acre split between planting and side-dressing when corn is knee-high.

## Designing and cutting the maze

Design your maze on paper or with software like Adobe Illustrator or a free tool like Inkscape. Keep paths at least 3 feet wide for easy walking. Use a string trimmer or a small tractor with a mower deck to cut paths. Many farmers use a walk-behind mower for precision. For large mazes, a riding mower with a 48-inch deck works well.

Cut when corn is 4 to 6 feet tall, usually late June to early July in zones 5-6. Cutting too early means the corn may regrow and cover paths; cutting too late makes it hard to see the design. After cutting, walk the maze to check for low hanging leaves or broken stalks that could trip visitors.

## Maintaining the maze through the season

Keep paths clear by mowing every 2-3 weeks during the growing season. Check for pests like corn earworm or stalk borers; treat only if damage is severe. Water during dry spells, especially in the first month. By September, the corn will be brown and dry, which is fine for the maze. After the season, chop the corn and till it under, or leave it for wildlife.

## FAQ

**Can I use sweet corn for a maze?**
Technically yes, but it is not recommended. Sweet corn is shorter and weaker, so paths may collapse. Field corn is the standard.

**How long does a corn maze last?**
Typically from late July or August through October, depending on your climate. Once frost hits, the stalks become brittle and may fall.

**Do I need a permit to open a corn maze?**
Check local regulations. Many areas require a business license, liability insurance, and possibly a temporary event permit. Contact your county extension office.

**Can I make a maze without a tractor?**
Yes. A good string trimmer or walk-behind mower can cut paths, but it takes longer. For a 2-acre maze, plan on 20-30 hours of cutting time with a walk-behind.

## The bottom line

A maze is a field corn labyrinth, not a type of corn itself. Choose a tall, sturdy field corn variety, plant in late spring, cut paths in early summer, and charge admission in the fall. Startup costs are modest, but success depends on design and marketing. Start small, learn the ropes, and expand next year. With careful planning, a corn maze can be a profitable agritourism venture for your farm.
