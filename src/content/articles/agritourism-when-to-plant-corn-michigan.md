In Michigan, the best time to plant corn is between late April and late May, depending on your USDA zone and soil temperature. Wait until the soil reaches at least 50°F at a 2-inch depth, and ideally 60°F for faster germination. This guide gives you exact dates, varieties, and steps to get your corn off to a strong start.

## Understanding Michigan's Growing Zones

Michigan spans USDA zones 4a to 6b. The Upper Peninsula and northern Lower Peninsula are cooler (zones 4a-5a), while the southern tier, including Detroit and Ann Arbor, falls in zones 5b-6a. A few lakeshore areas in the southwest can reach 6b. Your planting window shifts by about two weeks from north to south.

| Zone | Typical Last Frost | Safe Planting Window | Ideal Soil Temp |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| 4a-4b | May 15-25 | May 20 - June 5 | 55-60°F |
| 5a-5b | May 5-15 | May 10 - May 25 | 55-60°F |
| 6a-6b | April 25 - May 5 | April 25 - May 15 | 55-60°F |

These dates are based on 2026 climate averages. In a warm spring, you can plant a week early; in a cold, wet spring, wait. Corn seeds rot in cold, wet soil, so patience pays.

## Soil Temperature: The Real Trigger

Soil temperature matters more than the calendar. Corn germinates slowly below 50°F, and seeds can rot if the soil stays cold and wet. Use a soil thermometer (about $10 at garden centers) to check at 2 inches deep in the morning. If it reads 50°F or higher for three consecutive days, you're good to go. For best results, aim for 55-60°F.

To warm the soil faster, consider raised beds or black plastic mulch. Both can add 5-10°F to the top few inches in early spring. This is especially useful in northern zones.

## Choosing the Right Corn Variety

Pick varieties with a short days-to-maturity (DTM) for northern zones, and longer-season varieties for the south. Here are reliable options for Michigan:

| Variety | Type | DTM | Best Zones | Notes |
|---------|------|-----|------------|-------|
| 'Early Sunglow' | Sweet | 63 days | 4a-6b | Very early, good for short seasons |
| 'Golden Bantam' | Sweet | 80 days | 5a-6b | Classic heirloom, excellent flavor |
| 'Peaches and Cream' | Sweet (bicolor) | 75 days | 5a-6b | Popular, tender kernels |
| 'Bodacious' | Sweet (yellow) | 75 days | 5a-6b | High yields, disease resistant |
| 'Painted Mountain' | Flour/Flint | 70 days | 4a-5b | Cold-hardy, drought tolerant, colorful |
| 'Blue Clarage' | Dent | 110 days | 5b-6b | For flour or meal, long season |

For sweet corn, plant several varieties with different DTMs to extend your harvest. For field corn or cornmeal, choose a dent or flint type suited to your zone.

## Step-by-Step Planting

1. **Prepare the soil**: Corn prefers well-drained, fertile soil with a pH of 6.0-6.8. Test your soil (a kit costs $15-$30) and amend with compost or aged manure. Work in 2-3 inches of organic matter.

2. **Plant at the right depth**: Sow seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep. In sandy soil, go deeper (2 inches); in heavy clay, shallower (1.5 inches).

3. **Spacing**: Plant seeds 9-12 inches apart in rows 30-36 inches apart. For a block planting (better pollination), plant at least 4 rows side by side. If you plant a single long row, you'll get poor kernel fill.

4. **Water**: Keep the soil consistently moist but not soggy. Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, more during tasseling and silking.

5. **Fertilize**: Side-dress with nitrogen when plants are 12 inches tall and again when they tassel. Use 1/2 cup of 10-10-10 per 10 feet of row, or use compost tea.

## Planting Schedule for 2026

Here's a practical timeline for Michigan gardeners:

- **Late April (southern zones)**: Start planting when soil temps hit 55°F. Use a cold-tolerant variety like 'Early Sunglow'.
- **Early May (central zones)**: Plant main crop. Check soil temp; if below 50°F, wait a few days.
- **Mid-May (northern zones)**: Plant as soon as soil warms. Consider using black plastic to warm soil.
- **Succession planting**: For sweet corn, plant every 2 weeks from late April to early July for a continuous harvest. For field corn, plant once in May.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Planting too early: Cold, wet soil leads to poor germination and rot.
- Planting too late: In northern zones, late-planted corn may not mature before frost. Check DTM and count back from your first frost date (typically late September in zone 4).
- Poor pollination: Corn is wind-pollinated. Plant in blocks, not single rows.
- Skipping soil test: Corn is a heavy feeder. Without proper nutrients, yields drop.

## FAQ

**Can I plant corn before the last frost?**
Yes, if the soil is warm enough (50°F) and you're prepared to protect seedlings from frost. Corn can tolerate light frost, but a hard freeze (below 28°F) will kill it. Use row covers or cloches if frost is forecast.

**How long does corn take to grow in Michigan?**
From 63 to 110 days, depending on variety. Sweet corn usually takes 70-85 days. Field corn takes 100-110 days. Choose varieties with DTM that fit your growing season.

**What is the best corn variety for northern Michigan?**
For zones 4-5, choose 'Early Sunglow' (63 days) or 'Painted Mountain' (70 days). These mature quickly and tolerate cooler conditions.

**Do I need to soak corn seeds before planting?**
No. Soaking can cause seeds to rot if soil is cold. Plant dry seeds directly. If you want to speed germination, wait until soil is warm.

## The Bottom Line

Plant corn in Michigan when soil temperature reaches 50°F, typically late April to late May depending on your zone. Use a soil thermometer, choose short-season varieties for northern areas, and plant in blocks for good pollination. With the right timing and care, you'll enjoy fresh sweet corn or a field of grain by late summer. Start checking soil temps now, and be ready to plant when the time is right.
