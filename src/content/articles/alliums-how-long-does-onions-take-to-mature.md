Onions take 90 to 120 days from seed, 60 to 80 days from sets, and 70 to 90 days from transplants. The exact time depends on variety, day length, and your USDA zone. If you plant sets in early spring, you can harvest in mid-summer; from seed, plan for a full season.

## Understanding onion maturity

Onions are day-length sensitive. They bulb when daylight reaches a specific threshold. Short-day varieties (for Southern zones, 8 to 10 hours of daylight) mature in 90 to 110 days. Long-day varieties (for Northern zones, 14 to 16 hours) need 100 to 120 days. Intermediate-day types (zones 6 and 7) take 95 to 115 days.

## Maturity times by planting method

| Method | Time to maturity | Best for |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| Seeds | 90 to 120 days | Full-size bulbs, storage onions |
| Transplants | 70 to 90 days | Faster harvest, larger bulbs |
| Sets | 60 to 80 days | Quick green onions or early bulbs |

Seeds are cheapest ($2 to $4 per packet) but require the longest wait. Transplants cost more ($8 to $12 per dozen) but save 3 to 4 weeks. Sets are easiest ($5 to $8 per pound) but often bolt or produce smaller bulbs if planted late.

## Variety-specific timelines

Choose varieties that match your day length and desired use.

- **Long-day (zones 3 to 6):** 'Yellow Sweet Spanish' (110 days), 'Walla Walla' (110 days), 'Redwing' (105 days).
- **Intermediate-day (zones 6 to 7):** 'Candy' (100 days), 'Red Candy Apple' (100 days), 'Superstar' (95 days).
- **Short-day (zones 8 to 10):** 'Texas SuperSweet' (100 days), 'Granex' (90 days), 'Red Creole' (95 days).

For green onions (scallions), you can harvest any variety at 30 to 40 days after planting, when tops are 6 to 8 inches tall. Pull them early if you want a quick crop.

## How to tell when onions are ready

Onions are ready when the tops flop over. This usually happens 1 to 2 weeks before full maturity. The neck softens, and the leaves turn yellow and fall. Do not wait for all tops to flop; harvest when 50 to 75% have fallen.

To check, gently pull one bulb. If the skin is papery and the bulb is full, it's ready. If the neck is thick and green, wait another week.

## Steps to harvest at the right time

1. Stop watering 2 weeks before expected harvest. This reduces moisture and helps skins form.
2. When tops flop, loosen soil with a garden fork (about $20 to $30) and lift bulbs by hand.
3. Leave onions in the field for 1 to 2 days to dry, then move to a dry, shaded spot.
4. Cure for 2 to 3 weeks in a well-ventilated area (like a shed or garage) with temperatures around 70 to 80°F.
5. Cut tops to 1 inch and roots to 1/4 inch, then store in a cool, dry place (40 to 50°F).

Curing is critical for storage. Skip it and onions may rot within weeks.

## Tips to speed up maturity

- Start seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before last frost. Use a seed-starting mix and grow lights (a basic setup costs $40 to $60).
- Harden off transplants for 7 to 10 days before planting.
- Plant sets as soon as soil can be worked in early spring. In zone 5, that's late March to mid-April.
- Use a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at planting, then side-dress with nitrogen (blood meal or fish emulsion) every 3 weeks until bulbing begins.
- Keep soil consistently moist but not soggy. Onions need about 1 inch of water per week.

Faster maturity often means smaller bulbs. If you want big storage onions, you need the full 110 to 120 days.

## Common mistakes that delay maturity

- Planting too late: In zones 5 and colder, sets planted after May 1 may not bulb before fall.
- Planting too deep: Set bulbs just below soil surface, with the tip showing. Deep planting delays bulbing.
- Overcrowding: Space sets 4 inches apart, transplants 4 to 6 inches apart. Crowding reduces bulb size and slows maturity.
- Using the wrong day length: A short-day onion in zone 5 will not bulb until late summer, if at all.

## FAQ

### Can I harvest onions early?

Yes. You can pull green onions at any time after 30 days. For small bulbs, harvest when tops are 6 to 8 inches tall, but they won't store well. Eat them within a week.

### Why are my onions taking longer than expected?

Check your variety and zone. Also, cool weather (below 60°F) slows growth. If you planted from seed, add 2 to 3 weeks for germination and transplant shock.

### Do onions mature faster in warmer climates?

Generally, yes, but only if you choose short-day varieties. In zones 8 and warmer, long-day onions may not bulb at all because daylight never reaches 14 hours.

### What is the fastest way to grow onions?

Use sets. Plant them in early spring, and you can harvest green onions in 30 days or full bulbs in 60 to 80 days. Sets are the quickest method for beginners.

## The bottom line

Onions take 90 to 120 days from seed, 60 to 80 days from sets. For a reliable harvest, match your variety to your zone, plant at the right time, and watch for floppy tops. Start seeds indoors if you want full-size bulbs; use sets for speed. With proper timing and care, you'll have onions ready when you expect them.

Remember: curing is not optional. Give your onions 2 to 3 weeks of dry, airy storage before you put them away. That's the difference between a pantry staple and a pile of rot.
