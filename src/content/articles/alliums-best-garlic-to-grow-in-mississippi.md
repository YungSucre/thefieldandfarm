If you're in Mississippi, your best garlic choices are softneck varieties like 'California Early' and 'Inchelium Red', with hardneck 'Chesnok Red' as a possible but riskier option. Plant in October or November, about 2 inches deep and 6 inches apart, and you'll harvest in late May or June. Here's how to pick the right garlic and grow it successfully in the Magnolia State.

## Understanding Mississippi's Climate for Garlic

Mississippi spans USDA zones 7b to 9a, with mild winters and hot, humid springs. Garlic needs a cold period (vernalization) to form bulbs, but our winters are often not cold enough for true hardnecks. Softnecks are the reliable choice because they don't require a hard freeze and they store longer, which is a plus in our heat. Hardnecks can

For most Mississippi gardeners, stick with 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red'. If you want a spicier kick, 'Creole Red' is a great softneck that handles our winters well. Avoid hardnecks like 'Music' or 'German Extra Hardy' unless you're in the northernmost parts of the state and willing to risk smaller bulbs.

## When to Plant Garlic in Mississippi

Timing is critical. Plant garlic 4 to 6 weeks before the first hard frost, which gives roots time to establish before winter dormancy. In Mississippi, that's typically:

- **North Mississippi (Zone 7b):** Mid-October to early November
- **Central Mississippi (Zone 8a):** Late October to mid-November
- **South Mississippi (Zone 9a):** November to early December

If you plant too early, garlic may sprout too much before winter; too late, and roots won't establish. Use a soil thermometer: plant when soil temperature at 2 inches is around 50°F.

## How to Plant Garlic: Step-by-Step

1. **Buy seed garlic** from a reputable supplier. Do not use supermarket garlic; it's often treated to prevent sprouting. Expect to pay $10 to $20 per pound for seed garlic, which covers about 50 to 60 cloves.
2. **Prepare the soil**: Garlic prefers well-drained, loose soil with a pH of 6.0 to 7.0. Amend with compost or aged manure a few weeks before planting. Avoid fresh manure, which can burn roots.
3. **Break apart bulbs** into individual cloves, keeping the papery skin on. Choose the largest cloves for planting; they produce the biggest bulbs.
4. **Plant cloves** 2 inches deep, pointed end up, and 6 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart. In heavy clay, plant 1 inch deep and mulch heavily.
5. **Water thoroughly** after planting, then apply a 4 to 6 inch layer of straw or shredded leaves. Mulch conserves moisture, suppresses weeds, and moderates soil temperature.

## Caring for Garlic Through the Season

- **Fertilize** in early spring when shoots emerge (usually February) with a high-nitrogen fertilizer like blood meal or a balanced 10-10-10 at a rate of 1 pound per 100 square feet. Side-dress again in April.
- **Water** about 1 inch per week if rainfall is scarce. Reduce watering in May as bulbs mature; too much water can cause rot.
- **Remove scapes** from hardneck varieties (if you planted any) in late spring to direct energy to bulb growth. Scapes are edible and make great pesto.
- **Weed regularly**; garlic doesn't compete well with weeds.

## Harvesting and Curing Garlic

Garlic is ready to harvest when the lower leaves turn brown but the upper leaves are still green, usually late May to June. Dig carefully with a fork, don't pull, to avoid bruising. Cure in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated area for 2 to 3 weeks. Then trim roots and cut stems to 1 inch, or braid softnecks. Store in a cool, dry place (50-60°F) with good air circulation. Properly cured softnecks can last 6 to 8 months.

## Common Problems and Solutions

- **Rust or white rot**: These fungal diseases thrive in humid conditions. Rotate crops, avoid overhead watering, and ensure good drainage.
- **Small bulbs**: Often due to planting too late, overcrowding, or insufficient water in spring.
- **Bolting in softnecks**: Rare, but can happen if the weather swings wildly. Remove flower stalks immediately.

## FAQ

**Can I grow garlic from grocery store bulbs?**

It's possible, but not recommended. Store garlic is often treated to prevent sprouting, and it may carry diseases. Buy certified disease-free seed garlic from a nursery or online supplier for best results.

**When should I fertilize garlic in Mississippi?**

Apply a balanced fertilizer at planting, then side-dress with nitrogen in early spring (February) and again in April. Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers after April, as they can delay bulb formation.

**How much garlic can I expect to harvest?**

With proper care, you can expect 10 to 20 bulbs per pound of seed garlic planted. Each clove yields one bulb, so a 1-pound bag (about 50 cloves) yields 40 to 50 bulbs, depending on losses.

**Do I need to refrigerate garlic before planting in Mississippi?**

No. Our winters are mild enough that garlic doesn't need artificial vernalization. If you're in a warmer area (south Mississippi), choose softneck varieties that adapt to mild winters.

## The Bottom Line

For Mississippi, choose softneck garlic like 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red' for reliable yields and good storage. Plant in October or November, 2 inches deep and 6 inches apart, and mulch well. With minimal care, you'll harvest a crop that lasts through the summer. Avoid hardnecks unless you're in the far north and want to experiment. Start with a pound of seed garlic, and you'll have enough to cook with and save some for next year's planting.
