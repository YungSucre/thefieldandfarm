If you're out of fresh garlic and reaching for the powder, here's the direct answer: 4 average cloves equal about 1.5 to 2 teaspoons of garlic powder. This range accounts for clove size and whether the powder is finely ground or more coarse. Use the lower end for mild dishes, the higher end for bold flavor. Keep reading for the breakdown, how to adjust for your specific cloves, and what to do when a recipe calls for minced or granulated garlic.

## Why the conversion varies

Garlic cloves vary in size. A small clove might weigh 3 grams, a large one 7 grams. On average, a medium clove weighs about 5 grams. When you mince that, you get roughly 1 teaspoon of fresh minced garlic. Garlic powder is concentrated: it's dehydrated and ground, so the flavor is more intense. ## Measuring garlic powder: teaspoons and grams

For accuracy, use measuring spoons, not a kitchen spoon. Here's the practical conversion for 4 cloves:

| Form | Amount for 4 cloves |
| --- | --- |
| Garlic powder (fine) | 1.5 to 2 teaspoons |
| Granulated garlic (coarser) | 2 to 2.5 teaspoons |
| Garlic salt | 3 to 4 teaspoons (reduce salt in recipe) |

In grams, 1 teaspoon of garlic powder weighs about 3 grams. So 4 cloves equal roughly 4.5 to 6 grams of powder. If you have a digital scale, that's your most precise measure. A typical jar of garlic powder costs $3 to $6 for 3 ounces, which lasts a long time.

## How to adjust for your cloves

If your cloves are unusually large or small, adjust accordingly. Here's a simple method:

1. Mince the 4 cloves you would have used.
2. Measure the minced garlic in teaspoons.
3. Use that same volume of garlic powder, but start with 3/4 of it, then taste and add more.

For example, if your 4 cloves yield 2 teaspoons of minced garlic, start with 1.5 teaspoons of powder. That prevents over-garlicking. Remember, powder is more concentrated, so you need less by volume.

## When to use powder vs. fresh

Fresh garlic has a pungent, sharp bite that mellows with cooking. Powder is more mellow and slightly sweet, with a uniform flavor. Use powder when you want a quick, even distribution, like in rubs, marinades, or dry seasoning blends. Fresh is better for dishes where you want texture or a strong garlic presence, like in a stir-fry or garlic bread. For slow-cooked dishes, powder can work well, but add it early so it rehydrates and blends.

If you're substituting in a recipe that calls for fresh garlic and you're using powder, add it at the beginning of cooking. That gives it time to release flavor. If you add it at the end, it might taste raw and dusty.

## Storing garlic powder for best flavor

Garlic powder lasts about 2 to 3 years if stored properly. Keep it in an airtight container in a cool, dark place, away from heat and moisture. Do not store it above the stove or near the sink. Humidity causes clumping and flavor loss. If it clumps, it's still safe but may have lost potency. To test, smell it: if it smells weak, use a bit more.

For homegrown garlic, you can make your own powder. Slice peeled cloves thinly, dehydrate at 115°F for 8 to 12 hours until brittle, then grind in a spice grinder. This yields a fresher, more potent powder. A pound of fresh garlic makes about 1/4 cup of powder. It's a great way to use surplus harvest.

## Practical tips for cooking with garlic powder

- Add powder to oil or butter first to bloom the flavor.
- For a quick garlic salt, mix 3 parts salt to 1 part powder.
- In soups or sauces, start with 1 teaspoon per 4 servings, then adjust.
- If a recipe calls for garlic powder and you only have fresh, use 1 clove per 1/8 teaspoon powder, but that's rare.

## FAQ

**Can I use garlic powder in place of fresh in any recipe?**
Yes, but expect a milder, less sharp flavor. For raw applications like salad dressings, fresh is better. For cooked dishes, powder works well.

**How much garlic powder equals one clove?**
About 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon, depending on clove size and your taste. Start with 1/4 teaspoon and adjust.

**Is granulated garlic the same as garlic powder?**
No. Granulated garlic has a coarser texture and is slightly less potent by volume. Use about 1.5 times more granulated garlic than powder.

**Does garlic powder go bad?**
It doesn't spoil, but it loses flavor over time. Keep it sealed and use within 2 to 3 years for best taste.

## The bottom line

For 4 cloves, use 1.5 to 2 teaspoons of garlic powder. Start with 1.5 teaspoons, taste, and add more if needed. Remember that powder is concentrated, so adjust for your clove size and the dish. With this conversion, you can confidently swap fresh for powdered garlic in any recipe.
