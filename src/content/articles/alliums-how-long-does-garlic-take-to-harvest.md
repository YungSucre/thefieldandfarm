Garlic takes about 8 to 9 months from planting to harvest, depending on your climate and variety. In most US zones, you plant in fall (October to November) and harvest in early to mid-summer (June to July). If you plant in spring, you will harvest later, but the bulbs will be smaller. The exact timing depends on your USDA zone and the garlic type, so here is how to nail it.

## Planting to harvest timeline

Garlic is a long-season crop. It needs a cold period (vernalization) to form bulbs, so fall planting is standard. Here is a typical schedule for zones 3 to 8:

## How to know when garlic is ready

The most reliable sign is the leaves. Wait until the lower leaves turn brown and dry, but the top 4 to 6 leaves are still green. For most varieties, this happens about 3 to 4 weeks after the scapes (flower stalks) appear. If you cut the scapes (which you should for larger bulbs), count about 3 to 4 weeks from that date.

Another test: dig up one bulb and check the wrapper. The bulb should be fully formed with distinct cloves, and the skin should be tight and papery, not split. If the cloves are bulging and the skin is splitting, you are too late. If the bulb is smooth and round with no visible cloves, it is too early.

## Harvesting steps

1. Stop watering 1 to 2 weeks before harvest. This helps the soil dry and the skins tighten.
2. Loosen the soil with a garden fork, 4 to 6 inches away from the row, to avoid slicing bulbs.
3. Pull the bulbs by hand, or use a spade if the soil is compact. Do not pull by the leaves alone, as they may break.
4. Shake off excess soil, but do not wash the bulbs. Moisture invites rot.
5. Cure the garlic in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated area for 2 to 4 weeks. Hang in bundles or lay on a screen. After curing, trim the roots and cut the stalks to 1 inch, or braid if you prefer.

## Varieties and timing differences

Hardneck garlic (like 'Music', 'German Red', 'Chesnok Red') forms scapes and matures earlier, usually 7 to 8 months. Softneck garlic (like 'California Early', 'Inchelium Red', 'Silverskin') does not scape, stores longer, and matures a bit later, 8 to 9 months. Elephant garlic is not true garlic; it is a leek relative and takes 9 to 10 months, with a milder flavor.

For a fall planting, choose varieties suited to your zone. In cold zones, hardnecks are more winter-hardy. In warm zones, softnecks do better. Buy seed garlic from a local nursery or online supplier; expect to pay $10 to $15 per pound, and you will need about 1 pound per 50 cloves (roughly 10 to 15 feet of row).

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Harvesting too early**: Bulbs are small and lack flavor. Wait for the leaf sign.
- **Harvesting too late**: Bulbs split and store poorly. If you see split skins, harvest immediately.
- **Pulling by the leaves**: Leaves break, and you lose the bulb. Use a fork.
- **Washing before curing**: This causes mold. Brush off soil only.
- **Curing in direct sun**: Sun can scald the bulbs. Use shade.

If you miss the ideal window by a week, do not panic. A few days early is better than late, because late bulbs do not store well. Use the late ones first.

## FAQ

**Can I harvest garlic in the first year if I plant in spring?**
Yes, but the bulbs will be smaller, often 1 to 2 inches in diameter. Plant in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked, and harvest in late summer to early fall. The cloves will not be as large, but they are still usable.

**What if I leave garlic in the ground too long?**
The bulbs will split open, and the cloves may start to sprout. They are still edible but will not store well. Use them within a few weeks.

**How do I store garlic after curing?**
Keep it in a cool, dry, dark place with good air circulation. Ideal temperature is 50-60°F with 60-70% humidity. Softnecks store 6-8 months; hardnecks 3-5 months. Check regularly and remove any soft or sprouting cloves.

**Do I need to cut scapes?**
Yes, for hardneck varieties. Cut the scape when it curls, about 1 to 2 weeks after it appears. This directs energy to the bulb, increasing size by up to 30%. Scapes are edible and make great pesto.

## The bottom line

Plan for 8 to 9 months from fall planting to summer harvest. Mark your calendar with a harvest window based on your zone, and use the leaf test to confirm. Harvest on a dry day, cure properly, and you will have garlic that lasts for months. Start with a small patch, learn your local timing, and adjust next year. Garlic is forgiving, and even a so-so harvest beats anything from the store.
