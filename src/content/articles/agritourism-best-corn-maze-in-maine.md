If you are looking for the best corn maze in Maine, start with these five farms: they have the largest, most creative, and best-maintained mazes in the state. In 2026, expect to pay $12 to $18 per adult and $8 to $12 per child, with most mazes open from late August through October 31. Below, I break down the top choices by region, what makes each unique, and how to plan your visit so you get the most out of your trip.

## Top Corn Mazes in Maine by Region

### 1. Treworgy Family Orchards (Central Maine, near Bangor)
Located in Levant, this farm has been a local favorite for over a decade. Their 2026 maze covers 6 acres and features a design that changes yearly, often tied to a theme like a giant pumpkin or a farm animal. The maze takes about 45 to 90 minutes to complete, depending on how many dead ends you hit. They also have a pumpkin patch, hayrides, and a farm store with cider donuts. Admission is $14 for adults, $10 for children 3-12, and free for kids under 3.

### 2. Pumpkin Valley Farm (Southern Maine, near Portland)
In Dayton, this farm offers a 5-acre maze with a 3D design that includes a bridge and a tower, giving you a bird's-eye view of the field. The maze is designed for all ages, with a shorter path for little ones. They also have a corn cannon, a jumping pillow, and a petting zoo. Tickets are $16 for adults, $12 for children, and include access to all non-ride activities. Peak weekends in October can get busy, so go on a weekday if you can.

### 3. Libby & Sons Farm (Midcoast Maine, near Rockland)
This family-run farm in Union has a 4-acre maze that is known for its challenging twists and turns. It is a favorite among locals who want a real puzzle, not just a walk in the field. The maze takes about 60 to 75 minutes to complete. They also have a corn pit for kids, a hay bale pyramid, and a farm stand with fresh produce. Admission is $12 for adults, $8 for children, making it one of the most affordable options.

### 4. Spiller Farm (Southern Maine, near Kennebunk)
In Wells, Spiller Farm has been running mazes for over 20 years. Their 2026 maze is 3 acres, smaller but very intricate, with a design that includes a hidden scavenger hunt for kids. They also have a pumpkin patch, a corn maze flashlight night on Fridays and Saturdays in October, and a bakery with homemade pies. Tickets are $13 for adults, $9 for children, and $11 for seniors.

### 5. Green Thumb Farms (Western Maine, near Fryeburg)
This farm in Fryeburg offers a 7-acre maze, the largest in the state, with a design that changes annually. The maze is split into two sections: a family-friendly loop and a more challenging outer loop. They also have a corn maze at night with glow sticks on select weekends. ## How to Choose the Right Maze for You

When picking a maze, consider your group's age and patience level. If you have young kids, choose a farm with a shorter path or a scavenger hunt, like Spiller Farm or Pumpkin Valley. If you want a serious challenge, go for Green Thumb Farms or Libby & Sons. For a full day of activities, Treworgy Family Orchards has the most to offer beyond the maze.

Check each farm's website before you go: some require online ticket purchases on weekends, and many have specific entry times to avoid overcrowding. Also, be aware that rain can close mazes, so have a backup date.

## When to Go: Peak Dates and Times

Mazes open in late August, but the corn is not fully grown until mid-September. The best time to visit is from mid-September to mid-October, when the corn is at its tallest and the weather is cool. October weekends are the busiest, especially around Columbus Day and Halloween. If you want a quieter experience, go on a weekday morning or a Sunday afternoon.

Here is a general timeline for Maine corn mazes:

| Time of Year | Status | Crowd Level |
|--------------|--------|-------------|
| Late August | Open, corn is short | Low |
| Mid-September | Peak height, good weather | Moderate |
| Early October | Peak season | High |
| Late October | Still open, but some farms close early | Moderate to Low |

## What to Bring and Expect

Wear sturdy shoes: the ground can be uneven, and you will walk a lot. Bring water, especially on warm days, and a phone for photos, but note that some mazes have no cell service. Most farms have port-a-potties, but not all have flush toilets. Expect to spend 2 to 3 hours total if you do the maze plus other activities.

If you go at night, bring a flashlight or buy glow sticks at the farm. Night mazes are a different experience, but they are not recommended for young children who might get scared.

## FAQ

### Are corn mazes in Maine open after Halloween?
Most mazes close by October 31, but some stay open into the first week of November if the weather holds. Check the farm's website for exact closing dates.

### Can I bring my dog to a corn maze?
Some farms allow dogs on a leash, but many do not due to hygiene and safety. Call ahead or check the farm's pet policy before bringing your dog.

### Do I need to buy tickets in advance?
On weekends, yes. Many farms sell out, especially in October. Weekdays are usually fine to buy at the gate, but buying online saves time.

### Are corn mazes accessible for strollers or wheelchairs?
Most mazes have paths wide enough for strollers, but the ground is uneven and may be muddy. Wheelchair access is limited; some farms offer a paved path around the maze, but not through it. Contact the farm directly for specific accessibility information.

## The Bottom Line

The best corn maze in Maine depends on what you want: the largest is Green Thumb Farms, the most family-friendly is Treworgy Family Orchards, and the best value is Libby & Sons. Go in mid-September to mid-October, buy tickets online for weekends, and check the weather before you head out. With a little planning, you will have a great day in the field, and you will not waste time or money on a maze that does not fit your group. Pick one, pack a snack, and enjoy the crisp Maine fall air.
