Making a corn maze is a rewarding fall project that can draw visitors to your farm, but it takes planning and work. Start by choosing a field with good drainage and full sun, then plant a tall, sturdy corn variety in late spring. Cut the maze paths in late summer, and you will have a maze ready for October visitors. Here is the step-by-step process, with realistic numbers and tips from someone who has done it.

![How to make a corn maze](/images/articles/how-to-make-a-corn-maze/section-1.jpg)

## Planning your maze

First, decide on the maze size and design. A simple maze for kids can be 100 by 100 feet, while a larger maze for paying customers might be 200 by 200 feet or more. The bigger the maze, the more corn you need and the more time to cut paths. For a first maze, start small: 100 by 100 feet is manageable and still fun.

Draw your design on graph paper or use a simple design software. Keep paths at least 3 feet wide so visitors can walk comfortably. Include dead ends and loops to make it challenging, but not so complex that it takes hours to solve. A good rul

## Choosing the right corn variety

Not all corn works for mazes. You need a tall, sturdy variety that can withstand wind and foot traffic. Field corn, not sweet corn, is the best choice. Popular varieties include:

- **Pioneer P1197**: A common field corn that grows 8 to 10 feet tall, with strong stalks. Seeds cost about $150 per 80,000 seeds, enough for about 2 acres.
- **Dekalb DKC62-08**: Another reliable field corn, similar height and strength. Price is comparable.
- **Blue corn** (e.g., Hopi Blue): Grows 7 to 9 feet, but stalks are thinner. Use only for small mazes in sheltered areas.

Avoid sweet corn varieties; they are shorter and weaker. For a 100 by 100 foot maze (about 0.23 acres), you will need about 15,000 seeds, which costs roughly $30 to $50. For a larger 200 by 200 foot maze (about 0.92 acres), plan on 60,000 seeds, costing $100 to $200.

## Planting and growing

Plant corn after the last frost, when soil temperature is at least 50°F. In most of the US, that is mid-April to late May. Check your USDA zone:

- Zone 5: Plant late April to early May.
- Zone 6: Plant mid-April to early May.
- Zone 7: Plant early to mid-April.

Use a corn planter or a seed drill for even spacing. Plant seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep, with 6 to 8 inches between seeds in the row, and 30 to 36 inches between rows. This gives about 25,000 to 30,000 plants per acre. For a 100 by 100 foot maze, you will have about 10 rows, each 100 feet long.

Fertilize with a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at planting, at a rate of 300 pounds per acre. Side-dress with nitrogen when corn is knee-high, about 100 pounds of urea per acre. Water if rainfall is less than 1 inch per week. Corn needs about 20 inches of water during the growing season.

Weed control is critical. Use a pre-emergence herbicide like atrazine, or cultivate between rows. Weeds compete for nutrients and water, and they can make paths hard to see.

## Cutting the maze

When corn is mature, usually 60 to 80 days after planting, it will be 8 to 10 feet tall. Cut the maze paths in late August or early September, before the corn dries out too much. Use a string trimmer with a heavy-duty blade, or a brush cutter for larger paths. For a 100 by 100 foot maze, cutting paths takes about 4 to 6 hours. For a larger maze, plan on a full day or more.

Mark the paths with flags or spray paint before cutting. Walk the design and place flags at each turn. Then cut the paths, making them 3 to 4 feet wide. Remove the cut corn stalks from the paths so visitors can walk easily.

## Adding features and safety

To make your maze more attractive, add features like:

- **Checkpoints**: Place numbered flags or signs at decision points so visitors can find their way.
- **Themes**: Cut shapes like a pumpkin or a scarecrow into the maze, but this requires extra cutting time.
- **Lighting**: For evening hours, use solar path lights along the main paths. Cost: $20 to $50 per light.

Safety is important. Make sure paths are wide enough for emergency access. Have a map at the entrance and a phone number for help. Check the maze daily for holes or fallen stalks.

## Pricing and marketing

If you charge admission, typical rates in 2026 are $8 to $12 per adult, $5 to $8 for children under 12, and free for under 3. For a small maze, you might attract 200 to 500 visitors in a season, earning $1,500 to $5,000. Larger mazes can earn more, but costs also rise.

Market your maze through local social media, a simple website, and signs on the road. Partner with local schools for field trips. Open weekends in October, and consider a pumpkin patch or hayride to increase revenue.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to grow a corn maze?**
From planting to cutting, about 90 to 110 days. Planting in late April to early May, cutting in late August, and opening in October.

**Can I make a corn maze in a small garden?**
Yes, but it will be small. A 50 by 50 foot maze is possible, but it may not be worth the effort unless it is for personal use or a small event.

**What if I don't have a tractor?**
You can plant by hand using a jab planter, but it is labor-intensive. For a 100 by 100 foot maze, expect to spend 8 to 10 hours planting. Cutting can be done with a string trimmer.

**How do I keep the maze from falling over?**
Choose sturdy varieties, plant at the right density, and avoid over-fertilizing with nitrogen, which makes stalks weak. Also, cut paths after the corn is fully mature.

## The bottom line

A corn maze is a fun and profitable agritourism project if you plan carefully. Start small, choose a sturdy field corn, and cut paths in late summer. With a modest investment of $100 to $500 for seeds and supplies, you can create a maze that brings visitors to your farm in October. Remember, the key is preparation: plan your design, plant on time, and cut paths when the corn is tall but still green. Happy mazing!
