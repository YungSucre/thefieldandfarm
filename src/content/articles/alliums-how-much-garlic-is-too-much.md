If you're asking how much garlic is too much, the short answer is: plant no more than you can eat in 10 months, plus a little for replanting. For most households, that's 20 to 40 cloves per person, which translates to about 1 to 2 pounds of garlic bulbs at harvest. Planting more than that without a clear plan for storage, selling, or giving away often leads to waste and disappointment.

## Why garlic quantity matters

Garlic is a long-season crop: you plant in fall and harvest in summer, taking up garden space for 8 to 9 months. ## How much garlic does one person actually eat?

A typical garlic eater uses about 2 to 3 cloves per week in cooking. That's roughly 100 to 150 cloves per year. Since each bulb has 6 to 12 cloves depending on variety, you need about 12 to 20 bulbs per person per year. But you don't need to plant all of that: you'll also buy garlic from the store during the gap between harvest and curing, or you'll use your own stored garlic for most of the year.

Here's a practical rule: plant 1 pound of garlic per person for eating, plus 10% extra for replanting and losses. One pound of seed garlic contains about 40 to 50 cloves, which yields 40 to 50 bulbs (if each clove grows into one bulb). That's roughly 4 to 5 pounds of harvested garlic, which should cover a year of cooking for one person.

## Spacing and yield: the real numbers

Garlic spacing directly affects bulb size. For maximum yield per square foot, plant closer, but you'll get smaller bulbs. For eating, aim for medium to large bulbs.

| Spacing (inches) | Bulbs per square foot | Average bulb size | Yield per 10-foot row |
|------------------|----------------------|------------------|-----------------------|
| 6 x 6            | 4                    | Small to medium  | 40 bulbs (about 4 lb) |
| 8 x 6            | 3                    | Medium           | 30 bulbs (about 3.5 lb) |
| 8 x 8            | 2.25                 | Large            | 22 bulbs (about 3 lb) |

For a family of four, planting 4 pounds of seed garlic (about 160 cloves) at 8x6 spacing will take a bed of about 7 feet by 4 feet (28 square feet). That yields about 120 bulbs, or 12 to 14 pounds of garlic, which is plenty for a year if you store properly.

## How much is too much for storage?

Garlic storage life varies by variety. Softnecks like 'California Early' and 'Inchelium Red' store 6 to 8 months. Hardnecks like 'Music' and 'German Extra Hardy' store 4 to 6 months. Elephant garlic (not true garlic) stores only 3 to 4 months.

If you plant 50 bulbs per person, you'll have more than you can use before it spoils. A good rule: plant only what you can use within 8 months of harvest, plus a bit for seed. For most people, that's 20 to 30 bulbs per person.

## Plan for your harvest window

Garlic is ready when the lower leaves turn brown, usually in July for most zones. You'll cure it for 2 to 4 weeks in a dry, shaded place. After curing, you can store at room temperature (60-70°F) with low humidity. If you have a cool, dry basement (50-60°F), you can extend storage by a few months.

If you find yourself with surplus garlic, consider these options:

- **Sell at farmers market**: A pound of organic garlic sells for $8 to $12 in 2026. A 10-pound surplus could net $80 to $120.
- **Make garlic powder or flakes**: Dehydrate and grind. A pound of fresh garlic yields about 3 ounces of powder.
- **Give away**: Neighbors and family usually appreciate fresh garlic.
- **Plant more next year**: Save the biggest, best bulbs for seed.

## How to decide your planting amount: a step-by-step

1. **Count your household**: How many people cook with garlic regularly? Estimate cloves per week. For example, a couple might use 4 cloves per week, a family of four might use 6.
2. **Calculate annual need**: Multiply weekly cloves by 52. For a couple: 4 x 52 = 208 cloves. For a family: 6 x 52 = 312 cloves.
3. **Convert to bulbs**: Divide by average cloves per bulb (say 8 for hardneck, 10 for softneck). Couple: 208 / 8 = 26 bulbs. Family: 312 / 10 = 31 bulbs.
4. **Add 10% for losses and replanting**: Couple: 26 x 1.1 = 29 bulbs. Family: 31 x 1.1 = 34 bulbs.
5. **Convert to seed weight**: Each bulb has about 8 to 10 cloves, and a pound of seed has 40 to 50 cloves. So 29 bulbs is about 0.6 to 0.7 pounds of seed. That's a small amount. In practice, you'll buy a pound or two, because seed garlic is sold in pounds.

For most households, 1 to 2 pounds of seed garlic is plenty. If you want to sell or share, scale up to 5 pounds, but only if you have a market.

## Common mistakes with overplanting

- **Planting too close**: If you plant more cloves in a small space, you get small bulbs that are hard to peel and store poorly.
- **Ignoring storage**: Even if you plant 100 cloves, you can't store them all in a kitchen cabinet. You need a cool, dry place.
- **Forgetting to replant**: You need to save some bulbs for next year's seed. If you eat all, you'll buy again.

## FAQ

**Can I plant garlic in spring instead of fall?**

Yes, but you'll get smaller bulbs. Spring planting works in zones 5 and warmer if you plant as soon as the soil thaws. Use softneck varieties that don't require vernalization. Expect 30-50% smaller yields.

**How long does garlic last after harvest?**

Cured garlic stored at 60-70°F with 60% humidity lasts 4 to 8 months depending on variety. Softnecks last longer than hardnecks. Check regularly for sprouts or mold.

**What if I have too much garlic? Can I freeze it?**

Yes, you can freeze peeled cloves or chopped garlic in oil (but be careful with botulism risk if not used quickly). Freezing changes texture, so it's best for cooking, not raw use. You can also pickle garlic.

**How much garlic seed should I buy for a 100-square-foot bed?**

At 8x6 spacing, you can fit about 300 cloves, which is about 6 to 7 pounds of seed. That will yield about 300 bulbs, or 30 to 35 pounds. That's a lot for a family, so consider selling or sharing.

## The bottom line

Plant no more than 1 pound of seed garlic per person in your household, plus a little extra for replanting. That gives you about 40 to 50 bulbs, which is enough for a year of cooking. If you want to sell or share, you can plant up to 5 pounds, but only if you have a plan for storage and marketing. Overplanting leads to waste and frustration. Start small, learn your storage conditions, and adjust next year.
