If you're looking for the best corn maze in Kansas, start with these proven favorites: Grinter Farms in Lenexa, Schaake's Pumpkin Patch in Lawrence, and Louisburg Cider Mill in Louisburg. Each offers a unique experience, but for sheer size and family-friendly fun, Grinter Farms is the top pick. Here's how to choose based on your location and what you want.

## Top Corn Mazes in Kansas by Region

### Eastern Kansas (Kansas City Metro)

**Grinter Farms (Lenexa)**
- **Maze size:** 10 acres, with a 4-acre kids' maze
- **2026 dates:** Open late September through October, daily 9 AM to 9 PM
- **Price:** $12 per person, kids under 3 free
- **Highlights:** Hayrides, pumpkin patch, farm animals, and a giant slide. The maze design changes yearly; 2026 theme is "Sunflower State."
- **Why it's best:** It's the largest maze in the metro, well-maintained, and has clear signage. Expect crowds on weekends, so go on a weekday morning.

**Louisburg Cider Mill (Louisburg)**
- **Maze size:** 5 acres
- **2026 dates:** Open September 15 through October 31, daily 9 AM to 6 PM
- **Price:** $10 per person, includes a cider donut
- **Highlights:** Famous for its apple cider and donuts, plus a corn maze, pumpkin patch, and petting zoo. The maze is moderately challenging, with trivia questions along the path.
- **Why it's best:** Great for combining a maze visit with fresh cider and a relaxed atmosphere. Less crowded than Grinter.

### Central Kansas

**Schaake's Pumpkin Patch (Lawrence)**
- **Maze size:** 8 acres, plus a 2-acre toddler maze
- **2026 dates:** Open September 20 through October 31, weekends 10 AM to 6 PM, weekdays by reservation
- **Price:** $15 per person, includes access to all activities
- **Highlights:** Corn maze, pumpkin cannon, cow train, and a giant jumping pillow. The maze is designed with a scavenger hunt for kids.
- **Why it's best:** It's a full agritourism experience, not just a maze. The weekday reservation option is great for homeschool groups or quiet visits.

**Dillon Nature Center (Hutchinson)**
- **Maze size:** 3 acres
- **2026 dates:** Open October 1 to October 31, daily 10 AM to 5 PM
- **Price:** $5 per person, kids under 5 free
- **Highlights:** A smaller, budget-friendly maze with nature trails and a butterfly garden. It's run by the city, so it's more educational.
- **Why it's best:** If you're near Hutchinson and want a low-cost, low-crowd option, this is it. Not as elaborate, but perfect for young kids.

### Western Kansas

**Prairie Pines Farm (Hays)**
- **Maze size:** 6 acres
- **2026 dates:** Open October 1 to October 31, weekends 10 AM to 7 PM
- **Price:** $10 per person
- **Highlights:** Corn maze, hayrides, and a pumpkin patch. The maze is cut into a design that celebrates local history, with a windmill and wheat motif.

## How to Choose the Right Maze for You

Consider these factors:

- **Distance:** Pick a maze within an hour's drive to avoid a long day. For example, if you're in Wichita, consider Schaake's (about 2 hours) or a smaller local option like the one at the Sedgwick County Extension Arboretum (not a full maze, but a small one).
- **Group size:** Large groups (10+) should call ahead for group rates. Most farms offer discounts for groups of 15 or more, typically 10-15% off.
- **Age of kids:** For toddlers, look for mazes with a separate kids' area, like Grinter or Schaake's. For teens, a larger maze with challenges is better.
- **Budget:** Prices range from $5 to $15 per person. Add costs for pumpkins, food, and activities; a family of four can easily spend $50-$80.

## What to Expect in 2026

Most Kansas corn mazes open in late September and run through October. The corn is typically 6-8 feet tall, and paths are about 3 feet wide. Expect to spend 45 minutes to 2 hours inside, depending on the maze size and your navigation skills.

Bring water, wear closed-toe shoes, and use the provided maps. Many mazes have checkpoints with flags or signs to help you find your way. Cell service is often spotty, so download any app or map beforehand.

## Tips for a Great Visit

- **Go early:** Arrive right at opening to avoid crowds and heat. Mornings are cooler and less busy.
- **Check the weather:** Rain can make paths muddy. Call ahead if it's been raining.
- **Wear sunscreen and hats:** Even in October, Kansas sun can be strong.
- **Bring cash:** Some smaller farms are cash-only, though most accept cards.
- **Plan for extras:** Budget for pumpkins ($5-$15 each), food (donuts, cider, kettle corn), and activities like hayrides ($3-$5 per person).

## FAQ

**Q: When is the best time to visit a corn maze in Kansas?**
A: Late September to mid-October is ideal. The corn is fully grown, and temperatures are mild. Avoid Halloween weekend if you want fewer crowds.

**Q: Are corn mazes wheelchair accessible?**
A: Most are not, due to uneven terrain and narrow paths. Call ahead to ask about accessibility; some farms offer a separate accessible path or a hayride alternative.

**Q: Can I bring my dog?**
A: Generally no, for safety and hygiene reasons. Service animals are allowed. Check each farm's policy.

**Q: How long does it take to complete a corn maze?**
A: Varies by size. A 5-acre maze takes about 45 minutes, while a 10-acre maze can take 1.5 to 2 hours. If you're in a hurry, ask for a shortcut map.

## The Bottom Line

The best corn maze in Kansas depends on your location and needs. For a classic, large maze with lots of activities, Grinter Farms is the winner. For a more relaxed, cider-focused trip, Louisburg Cider Mill is excellent. If you're in central or western Kansas, Schaake's and Prairie Pines are solid choices. Check websites for 2026 dates and prices, and go on a weekday to avoid the rush. Enjoy the fall!
