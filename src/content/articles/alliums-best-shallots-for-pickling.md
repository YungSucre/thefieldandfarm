If you want pickled shallots that stay crisp and flavorful, choose varieties with firm flesh, thin skins, and a mild but distinct oniony bite. The best options are 'Gray Griselle', 'French Red', and 'Champion'. Plant them in fall for zones 7-10 or early spring for zones 3-6, and harvest when the tops fall over. This guide covers the top varieties, planting specifics, and a foolproof pickling brine.

## Top shallot varieties for pickling

Not all shallots pickle equally. Look for varieties that hold their shape and don't turn mushy. Here are the ones I trust:

| Variety | Flavor | Pickling quality | Best zones |
|---------|--------|------------------|------------|
| Gray Griselle | Rich, complex | Excellent: firm, crisp | 7-10 (fall), 3-6 (spring) |
| French Red | Mild, sweet | Very good: holds shape | 7-10 (fall), 3-6 (spring) |
| Champion | Mild, slightly sharp | Good: crisp but smaller | 7-10 (fall), 3-6 (spring) |

## Planting and growing for pickling

Shallots for pickling need firm, well-cured bulbs. Start with sets (small bulbs) rather than seeds; they mature faster and produce more uniform sizes.

- **Planting dates:**
  - Zones 7-10: Plant in October or November for a June harvest.
  - Zones 3-6: Plant in early spring (March or April) as soon as soil is workable, harvest in August.
- **Spacing:** Plant sets 6 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart. Set them 1 inch deep, with the tip just below the surface.
- **Soil:** Well-drained, sandy loam with pH 6.0-7.0. Add compost before planting.
- **Water:** Keep soil evenly moist but not soggy. Stop watering once the tops start to yellow.

Harvest when the tops fall over and brown. Cure in a dry, shaded spot for 2-3 weeks until the necks are papery. For pickling, use bulbs that are 1-2 inches in diameter; larger ones can be halved or quartered.

## The pickling process

Once your shallots are cured, you can pickle them within days. Here is a simple brine that keeps them crisp:

**Ingredients:**
- 1 pound shallots, peeled (about 20-25 small bulbs)
- 1 cup white vinegar (5% acidity)
- 1 cup water
- 1 tablespoon pickling salt (not table salt)
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- Optional: 1 teaspoon black peppercorns, 1 bay leaf, 1 garlic clove

**Steps:**
1. Peel the shallots and trim the root ends, leaving the base intact so they don't fall apart.
2. Pack them into sterilized pint jars, leaving 1/2 inch headspace.
3. In a saucepan, combine vinegar, water, salt, sugar, and spices. Bring to a boil, stirring to dissolve.
4. Pour the hot brine over the shallots, covering them completely.
5. Wipe the rims, seal with lids, and process in a boiling water bath for 10 minutes (adjust for altitude).
6. Let cool, then store in a cool, dark place for at least 2 weeks before eating. They keep for up to a year.

For a quick refrigerator version, skip the water bath and store in the fridge; they'll be ready in 3 days and last 2 months.

## Tools and costs

You don't need fancy equipment. Here's what I use:

- **Canning jars:** Pint jars, about $12 for a dozen.
- **Water bath canner:** $30-$50 if you don't have one; a large stockpot works too.
- **Pickling salt:** $4-$6 per pound.
- **Shallot sets:** $10-$15 per pound, enough for a 10-foot row.

Total start-up cost is under $50 if you already have basic kitchen tools.

## FAQ

**Can I pickle shallots without canning?**
Yes, use the refrigerator method. Just skip the water bath and store in the fridge. They'll be ready in 3 days and last up to 2 months.

**How long do pickled shallots last?**
Processed in a water bath, they last up to a year in a cool, dark pantry. Once opened, refrigerate and use within 1 month.

**What if my shallots are too large?**
Halve or quarter them so they pickle evenly. Just make sure the pieces are uniform in size.

**Can I reuse the brine?**
You can, but it will be weaker. For best flavor, make fresh brine for each batch.

## The bottom line

For pickling, choose 'Gray Griselle' or 'French Red' shallots. Plant in fall or early spring depending on your zone, harvest when tops fall, cure properly, and use a vinegar brine with pickling salt. With minimal equipment and less than an hour of active time, you'll have crisp, tangy pickled shallots that beat anything from the store. Start with a small batch this season to dial in your preferred flavor.
