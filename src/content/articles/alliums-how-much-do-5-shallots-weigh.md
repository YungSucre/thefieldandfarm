If you are holding five shallots and wondering if that is enough for your recipe or your garden bed, here is the direct answer: five average shallots weigh about 5 ounces (140 grams), but the range is wide, from 3.5 ounces (100 grams) for small ones to 8 ounces (225 grams) for jumbo varieties. The exact weight depends on the variety, growing conditions, and whether you are measuring with or without the papery skin. This guide breaks down the numbers, explains why weight matters for planting and cooking, and gives you practical ways to estimate without a scale.

## Average weight of 5 shallots

A single shallot bulb typically weighs between 0.7 and 1.6 ounces (20 to 45 grams) when harvested at the right time. That means five shallots will usually land in the 3.5 to 8 ounce range (100 to 225 grams). The most common supermarket shallot, the French gray or Griselle, averages about 1 ounce (28 grams) per bulb, so five of those weigh around 5 ounces (140 grams).

Here is a quick reference table for common shallot sizes:

| Size category | Single bulb weight | 5 bulbs total weight |
|---------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Small (pickling) | 0.5 oz (15 g) | 2.5 oz (75 g) |
| Medium (standard) | 1 oz (28 g) | 5 oz (140 g) |
| Large (jumbo) | 1.5 oz (42 g) | 7.5 oz (210 g) |

These weights are for bulbs with the dry outer skin still on. Once you peel away the skin and trim the roots, you lose about 10% of the weight, so five peeled shallots weigh closer to 4.5 ounces (125 grams) for medium ones.

## Why weight matters for planting

When you plant shallots, the size of the bulb you start with directly affects the size of the harvest. A medium bulb, about 1 ounce (28 grams), will produce a cluster of 5 to 10 new bulbs, each roughly the same size as the parent. If you plant small bulbs, you get smaller yields. If you plant jumbo bulbs, you might get larger individual shallots, but you will also need more spacing.

For a 10-foot row, you will need about 20 to 25 medium shallot sets. That is roughly 1.5 pounds (680 grams) of planting stock. At a typical price of $8 to $12 per pound for seed shallots in 2026, that is a $12 to $18 investment for a row that yields 10 to 15 pounds of shallots at harvest.

## Why weight matters for cooking

Recipes often call for a specific number of shallots, but weight is a more reliable measure. A medium shallot yields about 2 tablespoons (30 ml) of minced shallot. So five medium shallots give you about 10 tablespoons, which is a generous 2/3 cup (160 ml). If a recipe calls for 1/2 cup of minced shallot, you will need about 4 medium bulbs.

Here is a quick conversion guide:

- 1 medium shallot = 2 tablespoons minced
- 5 medium shallots = 2/3 cup minced
- 1 large shallot = 3 tablespoons minced

If you are substituting shallots for onions, remember that 1 medium shallot equals about 1/4 cup chopped onion, but with a milder, sweeter flavor.

## How to estimate weight without a scale

Most home kitchens do not have a scale handy, but you can estimate shallot weight by size. A medium shallot is about the size of a golf ball, roughly 1.5 inches (4 cm) in diameter. A small one is the size of a walnut, and a large one is the size of a small hen's egg.

If you need exact weight for a recipe, use this simple trick: fill a measuring cup with water, note the level, drop in the shallots, and see how much the water rises. One fluid ounce of water weighs about 1 ounce (28 grams), so the displacement gives you the weight in ounces. This works for any vegetable and takes less than a minute.

## Varieties and their typical weights

Different shallot varieties have distinct size profiles. Here are three common ones and what you can expect per bulb:

- **French Gray (Griselle)**: Small to medium, 0.7 to 1 ounce (20 to 28 grams). Strong, classic flavor. Five bulbs weigh about 4 to 5 ounces (115 to 140 grams).
- **Dutch Yellow**: Medium to large, 1 to 1.5 ounces (28 to 42 grams). Mild and sweet. Five bulbs weigh 5 to 7.5 ounces (140 to 210 grams).
- **Prisma (hybrid)**: Uniform medium, about 1 ounce (28 grams) each. Good for storage. Five bulbs weigh about 5 ounces (140 grams).

When buying seed shallots, look for bulbs that are firm and free of sprouts. Avoid any that feel light for their size, as they may have dried out.

## Practical steps for this week

If you are planning to plant shallots this season, here is what to do:

1. **Weigh your sets**: Use a kitchen scale to check that your seed shallots average at least 0.7 ounces (20 grams) each. Discard any that are much smaller.
2. **Space them properly**: Plant sets 6 inches (15 cm) apart in rows 12 inches (30 cm) apart. This gives each cluster room to expand.
3. **Plant at the right depth**: Set the bulb so the tip is just below the soil surface, about 1 inch (2.5 cm) deep. In USDA zones 5 to 7, plant in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked. In zones 8 and warmer, plant in fall for a spring harvest.
4. **Water consistently**: Shallots need about 1 inch (2.5 cm) of water per week. Mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

If you are cooking, use the water displacement method to measure your shallots for a recipe that calls for a specific weight. This takes less than a minute and ensures you do not over or under season your dish.

## FAQ

**Q: How many shallots are in a pound?**
A: A pound (16 ounces) of medium shallots contains about 16 bulbs, since each weighs about 1 ounce. If the shallots are large, you might get only 10 to 12 per pound.

**Q: Do shallots weigh more when they are fresh from the garden?**
A: Yes, freshly harvested shallots have a higher moisture content and can weigh 10 to 15% more than cured, dried ones. Once you cure them for 2 to 3 weeks in a dry, airy place, they lose that extra water.

**Q: Can I use the weight of shallots to estimate onion equivalents?**
A: Yes. One medium shallot (1 ounce) equals about 1/4 cup chopped onion. For a recipe that calls for 1 cup of chopped onion, use 4 medium shallots, which will weigh about 4 ounces (115 grams).

**Q: Why do some shallots have double bulbs?**
A: Shallots naturally grow in clusters, and a single planted bulb can split into multiple new bulbs. This is normal and does not affect weight per bulb, but it does mean your harvest will have many small to medium bulbs rather than one large one.

## The bottom line

Five shallots weigh about 5 ounces (140 grams) for medium bulbs, but expect anywhere from 3.5 to 8 ounces depending on size and variety. For planting, aim for sets that weigh at least 0.7 ounces each. For cooking, use the water displacement method if you need precision. Knowing these numbers helps you buy the right amount, plant the right spacing, and cook with confidence. Keep a kitchen scale handy, and you will never guess again.
