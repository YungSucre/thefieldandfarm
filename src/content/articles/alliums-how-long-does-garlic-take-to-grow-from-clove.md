Garlic takes about 8 to 9 months from planting a clove to harvest, depending on your zone and variety. In most of the US, you plant in fall (October or November) and harvest the following summer (June or July). If you plant in spring, you will harvest smaller bulbs in late summer, but it still takes roughly 5 to 6 months. Here is the exact timeline and what to do at each stage.

## The full timeline from clove to bulb

Garlic is a long-season crop. The clove needs a cold period (vernalization) to trigger bulb formation, which is why fall planting works best. Here is a month-by-month breakdown for zones 3 to 8:

| Stage | Time from planting | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planting | Day 0 | Clove roots begin to grow before winter |
| Dormancy | 1 to 3 months | Cold weather, little top growth |
| Spring growth | 4 to 5 months | Leaves emerge, bulb starts to form |
| Bulb expansion | 6 to 7 months | Bulb swells, lower leaves yellow |
| Harvest | 8 to 9 months | Bulb is mature, tops dry |

In warmer zones (9 to 10), you may need to pre-chill cloves in the fridge for 6 to 8 weeks before planting, or plant in late fall and harvest in

If you miss fall planting, you can still plant in spring, but expect smaller bulbs. Choose a softneck variety like 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red' for spring planting, as they need less cold.

## How to plant a clove: step by step

1. **Break the bulb** into individual cloves about 24 hours before planting. Keep the papery skin on. Use the largest cloves; small ones produce weak plants.
2. **Prepare the bed**: full sun, well-drained soil with pH 6.0 to 7.0. Add 2 inches of compost and a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at 1 pound per 10 square feet.
3. **Plant each clove** pointed end up, 2 inches deep in heavy soil, 1 inch deep in sandy soil. Space cloves 6 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart.
4. **Water well** after planting, then mulch with 4 to 6 inches of straw or shredded leaves. Mulch protects against freeze-thaw cycles and suppresses weeds.
5. **In spring**, pull back mulch when shoots are 2 to 3 inches tall. Keep soil evenly moist, about 1 inch of water per week.
6. **Stop watering** 2 to 3 weeks before harvest to let the bulbs cure.

## When to harvest: signs and timing

Garlic is ready when the lower leaves turn brown but the upper 3 to 4 leaves are still green. This usually happens 8 to 9 months after fall planting. Dig up a bulb to check: the bulb should be well formed with distinct cloves, and the wrapper should be tight.

Do not wait too long. If the leaves are all brown, the bulbs may split and store poorly. Harvest on a dry day, and gently lift with a fork, not a shovel, to avoid bruising.

## Common mistakes that delay or ruin garlic

- **Planting too shallow**: cloves must be deep enough to avoid frost heave. If you see cloves pushed out of the soil, replant immediately.
- **Skipping cold period**: in warm zones, garlic may not form bulbs without chilling. Pre-chill cloves in the fridge at 40°F for 6 to 8 weeks before planting.
- **Harvesting too early or late**: early harvest gives small bulbs, late harvest splits wrappers. Use the leaf count method.
- **Overwatering in spring**: soggy soil causes rot. Water only when the top inch is dry.

## FAQ

**Can I plant garlic from the grocery store?**
Yes, but it may be treated to prevent sprouting, and varieties are often unknown. For best results, buy seed garlic from a reputable supplier.

**What if I plant in spring?**
You will still get garlic, but bulbs will be smaller. Plant as early as the soil can be worked, and choose softneck varieties. Expect harvest in late August or September.

**How do I know when to stop watering?**
Stop watering when the lower leaves start to yellow, usually 2 to 3 weeks before harvest. This helps the bulbs cure and store longer.

**Can I grow garlic in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 8 inches deep, with drainage holes. Plant cloves 4 inches apart, and water regularly. Harvest on the same schedule as garden garlic.

## The bottom line

Garlic takes 8 to 9 months from clove to harvest when planted in fall, and about 5 to 6 months if planted in spring. The key is to plant at the right time for your zone, give it a cold period, and harvest when the lower leaves brown. With proper care, you will have fresh garlic for months. Start with a reliable variety like 'Music' or 'California Early', and you will see success in your first season.
