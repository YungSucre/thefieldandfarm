If you're out of fresh garlic or just want a quicker option, 2 cloves of garlic equal about 1 teaspoon of garlic powder. That's the standard conversion for dried, ground garlic. For minced or chopped fresh garlic, 2 cloves give you roughly 2 teaspoons, so the powder is more concentrated. Keep this ratio in mind when swapping in your kitchen, and adjust to taste if you prefer a milder or stronger garlic flavor.

## Why the conversion matters

Garlic powder is dehydrated and ground, which removes water and concentrates flavor. That's why you need less powder than fresh. The typical ratio is 1/2 teaspoon powder per clove, so 2 cloves equal 1 teaspoon. This works for most recipes, but remember that powder lacks the texture and pungency of fresh garlic. If you're

cooking a dish where garlic is a star, like a garlic bread or aioli, you might want to use fresh cloves or a mix.

## How to measure accurately

Use a standard measuring spoon, not a kitchen spoon, for accuracy. Level the powder with a knife. If you're using granulated garlic (coarser than powder), the conversion is slightly different: 1/4 teaspoon granulated equals 1 clove, so for 2 cloves you'd use 1/2 teaspoon. Always check the label: some blends include salt or other spices, which will affect the flavor and amount you need.

## Substituting in recipes

When you swap powder for fresh, add it earlier in cooking so it rehydrates and releases flavor. For uncooked dishes like salads or dips, let the powder sit in a little water or lemon juice for 5 minutes before adding. If a recipe calls for 2 cloves and you only have powder, use 1 teaspoon. If you have fresh and need powder, use 1 teaspoon for every 2 cloves. For a stronger punch, you can add an extra 1/4 teaspoon, but don't overdo it: powder can turn bitter if you use too much.

## Growing your own garlic for powder

If you're a gardener, you can dry and grind your own garlic powder. Hardneck varieties like 'Music' or 'German Extra Hardy' have bold flavor, while softnecks like 'California Early' store longer. Harvest when the lower leaves brown, then cure for 3 to 4 weeks in a dry, airy place. Peel the cloves, slice thinly, and dry in a dehydrator at 115°F for 8 to 12 hours, or in an oven on the lowest setting with the door ajar. Grind in a spice grinder or blender, then sift. Store in an airtight jar in a cool, dark place; it will keep for up to a year. A pound of fresh garlic yields about 1/2 cup of powder, so plan accordingly.

## Cost and convenience

A 3-ounce jar of commercial garlic powder costs $4 to $6, which equals about 6 tablespoons. That's roughly 36 teaspoons, so the equivalent of 72 cloves. Compare that to fresh garlic at $0.50 to $1 per bulb (about 10 cloves), and powder is cheaper per clove, but you lose the fresh flavor. For homesteaders, making your own is cost-effective if you grow garlic, but it takes time and energy. For most, keeping both on hand is the best approach.

## FAQ

### Can I use garlic salt instead of powder?

Garlic salt is a mix of garlic powder and salt, usually 3 parts salt to 1 part garlic. If a recipe calls for 1 teaspoon garlic powder, you'd need 4 teaspoons of garlic salt, but then reduce the salt in the recipe by 3 teaspoons. It's easier to just use powder and add salt separately.

### How do I convert 2 cloves to minced garlic?

Two average cloves yield about 2 teaspoons of minced garlic. If you buy pre-minced garlic in a jar, 1 teaspoon equals about 1 clove, so use 2 teaspoons for 2 cloves. Check the label for added water or preservatives.

### Does garlic powder lose potency over time?

Yes, it does. Ground garlic begins to lose flavor after about 6 months. For best results, use within a year, and store it away from heat and light. If it smells weak, use a bit more, but taste as you go.

### What if I only have garlic flakes?

Garlic flakes are larger and less concentrated. Use 1 tablespoon of flakes for 2 cloves, and rehydrate them in warm water for 10 minutes before adding to your dish. They work well in soups and stews.

## The bottom line

For 2 cloves of garlic, use 1 teaspoon of garlic powder. This conversion works for most cooking, but adjust for your taste and the dish. If you're growing garlic, consider making your own powder for a fresher, more flavorful result. Keep your powder in a cool, dark cabinet and replace it yearly. With this simple ratio, you'll never be stuck without garlic again.
