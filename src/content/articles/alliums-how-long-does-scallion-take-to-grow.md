Scallions, also called green onions, are one of the fastest alliums you can grow. From seed, expect 60 to 90 days to full size, but you can start harvesting earlier as thin scallions at 30 to 40 days. If you plant sets (small bulbs), you'll have scallions in about 30 days. The exact time depends on variety, season, and how you harvest. Here's the breakdown.

## Why scallions are quick

Scallions don't form a large bulb, so the plant puts all its energy into leafy growth. That means faster harvests than bulb onions, which need 100 to 150 days. You can also harvest scallions at any stage, from pencil-thin to thick-stalked, which gives you flexibility.

## From seed: 60 to 90 days

Direct sow seeds in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked, or start indoors 6 to 8 weeks before your last frost. Soil temperature should be at least 50°F for germination, but 60 to 70°F is ideal. Seeds sprout in 7 to 14 days.

For a continuous supply, sow a new batch every 2 to 3 weeks from early spring through late summer. In mild climates (zones 8 to 10), you can also sow in fall for winter harvest.

Plant seeds 1/4 inch deep, 1 inch apart in rows 12 inches apart. Thin to 2 inches apart for larger stalks, or leave them crowded for thinner scallions. Keep the soil consistently moist; dry spells slow growth and make the stalks tough.

### Expected timeline from seed

- Germination: 7 to 14 days
- Thin scallions (pencil-thick): 30 to 40 days
- Full-size scallions (12 to 18 inches): 60 to 90 days
- If left longer, they will start to bulb and become more like small onions.

## From sets: 30 days

Sets are small, dormant bulbs sold in garden centers. Plant them 1 inch deep, 2 inches apart, with the pointed tip up. They root and start growing within a week. You can pull them as scallions as soon as they reach usable size, usually 30 days. If you leave them, they'll form larger bulbs in 60 to 90 days.

Sets are more expensive than seeds: a 1-pound bag costs $8 to $12 and plants about 50 to 60 sets. Seeds cost $2 to $4 per packet and yield hundreds of plants. If you want speed and don't mind the cost, sets are the way to go.

## Best varieties for speed

Some scallion varieties are bred for quick growth and bolt resistance. These are reliable choices for 2026:

- 'White Lisbon': classic, fast, 60 days to full size, hardy in cold.
- 'Evergreen Hardy White': 60 days, overwinters in zones 5 to 9.
- 'Red Beard': red-stemmed, 65 days, adds color to salads.
- 'Tokyo Long White': 70 days, thick stalks, mild flavor.
- 'Nabechan': Japanese bunching, 65 days, very uniform.

For sets, look for 'Stuttgarter' or 'Ebenezer' in the onion section; they work fine as scallions.

## Factors that affect growth time

- Temperature: scallions grow best in 55 to 75°F. Heat above 80°F slows growth and can cause bolting (flowering), which makes them tough. In hot climates, plant in early spring or fall.
- Sun: full sun (6+ hours) is best, but they tolerate partial shade, which can slow growth.
- Soil: loose, well-drained soil with organic matter. Heavy clay can stunt roots and delay harvest.
- Water: consistent moisture is key. Dry spells cause slow growth and a stronger flavor.
- Fertilizer: a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at planting, then a side dressing of nitrogen (like blood meal) every 3 weeks. Too much nitrogen can make them floppy, but a little helps speed leaf growth.

## How to harvest for continuous supply

You don't have to wait for full size. Start harvesting when stalks are about 6 inches tall. Use scissors to snip leaves, leaving the bulb in the ground; it will regrow for multiple cuts. Or pull entire plants, roots and all, for a clean bunch. If you pull, you can replant the roots in a pot or garden bed and they'll regrow.

For a continuous harvest, plant a "cut and come again" row: sow seeds thickly, then cut the tops when they reach 6 to 8 inches. They'll regrow 2 to 3 times before they lose vigor.

## Storing and using your harvest

Fresh scallions keep in the fridge for 1 to 2 weeks in a plastic bag. To store longer, chop and freeze them: they'll keep for 3 to 4 months. You can also regrow the white ends in a glass of water on the windowsill; they'll produce new greens in a week, though they won't be as vigorous as garden-grown.

## FAQ

**Can I grow scallions indoors?**
Yes, in a sunny windowsill or under a grow light. Use a pot at least 6 inches deep, sow seeds 1/4 inch deep, and keep soil moist. Expect 60 to 70 days to full size, but you can snip leaves earlier. Indoor scallions are slower in winter due to lower light.

**Why are my scallions taking longer than 90 days?**
Check for cold soil (below 50°F), poor drainage, or lack of sun. Also, if you planted in midsummer heat, growth slows. Try a bolt-resistant variety and keep them well watered.

**Can I harvest scallions multiple times?**
Yes, if you cut leaves above the bulb, the plant will regrow. You can do this 2 to 3 times. For best regrowth, leave at least 1 inch of leaf above the bulb.

**What's the difference between scallions and green onions?**
Nothing, they're the same thing. Some varieties are bunching onions (never bulb), while others are regular onions harvested early. Both work for scallions.

## The bottom line

Scallions are a fast, foolproof crop for any gardener. From seed, plan on 60 to 90 days, but start harvesting at 30 days for baby scallions. From sets, you'll be eating in a month. Plant a new batch every few weeks to keep a steady supply. Choose a quick variety like 'White Lisbon' or 'Evergreen Hardy White', keep the soil moist, and you'll have scallions all season. For $3 worth of seeds, you can grow hundreds of scallions, a better deal than any store. So get sowing: you'll be chopping fresh scallions in no time.
