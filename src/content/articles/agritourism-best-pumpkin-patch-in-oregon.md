If you're looking for the best pumpkin patch in Oregon, start with the Willamette Valley, where most of the state's pumpkin farms cluster. The top picks for 2026 are The Pumpkin Patch on Sauvie Island (near Portland), Bauman's Farm & Garden (Gervais), and Kruger's Farm (also on Sauvie Island). Each offers strong variety, fair prices, and family-friendly activities. Below, I break down what each does best, what it costs, and how to pick the right one for your trip.

## What Makes a Pumpkin Patch "Best"?

A great pumpkin patch combines three things: healthy, diverse pumpkins; reasonable prices; and a setting that's worth the drive. For gardeners and homesteaders, variety matters more than bounce houses. You want pie pumpkins, carving giants, and storage types

. The farms below all grow multiple varieties, so you can compare sizes and colors in person.

## Top Pumpkin Patches in Oregon for 2026

### 1. The Pumpkin Patch on Sauvie Island (Portland area)

This is the classic. It's been running for over 40 years and sits on 400 acres. You'll find over 50 varieties of pumpkins, from tiny 'Jack-Be-Little' to 100-pound 'Atlantic Giant'. They also have a corn maze, hay rides, and a farm store with local produce.

- **Price**: $8 admission (includes hay ride), pumpkins priced by size, typically $0.50 to $1.50 per pound. A medium carving pumpkin (15-20 lbs) runs $10-$25.
- **Best for**: Families and first-timers. The variety is unmatched.
- **Crowds**: Weekends are packed. Go on a weekday morning if you can.

### 2. Bauman's Farm & Garden (Gervais, near Salem)

Bauman's is a working farm with a huge pumpkin patch, plus a bakery, cider press, and petting zoo. They grow 40 acres of pumpkins, including 'Cinderella' and 'Fairytale' for decoration, and 'Sugar Pie' for baking.

- **Price**: Free admission, but parking is $5. Pumpkins are $0.79 per pound. A 15-pound carving pumpkin costs about $12.
- **Best for**: Homesteaders who want to buy in bulk. They sell by the half-ton for canning.
- **Extras**: Their apple cider donuts are worth the trip alone.

### 3. Kruger's Farm (Sauvie Island)

Kruger's is smaller but focuses on organic practices. They grow 20 varieties, including 'Jarrahdale' (blue-gray) and 'Lumina' (white). They also have a farm stand with seasonal vegetables.

- **Price**: $5 entry, pumpkins $0.99 per pound. A medium pumpkin runs $12-$18.
- **Best for**: Organic-minded visitors. Their soil is amended with compost, and they rotate crops to reduce pests.
- **Note**: They close earlier in October, so check their calendar.

## Other Notable Patches by Region

- **Central Oregon**: Smith Rock Ranch (Terrebonne) has a small patch with 'Howden' and 'Connecticut Field'. Prices are $0.60/lb. It's a dry climate, so pumpkins are smaller but dense.
- **Southern Oregon**: Hanley Farm (Central Point) runs a historic farm with a pumpkin patch in October. They charge $5 per car, and pumpkins are $0.50/lb. Great for a low-key visit.
- **Eastern Oregon**: The Eastern Oregon Museum of Agriculture (Haines) hosts a fall festival with a small patch. It's not a major destination, but if you're in the area, it's fine.

## How to Choose the Right Pumpkin

When you're at the patch, look for these signs of a good pumpkin:

- **Stem**: It should be green and firm. A dry, brown stem means it's been off the vine too long.
- **Skin**: No soft spots or cuts. A blemish can turn into rot in a week.
- **Sound**: Thump it. A hollow sound means it's mature. A dull thud might mean it's still wet inside.
- **Weight**: For its size, a heavy pumpkin has thicker walls, which is good for carving but not for pie (you want thin walls for baking).

## Tips for a Smooth Visit

- **Go early**: Arrive by 10 AM on weekends to beat crowds and get the best pick.
- **Bring cash**: Some smaller patches don't take cards.
- **Wear boots**: Patches can be muddy, especially after rain.
- **Check crop status**: Call ahead or check social media. Some years, a late frost or dry spell reduces yields.
- **Bring your own wagon**: If you have one, it saves your back. Farms often have wagons, but they run out.

## FAQ

### When is pumpkin season in Oregon?

Most patches open in late September and run through October 31. Peak picking is mid-October, when pumpkins are fully mature. Some farms close earlier if they're picked clean.

### Can I grow my own pumpkins instead?

Yes, but it takes space and time. Plant seeds in late May to early June (after last frost) in well-drained soil. Give each vine 4-6 square feet. Harvest when the rind is hard and the stem is dry. Expect 1-2 pumpkins per vine for varieties like 'Howden'.

### Do these farms sell pie pumpkins?

Yes. Bauman's and Kruger's sell 'Sugar Pie' and 'New England Pie' by the pound. You can also buy them in bulk for canning. Ask at the farm stand.

### Are there any u-pick pumpkin patches that are free?

Some smaller farms, like Hanley Farm, charge only for parking. But most charge per pound. Free admission doesn't mean free pumpkins. Budget for both.

## The Bottom Line

For the best overall experience, head to The Pumpkin Patch on Sauvie Island for variety and tradition. If you want to buy in bulk for canning or baking, Bauman's is your spot. For organic options, Kruger's is solid. No matter which you choose, go on a weekday, wear boots, and check the farm's website before you drive. A good pumpkin patch is about more than the pumpkins: it's about the day you spend there. Pick a farm that fits your needs, and you'll leave with a car full of squash and a memory worth keeping.
