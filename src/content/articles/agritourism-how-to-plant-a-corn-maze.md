Planting a corn maze is a big project, but it is doable if you plan ahead and follow a few key steps. Start by choosing a field with good drainage and full sun, then prepare the soil, plant the right corn variety, and cut the paths after the corn is knee-high. Here is the exact process, with real numbers for 2026.

## Site selection and soil prep

Pick a field that is at least 1 acre for a commercial maze, but even a quarter acre can work for a home or small farm maze. The soil should be well-drained and have a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. Test your soil in the fall or early spring, and add lime if needed to raise pH, or sulfur to lower it. Work in 200 to 300 pounds of 10-10-10 fertilizer per acre, or use compost at a rate of 2 to 3 tons per acre. Till the soil to a depth of 6 to 8 inches, and remove any rocks or debris that could interfere with planting or walking.

## Choosing the right corn variety

For a maze, you need a corn that grows tall and strong, with thick stalks that can withstand foot traffic. Field corn, not sweet corn, is the best choice. Popular varieties include:

- **Pioneer P1197**: A classic field corn that reaches 8 to 9 feet tall, with sturdy stalks. Good for most zones.
- **Dekalb DKC62-08**: Another tall variety, 8 to 10 feet, with good drought tolerance.
- **Blue River 4B508**: A high-yield variety that grows 8 to 9 feet, ideal for northern zones.

For a longer season, consider a variety with a maturity of 100 to 110 days. In colder zones (3-4), choose a shorter season variety like **Pioneer P0157** (95 days). In warmer zones (5-7), you can use a 110-day variety. Always buy seed from a reputable supplier, and plan for 20 to 25 pounds of seed per acre. At 2026 prices, that is about $40 to $60 per bag, and one bag covers 1 to 2 acres.

## Planting dates and spacing

Plant your corn after the last frost date, when soil temperature is at least 50°F. Here are typical planting windows by zone:

| Zone | Planting window |
|------|-----------------|
| 3-4  | May 15 - June 1 |
| 5-6  | May 1 - May 20 |
| 7-8  | April 15 - May 10 |

Plant seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep, with 6 to 8 inches between seeds in the row, and 30 to 36 inches between rows. This spacing gives you about 25,000 to 30,000 plants per acre. If you are using a precision planter, set it for 2 seeds per foot. For a small maze, you can plant by hand using a jab planter, but that is slow. A walk-behind planter, like the Earthway 1001B, costs about $100 and works well for a quarter-acre maze.

## Designing and cutting the maze

Before planting, design your maze on paper or using software like AutoCAD or a simple grid. Mark the paths with flags or stakes so you know where to cut. After the corn reaches 12 to 18 inches tall (usually 4 to 6 weeks after planting), it is time to cut the paths. Use a string trimmer or a small walk-behind mower with a sharp blade. Cut a path width of 3 to 4 feet for easy walking. Be careful not to cut too early, as the corn is still tender, but do not wait too long or the stalks become tough.

If you want a more intricate design, you can use GPS-guided equipment, but that is expensive. For most small farms, a simple grid or spiral pattern is fine. Cut the paths in a single pass, and then walk the maze to check for any missed spots.

## Maintenance and care

Water the maze regularly, especially during dry spells. Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week. If you have irrigation, set up soaker hoses or overhead sprinklers. Fertilize with a side dressing of nitrogen (34-0-0) at a rate of 100 pounds per acre when the corn is 12 inches tall. Watch for pests like corn earworm and armyworm, and treat with Bt or spinosad if you see damage. Weeds can be controlled with a pre-emergent herbicide like atrazine, applied before planting, or by cultivating between rows.

## Harvest and after-season

Your maze will be ready for visitors in late summer, about 90 to 110 days after planting. The corn will stay standing for several weeks, but it will start to dry and fall over by late fall. After the season, you can cut the stalks and compost them, or leave them to add organic matter to the soil. If you want to use the field for another crop, till the stalks under in the spring.

## Costs and revenue

Startup costs for a 1-acre maze: seed ($40-$60), fertilizer ($50-$100), soil test ($20-$30), and planting/cutting equipment (if you do not own, rent or borrow). Total is about $150 to $250. If you charge $8 to $12 per person, and you get 200 visitors, you can make $1,600 to $2,400. That is a good return for a small investment. For a larger maze, you will need more seed and labor, but the profit margin stays similar.

## FAQ

**How long does it take for corn to grow tall enough for a maze?**

Corn reaches 12 to 18 inches in about 4 to 6 weeks after planting, depending on temperature and moisture. In cooler zones, it may take longer. Plan to cut paths when the corn is knee-high, which is usually 6 to 8 weeks after planting.

**Can I use sweet corn for a maze?**

Sweet corn is not ideal because it is shorter and has weaker stalks. Field corn is taller and more durable. If you only have sweet corn, plant it thicker, but expect more lodging.

**How do I make the maze interesting for visitors?**

Add checkpoints with trivia questions, or create a scavenger hunt. Use colored flags or signs at intersections. You can also cut a few dead-end paths to add challenge. Keep paths wide enough for two people to walk side by side.

**What if my corn does not grow well?**

If you have poor germination, replant immediately. If the corn is stunted, check soil pH and fertility. Water deeply during dry spells. If pests are a problem, apply an appropriate pesticide. Do not give up; corn is resilient.

## The bottom line

Planting a corn maze is a rewarding project that can bring in extra income or just provide fun for your family. The key is to choose the right site, plant the right variety, and cut paths at the right time. With careful planning and a little patience, you will have a maze that is ready for visitors in late summer. Start with a small maze if you are new, and expand as you learn. Good luck, and enjoy the harvest!
