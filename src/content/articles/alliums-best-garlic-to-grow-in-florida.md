If you want to grow garlic in Florida, you need softneck varieties, and you need to plant them in the fall, from October through December. Hardneck garlic won't form bulbs here because it needs a cold period that Florida rarely provides. Stick with softnecks like 'Creole' or 'Artichoke' types, and you can harvest real bulbs by May or June.

## Why Softneck Garlic Wins in Florida

Garlic types fall into two camps: hardneck and softneck. Hardneck garlic sends up a flower stalk (the scape) and requires a period of cold (below 40°F for several weeks) to trigger bulb formation. Most of Florida, especially Zones 9a and 10a, doesn't get that cold for long enough. Softneck garlic, on the other hand, doesn't need that chi

## When to Plant Garlic in Florida

Plant garlic in Florida from October to December, but the exact window depends on your zone. The goal is to give the cloves time to establish roots before winter, but not so much time that they sprout too early and get hit by a warm spell.

| USDA Zone | Best Planting Window | Harvest Window |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------|
| 8b (Panhandle) | October 15 - November 15 | May - June |
| 9a (North/Central) | November 1 - December 1 | May - June |
| 9b (Central/South) | November 15 - December 15 | June - July |
| 10a (South Florida) | December 1 - December 31 | June - July |

If you plant too early, the garlic may put on too much top growth before winter, which can stress the plant. Too late, and the roots won't establish before the heat arrives. For most of Florida, mid-November is a safe bet.

## How to Plant Garlic: Step by Step

1. **Buy seed garlic**: Order from a reputable supplier like Southern Exposure Seed Exchange or Burpee. You can also use garlic from a grocery store, but it may be treated to prevent sprouting. Seed garlic costs about $10 to $15 per pound, and a pound plants about 50 cloves.
2. **Prepare the soil**: Garlic needs well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. If your soil is heavy clay, raise beds or add compost. Work in a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at a rate of 2 pounds per 100 square feet.
3. **Break the bulbs into cloves**: Do this just before planting. Keep the papery skin on each clove.
4. **Plant the cloves**: Push each clove into the soil, pointed end up, about 2 inches deep. Space cloves 6 inches apart in rows that are 12 inches apart. In Florida, you can plant a bit shallower (1-2 inches) because the soil stays warmer.
5. **Mulch**: After planting, cover the bed with 2 to 3 inches of straw or shredded leaves. This keeps the soil cool and suppresses weeds.
6. **Water**: Water thoroughly after planting, then keep the soil moist but not soggy. In Florida, you may need to water twice a week during dry spells.

## Caring for Garlic in Florida

Garlic is a heavy feeder. In January, side-dress with nitrogen fertilizer (like blood meal) at a rate of 1 tablespoon per plant. In March, do it again. Stop fertilizing once the leaves start to yellow in late spring.

Water deeply once a week if it doesn't rain. Florida's spring rains can be heavy, so make sure your soil drains well to avoid rot.

Watch for pests: aphids and thrips can be a problem. Knock them off with a strong spray of water or use insecticidal soap. Also, keep the bed weed-free; weeds compete for nutrients.

## When and How to Harvest

Garlic is ready to harvest when the lower leaves turn brown but the upper leaves are still green. This usually happens in May or June in Florida. Dig up a bulb to check: if the cloves are plump and the skin is tight, it's ready.

To harvest, loosen the soil with a garden fork, then pull the bulbs gently. Do not wash them. Instead, let them cure in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated area for 2 to 3 weeks. After curing, trim the roots and cut the stems to about 1 inch above the bulb. Store in a cool, dry place (60-70°F) with good air circulation.

## FAQ

**Can I grow hardneck garlic in Florida?**

Only in the Panhandle (Zone 8b) and only if you get a cold winter. Even then, it's a gamble. Softneck garlic is much more reliable.

**What if I plant garlic in the spring?**

Spring planting rarely works in Florida because the heat comes too fast. The bulbs won't form properly. Stick to fall planting.

**Do I need to refrigerate garlic before planting?**

No. In Florida, the winter is mild enough that garlic doesn't need a cold treatment. Just plant it directly.

**How much garlic can I expect from one bulb?**

One bulb typically yields 8 to 12 cloves, and each clove grows into a new bulb. So a pound of seed garlic (about 50 cloves) gives you 50 bulbs, roughly 5 to 10 pounds of garlic.

## The Bottom Line

For Florida, choose softneck garlic varieties like 'Creole Red' or 'California Early'. Plant in November or December, space cloves 6 inches apart, and mulch well. Harvest in May or June when the lower leaves brown. With the right variety and timing, you can grow a full season's worth of garlic in your Florida garden.

Garlic is a low-maintenance crop once it's in the ground. The key is to start with the right type and plant at the right time. Do that, and you'll be pulling bulbs before the summer heat sets in.
