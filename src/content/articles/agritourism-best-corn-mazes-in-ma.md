- **Design complexity**: Mazes with multiple checkpoints or a themed pattern (like a tractor or a state shape) are more engaging.
- **Size**: Larger mazes (5+ acres) take 45 to 90 minutes to complete, which justifies the ticket price.

## Top Corn Mazes in Massachusetts for 2026

### 1. Connors Farm (Danvers)

Connors Farm is the most famous maze in the state, with a 10-acre design that changes annually. In 2026, expect a tribute to the Boston Red Sox, complete with a home plate checkpoint. Tickets run $15 to $20 per person, with a $2 discount for online advance purchase. The maze takes about 60 to 90 minutes to finish. They also offer a 3-acre kids' maze, a corn cannon, and a giant pumpkin patch. Arrive early on weekends; the lot fills by 11 AM.

### 2. Hanson's Farm (Framingham)

Hanson's Farm has been running mazes since 2003, and their 8-acre maze is a local favorite. The 2026 theme is a map of Massachusetts, with checkpoints at major cities. Admission is $12 to $15, and you can add a hayride for $5. The maze is moderately challenging; most visitors finish in 45 minutes. They also have a farm store with fresh apples and cider donuts. Note that the maze is closed on Mondays, so plan accordingly.

### 3. Park Hill Orchard (Easthampton)

Park Hill Orchard offers a 6-acre maze with a scenic view of the Pioneer Valley. The 2026 design is a sunflower pattern, and the maze is cut into a field of corn and sunflowers, making it a photo favorite. Tickets are $10 to $12, with a family pass for $35. The maze takes about 30 to 45 minutes. They also have a petting zoo and a cider press demo on weekends. It's a smaller operation, so check their Facebook page for weather closures.

## When to Go: Timing and Weather

Corn mazes in Massachusetts typically open in late August and run through the end of October. The best time to visit is mid-September to early October, when the corn is fully grown but before the stalks start to dry and thin out. October weekends are the busiest, so if you want a quieter experience, go on a weekday or a Sunday afternoon. Check the forecast: heavy rain can make paths muddy, and some farms close in high winds. Call ahead or check their website for live updates.

## Ticket Prices and What to Expect

Prices vary by farm and age. Expect to pay:

- Adults: $10 to $20
- Children (4-12): $8 to $12
- Under 4: Free
- Group rates (10+): Often 10% to 20% off, but require booking in advance.

Most farms accept credit cards, but bring cash for smaller vendors. Some farms charge extra for hayrides or pumpkin picking, so budget $5 to $10 per activity. A family of four can expect to spend $40 to $60 for a full afternoon, including snacks.

## Practical Tips for Your Visit

- **Wear sturdy shoes**: Corn fields are uneven, and mud happens. Sneakers or boots are best.
- **Bring water**: You'll be walking for an hour or more, and hydration is key.
- **Use the map**: Most farms provide a map or a phone app. Don't rely on memory; the maze is designed to confuse.
- **Set a meeting point**: If you split up, agree on a central checkpoint to regroup.
- **Check for coupons**: Many farms offer discounts through local tourism boards or their email newsletters.

## FAQ

### Are corn mazes suitable for young children?

Yes, most farms have a smaller, easier maze for kids under 8. For example, Connors Farm has a 3-acre kids' maze with simple paths and no dead ends. Always keep an eye on little ones; corn can be taller than them, making it easy to get lost.

### Can I bring my dog?

Some farms allow leashed dogs, but many do not due to crop damage and hygiene. Check the farm's website before you go. Hanson's Farm is dog-friendly on weekdays, but not on weekends.

### What happens if it rains?

Mazes are open in light rain, but heavy rain can make paths impassable. Farms often close for safety. If you've bought tickets in advance, most will honor them for another day. Check the farm's social media for updates.

### How long does it take to complete a maze?

It depends on the size and your navigation skills. A 6-acre maze takes about 30 to 45 minutes, while a 10-acre maze can take up to 90 minutes. If you're in a hurry, ask for a shortcut at the entrance; most farms have a quick exit path.

## The Bottom Line

For the best corn maze experience in Massachusetts, prioritize Connors Farm for size and spectacle, Hanson's Farm for a classic New England feel, and Park Hill Orchard for a scenic, family-friendly outing. Go on a weekday in late September to avoid crowds, and budget $40 to $60 for a family of four. Check the farm's website for 2026 dates and ticket discounts, and don't forget to bring a camera for the fall colors. A corn maze is a simple pleasure, but with the right farm, it's a memory that lasts.
