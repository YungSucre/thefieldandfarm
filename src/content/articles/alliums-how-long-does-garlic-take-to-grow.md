Garlic takes about 8 to 9 months from planting to harvest, but the exact time depends on your climate, the variety, and when you plant. In most US zones, you plant in fall (October to November) and harvest in early to mid-summer (June to July). If you plant in spring, you will harvest later, but bulbs will be smaller. Here is the breakdown you need to plan your garlic crop.

![How long does garlic take to grow](/images/articles/how-long-does-garlic-take-to-grow/section-1.jpg)

## Why garlic takes so long

Garlic is a cold-hardy perennial that needs a period of cold (vernalization) to trigger bulb formation. That is why fall planting works best: the cloves establish roots before winter, go dormant, then grow rapidly

 in spring. The long growing season allows the plant to develop a full-sized bulb with multiple cloves.

## Fall planting timeline (recommended)

For most gardeners, fall planting gives the best yields. Here is the timeline:

- **Planting date:** 4 to 6 weeks before the first hard frost. In USDA zones 3-5, that is late September to mid-October. In zones 6-8, mid-October to mid-November. In zones 9-10, you may need to pre-chill cloves in the refrigerator for 6-8 weeks before planting in late November or December.
- **Root development:** 2-4 weeks after planting, roots establish. Top growth may appear if warm, but that is fine.
- **Winter dormancy:** Garlic stays dormant through winter. Mulch heavily (4-6 inches of straw) in zones 5 and colder to protect from freeze-thaw cycles.
- **Spring growth:** As soil warms (40°F and above), garlic resumes growth. This is when you fertilize and water.
- **Bulb formation:** In late spring, the plant starts forming bulbs. For hardneck garlic, scapes (flower stalks) appear in late May to early June. Cut them off to direct energy to the bulb.
- **Harvest:** When the lower leaves turn brown but 3-4 green leaves remain, usually 3-4 weeks after scapes appear. In most zones, that is late June to mid-July.

Total: 8-9 months from planting to harvest.

## Spring planting timeline (alternative)

If you missed fall planting, you can plant in early spring as soon as the soil is workable (February to April depending on zone). However, spring-planted garlic will not have enough cold exposure unless you pre-chill the cloves. Here is what to expect:

- **Pre-chilling:** Store cloves in a paper bag in the refrigerator (35-40°F) for 6-8 weeks before planting.
- **Planting:** As soon as soil can be worked, plant cloves 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart.
- **Growth:** Garlic will grow through spring and summer, but bulbs will be smaller, often 1-2 inches in diameter, because the season is shorter.
- **Harvest:** Late August to September, when leaves brown. Total time: 5-6 months, but yields are lower.

## Garlic variety and maturation times

Different garlic types have slightly different days to maturity:

| Type | Examples | Days to maturity (from planting) | Harvest window (fall-planted) |
|------|----------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Hardneck | 'Music', 'German Extra Hardy', 'Chesnok Red' | 240-270 days | Late June to mid-July |
| Softneck | 'California Early', 'Inchelium Red', 'Artichoke' | 260-290 days | Mid-July to early August |
| Elephant garlic | (actually a leek) | 270-300 days | Late July to August |

Hardneck garlic matures a bit earlier than softneck. If you want storage garlic, softneck stores longer (up to 8-10 months), while hardneck stores 4-6 months.

## How to know when garlic is ready to harvest

The key is to watch the leaves. When the lower 3-4 leaves have turned brown, but the upper leaves are still green, that is the ideal time. Dig up a bulb to check: the skin should be tight, and the cloves should be plump, not split. If you wait too long, the cloves will separate and store poorly.

Here is a simple check: stop watering 1-2 weeks before harvest to let the soil dry. Then gently dig with a garden fork, not a shovel, to avoid cutting bulbs.

## Practical steps for this week

If you are planning a fall crop, do these tasks now:

1. **Order seed garlic:** Buy from a reputable supplier like Keene Garlic or Johnny's Selected Seeds. Expect to pay $10-$15 per pound for seed garlic, which plants about 50-60 cloves (roughly 10-15 feet of row).
2. **Prepare the soil:** Garlic prefers loose, well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0. Work in 2 inches of compost or aged manure. Avoid fresh manure, which can burn roots.
3. **Choose a sunny spot:** Garlic needs at least 6 hours of direct sun daily.
4. **Separate cloves:** Break apart bulbs just before planting, leaving the papery skin on. Use the largest cloves for planting; smaller ones can be eaten.
5. **Plant:** Space cloves 6 inches apart, in rows 12 inches apart. Plant 2 inches deep, pointed side up.
6. **Mulch:** After the ground freezes, apply 4-6 inches of straw or shredded leaves. This insulates the soil and prevents heaving.

If you are in a warm zone (9-10), you can still plant in fall, but you may need to refrigerate cloves for 6-8 weeks before planting. Start that now.

## Common mistakes that delay or ruin garlic

- **Planting too late:** If cloves do not establish roots before freeze, they may rot or produce small bulbs.
- **Planting too shallow:** Cloves need to be 2 inches deep to protect from cold. Shallow planting leads to frost damage.
- **Watering too much in summer:** Garlic needs about 1 inch of water per week during growth, but stop watering when leaves start to brown. Excess moisture causes rot.
- **Not removing scapes:** For hardneck garlic, cut scapes when they curl. Leaving them reduces bulb size by up to 20%.

## FAQ

**Can I grow garlic from store-bought bulbs?**
Yes, but it is risky. Store garlic may be treated with sprout inhibitors, and it may carry diseases. For best results, buy seed garlic from a nursery or online supplier.

**What happens if I harvest too early or too late?**
If too early, bulbs are small and not fully developed. If too late, cloves split and the bulb does not store well. Watch the leaves and dig up a sample bulb to check.

**Can I grow garlic in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 8 inches deep and 12 inches wide. Plant 3-4 cloves per pot, water regularly, and protect from winter cold by moving the pot to a sheltered spot or wrapping it.

**How long does garlic take to grow from a clove versus a bulb?**
You always plant cloves, not whole bulbs. A clove takes 8-9 months to become a full bulb. If you plant a whole bulb, you will get a cluster of small bulbs, but it is not recommended.

## The bottom line

Garlic is a long-term investment: 8-9 months from fall planting to summer harvest. The key is to plant at the right time for your zone, choose the right variety, and be patient. Start with fall planting for the best yields, and you will have fresh garlic by next July. If you miss the fall window, spring planting with pre-chilled cloves works, but you will get smaller bulbs. Plan ahead, order your seed garlic now, and you will be rewarded with a harvest that beats anything from the store.
