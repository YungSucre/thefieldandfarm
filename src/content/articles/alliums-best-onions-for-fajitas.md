If you're growing onions for fajitas, you want varieties that are sweet, crisp, and mild enough to eat raw or lightly cooked. The best choices are sweet onions like 'Texas SuperSweet' or 'Walla Walla', but you also need to consider your growing zone and whether you plant sets, seeds, or transplants. Here's what works, when to plant, and how to get the most flavor from your harvest.

## Why sweet onions win for fajitas

Fajitas typically feature onions that are sautéed until soft and caramelized, but they also shine raw as a topping. Sweet onions have higher sugar content and lower sulfur compounds, which means they're less pungent and more pleasant raw. They also caramelize beautifully, adding a rich, sweet depth to the dish. ## Planting by zone and day length

Onions are categorized by day length: short-day (12-14 hours), intermediate-day (14-16 hours), and long-day (16+ hours). Your zone determines which type you can grow:

- **Short-day onions** (e.g., 'Texas SuperSweet', 'Vidalia'): Best for zones 7-10. Plant in fall (October to November) for summer harvest.
- **Intermediate-day onions** (e.g., 'Candy', 'Red Burgermaster'): Best for zones 5-7. Plant in early spring (March to April) for summer harvest.
- **Long-day onions** (e.g., 'Walla Walla', 'Yellow Globe'): Best for zones 3-6. Plant in early spring (April to May) for summer harvest.

Check your zone: if you're in zone 6, you can grow intermediate-day onions, but long-day onions will also do well. For fajitas, you want fresh onions, so harvest in summer and use within a few weeks.

## How to plant and care for onions

1. **Choose your method**: Sets (small bulbs) are easiest for beginners, but transplants give you more variety. Seeds are cheapest but require more time. For fajitas, I recommend transplants for the best flavor and size.
2. **Planting depth and spacing**: Plant sets or transplants 1 inch deep, with 4-6 inches between plants and 12-18 inches between rows. For seeds, sow 1/4 inch deep and thin to 4 inches apart.
3. **Soil and fertilizer**: Onions need well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0. Add compost before planting. Fertilize with a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at planting, then side-dress with nitrogen (e.g., blood meal) every 3 weeks until bulbs start to form.
4. **Watering**: Keep soil consistently moist, about 1 inch per week. Mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds.
5. **Harvesting**: When tops start to yellow and fall over, stop watering and let them cure for a week. Pull them up and dry in a warm, airy place for 2-3 weeks. For fajitas, you can also harvest green onions early for a milder flavor.

## Cooking with your onions

To get the best fajita flavor, slice onions thinly against the grain. For sautéing, use a cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat with a little oil. Cook for 5-7 minutes until they start to brown, then add peppers and meat. For raw toppings, soak sliced onions in ice water for 10 minutes to mellow the bite.

## FAQ

**Can I grow sweet onions in a short growing season?**
Yes, choose short-day or intermediate-day varieties that mature in 90-110 days. In cooler zones, start seeds indoors 8-10 weeks before the last frost.

**What's the difference between sets, transplants, and seeds?**
Sets are small bulbs that mature quickly but may bolt (flower) if stressed. Transplants are young plants, more reliable for size. Seeds are cheapest but take longer and require thinning.

**How do I store sweet onions?**
Sweet onions have high moisture content, so they don't store long. Use within 1-2 months. For longer storage, keep them in a cool, dry place (40-50°F) with good air circulation. You can also freeze chopped onions for cooking.

**Can I grow onions in containers?**
Yes, use a container at least 12 inches deep and 12 inches wide per onion. Fill with well-draining potting mix and water regularly. 'Candy' and 'Red Burgermaster' do well in containers.

## The bottom line

For the best fajitas, grow sweet onions like 'Texas SuperSweet' or 'Candy' that are mild enough to eat raw and caramelize well. Plant according to your zone's day length, give them consistent water and nitrogen, and harvest in summer for peak flavor. With a little planning, you'll have fresh, sweet onions that elevate any fajita night.
