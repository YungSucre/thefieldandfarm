## How to evaluate a compost deal

Not all compost is created equal. Before you buy, check these three things:

1. **Texture**: It should be crumbly, dark, and have an earthy smell. Avoid anything with large chunks of wood or a sour odor.
2. **Screening**: Fine-screened compost is better for seed starting and vegetable gardens. Coarse compost is fine for mulching.
3. **Certification**: Look for the OMRI (Organic Materials Review Institute) label if you garden organically. It ensures no synthetic additives.

**Quick test**: Squeeze a handful. It should hold its shape but crumble easily. If it stays in a tight ball, it is too wet.

## Stretch your compost: DIY and mixing options

If you cannot find a great deal, you can stretch your compost by mixing it with other amendments:

- **Mix with topsoil**: Use 1 part compost to 2 parts topsoil for raised beds. This reduces cost while still improving soil structure.
- **Make your own compost**: Start a pile with kitchen scraps, leaves, and grass clippings. It takes 3 to 6 months, but it is free. Use a compost bin like the Geobin (about $30) or build a simple wire bin.
- **Use compost as a top dressing**: Instead of mixing into the entire bed, apply a 1-inch layer on top. This uses less compost and still feeds plants as it breaks down.
- **Buy in bulk with neighbors**: Split a cubic yard with a friend. You each get half, and the cost per person drops.

**Example**: If you need 10 cubic feet of compost for a small garden, buying 5 bags at $4 each (on sale) costs $20. But if you buy a cubic yard (27 cu ft) for $40, you have enough for three seasons, and the cost per cubic foot drops from $0.40 to $0.15.

## FAQ

**Is bagged compost worth it compared to bulk?**
Bagged is convenient and easy to transport, but you pay 2 to 3 times more per cubic foot. If you have a truck or trailer, bulk is the better deal for large projects. For small containers, bags are fine.

**Can I use compost from a municipal facility safely?**
Yes, if it is properly composted. Ask if it meets EPA standards for pathogens. Most municipal compost is safe for vegetable gardens, but avoid using it for edibles if it contains biosolids unless you are comfortable with that.

**How much compost do I need for my garden?**
For a new raised bed (4x8 feet, 12 inches deep), you need about 1.5 cubic yards. For a 10x10 foot vegetable garden, a 2-inch layer requires about 0.6 cubic yards. Use an online calculator to be precise.

**What is the best time to buy compost?**
Early spring (March to May) has the most sales, but late fall (September to November) often has clearance deals as stores make room for winter. This week is a good time because of spring promotions.
