Most agritourism tours run 45 to 90 minutes for a standard farm walk, but specialty experiences like harvest days or cheese-making workshops can stretch to 4 to 6 hours. The exact length depends on your operation's size, the activities offered, and the audience. Here's how to decide what works for your farm and your visitors.

| Guided walking tour | 45-90 minutes | Medium farms with 5-10 stops |

## Factors That Affect Tour Duration

- **Group size**: Larger groups move slower. A group of 20 will take 30% longer than a group of 8.
- **Age of visitors**: School groups and seniors need more time for restrooms and slower walking.
- **Season**: In peak harvest, you may want to shorten tours to fit more groups. In shoulder season, longer tours fill the day.
- **Activities included**: Adding a hands-on component (planting, harvesting, feeding animals) adds 15-20 minutes per activity.
- **Farm size**: A 5-acre farm can be covered in 45 minutes; a 50-acre operation may need 90 minutes just to walk the main stops.

## Scheduling Tips for 2026

Start by planning your tour slots around your busiest times. For most farms, that's weekends from 10 AM to 4 PM. Offer two to three tour times per day, with at least 30 minutes between them for cleanup and reset.

- **Morning tours (9-11 AM)**: Best for school groups and serious gardeners. Keep them to 60 minutes.
- **Midday tours (11 AM-1 PM)**: Popular with families. Add a lunch option to extend to 90 minutes.
- **Afternoon tours (2-4 PM)**: Good for seniors and casual visitors. Keep to 45-60 minutes.

If you're new to agritourism, start with one 60-minute tour per day on weekends. Track how long groups actually stay, then adjust. You can always add more slots later.

## Pricing and Revenue Considerations

Tour length directly affects what you can charge. In 2026, typical prices are:

- 45-60 minute guided tour: $15-$25 per adult, $8-$12 per child
- 90-minute tour with tasting: $25-$40 per adult
- Half-day workshop: $50-$75 per person
- Full-day experience: $100-$150 per person

Longer tours justify higher prices, but they also tie up your staff. A 60-minute tour with one guide can handle up to 15 people comfortably. For larger groups, you'll need two guides, which cuts into profit.

## Designing a Tour That Fits Your Time

Map out your tour route before you set a length. Walk it yourself and time each stop. A good rule: 5-7 minutes per stop, plus 10 minutes of walking between stops. For a 60-minute tour, that's 6 stops with 5 minutes each and 10 minutes of walking.

Example 60-minute tour for a vegetable farm:

- Welcome and intro: 5 minutes at the farm stand
- Greenhouse or nursery: 10 minutes (show seedlings, talk about planting dates)
- Field row crops: 10 minutes (discuss crop rotation, soil health)
- Orchard or berry patch: 10 minutes (sampling if in season)
- Livestock area (if applicable): 10 minutes (safety rules, animal care)
- Compost or sustainability station: 5 minutes
- Wrap-up and Q&A: 10 minutes at the barn or stand

Use a timer on your phone to keep each stop on track. If a group gets engaged, you can let them linger, but have a plan to gently move them along.

## Tools and Equipment

You don't need much to run a tour, but these help:

- **Wagon or trailer** (if you offer rides): $2,000-$5,000 for a used utility trailer, plus a tractor or ATV to pull it.
- **Headset microphone** for guides (for large groups): $100-$200 for a basic system.
- **Printed maps or signs**: $50-$150 for materials, depending on size.
- **First aid kit**: $30-$50, always on hand.

If you're just starting, skip the wagon and do walking tours. You can add a wagon later when demand grows.

## FAQ

**Can I offer both short and long tours?**
Yes. Many farms offer a 45-minute express tour and a 90-minute in-depth tour. Price them differently and schedule the longer one less frequently, like once a day.

**What if a tour runs over the scheduled time?**
Build in a 15-minute buffer between tours. If you run over, you won't delay the next group. Also, train guides to watch the clock and wrap up the last stop on time.

**How do I handle bad weather?**
Have a rain plan. Move tours to a barn, greenhouse, or covered pavilion. Shorten the tour to 30-45 minutes if needed. Always check the forecast the night before and communicate any changes to booked guests.

**Do I need to charge for tours?**
Not necessarily. Some farms offer free tours and make money on u-pick sales or farm stand purchases. But a paid tour signals value and helps cover your time. Start with a modest fee and adjust based on demand.

## The Bottom Line

Agritourism tours typically last 45 to 90 minutes, but the right length depends on your farm, your audience, and what you want to show. Start with a 60-minute guided walking tour, time each stop, and adjust based on feedback. Keep your schedule flexible, build in buffers, and always have a rain plan. With clear planning, you'll create a tour that works for both you and your visitors, and that keeps them coming back year after year.
