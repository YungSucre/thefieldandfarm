Drawing a corn maze starts with a map and ends with a sharp mower. You do not need a degree in landscape architecture, but you do need a plan that matches your field size, your corn variety, and your cutting window. Here is the direct path from blank paper to a maze that actually works.

![How to draw a corn maze](/images/articles/how-to-draw-a-corn-maze/section-1.jpg)

## Plan the maze on paper first

Sketch your maze to scale on graph paper or use a free tool like Google Drawings. One square equals 10 feet is a good starting scale. Keep paths at least 3 feet wide so adults and strollers fit, and 4 feet is better for wheelchairs. Walls should be at least 4 feet thick so they read as solid from inside the maze. A simple design with one or two loops is easier to maintain than a complex spiral. 
![How to draw a corn maze](/images/articles/how-to-draw-a-corn-maze/section-2.jpg)
For your first maze, aim for a 10 to 15 minute walk-through, not an hour long epic.

## Choose the right corn

Field corn is the standard for mazes. Sweet corn gets too short and the stalks are brittle. Dent corn varieties like 'Pioneer 1197' or 'DeKalb DKC62-08' grow 8 to 10 feet tall and hold up to foot traffic. Plant in rows 30 inches apart, with seeds 6 inches apart in the row. This gives about 35,000 plants per acre, which is dense enough for solid walls. If you want a longer season, choose a 110 day variety for northern zones (USDA zone 4 to 5) and a 115 day variety for southern zones (zone 6 to 7).

## Mark the field before you cut

You cut the maze after the corn is knee high, usually 4 to 6 weeks after planting. Before you cut, mark the path with flags or spray paint. Use a string line and measuring wheel to lay out the main paths. For a simple design, mark the center line of each path with a walking tractor or a small mower. Do not cut freehand; you will drift and ruin the symmetry.

## Cut the paths with the right tool

A walk-behind string trimmer works for narrow paths, but for a 3 foot path you want a riding mower or a small tractor with a finish mower. A 48 inch deck is ideal. Set the mower deck at 4 to 6 inches above the ground so you leave stubble that hides the cut line. Cut in dry weather, early morning when the corn is crisp. Wet corn bends and the mower will miss patches. For curved paths, go slow and use the edge of the mower deck as your guide.

## Add features and exits

A maze is not just a path; it is an experience. Add a central clearing with a picnic table or a photo spot. Put in two or three dead ends with a bench or a sign. Mark emergency exits with a different colored flag or a rope line. If you have kids, add a separate mini maze with 2 foot paths and lower walls. For 2026, expect to spend $200 to $400 on flags, paint, and signs. A basic GPS unit for marking paths costs $150 to $300, but you can skip it if your design is simple.

## Maintain the maze through the season

The corn will keep growing, so you will need to recut paths every 2 to 3 weeks. Use the same mower, but raise the deck as the corn grows. Check for broken stalks after heavy rain or wind. Replace any gaps with potted corn plants or just close that path. Keep a map at the entrance so visitors know where they are. A simple kiosk with a printed map costs $50 to $100.

## Price and yield expectations

Seed corn for a 1 acre maze costs $80 to $120. Fertilizer and soil prep add $100 to $150 per acre. Your time is the biggest cost: expect 10 to 15 hours for planning and marking, plus 2 to 3 hours per cutting. A well-designed maze can attract 500 to 1,000 visitors in a season, with ticket prices at $8 to $12 per person. That is $4,000 to $12,000 in revenue, but you will need liability insurance, which runs $500 to $1,000 per year for agritourism.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to draw a maze?**

Drawing the plan takes 2 to 4 hours. Marking and cutting takes a full day for a 1 acre maze.

**Can I use sweet corn for a maze?**

Sweet corn is too short and weak. Use field corn varieties like 'Pioneer 1197' or 'DeKalb DKC62-08'.

**What if my corn does not grow tall enough?**

Plant a longer season variety and fertilize with nitrogen at planting and again at knee high. If your season is short, choose a 100 day variety and plant early.

**Do I need a permit to open a corn maze?**

Check your local zoning and agritourism laws. Many states require a temporary event permit if you charge admission. Contact your county extension office for specifics.

## The bottom line

Drawing a corn maze is a two step process: plan on paper, then cut in the field. Start small, use field corn, and mark your paths clearly before you mow. With $300 to $500 in supplies and a weekend of work, you can have a maze that draws visitors and adds income to your farm. Keep the design simple, maintain the paths, and you will have a solid agritourism feature for years.
