## What to Look for in a Compost Deal

The cheapest bag isn't always the best value. A bargain compost that's full of woody bits or poor in nutrients will set your plants back. Look for these key features:

- **Peat-free or reduced peat**: Most UK retailers now stock peat-free options, which are better for the environment and often perform just as well.
- **Nutrient content**: Check the label for added fertiliser. Multi-purpose composts with added feed can save you buying separate fertiliser.
- **Texture**: Good compost should be dark, crumbly, and free of large lumps. Avoid anything that looks like shredded bark.

## Where to Find the Best Deals

### Online Retailers

Online shopping often offers the lowest prices, especially if you buy multiple bags. In 2026, expect these typical prices:

- **Amazon UK**: 50L bags of Westland Multi-Purpose Compost often drop to £4-£5 during sales. Look for Subscribe & Save discounts if you buy regularly.
- **eBay**: You can find bulk deals, but check postage costs. Some sellers offer free delivery on orders over £30.
- **Garden centre websites**: Many now offer click-and-collect with discounts for online orders. For example, Dobbies often has 3-for-2 offers on compost in spring.

### High Street and DIY Stores

- **B&Q**: Their own-brand Verve compost is a budget favourite, often £3.50 for 50L. They run regular promotions, especially in March and April.
- **Homebase**: Look for clearance sales in late summer. You can often pick up bags for half price.
- **Wilko**: If you have one nearby, Wilko's own-brand compost is reliable and cheap, around £3 for 50L.

### Local Garden Centres

Independent garden centres may not always be cheapest, but they often have end-of-season sales. In autumn, you can find compost at 50% off as they clear stock for winter. Also, some offer loyalty schemes or bulk discounts if you buy 10 or more bags.

## When to Buy for the Best Prices

Timing is everything. Compost prices follow a seasonal pattern:

- **January to February**: Prices are low as demand is minimal. You can find deals, but stock may be limited.
- **March to May**: Peak season. Prices are highest, but you'll see multi-buy offers like 3 for 2. This is when you'll find the biggest discounts per bag if you buy in bulk.
- **June to August**: Prices start to drop. Look for clearance sales as retailers make room for autumn products.
- **September to November**: Best time for bargains. Many stores slash prices to clear stock. You can often get premium compost for less than £3 per 50L.

## Bulk Buying: The Cheapest Option

If you have a large garden or several raised beds, buying in bulk is the most cost-effective route. In 2026, a bulk bag (around 1000L) of multi-purpose compost costs between £80 and £120 delivered. That works out at £4 to £6 per 50L, which is comparable to mid-range bagged compost, but you save on packaging and delivery.

Where to buy bulk:

- **Local landscaping suppliers**: Often the cheapest, but you'll need a trailer or pay for delivery. Expect £70-£100 for a bulk bag.
- **Online bulk suppliers**: Companies like Compost Direct or Soil Association certified suppliers offer delivered bulk bags. Prices vary, but you can often get free delivery on orders over £100.
- **Council compost**: Some local councils sell compost made from green waste at very low prices, sometimes as little as £2 per bag. Quality varies, so check it's well-screened.

## How to Make Your Compost Go Further

Even with the best deals, you can stretch your compost further:

- **Mix with garden soil**: For beds and borders, mix compost with existing soil at a 1:1 ratio. This saves compost and improves soil structure.
- **Use compost as a mulch**: Instead of filling entire containers, use a layer of compost on top of the soil. This feeds plants as it breaks down.
- **Make your own**: Start a compost heap. It takes time, but you'll have free compost in 6-12 months. Combine kitchen scraps, grass clippings, and shredded paper.

## FAQ

### Is cheap compost as good as expensive compost?

Not always. Cheap compost often has less nutrient content and may contain more woody material. However, for general use in beds or as a soil conditioner, a budget option can work fine. For seed sowing or containers, spend a bit more on a reputable brand.

### When is the best time to buy compost in the UK?

Late summer to autumn (August to November) is when you'll find the biggest discounts. Spring (March to May) offers multi-buy deals, but prices are higher overall.

### Can I use compost from my local council?

Yes, many councils sell compost made from green waste. It's usually cheap, but quality varies. Check that it's well-screened and free of contaminants. It's best used as a soil improver rather than for seed sowing.

### How much compost do I need for a raised bed?

A 4x8 foot raised bed, 1 foot deep, needs about 32 cubic feet of compost. That's roughly 16 bags of 50L. Buying in bulk is more economical for this scale.
