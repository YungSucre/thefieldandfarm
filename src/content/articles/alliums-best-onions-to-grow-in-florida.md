If you're gardening in Florida, the best onions to grow are short-day and intermediate-day varieties that bulb with 10 to 12 hours of daylight. These include 'Texas SuperSweet', 'Vidalia' (Granex types), and 'Red Creole'. Plant them from October through January, depending on your zone, and harvest in late spring. Here's the full breakdown.

## Understanding Onion Day-Length Requirements

Onions form bulbs based on day length, not just temperature. Florida's latitude (roughly 25°N to 31°N) means winter days are short. Long-day onions, which need 14 to 16 hours of daylight, won't bulb well here. Stick with short-day (10 to 12 hours) or intermediate-day (12 to 14 hours) types.

## Best Varieties for Florida

### Short-Day Onions (Recommended for South and Central Florida)

| Variety | Type | Days to Maturity | Notes |
|---------|------|------------------|-------|
| 'Texas SuperSweet' | Yellow, sweet | 110-120 | Very sweet, stores 1-2 months |
| 'Vidalia' (Granex) | Yellow, sweet | 100-110 | Classic sweet onion, short storage |
| 'Red Creole' | Red, pungent | 95-110 | Stores well, good for fresh use |
| 'White Bermuda' | White, mild | 100-110 | Mild flavor, crisp |

### Intermediate-Day Onions (Better for North Florida)

| Variety | Type | Days to Maturity | Notes |
|---------|------|------------------|-------|
| 'Candy' | Yellow, sweet | 100-110 | Reliable, stores moderately |
| 'Red Torpedo' | Red, elongated | 100-110 | Italian heirloom, sweet |
| 'Super Star' | White, sweet | 100-110 | Large, mild |

For the Panhandle (Zone 8b and north), intermediate-day onions often do better than short-day because they can handle slightly longer days. In South Florida (Zone 10), stick strictly with short-day.

## When to Plant in Florida

Plant onion sets or transplants, not seeds, for easiest success. Seeds take longer and are more finicky. Here are the planting windows by zone:

| USDA Zone | Best Planting Time | Harvest Time |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------|
| 8a-8b (North FL) | October 15 - November 15 | May - June |
| 9a-9b (Central FL) | November 1 - December 15 | April - May |
| 10a-10b (South FL) | December 1 - January 15 | April - May |

If you plant too early, heat and rain can rot bulbs. Too late, and bulbs may not form before summer heat arrives. In South Florida, you can also plant in January, but expect smaller bulbs.

## Soil Preparation and Planting Steps

Onions need well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 6.8. Florida soil is often sandy and acidic, so amend with compost and lime if needed. Here's how to plant:

1. **Test your soil**: Use a home test kit ($10-$20) or send to a lab ($20-$30). Adjust pH with dolomitic lime (about 5-10 lbs per 100 sq ft, but follow test results).
2. **Prepare beds**: Work in 2-3 inches of compost or aged manure. Onions are heavy feeders.
3. **Plant sets or transplants**: Space them 4-6 inches apart in rows 12-18 inches apart. Set bulbs just below the soil surface, about 1 inch deep.
4. **Water thoroughly**: Keep soil consistently moist but not soggy. Onions have shallow roots.
5. **Mulch**: Apply 1-2 inches of straw or pine straw to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

## Fertilizing and Care

Onions need steady nutrition. Apply a balanced fertilizer (like 10-10-10) at planting, then side-dress every 3-4 weeks with nitrogen (like blood meal or 21-0-0). Stop fertilizing once bulbs start to swell (when the tops are about 12 inches tall). Too much nitrogen later makes bulbs soft and reduces storage.

Water deeply once or twice a week, about 1 inch per week. In Florida's sandy soil, you may need more frequent watering during dry spells. Avoid overhead watering in the evening to reduce fungal diseases.

## Common Florida Onion Problems

- **Bolting (flowering early)**: Caused by cold snaps or stress. Plant at the right time and avoid temperature swings by mulching.
- **Purple blotch**: A fungal disease that appears as purple spots on leaves. Use resistant varieties, rotate crops, and apply fungicide if severe (copper-based, $15-$25).
- **Thrips**: Tiny insects that cause silvery streaks. Use insecticidal soap or neem oil ($10-$20).
- **Root rot**: In heavy clay or overwatered soil. Improve drainage with raised beds.

## Harvesting and Storing

Harvest when tops fall over and begin to yellow. Gently lift bulbs with a fork, then cure in a warm, dry, shaded area for 1-2 weeks. Cut tops to 1 inch and roots, then store in a cool, dry place (50-60°F). Sweet onions like 'Vidalia' store only 1-2 months; pungent types like 'Red Creole' can last 3-4 months.

## FAQ

**Can I grow onions from seeds in Florida?**
Yes, but it's slower and more challenging. Start seeds indoors 8-10 weeks before planting time, or direct sow in fall. Transplants are easier for beginners.

**What's the best onion for sweet flavor in Florida?**
'Texas SuperSweet' and 'Vidalia' are top choices. They're both short-day and produce mild, sweet bulbs, though they don't store long.

**Do I need to hill soil around onions?**
No, onions bulb at the surface. Hilling can encourage rot. Just keep the soil loose and free of weeds.

**Can I grow onions in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 12 inches deep and wide, with drainage holes. Plant 3-4 sets per 12-inch pot, and use a quality potting mix.

## The Bottom Line

For Florida, choose short-day varieties like 'Texas SuperSweet' or 'Red Creole' in the south, and intermediate-day types like 'Candy' in the north. Plant from October to January, space 4-6 inches apart, and keep soil moist but well-drained. With the right timing and care, you'll have a solid harvest of homegrown onions by late spring. Start with transplants for the easiest path, and don't forget to cure them properly for storage.
