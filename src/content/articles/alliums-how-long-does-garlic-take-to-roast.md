Roasting garlic takes 35 to 60 minutes at 400°F (204°C), depending on the size of the bulbs and your desired caramelization. A whole head of garlic, cut in half and drizzled with oil, typically needs 40 to 50 minutes. Individual cloves roast faster, in 25 to 35 minutes. The key is to roast until the cloves are soft, golden, and easily squeezed from their skins.

## The Basic Method: Whole Heads

This is the classic approach for a rich, concentrated result.

1. **Preheat your oven** to 400°F (204°C).
2. **Prep the garlic:** Peel away the loose, papery outer layers, but leave the head intact. Slice off the top 1/4 to 1/2 inch to expose the cloves.
3. **Drizzle and wrap:** Place the garlic on a piece of aluminum foil, drizzle with 1 to 2 teaspoons of olive oil, and season with salt and pepper if desired. Wrap the foil tightly to create a packet.
4. **Roast:** Place the packet on a baking sheet and roast for 40 to 50 minutes. Check at 35 minutes for smaller heads.
5. **Test for doneness:** The cloves should be soft and easily pierced with a knife. The skins will be lightly browned.
6. **Cool and squeeze:** Let it cool for 5 minutes, then squeeze the roasted cloves from their skins. They should pop out easily.

## Roasting Individual Cloves

If you only need a few cloves or want them to roast faster, this method works well.

1. **Peel** the cloves and toss them with a little olive oil and salt.
2. **Spread** them in a single layer on a baking sheet or in a small ovenproof dish.
3. **Roast** at 400°F (204°C) for 25 to 35 minutes, stirring once halfway through. They are done when they are golden and tender.

## Temperature and Time Variations

You can adjust the temperature to suit your schedule, but the time will change accordingly.

| Temperature | Whole Head Time | Individual Cloves Time |
|-------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| 350°F (177°C) | 55-65 minutes | 35-45 minutes |
| 400°F (204°C) | 40-50 minutes | 25-35 minutes |
| 425°F (218°C) | 30-40 minutes | 20-30 minutes |

Lower temperatures yield a sweeter, more mellow garlic, while higher temperatures produce a more caramelized, slightly nuttier flavor. Watch closely at higher temperatures to prevent burning.

## How to Tell When It's Done

- **Visual cue:** The garlic should look golden and slightly shriveled. The exposed cloves will be lightly browned.
- **Texture test:** Insert a sharp knife or skewer into a clove. It should slide in with no resistance.
- **Squeeze test:** Once cooled slightly, squeeze a clove. It should pop out of its skin easily and be soft enough to mash with a fork.

## Tools and Prices

You likely already have what you need: a baking sheet, aluminum foil, and a knife. If you want a dedicated garlic roaster, a ceramic garlic baker costs $15 to $30. A simple garlic peeler (a silicone tube) runs $10 to $15 and speeds up peeling for individual cloves, but it's not essential.

## Storing Roasted Garlic

Roasted garlic keeps well, so you can make a batch ahead of time.

- **Refrigerator:** Store in an airtight container with a little olive oil covering the cloves for up to 2 weeks.
- **Freezer:** Freeze roasted cloves on a parchment-lined tray, then transfer to a freezer bag. They'll keep for 3 months. You can also puree with oil and freeze in ice cube trays for easy portions.

## Using Roasted Garlic

- Spread on crusty bread or crackers.
- Mash into mashed potatoes or cauliflower.
- Whisk into salad dressings or vinaigrettes.
- Stir into pasta, risotto, or soups.
- Mix with softened butter for a flavored compound butter.

## FAQ

### Can I roast garlic without foil?

Yes. Place the cut heads in a small ovenproof dish (like a ramekin or small baking dish), drizzle with oil, and cover with a lid or another piece of foil. If you don't cover it, the garlic may dry out or burn; you'll need to check and add a little water or oil if it looks dry.

### Why is my roasted garlic bitter?

Bitter garlic is usually overcooked or roasted at too high a temperature. The natural sugars burn, creating a bitter taste. Stick to 400°F or lower, and check early. Also, avoid using old, sprouting garlic, which can be bitter.

### How long does roasted garlic last in the fridge?

Properly stored in an airtight container with oil, roasted garlic lasts up to 2 weeks. Always use a clean spoon to scoop it out to prevent contamination.

### Can I roast garlic in an air fryer?

Yes. Cut the top off the head, drizzle with oil, wrap in foil, and air fry at 380°F (193°C) for 25 to 35 minutes. Check at 20 minutes for smaller heads.

## The Bottom Line

Roasting garlic is a forgiving process. Start checking at 35 minutes for whole heads and 25 minutes for cloves. The result is a sweet, spreadable ingredient that adds depth to countless dishes. With these times and temperatures, you'll get perfect roasted garlic every time, whether you're using a conventional oven or an air fryer.
