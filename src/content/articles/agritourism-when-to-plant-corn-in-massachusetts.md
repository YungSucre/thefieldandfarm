In Massachusetts, plant corn after the last frost and when soil temperatures reach 50°F or warmer, typically from late April to early June depending on your zone. For most of the state, that means aiming for May 1 to May 30, with coastal areas starting a bit earlier and inland or northern regions later. This guide gives you the exact dates, varieties, and steps to get your corn in the ground at the right time.

## Understanding Massachusetts Growing Zones

Massachusetts spans USDA zones 5a to 7a. The eastern coast and Boston area are warmer (zone 6b to 7a), while the western and central parts are cooler (zone 5a to 5b). This affects your planting window:

- **Zone 5a/5b (western and central MA):** Last frost typically May 15 to May 30. Plant corn from May 20 to June 10.
- **Zone 6a/6b (eastern MA, including Boston):** Last frost typically April 30 to May 15. Plant from May 1 to May 20.
- **Zone 7a (Cape Cod and islands):** Last frost typically April 15 to May 1. Plant from April 25 to May 10.

Always check your specific frost date with your local extension service, as microclimates vary. A soil thermometer is your best friend: corn seeds will rot in cold, wet soil, so wait until the soil is at least 50°F, but ideally 60°F to 65°F for fast germination.

## Soil Temperature and Corn Germination

Corn is a warm-season crop. If soil is below 50°F, seeds may sit and rot. At 50°F, germination takes 20 days or more; at 60°F, it takes about 10 days; at 70°F, about 7 days. Use a soil thermometer (available for $10 to $15 at garden centers) to check at 2 inches deep in the morning.

To warm the soil faster:
- Choose a south-facing, well-drained spot.
- Remove any mulch or debris in early spring.
- Consider using black plastic mulch for early planting, but remove it once seedlings emerge to avoid overheating.

## Choosing the Right Corn Varieties

For Massachusetts, choose varieties with short to moderate maturity (60 to 80 days) to ensure they ripen before fall frosts. Here are proven options:

- **Early (60-70 days):** 'Early Sunglow' (yellow, sweet), 'Spring Treat' (yellow, very early), 'Sugar Buns' (yellow, sugary enhanced).
- **Main season (70-80 days):** 'Golden Bantam' (heirloom, yellow, excellent flavor), 'Silver Queen' (white, classic, 92 days, but worth the wait), 'Incredible' (yellow, disease resistant).
- **Late (80-90 days):** 'Honey Select' (yellow, supersweet), 'Peaches and Cream' (bi-color, 75 days, but can be planted late).

For small gardens, consider 'Tom Thumb' (a popcorn that matures in 60 days) or 'Blue Jade' (a blue corn that does well in cool climates).

## Step-by-Step Planting Instructions

1. **Prepare the soil:** After the last frost, till or spade the soil to a depth of 6 to 8 inches. Remove rocks and clods. Rake smooth.
2. **Mark rows:** Space rows 30 to 36 inches apart. For small plots, you can plant in blocks of 4 to 6 short rows instead of long single rows; this improves pollination.
3. **Plant seeds:** Sow seeds 1 to 2 inches deep, spacing them 9 to 12 inches apart in the row. If you plant in hills (for short rows), put 3 to 4 seeds per hill, spacing hills 12 to 15 inches apart.
4. **Water:** After planting, water gently but thoroughly. Keep the soil consistently moist until germination.
5. **Thin:** Once seedlings are 4 inches tall, thin to one plant every 12 inches (or one per hill).
6. **Fertilize:** Side-dress with a balanced fertilizer (like 10-10-10) when plants are 12 inches tall and again when they tassel. Use about 1 pound per 100 square feet each time.
7. **Mulch:** After soil warms, mulch with straw or grass clippings to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

- **Planting too early:** Cold soil leads to poor germination. Wait for 50°F.
- **Planting too late:** If you plant after June 15 in most of MA, you risk frost damage in the fall. Check your first frost date (usually late September to mid-October) and count back the days to maturity.
- **Not enough space:** Corn is wind-pollinated, so plant in blocks, not single rows. A block of at least 4 rows is best.
- **Ignoring soil pH:** Corn prefers slightly acidic soil. If pH is below 6.0, lime in the fall or early spring.

## FAQ

**Can I start corn indoors?**
No, corn does not transplant well. Direct sow seeds outdoors. If you want an early start, use a cold frame or row cover after planting.

**How do I know when to harvest?**
Corn is ready when the silks turn brown and dry, and the kernels are plump and milky when pressed. Usually 18 to 20 days after silks appear.

**What if I miss the planting window?**
You can still plant a short-season variety (60 days) as late as June 20 in zones 5 and 6, and July 1 in zone 7, but yields may be lower. Consider 'Early Sunglow' or 'Tom Thumb'.

**Do I need to plant more than one variety for pollination?**
No, corn is wind-pollinated. Just plant in blocks, not rows. If you plant different varieties, separate them by at least 100 feet or plant them with different maturity dates to avoid cross-pollination.

## The Bottom Line

The key to successful corn in Massachusetts is timing: wait for soil to reach 50°F, plant after the last frost, and choose varieties that match your zone's growing season. For most of the state, that means planting between May 1 and June 10, with a second planting in late May for a fall crop. Use a soil thermometer, plant in blocks, and keep the soil moist. With these steps, you'll be enjoying fresh corn from late July through October. For specific frost dates, check with your local extension service or use an online tool like the Old Farmer's Almanac. Happy planting!
