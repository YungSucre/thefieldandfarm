Garlic is more toxic to cats than many people realize. Even a single clove can cause serious harm, and the toxic dose is roughly 1 gram per 5 pounds of body weight, but smaller amounts can still cause illness. If your cat eats any garlic, call your vet immediately; do not wait for symptoms.

## Why garlic is dangerous for cats

Garlic contains thiosulfates, which cats cannot digest properly. These compounds damage red blood cells, leading to hemolytic anemia. Unlike dogs, cats are especially sensitive because their liver lacks the enzymes to break down these toxins. The effect is cumulative, so repeated small exposures can be as dangerous as one large dose.

## Toxic dose: how much is too much?

For a typical 10-pound cat, the toxic dose is about 2 grams of fresh garlic. A single clove weighs 3 to 7 grams, so one clove can be lethal. Garlic powder is more concentrated: 1 teaspoon of powder equals about 8 grams of fresh garlic, which is toxic for a 20-pound cat. Even 1/8 teaspoon of garlic powder can make a small cat sick.

Here is a quick reference table for fresh garlic and garlic powder (based on average clove weight of 5 grams):

| Cat weight | Toxic fresh garlic | Toxic garlic powder |
|------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| 5 lbs      | 1 gram (about 1/5 clove) | 1/8 teaspoon |
| 10 lbs     | 2 grams (about 1/2 clove) | 1/4 teaspoon |
| 15 lbs     | 3 grams (about 3/4 clove) | 1/3 teaspoon |

These are conservative estimates based on veterinary toxicology data. Some cats show symptoms at lower doses, especially kittens or cats with existing health issues.

## Symptoms of garlic poisoning

Symptoms usually appear within 24 hours but can be delayed up to 4 days. Watch for:

- Vomiting and diarrhea
- Lethargy and weakness
- Pale gums
- Increased heart rate
- Collapse or difficulty breathing

If you see any of these signs, get to a vet right away. Garlic poisoning can be fatal if untreated.

## What to do if your cat eats garlic

1. **Call your vet or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435) immediately.** Have the garlic type and amount ready.
2. **Do not induce vomiting unless told to do so.** Some toxins cause more damage coming up.
3. **If the ingestion was within 2 hours, the vet may induce vomiting or give activated charcoal.**
4. **Follow your vet's instructions for blood tests and supportive care.**

Treatment often includes IV fluids, oxygen therapy, and in severe cases, blood transfusions. The prognosis is good if caught early.

## Keeping your garden and kitchen safe

If you grow garlic, take these precautions to protect your cat:

- **Store garlic in a sealed container** in a cool, dark place. Cats can chew through paper bags.
- **Compost garlic scraps carefully.** Use a covered compost bin or bury scraps deep.
- **Fence off your garlic bed.** A simple chicken wire fence (about $20 for 25 feet) keeps cats out.
- **Plant garlic in raised beds or containers** that are less accessible to curious cats.

Remember, garlic is not a necessary part of a cat's diet. There are no health benefits, only risks. If you want to give your cat a treat, stick to commercial cat treats or small amounts of cooked plain meat.

## FAQ

**Can a small amount of garlic, like in baby food, be safe?** No. Even 1/8 teaspoon of garlic powder can be toxic to a small cat. There is no safe amount.

**What if my cat only licked a garlic clove?** Licking may not cause toxicity, but any ingestion of a piece larger than a pea should be treated as an emergency. Call your vet.

**Are onions and chives equally toxic?** Yes, all alliums (onions, garlic, chives, leeks) contain thiosulfates. Onions are slightly less concentrated, but still dangerous.

**How long after eating garlic will symptoms show?** Symptoms usually appear within 24 hours, but can take up to 4 days. Monitor your cat closely for at least 72 hours.

## The bottom line

Garlic is highly toxic to cats, and there is no safe amount. If your cat eats any garlic, call your vet immediately. Prevention is key: store garlic securely, keep your garden cat-free, and never feed garlic to your cat. As a gardener, you can still enjoy growing garlic, but take simple steps to protect your feline friends.
