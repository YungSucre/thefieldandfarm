Shallots take about 90 to 120 days from planting to harvest, depending on your climate and variety. In most US zones, you plant in fall or early spring and harvest in midsummer. This guide gives you the exact timeline, step by step, so you can plan your garden with confidence.

## Planting windows by zone

Shallots are day-length sensitive: they bulb when days reach a certain length. In practice, this means timing is tied to your growing season.

- **Zones 3-5**: Plant in early spring, as soon as soil can be worked (March to April). Harvest in late July to August.
- **Zones 6-7**: Plant in fall (October to November) for an early summer harvest (June to July), or in early spring for a later harvest.
- **Zones 8-10**: Plant in fall (October to December) for a spring harvest (April to May).

If you plant in fall, shallots grow roots before winter, go dormant, then resume growth in spring. This gives them a head start and often yields larger bulbs.

## From planting to harvest: the timeline

Here is a week-by-week breakdown for a typical spring planting in zone 6:

- **Week 0 (planting)**: Plant sets (small bulbs) 1 inch deep, 6 inches apart, in rows 12 inches apart. Water in.
- **Weeks 1-4**: Roots establish, green shoots emerge. Keep soil moist but not soggy.
- **Weeks 5-8**: Leaves grow rapidly. Side-dress with nitrogen fertilizer (e.g., blood meal) at week 6.
- **Weeks 9-12**: Bulbs start to swell. Stop fertilizing once bulbs begin to form.
- **Weeks 13-16**: Bulbs mature. Leaves yellow and fall over. Harvest when about half the leaves have flopped.

For fall planting, add about 4-6 weeks of pre-winter growth, then the timeline resumes in spring.

## How to know when shallots are ready

Shallots are ready to harvest when the leaves turn yellow and fall over, but before they dry completely. This usually happens 90-120 days after planting. Gently dig up one bulb to check size: mature shallots are 1-2 inches in diameter, with papery skins.

Do not wait too long: if the skins split, they store poorly. Harvest on a dry day, then cure them.

## Curing and storage

Curing is essential for long storage. After harvest, leave the shallots in a warm, dry, airy spot (out of direct sun) for 2-3 weeks. Then trim the tops and roots, and store in a cool, dry place (50-60°F) with good air circulation. Properly cured shallots can last 6-8 months.

## Best varieties for different zones

Choose varieties that match your day length and climate:

| Variety | Type | Best for | Days to harvest |
|---------|------|----------|-----------------|
| 'French Red' | Short-day | Zones 8-10 | 90-100 |
| 'Dutch Yellow' | Intermediate | Zones 6-7 | 100-110 |
| 'Gray Shallot' | Long-day | Zones 3-5 | 110-120 |
| 'Prisma' | Intermediate | Zones 6-8 | 95-105 |

'French Red' is a classic with pink skin and mild flavor. 'Dutch Yellow' is productive and stores well. 'Gray Shallot' is more pungent and rare, but worth seeking out.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Planting too deep**: Shallots need shallow planting. If buried too deep, they rot. Keep the tip just below the surface.
- **Overwatering**: Shallots dislike wet feet. Water only during dry spells, and stop once bulbs swell.
- **Skipping fertilizer**: Shallots are moderate feeders. Work in compost at planting, and side-dress with a balanced fertilizer at mid-growth.
- **Harvesting too early or late**: Early harvest gives small bulbs; late harvest reduces storage life. Watch the leaves.

## FAQ

**Can I grow shallots from seed?**
Yes, but it takes longer: 120-150 days from seed to harvest. Sets are easier and faster, but seeds are cheaper and less likely to carry disease.

**How many shallots do I get from one set?**
One set typically produces a cluster of 5-10 shallots, depending on variety and growing conditions.

**Can I plant shallots in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 12 inches deep with drainage holes. Space sets 6 inches apart, and water more frequently than in-ground plants.

**What if my shallots bolt (send up flower stalks)?**
Bolting is usually caused by cold snaps or stress. Cut off the flower stalk immediately to redirect energy to the bulb. The bulb will be smaller but still edible.

## The bottom line

Shallots are a low-effort crop that rewards patience. From planting to harvest, expect 90-120 days. Plant at the right time for your zone, keep the soil moist but not wet, and harvest when the leaves flop. Cure them properly, and you will have homegrown shallots for months. Start with a reliable variety like 'Dutch Yellow' or 'French Red', and you will be glad you did.
