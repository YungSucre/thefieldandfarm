Plant pumpkins so they mature before your first fall frost, typically 90 to 120 days before that date. Count back from your expected first frost, add a week for germination, and you have your planting window. For most zones, that means sowing seeds from late May in the North to mid-July in the South. This guide gives you exact dates, varieties, and steps to get it right.

## Know your frost dates and planting window

Pumpkins need warm soil (at least 60°F) and a long growing season. The key is to have the fruit mature before frost kills the vines. Use your average first fall frost date, then count back the days to maturity for your chosen variety, plus 7 to 10 days for germination and early growth. That gives you the latest safe planting date. For earliest harvest, plant as soon as soil warms in spring, but avoid frost risk.

Here are typical planting windows by USDA zone (based on average frost dates):

| USDA Zone | Last Spring Frost | First Fall Frost | Planting Window |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| 3 | May 15 | Sep 15 | May 20 to Jun 1 |
| 4 | May 15 | Sep 25 | May 20 to Jun 10 |
| 5 | May 15 | Oct 5 | May 25 to Jun 20 |
| 6 | Apr 15 | Oct 15 | May 25 to Jul 1 |
| 7 | Apr 1 | Nov 1 | Jun 1 to Jul 10 |
| 8 | Mar 15 | Nov 15 | Jun 10 to Jul 20 |
| 9 | Feb 15 | Dec 1 | Jun 20 to Jul 30 |

These are averages. Check your local extension office for exact dates. If you miss the window, choose a faster-maturing variety or start seeds indoors 3 weeks before planting out.

## Choose the right variety for your season

Varieties range from 75 to 120 days to maturity. Match the days to your growing season. For short seasons, pick early varieties. For carving or storage, pick longer-season types if you have time.

- **Early (75-85 days):** 'Small Sugar' (100 days, but often earlier in warm zones), 'Baby Bear' (105 days, small), 'Jack Be Little' (95 days, miniature). For true early, try 'Early Frost' (85 days) or 'Autumn Gold' (90 days).
- **Mid (90-110 days):** 'Howden' (110 days, classic carving), 'Connecticut Field' (110 days), 'Sugar Pie' (100 days, baking).
- **Late (110-120 days):** 'Atlantic Giant' (120 days, competition), 'Big Max' (120 days), 'Lumina' (100 days, white).

In zone 5 or colder, stick to varieties under 100 days. In zone 7 and warmer, you can grow any type.

## Soil prep and planting steps

Pumpkins need full sun (6+ hours), fertile, well-drained soil with pH 6.0 to 6.8. Prepare beds in fall or early spring by mixing in 2 to 3 inches of compost. A soil test helps; add lime if pH is low.

**Direct sowing:** After last frost, plant seeds 1 inch deep, 2 to 3 seeds per hill. Space hills 6 to 8 feet apart for vining types, 4 feet for bush types. Thin to the strongest seedling per hill. For rows, space seeds 6 to 12 inches apart, then thin to 18 to 36 inches, depending on variety.

**Starting indoors:** For short seasons, start seeds in peat pots 3 to 4 weeks before last frost. Harden off for a week, then transplant carefully to avoid root disturbance.

**Watering and fertilizing:** Keep soil consistently moist, about 1 inch per week. Use drip irrigation to avoid leaf disease. Side-dress with a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) when vines start to run, then again when fruits set. Avoid high nitrogen, which causes lush vines but few fruits.

## Care through the season

- **Weed control:** Mulch with straw or black plastic to suppress weeds and retain moisture. Hand-pull weeds early.
- **Pollination:** Bees are essential. If you see few bees, hand-pollinate by transferring pollen from male to female flowers with a small brush.
- **Pest and disease:** Watch for squash bugs, vine borers, and powdery mildew. Rotate crops yearly, and remove infected leaves promptly.
- **Thinning fruit:** For larger pumpkins, remove all but 2 to 3 fruits per vine. For smaller varieties, you can leave more.

## Harvest and storage

Pumpkins are ready when the rind is hard (fingernail can't pierce it) and the stem has started to dry. Cut with a sharp knife, leaving 3 to 4 inches of stem. Cure in the sun for 10 to 14 days, then store in a cool, dry place (50 to 60°F) for up to 3 months.

**Yield expectations:** A healthy vine produces 1 to 2 pumpkins per plant for large varieties, 3 to 5 for small ones. A 10x10 foot patch can yield 10 to 20 pumpkins, depending on variety and care.

## FAQ

**Can I plant pumpkins in July for Halloween?**
In zones 7 and warmer, yes, if you choose a 90-day variety and plant by July 1. In cooler zones, it's too late; the fruit won't mature before frost.

**How late can I plant pumpkins?**
The absolute latest is 90 days before your first frost, but that's risky. Aim for 100 to 110 days for a buffer. In zone 5, that means no later than June 20.

**Do pumpkins need full sun?**
Yes, at least 6 hours, ideally 8. Less sun reduces yield and increases disease risk.

**Should I start pumpkins indoors?**
Only if your season is short (zone 4 or colder). Pumpkins transplant poorly due to root disturbance, so use peat pots and handle gently.

## The bottom line

Plant pumpkins 90 to 120 days before your first fall frost, adjusting for variety and zone. Prepare soil with compost, sow seeds after frost, and keep vines watered and weeded. Choose early varieties for short seasons, and harvest when rinds are hard. With these dates and steps, you'll have pumpkins ready for fall displays and pies.

For specific frost dates, check your local extension service or use an online calculator. And if you miss the window, don't despair: you can still buy pumpkins from a local farm, but growing your own is deeply satisfying.
