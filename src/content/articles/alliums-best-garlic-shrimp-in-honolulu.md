## How to grow garlic for your own garlic shrimp

Growing your own garlic is easy and gives you the freshest flavor for your shrimp. Garlic needs a cold period to form bulbs, so in Hawaii's warm climate, you have to choose the right type. Softneck garlic, like 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red', works best in zones 9-11. Plant in October or November, and harvest in May or June.

Here is a step-by-step guide:

1. **Buy seed garlic**: Get bulbs from a nursery or online. Avoid grocery store garlic, which may be treated to prevent sprouting. Expect to pay $10-$15 per pound for seed garlic.
2. **Prepare the soil**: Garlic likes well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0. Mix in compost and a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10.
3. **Plant cloves**: Break the bulb into individual cloves, leaving the papery skin on. Plant each clove pointy side up, 2 inches deep and 6 inches apart, in rows 12 inches apart.
4. **Water and mulch**: Water well after planting, then mulch with straw to keep weeds down and retain moisture.
5. **Fertilize**: Side-dress with nitrogen in early spring, around February, to boost growth.
6. **Harvest**: When the lower leaves turn brown, usually 8-9 months after planting, dig up the bulbs. Cure them in a dry, shaded spot for 2-3 weeks.

In Hawaii, you can also try growing garlic in pots if your soil is too wet. Use a 5-gallon bucket with drainage holes, and plant 3-4 cloves per bucket.

## The perfect garlic shrimp recipe

Once you have your garlic, here is a simple recipe that mimics the trucks' style. It serves 2-3 people.

**Ingredients**:
- 1 pound large shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 6-8 cloves garlic, minced (about 2 tablespoons)
- 4 tablespoons butter
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- Cooked rice for serving

**Steps**:
1. Pat the shrimp dry and season with salt, pepper, and paprika.
2. Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add olive oil and 2 tablespoons of butter.
3. When the butter is foaming, add the shrimp in a single layer. Cook for 1-2 minutes per side, until pink and just opaque. Remove from the skillet.
4. Reduce heat to medium. Add the remaining butter and the garlic. Cook for 1 minute, stirring, until fragrant but not browned.
5. Return the shrimp to the skillet, toss to coat in the garlic butter, and cook for another minute.
6. Serve immediately over rice with lemon wedges on the side.

This recipe is fast, about 15 minutes total, and tastes just like the trucks, minus the beach view.

## Tips for getting the best garlic flavor

To get the most out of your garlic, follow these tips:

- **Use fresh garlic**: Homegrown or farmers market garlic is more pungent than store-bought.
- **Mince finely**: The finer you mince, the more flavor you release.
- **Don't burn it**: Garlic turns bitter if overcooked. Keep it on low heat and stir constantly.
- **Let it sit**: After mincing, let the garlic sit for 10 minutes before cooking. This allows enzymes to develop more flavor.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I grow garlic in Honolulu?**
Yes, but choose softneck varieties and plant in the cooler months. The warm climate can cause garlic to bolt, so harvest earlier if you see flower stalks.

**What is the best time to visit Giovanni's to avoid lines?**
Go on a weekday morning, right when they open at 10:30 AM. Weekends and lunchtime are the busiest.

**Are there vegetarian options at these shrimp trucks?**
Most trucks focus on shrimp, but some have chicken or tofu plates. Check the menu or ask. Fumi's has a spicy garlic shrimp that is a favorite.

**How do I store homegrown garlic?**
Cure it for 2-3 weeks, then store in a cool, dry place. Braid softneck varieties for easy hanging. Properly stored garlic can last 6-8 months.
