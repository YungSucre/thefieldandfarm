Garlic scapes are the curly flower stalks that hardneck garlic sends up in early summer. If you let them grow, the plant puts energy into the flower instead of the bulb, so cutting scapes gives you a bonus crop and bigger bulbs. This guide covers the differences, harvest timing, and uses for both.

## What are garlic scapes?

Garlic scapes are the tender, curly stems that emerge from hardneck garlic varieties in late spring or early summer. They start as a straight shoot, then curl into a loop as they grow. If left alone, they will straighten and produce a flower head with tiny bulbils. Cutting them off redirects energy to the bulb, increasing bulb size by 10 to 20 percent.

## Key differences: garlic bulbs vs scapes

| Feature | Garlic Bulb | Garlic Scape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Plant part** | Underground bulb | Above-ground flower stalk |
| **Harvest time** | Mid to late summer (when lower leaves brown) | Late spring to early summer (when scapes curl) |
| **Flavor** | Pungent, savory | Milder, slightly sweet, garlicky |
| **Storage** | Months (cured) | 1 to 2 weeks in fridge |
| **Uses** | Cooking, roasting, planting | Pesto, stir-fries, pickling |
| **Yield** | 5 to 10 bulbs per pound of seed garlic | 1 scape per plant, about 4 to 6 ounces per 10 plants |

## When to harvest scapes

Harvest scapes when they have made one full curl or just started to straighten. This is usually 3 to 4 weeks before bulb harvest. In USDA zones 3 to 5, that is typically late May to mid June. In zones 6 to 8, it is mid May to early June. Cut the scape with clean scissors or a knife, about 1 to 2 inches above the top leaf. Do not pull it, as you may damage the plant.

## When to harvest garlic bulbs

Harvest garlic bulbs when the lower 3 to 4 leaves have turned brown but the upper leaves are still green. This is usually 2 to 3 weeks after scape cutting. Dig gently with a fork, shake off soil, and cure in a dry, shaded spot for 2 to 4 weeks. Do not wash the bulbs before curing.

## How to use garlic scapes

Scapes are a spring delicacy. Use them fresh or store them in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. You can also chop and freeze them for later. Popular uses include:

- **Pesto**: Replace basil with chopped scapes, add nuts, parmesan, olive oil, and lemon.
- **Stir-fries**: Slice thin and add to any vegetable or meat dish.
- **Pickling**: Make a brine of vinegar, water, salt, and sugar, then refrigerate for a week.
- **Grilling**: Toss whole scapes with oil and grill for 2 to 3 minutes per side.

## How to use garlic bulbs

Garlic bulbs are the staple. Use them raw or cooked. Roasting whole heads mellows the flavor. Store cured bulbs in a cool, dry place (50 to 60°F) for 6 to 8 months. Save the largest cloves for planting in fall.

## Which is better for you?

Both are nutritious. Scapes have a milder flavor and are lower in allicin, the compound that gives garlic its pungency. Bulbs have higher concentrations of allicin, which is linked to health benefits. For everyday cooking, bulbs are more versatile. For a fresh, spring treat, scapes are a bonus.

## Can you get both from the same plant?

Yes. Cutting scapes does not harm the bulb, and you get both. Plant hardneck varieties if you want scapes. Softneck varieties rarely produce scapes. Hardnecks also store less long than softnecks, so plan accordingly.

## Varieties to grow for scapes and bulbs

- **Hardneck**: 'German Extra Hardy', 'Music', 'Chesnok Red', 'Spanish Roja'. These produce large, flavorful bulbs and scapes.
- **Softneck**: 'California Early', 'Inchelium Red'. No scapes, but they store longer.

## FAQ

**Can you eat garlic scapes raw?**
Yes, but they are fibrous. Slice them thin or chop finely. They are milder than raw garlic.

**Do scapes regrow after cutting?**
No. Each plant produces one scape. Once cut, it does not regrow.

**How long do scapes last?**
Fresh scapes last 1 to 2 weeks in the fridge. You can freeze them for up to 6 months.

**Can I plant scapes?**
No. Scapes do not grow into bulbs. Plant cloves or bulbils from the flower head if you want to propagate.

## The bottom line

Garlic scapes are a bonus crop, not a substitute for bulbs. Cut them to improve bulb size and enjoy a mild, garlicky green. Harvest bulbs later for long-term storage. Grow hardneck varieties if you want both. For a small effort, you get two harvests from one plant.
