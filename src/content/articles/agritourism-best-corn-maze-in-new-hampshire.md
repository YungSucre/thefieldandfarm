## Top corn mazes in New Hampshire for 2026

### 1. Coppal House Farm (Lee, NH)

Coppal House Farm is the most well-known corn maze in the state, and for good reason. Their maze covers 5 acres and features a different design each year. In 2026, the theme is "New Hampshire Wildlife," with a maze shaped like a moose and a loon. The maze typically takes 45 to 60 minutes to complete, depending on your navigation skills.

- **Address:** 118 North River Road, Lee, NH
- **2026 Season:** September 12 through October 31, open daily 10 AM to 6 PM
- **Admission:** $14 for adults, $10 for children ages 4-12, free for kids under 4
- **Extras:** Hayrides ($5), pumpkin patch, farm animals, and a corn cannon ($3 per shot)
- **Best for:** Families with older kids, groups looking for a challenge

### 2. Trombly Gardens (Milford, NH)

Trombly Gardens offers a more intimate experience with a 3-acre maze. Their 2026 design is a classic spiral pattern, which is easier to navigate than Coppal House's complex shape. This makes it a better choice for families with young children or first-time maze visitors. The farm also has a large play area with slides and a jumping pillow.

- **Address:** 150 North River Road, Milford, NH
- **2026 Season:** September 19 through October 31, open weekends only (Fri-Sun) 10 AM to 5 PM
- **Admission:** $10 for everyone ages 3 and up
- **Extras:** Hayrides included with admission, corn box, farm animals, and a small pumpkin patch
- **Best for:** Families with toddlers, those who prefer a shorter maze

### 3. Elwood Orchards (Londonderry, NH)

Elwood Orchards is a working apple orchard that adds a corn maze each fall. Their maze is smaller, about 2 acres, but it is part of a larger farm experience. You can pick your own apples before or after the maze, which makes it a great combo trip. The maze design for 2026 is a simple geometric pattern, perfect for a quick 20 to 30 minute walk.

- **Address:** 59 Range Road, Londonderry, NH
- **2026 Season:** September 26 through October 31, open daily 9 AM to 5 PM
- **Admission:** $8 for adults, $6 for children ages 5-12, free for kids under 5
- **Extras:** Apple picking (pay by the bag, $20 for a half-peck), hayrides ($4), farm store with cider donuts
- **Best for:** Apple lovers, families who want a shorter visit

## How to choose the right maze for you

Consider these factors when making your decision:

- **Group size:** Larger groups might prefer Coppal House for its wide paths and multiple checkpoints. Smaller groups can enjoy the intimacy of Trombly Gardens.
- **Age of children:** For kids under 6, choose Trombly Gardens or Elwood Orchards. The maze is less intimidating, and there are more play areas.
- **Budget:** Elwood Orchards is the cheapest at $8 per adult, but Trombly Gardens includes the hayride in the admission price, which adds value.
- **Time available:** If you only have an hour, Elwood Orchards is the best fit. Plan for a half-day at Coppal House or Trombly Gardens.

## Practical tips for visiting a corn maze

1. **Go early in the day.** Morning visits mean cooler weather and fewer crowds. By 1 PM on a sunny weekend, expect lines at the entrance.
2. **Wear closed-toe shoes.** Corn fields can be muddy, especially after rain. Sneakers or boots are best.
3. **Bring water.** You will be walking for 30 to 60 minutes, and hydration is key, especially in late September heat.
4. **Use the map.** All three farms provide a map at the entrance. Do not rely on your phone; cell service can be spotty in rural areas.
5. **Check the weather.** If rain is forecast, call ahead. Some farms close the maze during heavy rain to prevent damage to the stalks.

## What about other corn mazes in New Hampshire?

There are smaller mazes at farms like Sunnycrest Farm in Londonderry and Brookdale Fruit Farm in Hollis. These are fine for a quick visit, but they do not offer the scale or extra attractions of the top three. If you are already in the area, they are worth a stop, but do not go out of your way.

## FAQ

### When is the best time to visit a corn maze?

Late September to mid-October is ideal. The corn is fully grown, and the weather is usually mild. Avoid Columbus Day weekend if you want to skip the biggest crowds.

### Can I bring my dog to the corn maze?

Most farms do not allow dogs, including the three listed here. Service animals are permitted. Check individual farm policies before you go.

### Are the mazes wheelchair accessible?

Paths are generally wide enough for wheelchairs, but the ground is uneven. Trombly Gardens has the smoothest terrain. Call ahead to confirm accessibility.

### Do I need to buy tickets in advance?

Only Coppal House Farm requires advance tickets for weekends. You can buy them on their website. Trombly Gardens and Elwood Orchards sell tickets at the gate.

## The bottom line

For the best overall corn maze experience in New Hampshire, go to Coppal House Farm. It has the most impressive design, the longest maze, and the most activities. If you have young children, choose Trombly Gardens for a more relaxed visit. For a budget-friendly trip that includes apple picking, head to Elwood Orchards. Whichever you pick, go early, wear good shoes, and bring your sense of direction. You will have a memorable fall day.
