## Top 3 Farm Stays in Karjat (2026)

### 1. Farmstay Organic Retreat

This is our top pick for its balance of authenticity and comfort. Located 15 minutes from Karjat station, it sits on a 5-acre working farm with vegetables, poultry, and a small dairy.

- **Accommodations**: Four simple cottages with attached bathrooms, no AC (but nights are cool), and mosquito nets. Each cottage sleeps 2-3.
- **Farm Activities**: Morning milking, vegetable harvesting (seasonal: tomatoes, brinjal, okra), and feeding hens. You can also try your hand at plowing with a bullock if you visit between November and February.
- **Meals**: All meals are included: breakfast, lunch, evening tea, and dinner. Food is vegetarian, cooked on a wood-fired stove, and uses produce from the farm.
- **Price**: ₹2,500 to ₹3,500 per person per night, including all meals and activities. Children under 5 stay free.
- **Booking**: Directly via their website or phone. They require a 50% advance to confirm.

### 2. Green Valley Agro Farm

A larger operation (10 acres) with more amenities, good for families with older children. It has a small swimming pool (filtered, not chlorinated) and a nature trail.

- **Accommodations**: Six rooms in a main building, plus two family cottages. Rooms are basic but clean, with ceiling fans and attached baths.
- **Farm Activities**: Seasonal planting and harvesting, a guided tour of the organic compost setup, and a chance to make fresh cow dung cakes (a hit with kids).
- **Meals**: Buffet-style meals, including local Maharashtrian dishes like bhakri and pitla. Non-veg available on request.
- **Price**: ₹3,000 to ₹4,000 per person per night, all inclusive.
- **Booking**: Via their Instagram page or a call. They often have last-minute slots on weekdays.

### 3. Riverside Farm Cottages

Best for a quiet, off-grid experience. This small farm (2 acres) has just two cottages, so you get personalized attention.

- **Accommodations**: Two mud-and-timber cottages with thatched roofs. Compost toilets and solar-powered lights. No Wi-Fi, which is a plus for disconnecting.
- **Farm Activities**: You help with whatever is happening: sowing, weeding, or harvesting. The host, a retired agronomist, gives informal talks on soil health and companion planting.
- **Meals**: Simple home-style food, mostly grown on the farm. Meals are served at fixed times.
- **Price**: ₹1,800 to ₹2,500 per person per night, all inclusive.
- **Booking**: By phone only. They prefer a minimum two-night stay on weekends.

## What to Expect: A Typical Day

A typical day starts early, around 6:30 AM, with tea and a walk through the fields. You might milk a cow or collect eggs. Breakfast is at 8:30, followed by a farm activity like planting or harvesting. Lunch is at 1 PM, then you have free time to rest or explore nearby trails. Evening tea is at 5, and you can help with feeding animals. Dinner is at 8:30, often followed by a bonfire if the weather is cool.

## Practical Tips for Your Stay

- **Book at least 2 weeks in advance** for weekends, as most farm stays fill up quickly. Weekdays are easier to book.
- **Carry insect repellent** and wear long pants and closed shoes for farm work.
- **Ask about the current crops** before booking; you want to visit when there is active harvesting (e.g., rice in September-October, vegetables in winter).
- **Bring cash**; many farm stays do not accept cards, and ATMs are far.
- **Check train schedules**: Karjat is on the Mumbai-Pune route, and the station is well-connected. From the station, auto-rickshaws cost ₹200-₹300 to most farm stays.

## FAQ

### Are farm stays in Karjat suitable for children?

Yes, most farm stays welcome children and offer safe activities like feeding animals and picking vegetables. Green Valley Agro Farm has a pool, which is a hit with kids. Always confirm age-specific activities when booking.

### What is the best season to visit a farm stay in Karjat?

October to February is ideal: the weather is cool, and many crops are ready for harvest. Monsoon (June to September) is lush but can be muddy and leech-prone. Summer (March to May) is hot, but mornings and evenings are still pleasant.

### Do I need to bring my own food?

No, all farm stays include meals. If you have dietary restrictions (e.g., Jain, vegan), inform them in advance. They can usually accommodate.

### Can I buy farm produce to take home?

Yes, most farm stays sell fresh vegetables, eggs, and sometimes honey or rice. Prices are reasonable, and it is a nice way to support the farm.
