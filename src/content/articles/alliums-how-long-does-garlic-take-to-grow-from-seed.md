If you plant garlic cloves, expect 6 to 8 months from planting to harvest, depending on your zone and variety. If you mean true botanical seed (from garlic scapes), that takes 8 to 10 months from seed to harvest, plus a cold stratification step. For nearly all home gardeners, planting cloves is faster, easier, and more reliable. Here is the exact timeline and what to do this week.

## Garlic from cloves: the standard timeline

Most garlic is grown from individual cloves, not seed. Each clove is a clone of the parent plant, so you get a true-to-type bulb in one season. The timeline is straightforward:

- **Planting:** Fall, 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes. For most zones, that is late September to mid-November.
- **Root growth:** Fall and early winter. Roots establish before the ground freezes.
- **Vernalization:** Winter cold triggers bulb formation. Garlic needs 6 to 8 weeks of temperatures below 50°F (10°C).
- **Spring growth:** Shoots emerge in early spring, as soon as soil thaws.
- **Bulb formation:** Late spring to early summer, as days lengthen.
- **Harvest:** When lower leaves turn brown, usually 6 to 8 months after planting. In zone 5, that is typically late June to early July.

## Garlic from true seed: the longer path

True garlic seed comes from the small bulbils that form in the scape (the curly flower stalk) of hardneck garlic. Each bulbil can contain several seeds, but they are tiny and require patience. The timeline is:

- **Collect seeds:** In summer, when scapes dry and bulbils turn brown.
- **Cold stratify:** Mix seeds with moist sand or peat, seal in a plastic bag, and refrigerate at 33-40°F for 6 to 8 weeks. This mimics winter.
- **Plant:** In late winter or early spring, after stratification, sow seeds in trays indoors or in a cold frame. Keep soil moist and warm (60-70°F).
- **Germination:** 2 to 4 weeks, but sometimes erratic.
- **Grow seedlings:** Keep them in a bright, cool spot for the first season. They will grow small leaves and a tiny bulb, but not a harvestable garlic.
- **Transplant:** In fall, move the small bulbs to the garden, spacing them 4 inches apart.
- **Second season:** The following summer, you will get a full-sized bulb. Total time from seed to harvest: 8 to 10 months from planting seed, but that includes a full year of growth plus the stratification period.

In practice, most gardeners who start from true seed do it for breeding or to avoid soil-borne diseases. For a reliable crop, cloves are the way to go.

## Why cloves are faster and more reliable

- **Speed:** Cloves skip the seedling stage and the stratification requirement. You plant in fall, harvest in summer.
- **Success rate:** Cloves have a near 100% germination rate if planted correctly. True seed may have 50-70% germination, and seedlings are delicate.
- **Genetic consistency:** Cloves are clones, so you know exactly what you will get. Seed-grown garlic is a genetic mix, so bulbs may vary in size, flavor, and color.
- **Disease pressure:** True seed can be free of soil-borne diseases, but it takes time and care. Cloves can carry diseases if you plant infected stock, but buying certified seed garlic avoids that.

## What to do this week

If you are planting cloves this fall, here is your checklist:

1. **Order seed garlic now.** For fall planting, order by August or September. Good mail-order sources include Keene Garlic, Johnny's Selected Seeds, and Burpee. Expect to pay $10 to $20 per pound for eating garlic, and $15 to $30 per pound for certified seed garlic. You need about 1 pound per 50 cloves, which plants a 25-foot row.
2. **Choose the right type.** Hardneck varieties (like 'German Extra Hardy', 'Music', 'Chesnook Red') do best in zones 3-7 and produce scapes. Softneck varieties (like 'California Early', 'Inchelium Red', 'Kettle River Giant') are better for zones 6-9 and store longer.
3. **Prepare the bed.** Garlic needs full sun and well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0 to 7.0. Work in 2 inches of compost and a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at 1 pound per 100 square feet.
4. **Plant cloves.** Break the bulb into individual cloves, keep the papery skin on. Plant each clove pointy side up, 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart, with rows 12 inches apart.
5. **Mulch.** After planting, spread 3 to 4 inches of straw or shredded leaves. This insulates the soil and prevents frost heaving.
6. **Water.** Give the bed a good watering after planting, then let nature take over. In dry fall, water once a week if no rain.

If you are trying true seed, start stratification now if you have seeds. Mix seeds with damp sand, put in a labeled bag, and refrigerate for 6 to 8 weeks. Then sow in trays in late winter.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Planting too early or too late.** If you plant too early, cloves may sprout in fall and then die back. Too late, and roots won't establish before freeze. Aim for 4-6 weeks before the ground freezes.
- **Planting grocery store garlic.** It may be treated to prevent sprouting, or carry disease. Always buy from a reputable seed source.
- **Skipping mulch.** Without mulch, winter cold can heave cloves out of the ground, and spring weeds will compete.
- **Harvesting too late.** If you wait until all leaves are brown, the bulbs will split and not store well. Harvest when 3 to 4 lower leaves are brown, but the upper leaves are still green.

## FAQ

**Can I grow garlic from seed in one season?** No. True seed takes at least 8 months from planting seed to harvest, and that includes a cold stratification period and a full growing season. Cloves are the only way to get a crop in 6-8 months.

**What is the best garlic variety for beginners?** For zones 3-7, try 'Music' or 'German Extra Hardy'. They are reliable, large, and store well. For zones 8-10, 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red' are good choices.

**Do I need to refrigerate garlic cloves before planting?** Only if you live in a warm climate (zone 9 or higher). In cooler zones, natural winter cold provides the necessary vernalization. In warm zones, chill cloves in the fridge for 4-6 weeks before planting.

**How do I know when garlic is ready to harvest?** Watch the lower leaves. When 3 to 4 of the bottom leaves have turned brown, but the top leaves are still green, dig up a bulb to check. The bulb should be well-formed with tight skin. Harvest in dry weather, then cure in a dry, shaded place for 2 to 4 weeks.

## The bottom line

For a practical garlic crop, plant cloves in fall and harvest in summer, 6 to 8 months later. True seed is a project for patient breeders, taking 8 to 10 months and extra care. Order your seed garlic now, prepare the bed, and plant 4 to 6 weeks before the ground freezes. With proper planting and a little patience, you will have homegrown garlic next summer.
