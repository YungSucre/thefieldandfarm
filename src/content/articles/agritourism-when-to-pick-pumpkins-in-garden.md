The best time to pick pumpkins is when they have reached full color, the rind is hard, and the stem has started to dry and crack. In most regions, this falls between late August and October, depending on your planting date and variety. Check your pumpkin daily once it looks orange, because leaving it on the vine too long can invite rot, especially in wet weather.

## How to tell if a pumpkin is ready

A ripe pumpkin gives you clear signals. Look for these three signs before you cut:

1. **Full color**: The pumpkin has turned its mature color, whether that is deep orange, white, or another variety-specific shade. Green streaks may fade as it ripens, but some varieties keep green stripes, so know your variety.
2. **Hard rind**: Press your thumbnail gently into the skin. If it resists and does not puncture easily, the rind is hard. If it dents or breaks, it needs more time.
3. **Stem drying**: The stem begins to dry, turn brown, and crack near the fruit. A green, plump stem means the pumpkin is still receiving nutrients and is not fully mature.

Another test: thump the pumpkin. A ripe one sounds hollow, but this is not foolproof. The stem and rind tests are more reliable.

## Harvest timing by zone and variety

Pumpkins need about 75 to 110 days from planting to harvest, depending on the variety. If you planted in late May or early June, start checking in late August. Use these general windows for the United States:

| USDA Zone | Typical harvest window |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Zones 3-4 | September 1 to October 1 |
| Zones 5-6 | September 15 to October 15 |
| Zones 7-8 | October 1 to November 1 |
| Zones 9-10 | October 15 to November 30 |

These dates assume a spring planting after the last frost. If you planted later, add the difference. For example, a pumpkin planted on June 15 in Zone 5 with a 90-day variety will be ready around September 13.

Popular varieties and their days to maturity:

- **Small Sugar**: 100 days, classic pie pumpkin, 6-8 pounds
- **Jack-O'-Lantern**: 90-100 days, 15-25 pounds
- **Howden**: 110 days, large carving pumpkin, 20-30 pounds
- **Cinderella**: 100 days, flattened shape, 15-20 pounds
- **Baby Boo**: 90 days, miniature white, 1-2 pounds

## How to pick pumpkins correctly

Use a sharp knife or pruning shears to cut the stem, leaving 3 to 4 inches attached. A longer stem helps prevent rot and gives you a handle. Cut cleanly, do not twist or pull, as that can damage the vine or the pumpkin.

After cutting, handle pumpkins gently. Bruises and cuts invite decay. Do not carry them by the stem; support the bottom.

## Curing and storing pumpkins

Curing hardens the skin and extends storage life. Place freshly picked pumpkins in a warm, dry spot (80-85°F) with good air circulation for 10 to 14 days. A sunny porch or a greenhouse works well. After curing, store them in a cool, dry place at 50-60°F. A basement or pantry with low humidity is ideal.

Properly cured pumpkins can last 2 to 3 months. Check them weekly and remove any that show soft spots or mold.

## What if you pick too early or too late?

If you pick too early, the pumpkin will not ripen properly. It may turn orange, but the flavor and storage life suffer. If frost threatens, pick immediately, even if not fully colored. Frost damages the cells and causes rot.

If you leave them too long, they may become overripe, with a dull color and soft spots. In wet conditions, they can rot on the vine. Aim for the sweet spot: full color, hard rind, and a stem that is just starting to crack.

## Tools and costs

You do not need much to harvest pumpkins. A sharp knife or pruning shears costs $10 to $20. A pair of gloves protects your hands from prickly vines. A garden cart or wheelbarrow ($50 to $150) helps move heavy pumpkins. For large patches, a harvesting apron with a padded shoulder strap ($20 to $30) can make carrying easier.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I pick pumpkins before they are fully orange?**
A: Yes, if frost is coming. They will continue to color slightly after picking, but the flavor and storage life will not be as good. It is better than losing them to frost.

**Q: How do I know if a pumpkin is ripe if it is a white or green variety?**
A: Check the rind hardness and stem condition. Color is not a reliable indicator for all varieties. For white pumpkins like Baby Boo, the skin should be fully white and hard.

**Q: Can I eat pumpkins right after picking?**
A: Yes, you can, but curing improves sweetness and texture. If you plan to store them, cure first. If you are making pie, you can use them immediately.

**Q: How long can I leave pumpkins on the vine after they are ripe?**
A: A week or two is fine if the weather is dry. But once the stem cracks, they are vulnerable to rot. Pick them within a few days of that sign.

## The bottom line

Pick pumpkins when they have full color, a hard rind, and a drying stem. Cut with a sharp tool, leave a few inches of stem, and cure them before storing. Timing varies by zone and variety, so check your planting date and the days to maturity. With these steps, you will enjoy a healthy harvest that lasts through fall.
