If you're deciding between cardon (Pachycereus pringlei) and saguaro (Carnegiea gigantea) for your desert landscape, here's the short answer: cardon grows faster and tolerates more cold, but saguaro is the iconic, slower-growing classic. Choose cardon if you want quicker vertical impact and live in a colder zone; choose saguaro if you're in its native range and can wait decades. Both are protected in the wild, so always buy nursery-grown plants.

![Cardon vs saguaro](/images/articles/cardon-vs-saguaro/section-1.jpg)

## What's the difference?

Cardon and saguaro look similar: tall, ribbed, columnar cacti with arms that develop with age. But they differ in size, speed, and hardiness.

- **Size**: Cardon is the largest cactus in the world, reaching up to 60 feet tall and 3 feet thick. Saguaro maxes out around 40 feet and 2 feet thick.
- **Growth rate**: Cardon grows faster, especially when young, adding 4 to 6 inches per year in ideal conditions. Saguaro is notoriously slow, often only 1 to 2 inches per year for the first decade.
- **Cold tolerance**: Cardon handles down to about 15°F (-9°C) once established. Saguaro is more sensitive, suffering damage below 25°F (-4°C).
- **Native range**: Saguaro is native to the Sonoran Desert (Arizona, Mexico). Cardon is native to Baja California and coastal Sonora, but it adapts well to similar climates.
- **Lifespan**: Both can live 150 to 200 years, but saguaro often

## Which should you plant?

Your choice depends on your climate and patience.

- **If you live in USDA zones 9b to 11** (e.g., Phoenix, Tucson, Las Vegas, inland California), both can grow. Saguaro is native to these areas, but cardon also thrives.
- **If you get occasional frosts below 25°F**, cardon is safer. Saguaro may need frost cloth or heat lamps.
- **If you want a mature-looking cactus sooner**, cardon is the better bet. A 5-gallon cardon can reach 5 to 6 feet in 5 years; a saguaro of the same size might only grow 1 foot in that time.
- **If you want the classic saguaro silhouette for cultural or aesthetic reasons**, go with saguaro, but be prepared to buy a larger specimen (5 to 10 feet tall) to see arms in your lifetime.

## How to plant and care for them

Both cacti need well-draining soil and full sun. Here's a step-by-step guide for planting either.

1. **Choose a location**: Full sun (at least 6 hours daily), away from walkways and structures. Allow 10 feet of clearance for mature width.
2. **Prepare the soil**: Mix native soil with 50% pumice or coarse sand to ensure drainage. Avoid heavy clay.
3. **Plant in spring or fall**: Avoid summer heat and winter cold. Dig a hole twice as wide as the root ball, but no deeper. Place the cactus at the same depth it was in the pot.
4. **Backfill and water**: Water thoroughly after planting, then wait 2 weeks before watering again. After that, water every 2 to 3 weeks during the first summer, then taper off.
5. **Stake if needed**: For tall specimens (over 4 feet), use 3 wooden stakes and soft ties for the first year.
6. **Fertilize lightly**: Apply a low-nitrogen cactus fertilizer (like 2-7-7) once in spring and once in summer. Skip if you have rich soil.

## Where to buy and what it costs

Buy from reputable nurseries that grow from seed or cuttings, not wild-collected plants. In 2026, prices vary by size:

- **Cardon**: 1-gallon ($15-$25), 5-gallon ($40-$60), 15-gallon ($100-$150).
- **Saguaro**: 1-gallon ($20-$30), 5-gallon ($50-$80), 15-gallon ($150-$250).
- **Larger specimens (6-10 feet)**: $500 to $2,000, depending on arms and age.

Check local nurseries in Arizona, California, and Texas, or online retailers like Cactus Store and Mesa Garden. Always ask for proof of nursery cultivation.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Overwatering**: The #1 killer. Let soil dry completely between waterings. In winter, keep bone dry.
- **Planting in heavy soil**: Roots rot. Always amend with pumice.
- **Frost damage**: Even cardon can suffer in a hard freeze. Cover with frost cloth if temps drop below 20°F.
- **Sunburn**: Young plants may scorch in intense summer sun. Provide 50% shade cloth for the first year if you're in a hot inland area.

## FAQ

**Can I grow cardon or saguaro in a pot?**

Yes, for a few years. Use a heavy clay pot with drainage holes and cactus mix. Repot every 2-3 years. But they will eventually outgrow pots and need ground planting.

**Are these cacti legal to buy?**

Yes, if nursery-grown. In Arizona, saguaro is protected; you need a permit to move or remove wild ones. Always buy from licensed sellers.

**How long until they bloom?**

Cardon may bloom in 10-15 years from seed; saguaro takes 30-50 years. Flowers are white, open at night, and pollinated by bats and bees.

**Can they survive outside the desert?**

They need arid conditions. In humid climates, they are prone to rot. If you live outside zones 9b-11, grow them in a greenhouse or as indoor specimens.

## The bottom line

Choose cardon if you want faster growth and better cold tolerance. Choose saguaro if you want the classic icon and can wait. Both are rewarding, low-maintenance additions to a desert garden, but they require patience and careful watering. Start with a nursery-grown plant, give it full sun and excellent drainage, and you'll have a striking centerpiece for decades.
