Tomatillos grow best in loose, well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. They are heavy feeders, so enrich your soil with compost before planting, and side-dress with a balanced fertilizer mid-season. This guide covers everything from soil testing to container mixes, with specific steps you can take this week.

## What Makes Ideal Tomatillo Soil?

Tomatillos (Physalis philadelphica) are native to Mexico and thrive in warm, sunny conditions. The perfect soil is:

- **Loamy**: A mix of sand, silt, and clay that holds moisture but drains well.
- **Rich in organic matter**: Compost or aged manure provides nutrients and improves structure.
- **Slightly acidic to neutral**: pH 6.0 to 7.0. If your soil is too acidic (below 5.5) or too alkaline (above 7.5), nutrient uptake suffers.

## Testing Your Soil

Before planting, test your soil. You can buy a simple pH test kit at a garden center for $10 to $20, or send a sample to a lab like your local extension service for $20 to $40. The lab report will also show nutrient levels (N, P, K) and organic matter percentage.

If you don't want to wait, use a digital pH meter (around $15 to $30) for a quick reading. Collect soil from several spots in your garden, mix it, and test.

## Amending Your Soil

Once you know your soil's condition, adjust it:

- **pH too low (acidic)**: Add garden lime (calcitic or dolomitic) at the rate recommended on the package, usually 1 to 2 pounds per 100 square feet to raise pH by 0.5. Work it into the top 6 inches.
- **pH too high (alkaline)**: Add elemental sulfur, about 1 pound per 100 square feet to lower pH by 0.5. Or use acidic compost like pine needles.
- **Poor drainage**: Mix in 2 to 3 inches of compost or aged bark. For heavy clay, consider raised beds or grow in containers.
- **Low organic matter**: Add 2 to 3 inches of compost across the bed and till or dig it in to a depth of 8 to 10 inches.

## Fertilizer Needs

Tomatillos are heavy feeders, but too much nitrogen leads to lush leaves and fewer fruits. Use a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at planting time: apply 1 tablespoon per plant, worked into the soil around the roots. Then side-dress when the plants start to flower: sprinkle 1 tablespoon of the same fertilizer in a ring 6 inches from the stem, water it in. Repeat every 4 weeks until late summer.

If you prefer organic options, use a granular fertilizer like Espoma Garden-tone (about $10 for 4 pounds) or Dr. Earth Home Grown (about $12 for 4 pounds). Compost tea or fish emulsion are also good, but they are lower in phosphorus, so supplement with bone meal.

## Container Growing

If you have poor soil or limited space, grow tomatillos in containers. Use a pot at least 5 gallons (18 inches deep). Fill with a high-quality potting mix like FoxFarm Ocean Forest (about $20 for 1.5 cubic feet) or a mix of 50% compost and 50% perlite. Ensure the pot has drainage holes. Water daily in hot weather, as containers dry out fast.

## Mulching and Watering

Mulch around plants with straw or shredded leaves to retain moisture and suppress weeds. Apply a 2-inch layer after the soil warms up. Water deeply once a week, about 1 to 2 inches per week, more in hot, dry spells. Avoid overhead watering to prevent fungal diseases.

## Common Soil Problems and Fixes

- **Blossom end rot**: Dark, sunken spots on fruits. Caused by calcium deficiency and uneven watering. Add calcium to the soil (e.g., crushed eggshells or gypsum) and water consistently.
- **Poor fruit set**: Often due to lack of pollinators or too much nitrogen. Plant flowers nearby to attract bees, and cut back on nitrogen.
- **Yellow leaves**: Could be overwatering or nitrogen deficiency. Check drainage and feed with a balanced fertilizer.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use garden soil for containers?**
A: No, garden soil compacts in pots and may contain pests. Use a potting mix designed for containers.

**Q: How often should I fertilize tomatillos?**
A: Start with a balanced fertilizer at planting, then side-dress every 4 weeks until late summer. Over-fertilizing with nitrogen can reduce fruit yield.

**Q: Do tomatillos need full sun?**
A: Yes, they need at least 6 to 8 hours of direct sun daily. More sun means better growth and fruit production.

**Q: Can I grow tomatillos in raised beds?**
A: Absolutely. Raised beds offer excellent drainage and let you control the soil mix. Fill with a blend of 60% topsoil, 30% compost, and 10% perlite.

## The Bottom Line

The best soil for tomatillos is loose, loamy, rich in organic matter, with a pH of 6.0 to 7.0. Test your soil, amend with compost and lime or sulfur as needed, and fertilize with a balanced product. Plant after the last frost, space 24 to 36 inches apart, and keep the soil consistently moist. With these steps, you'll get a healthy harvest of tangy fruits for salsa and sauces. Start by testing your soil this week, then prepare your beds with compost before planting.
