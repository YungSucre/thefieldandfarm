Yes, you can regrow scallions from the rooted root ends you'd normally toss. It works, but with a caveat: you get one or two harvests from the same roots, not endless regrowth. The method is simple, costs nothing, and gives you a steady supply of green tops for salads, stir-fries, and garnishes. Here's how to do it right, with real numbers and varieties that perform.

## Why regrow scallions at all?

Regrowing scallions from roots is a free, low-effort way to get fresh greens in about 2 to 3 weeks. It's not a substitute for planting seeds or sets if you want full-sized bulbs, but it's perfect for windowsill gardening or extending a harvest. ## Step-by-step: regrowing in water (the easy start)

1. **Save the roots**: When you use scallions, cut off the top 4 to 5 inches, leaving the root end with about 1 inch of white base. Use a sharp knife for a clean cut.
2. **Place in water**: Put the root ends in a glass or jar with about 1 inch of water, root side down. Use a small jar or shot glass so they stand upright. Change the water every day or two to prevent bacteria.
3. **Wait for growth**: In 3 to 5 days, you'll see new green shoots emerging from the center. In 7 to 10 days, they'll be 3 to 4 inches tall.
4. **Harvest or transplant**: You can snip the greens as needed, leaving the base to regrow. Or, once roots are 1 inch long, transplant them into soil for stronger growth.

## The better long-term method: transplanting to soil

Water regrowing works, but soil-grown scallions have more flavor and last longer. Here's the transplant method:

1. **Prepare a pot or garden bed**: Use a pot at least 6 inches deep with drainage holes, or a garden bed with loose, well-draining soil. Scallions prefer a pH of 6.0 to 7.0.
2. **Plant the root ends**: After water rooting, plant each root end 1 inch deep, spacing them 2 inches apart. If planting in rows, space rows 6 inches apart.
3. **Water and place in sun**: Keep soil consistently moist, not soggy. Place in full sun (at least 6 hours) or under a grow light for 12 hours a day.
4. **Fertilize lightly**: Every 2 weeks, apply a balanced liquid fertilizer (like 10-10-10) at half strength. This supports regrowth.
5. **Harvest**: Cut the greens when they reach 6 to 8 inches tall, about 2 to 3 weeks after transplanting. Cut 1 inch above the root base, and they'll regrow once or twice more.

## Realistic yields and timeframes

Here's what you can expect, based on my experience and common reports:

- **First harvest**: 2 to 3 weeks after starting in water or soil.
- **Regrowth cycles**: 2 to 3 harvests per root base, then the plant weakens.
- **Yield per root**: About 1 to 2 ounces of greens per harvest. A bunch of 10 roots gives you roughly 10 to 20 ounces over a month.
- **Cost**: Free if using kitchen scraps. If buying organic scallions for regrowing, a bunch costs $1.50 to $3.00 in 2026.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Slimy roots**: Change water daily, and don't let roots sit in water too long once they have 1-inch roots. Transplant to soil.
- **Leggy, pale growth**: Not enough light. Move to a sunny windowsill or add a grow light.
- **No regrowth after second cut**: The plant has used up its energy. Compost the roots and start fresh.
- **Bulb formation**: Some varieties will try to bulb if they get too much light or heat. Harvest before that happens.

## FAQ

**Can I regrow scallions indefinitely?**

No. You'll get 2 to 3 harvests per root base, then the plant's energy depletes. After that, it's better to plant seeds or sets for a continuous supply.

**Do I need to use rooting hormone?**

No. Scallion roots form naturally in water or soil. Hormones are unnecessary and add cost.

**Can I regrow scallions in winter?**

Yes, indoors on a sunny windowsill or under a grow light. They need 6 to 8 hours of light and temperatures between 60°F and 70°F. Growth slows in low light, so expect longer times.

**What's the difference between scallions and green onions?**

In most US markets, they're the same thing: immature onions harvested before bulb formation. Some varieties are bunching onions (never bulb), while others are regular onions pulled early. Both regrow similarly.

## The bottom line

Regrowing scallions from roots is a simple, zero-cost way to get fresh greens in weeks. It's not a perpetual harvest, but it's a practical way to reduce waste and have a small supply on hand. Start with a bunch of organic scallions, use the tops in your cooking, and root the bases in a glass of water. Once roots form, transplant to soil for the best results. With 2 to 3 harvests per root, you'll get your money's worth. For a continuous supply, rotate new batches every 2 weeks. It's a small step, but it's one that works.
