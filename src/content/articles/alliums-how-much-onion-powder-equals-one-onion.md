If you're wondering how much onion powder to use when a recipe calls for one fresh onion, the simple answer is: 1 tablespoon of onion powder equals one medium onion (about 1 cup chopped). This conversion works for most cooking, but let's break down the details so you can adjust for your own dried onions and recipes.

![How much onion powder equals one onion](/images/articles/how-much-onion-powder-equals-one-onion/section-1.jpg)

## The Basic Conversion

For a standard medium onion (about 2.5 to 3 inches in diameter, yielding roughly 1 cup chopped), use **1 tablespoon of onion powder**. If you're using onion flakes, use **2 tablespoons** (they're less concentrated). For a large onion (1.5 cups chopped), use 1.5 tablespoons of powder. For a small onion (0.5 cup chopped), use 1.5 teaspoons.

Here's a quick reference table:

| Fresh onion size | Chopped amount | Onion powder | Onion flakes |
|------------------|----------------|--------------|--------------|
| Small (2" dia)   | 1/2 cup        | 1.5 tsp      | 1 tbsp       |
| Medium (2.5-3")  | 1 cup          | 1 tbsp       | 2 tbsp       |
| Large (3-4")     | 1.5 cups       | 1.5 tbsp     | 3 tbsp       |

These measurements assume you're using commercially ground onion powder. If

## Why the Conversion Works

Onion powder is dehydrated onion, ground to a fine consistency. The dehydration process removes about 90% of the water weight, concentrating the flavor. One medium onion contains roughly 1 tablespoon of dry matter after dehydration, which is why the powder measurement aligns so closely.

However, onion powder doesn't provide the moisture or texture of fresh onion. In recipes where onion is a main component (like salsa or salads), you'll need to rehydrate the powder or use fresh onion instead. For cooked dishes like soups, stews, or casseroles, the powder works perfectly as a substitute.

## How to Dry and Grind Your Own Onion Powder

If you grow your own onions, making powder is a great way to preserve a surplus. Here's a simple method:

1. **Choose the right variety**: Sweet onions like 'Walla Walla' or 'Vidalia' produce milder powder; storage onions like 'Copra' or 'Redwing' give a stronger, more pungent result. For a balanced powder, try a mix.

2. **Prep the onions**: Peel and slice them thinly (about 1/8 inch). A mandoline makes this quick and uniform.

3. **Dry them**: Use a dehydrator at 125°F for 8-12 hours, or an oven on its lowest setting (usually 170°F) with the door cracked for 4-6 hours. Onions are dry when they snap, not bend.

4. **Grind**: Use a spice grinder or high-powered blender to turn the dried slices into powder. A $20 coffee grinder dedicated to spices works well.

5. **Store**: Keep in an airtight jar in a cool, dark place. Properly dried and stored, your powder will last up to a year. A pint jar holds about 4 ounces of powder, which equals roughly 20 medium onions.

## Adjusting Recipes for Onion Powder

When substituting powder for fresh onion, remember these tips:

- **Add liquid**: Since you're removing water, add 1-2 tablespoons of water, broth, or other liquid per tablespoon of powder to maintain the recipe's moisture balance.
- **Add early**: Powder releases flavor slowly, so add it at the beginning of cooking, not at the end.
- **Taste and adjust**: Start with 3/4 of the recommended amount, then add more to taste. Onion powder can vary in strength depending on the brand or batch.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Using too much**: It's easy to overdo it. Remember, 1 tablespoon per medium onion is plenty. Too much powder can make a dish taste bitter or artificial.
- **Not rehydrating**: If you add powder to a dry rub or a dish with little liquid, it won't dissolve properly. Mix it with a little water first.
- **Storing incorrectly**: Onion powder absorbs moisture and odors. Keep it in a sealed container away from the stove or sink.

## FAQ

**Can I use onion powder in place of fresh onion in any recipe?**
Yes, but not always 1:1. For cooked dishes, it's a great substitute. For raw applications like salads or garnishes, rehydrate the powder in water for 10 minutes before using, or use fresh onion instead.

**How long does homemade onion powder last?**
If stored in an airtight container in a cool, dark place, it will keep for up to a year. Check for clumping or loss of aroma; if it smells musty, toss it.

**What's the difference between onion powder and onion salt?**
Onion salt is a mixture of onion powder and salt, usually 1 part powder to 3 parts salt. If you use onion salt in place of powder, reduce the salt in your recipe accordingly. For example, 1 tablespoon of onion salt equals about 1 teaspoon of powder plus 2 teaspoons of salt.

**Can I substitute onion flakes for powder?**
Yes, but use twice as much flakes as powder. Flakes are less dense and have a milder flavor. To rehydrate flakes, soak them in warm water for 5-10 minutes before adding to your dish.

## The Bottom Line

For most recipes, 1 tablespoon of onion powder equals one medium onion. Keep this conversion in mind, adjust for moisture and cooking time, and you'll get consistent results. If you're drying your own onions, you'll save money and have a reliable pantry staple. A 1-pound bag of commercial onion powder costs around $8 to $12, while a pound of fresh onions runs $1 to $2, so making your own is cheaper if you have a surplus. But for convenience, store-bought is fine. Now you can confidently swap fresh for powdered in any recipe.
