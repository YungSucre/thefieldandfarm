## What to look for in a garlic press

Not all garlic presses are created equal. Here are the key features to consider before you buy:

- **Material**: Stainless steel is best. It does not rust, does not react with garlic, and lasts longer than aluminum or plastic. Aluminum can bend and leave a metallic taste. Plastic presses crack under pressure.
- **Hinge and handle**: Look for a press with a sturdy hinge that does not wobble. The handle should be long enough to give you leverage, so you do not strain your hand. A comfortable grip is a bonus.
- **Hopper size**: The hopper is the cup where you put the clove. It should hold a whole clove without you needing to peel it first, but peeling is still recommended for best results. A wider hopper makes loading easier.
- **Cleaning**: The biggest hassle with garlic presses is cleaning the holes. Some models come with a cleaning brush or a removable insert. Look for a press that is easy to rinse and poke out leftover bits.
- **Price**: You can find presses for $10, but they often break or are hard to clean. A good mid-range press costs $25 to $45. High-end models can go up to $60, but you are paying for brand and design.

## How to use a garlic press effectively

Using a press is simple, but a few tricks make it better:

1. **Peel the clove first**: Even though some presses claim to handle unpeeled cloves, peeling gives you more juice and less mess. To peel quickly, smash the clove with the flat side of a knife, then slip off the skin.
2. **Cut off the root end**: If the root end is hard, trim it off before pressing. It can clog the holes.
3. **Load the clove**: Place the clove in the hopper, root end down if you left it on. Close the press and squeeze firmly.
4. **Squeeze once**: One firm squeeze is usually enough. Do not rock the press back and forth, as that can bend the hinge.
5. **Clean immediately**: Rinse the press under hot water right after use. Use the brush or a toothpick to clear any stuck bits. If you wait, garlic dries and becomes hard to remove.

## Garlic press vs. mincing by hand

You might wonder if a press is worth the counter space. Here is my honest take:

- **Speed**: A press is faster than mincing with a knife, especially if you use a lot of garlic. It takes about 5 seconds per clove.
- **Flavor**: Pressing releases more of the garlic's oils, giving a stronger, more pungent flavor. If you want a milder taste, mincing by hand is better.
- **Texture**: Pressed garlic is a fine paste, which is great for sauces, dressings, and rubs. Minced garlic has more texture, which is better for sautés where you want pieces.
- **Cleanup**: A press can be a pain to clean, but a knife and cutting board also need washing. With a good press and a brush, cleanup is quick.

For most recipes, a press is a convenient tool. But if you prefer chunky garlic in your cooking, stick with a knife.

## Caring for your garlic press

To make your press last:

- **Hand wash**: Even if the press says dishwasher safe, hand washing is gentler. The dishwasher can dull the edges and loosen the hinge over time.
- **Dry thoroughly**: After washing, dry the press with a towel to prevent water spots and rust, especially if it is stainless steel.
- **Store properly**: Keep it in a drawer or on a hook. Do not stack heavy items on top of it.
- **Replace when needed**: If the hinge becomes loose or the holes clog beyond cleaning, it is time for a new one. A good press should last 5 to 10 years with proper care.

## FAQ

**Can I press unpeeled garlic?**

Yes, some presses can handle unpeeled cloves, but you will get less juice and the skin may clog the holes. It is better to peel first for best results.

**How do I clean a garlic press with stuck bits?**

Rinse under hot water immediately, then use the included brush or a toothpick to poke out the holes. If garlic is dried, soak the press in warm soapy water for a few minutes.

**Is a garlic press worth it if I grow my own garlic?**

Absolutely. If you grow garlic, you likely have a lot of it. A press makes quick work of turning cloves into paste for cooking, and it saves your fingers from smelling like garlic all day.

**What is the best garlic press for large cloves?**

The OXO Good Grips has a larger hopper, so it can handle big cloves without cutting them down. The Kuhn Rikon also works, but you may need to slice very large cloves in half.
