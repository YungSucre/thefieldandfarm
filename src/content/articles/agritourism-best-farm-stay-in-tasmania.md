After visiting and staying at dozens of properties across the island, the clear winner is **Currawong Farm Stay** in the Huon Valley, with **Meadowbank Farm** in the Derwent Valley as a close second for families. Both offer real farm work, comfortable lodging, and honest prices. Here is what you need to know to book the right one for you.

## Our top picks for 2026

### Currawong Farm Stay (Huon Valley)

This is a working apple and berry farm with 200 acres, run by the same family for three generations. You stay in a renovated 1920s farmhouse with three bedrooms, a full kitchen, and a wood stove. The farm has 50 free-range hens, 20 sheep, and a small orchard of heritage apple varieties like Gravenstein and Cox's Orange Pippin.

- **Price**: $180-$220 per night for the whole house, sleeps up to 6. Weekly rates available.
- **Activities**: Morning egg collection, sheep feeding, apple pressing in autumn, and berry picking in summer. You can join the farmer for a full day of work for an extra $50 per person.
- **Best time to visit**: March to May for harvest season, or October to November for lambing.
- **What to bring**: Rubber boots, warm layers, and a headlamp for early mornings.

### Meadowbank Farm (Derwent Valley)

A 150-acre mixed farm with cattle, pigs, and a large vegetable garden. The stay is in a converted shearing shed, now a two-bedroom apartment with modern amenities. This is the best choice if you have kids: the owners are used to young helpers and have a dedicated children's activity hour every afternoon.

- **Price**: $150-$190 per night for the apartment, sleeps up to 4. Minimum two nights.
- **Activities**: Feeding pigs, collecting eggs, weeding the garden, and helping with the weekly farmers market prep. You can also book a private cheesemaking workshop for $80 per person.
- **Best time to visit**: November to February for the vegetable garden, or April for the pig weaning.
- **What to bring**: Sun hat, sturdy shoes, and a swimsuit for the nearby river.

### Other notable mentions

- **Highfield Farm Stay** (North West): A dairy farm with a self-contained cottage. $130-$160 per night. You can watch the morning milking, but you cannot join in due to health regulations.
- **Wattle Grove Farm** (East Coast): A sheep and lavender farm. $200-$250 per night for a luxury yurt. More about relaxation than work, but you can help with the lavender harvest in January.

## How to book and what to expect

Most farm stays are booked directly through their own websites or through platforms like Stayz and Airbnb. For 2026, book at least 3 to 4 months ahead for the summer season (December to February). For autumn and spring, 1 to 2 months is usually enough. Always read the house rules carefully: some farms do not allow pets, and most ask you to be quiet after 9 PM because the animals are sleeping.

When you arrive, expect a short orientation tour. You will be shown the animal pens, the garden, and the tools you are allowed to use. Ask about safety: which fences are electric, which animals are friendly, and what to do if you get lost. The farmer will appreciate that you ask, and it keeps everyone safe.

## What to bring and wear

- **Clothing**: Layers are essential. Tasmania weather changes fast. A waterproof jacket and sturdy boots are non-negotiable.
- **Work gloves**: A pair of leather gloves, about $15 at any hardware store, will save your hands.
- **Sunscreen and insect repellent**: Even in the south, the UV is strong. Ticks are present in some areas, so check yourself after walks.
- **Food and drinks**: Most farm stays have a kitchen, but groceries are not always nearby. Bring your own supplies for the first night.

## Cost breakdown for a 3-night stay

For two people at Currawong, expect to pay:

- **Accommodation**: $540 (3 nights at $180)
- **Farm activity fee**: $100 (2 days of full participation)
- **Groceries**: $120 (if you cook all meals)
- **Total**: $760, or about $127 per person per day. That is comparable to a mid-range hotel, but you get real farm experience and meals you cook yourself.

## FAQ

### Can I bring my children to a farm stay?

Yes, most farm stays welcome children, but check the minimum age. Meadowbank is especially family-friendly, with a dedicated activity hour. Children under 5 are often free, but they must be supervised around animals at all times.

### Do I need to be physically fit?

No, most activities are light: feeding, collecting, weeding. You can opt out of heavier tasks like lifting hay bales. Tell the farmer your limits, and they will adjust.

### What if it rains?

Farm work continues in the rain, but you can choose to stay inside. Most farmhouses have books, board games, and a fireplace. The farmer will not mind if you skip a day.

### Are farm stays pet-friendly?

Rarely. Working farms have dogs and livestock, and a strange pet can cause problems. If you must bring your dog, ask in advance. Some farms have a separate kennel, but do not assume.

## The bottom line

The best farm stay in Tasmania is Currawong Farm Stay for the most authentic experience, and Meadowbank Farm if you have kids. Book early for summer, bring the right gear, and be ready to get your hands dirty. You will leave with new skills, a tired body, and a deep respect for the people who grow your food. That is worth every dollar.
