If you've grown kohlrabi, you know it's a crisp, mild brassica that tastes like a cross between broccoli stems and cabbage. The best way to cook it is roasting: it caramelizes beautifully, turning sweet and tender. But raw kohlrabi is also excellent, and sautéing works well for quick meals. Here's how to handle each method so you get the most out of your harvest.

## Why roasting wins

Roasting kohlrabi at 425°F (220°C) brings out its natural sugars and gives it a golden, nutty flavor. Unlike boiling, which makes it watery and bland, roasting concentrates the taste and gives a pleasa

nt texture: crisp outside, tender inside. It's also hands-off: you just toss and roast.

## How to roast kohlrabi

1. **Prep**: Cut off the leaves and the root end. Peel the bulb with a vegetable peeler or paring knife. The skin is tough, so don't skip this. Cut into 1-inch cubes or wedges, about 1/2 to 3/4 inch thick for even cooking.
2. **Season**: In a bowl, toss with olive oil (about 1 tablespoon per pound), salt, and pepper. Add optional garlic powder, paprika, or thyme. For a sweet twist, drizzle with maple syrup or honey.
3. **Roast**: Spread in a single layer on a baking sheet. Roast at 425°F for 20 to 25 minutes, flipping halfway, until browned and fork-tender. If you're roasting other vegetables like carrots or potatoes, add them at the same time; they cook similarly.
4. **Finish**: Squeeze lemon juice over the top and sprinkle with fresh parsley or chives. Serve hot.

**Yield**: One pound of kohlrabi (about 2 medium bulbs) serves 2 to 3 as a side dish.

## Sautéing: the quick alternative

Sautéing is faster and works well for stir-fries or as a side. Peel and cut into thin strips or matchsticks. Heat a skillet over medium-high with 1 tablespoon butter or oil. Add kohlrabi and cook, stirring often, for 5 to 7 minutes until crisp-tender. Season with salt, pepper, and a splash of soy sauce or vinegar. This method keeps more crunch than roasting.

## Raw kohlrabi: no cooking needed

Raw kohlrabi is crunchy and juicy, perfect for salads or snacking. Peel and slice into rounds, sticks, or shred. Toss with a vinaigrette, or serve with hummus. It pairs well with apples and carrots. For a simple slaw: shred kohlrabi and carrot, mix with yogurt or mayo, lemon juice, and dill.

## Which varieties cook best?

All kohlrabi varieties cook similarly, but some are sweeter and more tender raw. For cooking, any variety works. For raw eating, try 'Kossak' (large, mild) or 'Kolibri' (purple, sweet). 'Early White Vienna' is a classic, quick-maturing variety. If you grow your own, harvest when bulbs are 2 to 3 inches across; larger ones can get woody.

## Storage and prep tips

- **Storage**: Keep kohlrabi in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. Remove leaves before storing; they draw moisture from the bulb.
- **Peeling**: Always peel, even if the skin looks thin. The skin is fibrous and unpleasant to eat.
- **Leaves**: Don't toss them: they're edible and can be cooked like kale or spinach. Sauté with garlic and olive oil.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Overcooking**: Kohlrabi turns mushy if boiled too long. Roast or sauté for best texture.
- **Skipping the peel**: The skin is tough and bitter. Always peel.
- **Cutting too large**: Large pieces take longer to cook and may burn outside before inside. Keep cubes around 1 inch.

## FAQ

**Can you eat kohlrabi skin?** Technically yes, but it's tough and fibrous. For best texture, peel it.

**Do you need to cook kohlrabi?** No, it's delicious raw. Slice it thin for salads or eat with dip.

**How do you know when kohlrabi is done roasting?** It should be easily pierced with a fork and have browned edges. Start checking at 20 minutes.

**Can you freeze cooked kohlrabi?** Yes, but it will be softer after thawing. Roast or sauté first, then freeze in airtight containers for up to 3 months.

## The bottom line

Roasting is the best way to cook kohlrabi: it's simple, brings out sweetness, and works with many seasonings. For a quick meal, sauté it; for a fresh crunch, eat it raw. Always peel, cut evenly, and don't overcook. With these methods, you'll enjoy your kohlrabi from garden to table without waste.
