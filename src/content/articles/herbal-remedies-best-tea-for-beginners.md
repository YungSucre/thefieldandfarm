If you're new to growing your own tea, start with mint, chamomile, and lemon balm. These three are forgiving, fast-growing, and make delicious herbal teas with minimal fuss. You can plant them this week in most zones, and harvest within a month. Here's exactly how to get started.

## Planting and spacing

**Mint** (Mentha spp.): Plant in spring after last frost, or indoors 6-8 weeks before. Space 18-24 inches apart. Mint spreads aggressively, so grow it in a container or buried pot to keep it in bounds. Full sun to part shade.

**Chamomile** (Matricaria recutita, German): Sow seeds directly in early spring, as soon as soil can be worked. Scatter seeds on the surface, press lightly, and keep moist. Thin to 6 inches apart. Full sun.

**Lemon balm** (Melissa officinalis): Plant in spring, space 12-18 inches apart. It's a hardy perennial that also self-seeds. Full sun to part shade.

## Harvesting and drying

Harvest mint and lemon balm just before flowering for the best flavor. Cut stems in the morning after dew dries. For chamomile, pick the flowers when they're fully open, usually mid-summer.

Dry herbs in a cool, dark, dry place. Bundle stems and hang upside down, or spread flowers on a screen. A food dehydrator on low (95°F) works too. Drying takes 1-2 weeks. Store in airtight glass jars away from light. Properly dried herbs keep for a year.

## Brewing your first cup

Use 1 tablespoon of dried herb (or 2 tablespoons fresh) per 8 ounces of boiling water. Steep 5-10 minutes, covered, to keep in the volatile oils. Strain and enjoy. For a blend, try equal parts mint and lemon balm with a few chamomile flowers.

## Varieties to try

- Mint: 'Spearmint' (Mentha spicata) is classic, 'Peppermint' (Mentha × piperita) is stronger. Both are easy.
- Chamomile: German chamomile is the standard for tea. 'Bodegold' is a high-yield variety.
- Lemon balm: Any variety works; 'Lemonella' has a stronger lemon scent.

## Costs and yields

Starter plants cost $3-5 each at garden centers. Seeds are cheaper: $2-4 per packet. A 4x4 foot bed can yield 1-2 pounds of dried herbs per season, enough for 100-200 cups of tea. Expect to spend $20-40 total for a small tea garden.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Mint taking over**: Always use a container or buried pot. If it escapes, dig out runners regularly.
- **Chamomile not germinating**: Seeds need light to germinate, so don't cover them with soil. Keep the area moist.
- **Bitter tea**: Over-steeping causes bitterness. Stick to 5-10 minutes.
- **Mold during drying**: Ensure good air circulation and dry in small bundles.

## FAQ

**Can I grow tea indoors?** Yes, mint and lemon balm do well on a sunny windowsill. Use a pot with drainage and a quality potting mix. Chamomile is trickier indoors due to light needs, but a grow light can work.

**When is the best time to harvest chamomile?** Pick flowers when they're fully open, usually in the morning. Harvest every few days to encourage more blooms.

**How do I store dried tea?** Keep in airtight glass jars in a cool, dark place. Label with the herb name and date. Use within a year for best flavor.

**Can I mix these herbs with other teas?** Absolutely. They blend well with black or green tea. For a calming evening blend, try chamomile and lemon balm.

Start with mint, chamomile, and lemon balm. They're easy, productive, and make great tea. Plant them this week, harvest in a month, and dry for year-round enjoyment. With minimal effort, you'll have a rewarding tea garden that keeps giving.
