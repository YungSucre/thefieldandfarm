If you're out of fresh garlic and reaching for the powder, here's the direct answer: 3 average cloves of garlic equal about 1.5 teaspoons of garlic powder. That's the standard conversion, but let's break down why it varies and how to adjust for your recipe.

![How much garlic powder equals 3 cloves](/images/articles/how-much-garlic-powder-equals-3-cloves/section-1.jpg)

## Why the conversion isn't exact

Garlic cloves vary in size, and garlic powder varies in grind and density. A medium clove from a typical supermarket bulb yields about 1/2 teaspoon of minced garlic, which dehydrates to roughly 1/4 teaspoon of powder. So 3 cloves give you about 3/4 teaspoon of powder if you're matching the dried weight. But most cooks use a more generous ratio: 1/2 teaspoon of powder per clove, because powder is less pungent than fresh. That gives you 1.5 teaspoons for 3 cloves, which is the most common recommendation.

If you're using granulated garlic (coarser than powder), use a bit more: about 2 teaspoons for 3 cloves, since granules are less dense. Conversely, if you're using garlic salt, reduce the salt in your recipe accordingly: 1/2 teaspoon of garlic salt equals about 1/8 teaspoon of garlic powder, so

## Measuring correctly

Use a standard measuring spoon, not a kitchen spoon. Level the powder with a knife for accuracy. If your recipe calls for 3 cloves and you're using powder, start with 1.5 teaspoons, then taste and adjust. Powder is more concentrated in some brands, so always taste before adding more.

## When to use powder vs. fresh

Powder works best in dry rubs, marinades, and soups where it can rehydrate. It won't give you the same bite as fresh, so for raw applications like salsa or bruschetta, stick with fresh or use powder sparingly and add a pinch of salt to mimic the sharpness. For cooking, powder is a fine substitute, but add it early so it can bloom in the oil or liquid.

If you're preserving your own garlic, you can make powder at home. Slice peeled cloves thinly, dry them in a dehydrator at 115°F for 8-12 hours until brittle, then grind in a spice grinder. Store in an airtight jar in a cool, dark place. It'll keep for up to a year, but flavor fades after 6 months.

## Realistic yields and costs

A typical garlic bulb has 10-12 cloves. If you buy a pound of garlic (about 8-10 bulbs), you'll get roughly 100 cloves. Dried and ground, that yields about 1/2 cup of powder, which would cost you $8-12 if you bought it at the store. Making your own saves money only if you're already growing garlic, which is a 6-month investment. For most, buying powder is cheaper: a 3-ounce jar of organic garlic powder runs $4-6, and that's equivalent to about 90 cloves.

## Adjusting in recipes

When converting a recipe that calls for fresh garlic, remember that powder is about 1/3 the strength of fresh by weight. So if a recipe calls for 3 cloves and you want a milder flavor, use 1 teaspoon. If you want it bold, use 2 teaspoons. Always err on the side of less, because you can add more, but you can't take it out.

For a practical example, in a tomato sauce that calls for 3 cloves, use 1.5 teaspoons of powder and let it simmer for at least 15 minutes to rehydrate and meld. In a salad dressing, dissolve the powder in the vinegar or lemon juice first to avoid clumps.

## FAQ

**Can I substitute garlic powder for minced garlic in a jar?**
Yes. 1/2 teaspoon of powder equals about 1 teaspoon of jarred minced garlic. So for 3 cloves (about 1.5 tablespoons of minced), use 1.5 teaspoons of powder.

**How much garlic powder equals one clove of elephant garlic?**
Elephant garlic is milder and larger. One clove can be 2-3 times the size of a regular clove, so use 1 to 1.5 teaspoons of powder for one elephant clove.

**Does garlic powder go bad?**
It doesn't spoil, but it loses potency after a year. Store it in a sealed container away from heat and light. If it smells musty or has clumps, it's past its prime.

**Is garlic powder the same as garlic salt?**
No. Garlic salt is a mixture of garlic powder and salt, usually 1 part garlic to 3 parts salt. If you use garlic salt, cut the salt in your recipe by the amount of garlic salt you add.

## The bottom line

For most cooking, 3 cloves of garlic equal 1.5 teaspoons of garlic powder. Measure it level, taste as you go, and remember that powder is a convenience, not a perfect match for fresh. If you're growing your own garlic, consider making your own powder for long-term storage, but for everyday use, a good store-bought powder is reliable and affordable. Keep a jar in your pantry and you'll never be stuck without garlic again.
