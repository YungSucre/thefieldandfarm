If you're deciding between garlic and beets for blood pressure, both have solid evidence, but they work differently. Garlic helps relax blood vessels over time, while beets provide a quicker boost through nitrates. For a garden that supports heart health, grow both, but if you must pick one, beets offer more immediate and measurable effects on blood pressure. Here's what the research says and how to grow and use each.

## How garlic affects blood pressure

Garlic contains allicin, a compound that helps relax blood vessels and improve circulation. Studies show that aged garlic extract can lower systolic blood pressure by about 8-10 mmHg in people with hypertension, similar to some prescription medications. The effect builds over weeks, so it's a long-term strategy, not a quick fix.

## How beets affect blood pressure

Beets are rich in dietary nitrates, which your body converts to nitric oxide. This relaxes and widens blood vessels, lowering blood pressure within hours. A 2015 study found that drinking 250 ml of beet juice reduced systolic blood pressure by about 4-5 mmHg within 3-6 hours. The effect is temporary, lasting up to a day, so you need to consume beets regularly for ongoing benefits.

Beet juice is the most concentrated source, but roasted beets or pickled beets also work. In 2026, a bunch of fresh beets costs $2-$4, and a 32 oz bottle of beet juice runs $5-$8.

## Growing garlic for health

Garlic is easy to grow in most climates. Plant cloves in fall, 4-6 weeks before the ground freezes, in USDA zones 3-8. In zones 9-10, plant in late winter. Choose hardneck varieties like 'Music' or 'German Extra Hardy' for cold climates, or softneck 'California Early' for milder areas.

Plant cloves 2 inches deep, 6 inches apart, in rows 12 inches apart. Mulch with straw to protect over winter. Harvest when the lower leaves turn brown, usually in mid-summer. Cure for 2-3 weeks in a dry, shaded spot. A pound of seed garlic costs $10-$15 and yields 8-10 pounds.

## Growing beets for health

Beets prefer cool weather. Sow seeds directly in the garden 2-4 weeks before the last frost, or in late summer for a fall crop. Plant seeds 1/2 inch deep, 1 inch apart, then thin to 3-4 inches apart. Rows should be 12-18 inches apart. Beets tolerate light frost, so you can extend the season.

Harvest when roots are 1.5-2 inches in diameter, usually 55-70 days after planting. 'Detroit Dark Red' is a reliable classic, while 'Chioggia' has striking rings. A packet of seeds costs $2-$4 and yields dozens of beets.

## How to use them for blood pressure

Here are practical ways to incorporate both into your diet:

- **Garlic**: Crush 1-2 cloves and add to salad dressings, stir-fries, or roasted vegetables. Let it sit 10 minutes before cooking. If you dislike the taste, try aged garlic extract capsules.
- **Beets**: Roast a batch of beets on Sunday and add to salads or grain bowls during the week. Or make a quick beet juice: blend 1 medium beet with apple and ginger, strain, and drink within 30 minutes.
- **Combination**: Make a beet and garlic dip by blending roasted beets with yogurt, garlic, and lemon. This gives you both benefits in one snack.

## Other lifestyle factors

Remember, no food is a magic bullet. Blood pressure is influenced by sodium intake, stress, exercise, and weight. Use garlic and beets as part of a heart-healthy diet that includes plenty of vegetables, whole grains, and limited processed foods. If you have high blood pressure, talk to your doctor before making major dietary changes, especially if you take medication.

## FAQ

**Can I take garlic supplements instead of eating fresh garlic?**
Yes, aged garlic extract is well-studied and effective. It's odorless and standardized for allicin content. Follow the label dosage, typically 600-1200 mg per day.

**How much beet juice should I drink daily?**
About 250 ml (8 oz) of beet juice is the amount used in studies. You can also eat 1-2 medium beets daily. Start with a smaller amount if you're new to beets to avoid digestive upset.

**Are there any side effects?**
Garlic can thin the blood, so consult your doctor if you take blood thinners. Beets may turn urine or stool pink, which is harmless. They also contain oxalates, so if you're prone to kidney stones, moderate your intake.

**Can I grow these in containers?**
Garlic needs deep soil, so use a container at least 10 inches deep. Beets can grow in 8-inch pots, but they'll be smaller. Both need full sun (6+ hours) and consistent moisture.

## The bottom line

Both garlic and beets have real benefits for blood pressure, but they work differently. Beets give a quick, temporary drop, while garlic provides a gradual, sustained effect. For the best results, grow both in your garden and use them regularly. Plant garlic in fall and beets in spring, and you'll have a year-round supply of heart-healthy produce. As with any dietary change, combine them with a healthy lifestyle and medical guidance for optimal blood pressure control.
