Scallions, also known as green onions or bunching onions, are easy to grow and harvest in as little as 60 days. They are simply onions harvested before the bulb forms, or they can be a specific bunching onion variety that never forms a large bulb. You can grow them from seed, from sets (small bulbs), or even from the rooted ends of store-bought scallions. Here is the direct path to a steady supply.

## Choose your method: seed, sets, or regrowth

**From seed:** This is the cheapest and offers the most variety. Sow seeds directly in the garden as soon as the soil can be worked in spring, or start indoors 6 to 8 weeks before your last frost. For a fall crop, sow in late summer. Seeds cost $2 to $4 per packet, and a packet plants a 10-foot row.

**From sets:** Sets are small, immature onion bulbs. They are faster than seeds, but you are limited to the varieties available at garden centers. A pound of sets costs $8 to $12 and plants about 50 feet of row. Plant sets 1 inch deep and 2 inches apart.

## When to plant: timing by zone

Scallions are cool-season crops. They tolerate frost and grow best in temperatures between 55°F and 75°F. In most zones, you can plant in early spring and again in late summer for a fall harvest.

| USDA Zone | Spring planting | Fall planting |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------|
| 3-4       | April to May    | July to August|
| 5-6       | March to April  | August to September|
| 7-8       | February to March | September to October|
| 9-10      | January to February or October to December | October to November |

In warm zones (9 and above), scallions can be grown through winter. In cold zones, you can extend the season with a cold frame or row cover.

## Soil, spacing, and planting steps

Scallions need well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. They are light feeders, but they appreciate compost worked into the top 6 inches. Do not add fresh manure; it can cause rotting.

**Spacing:** Sow seeds 1/4 inch deep and 1 inch apart in rows 12 inches apart. Thin to 2 inches apart when seedlings are 2 inches tall. If you want thicker stalks, thin to 3 inches. For sets, plant 1 inch deep and 2 inches apart.

**Steps for direct sowing:**
1. Rake the soil smooth and remove rocks.
2. Make a shallow furrow with a hoe or your finger, about 1/4 inch deep.
3. Sprinkle seeds thinly along the furrow, about 1 seed per inch.
4. Cover with soil and firm gently.
5. Water with a fine spray to avoid washing seeds away.
6. Keep the soil consistently moist until germination, which takes 7 to 14 days.

## Care: water, mulch, and weeds

Scallions have shallow roots, so they need consistent moisture. Water 1 inch per week, more in hot weather. Mulch with straw or grass clippings to retain moisture and suppress weeds. Weeds compete heavily, so hand-pull them weekly.

Fertilize once, three weeks after planting, with a balanced organic fertilizer like 10-10-10 at a rate of 1 pound per 50 square feet. Do not overdo nitrogen; it can cause lush tops but weak roots.

## Harvesting: when and how

You can harvest scallions as soon as they are pencil-thick, usually 60 to 90 days from seed, or 30 days from sets. Pull the entire plant, or cut the tops and leave the roots to regrow for a second harvest, though regrowth is weaker.

For a continuous supply, sow a new row every 3 weeks. In mild climates, you can harvest year-round.

**Yield:** A 10-foot row of scallions yields about 40 to 60 plants, roughly 2 to 3 pounds. At market prices of $2 to $3 per bunch, that is $10 to $18 worth of scallions from a $3 seed packet.

## Common problems and fixes

**Bolting (flowering):** Caused by temperature swings or stress. Plant at the right time and keep soil moist. If a plant bolts, pull it; the stalk becomes tough.

**Onion maggots:** Small white larvae that tunnel into the base. Use row covers to prevent egg-laying, and rotate crops yearly.

**Thrips:** Tiny insects that cause silvery streaks. Spray with insecticidal soap or neem oil, but avoid during heat.

**Damping off:** Seedlings rot at soil level. Use sterile potting mix if starting indoors, and avoid overwatering.

## FAQ

**Can I grow scallions indoors?** Yes, in a sunny windowsill or under a grow light. Use a pot at least 6 inches deep, and harvest in 8 to 10 weeks. Indoor yields are smaller, but it works for a kitchen supply.

**How long do scallions last after harvest?** In the fridge, unwashed, in a plastic bag, they last 1 to 2 weeks. Trim the roots and stand them in a glass of water for longer storage.

**Do scallions come back every year?** Some bunching varieties like 'Evergreen White' are perennial in zones 5 and warmer. Most are biennial, but if you leave them in the ground, they may self-seed.

**Can I plant scallions in containers?** Absolutely. Use a pot at least 6 inches deep and 12 inches wide. Space seeds 1 inch apart, and keep the soil moist. A 12-inch pot yields about 20 scallions.

## The bottom line

Scallions are one of the easiest alliums for a home garden. Start with seed or sets in early spring, keep the soil moist, and harvest in about two months. For a continuous crop, sow every few weeks. Choose 'White Lisbon' for a reliable start, or 'Evergreen White' for a perennial patch. With minimal effort, you can have fresh scallions from spring through fall, and even winter in mild zones.
