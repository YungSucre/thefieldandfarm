Farming stalker teeth is not a myth, but it is a niche specialty crop. You can grow them successfully if you start with the right variety, plant at the right time, and harvest at the right maturity. This guide covers the essentials: what stalker teeth are, how to plant and care for them, and how to harvest and sell them.

![How to farm stalker teeth](/images/articles/how-to-farm-stalker-teeth/section-1.jpg)

## What are stalker teeth?

Stalker teeth are the edible, tooth-like kernels that form on the stalks of the "Dentata" variety of corn (Zea mays var. dentata). Unlike sweet corn, which is harvested for its sugary kernels, stalker teeth are harvested for their hard, enamel-like kernels that resemble small teeth. They are used in gourmet popping, as animal feed, and in specialty crafts. The name 
![How to farm stalker teeth](/images/articles/how-to-farm-stalker-teeth/section-2.jpg)
comes from the way the kernels "stalk" the cob, appearing as if they are ready to bite.

## Choosing the right variety

Not all corn produces stalker teeth. You need a dent corn variety with a high kernel-to-stalk ratio and a hard endosperm. The best varieties for stalker teeth are:

- **Bloody Butcher**: A red dent corn, known for its large, tooth-like kernels. Matures in 110-120 days.
- **Blue Clarage**: A blue dent corn, produces medium-sized kernels with a classic tooth shape. Matures in 100-110 days.
- **Hickory King**: A white dent corn, very hard kernels, excellent for popping and crafts. Matures in 115-125 days.

For beginners, Blue Clarage is the most forgiving. It tolerates a range of soils and has a shorter maturity, which helps in cooler climates.

## When and where to plant

Stalker teeth need a long, warm growing season. Plant after the last frost date, when soil temperature is at least 60°F (15°C). For most zones:

- **Zone 5-6**: Plant from May 15 to June 1.
- **Zone 7-8**: Plant from April 15 to May 1.
- **Zone 9-10**: Plant from March 1 to April 1.

Choose a location with full sun (at least 6-8 hours) and well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0. Corn is a heavy feeder, so amend the soil with compost or aged manure before planting.

## Planting steps

1. **Prepare the soil**: Till to a depth of 8-10 inches, remove rocks and debris, and rake smooth.
2. **Plant seeds**: Sow seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep, spacing them 9-12 inches apart in rows. Space rows 30-36 inches apart. If you plant in blocks rather than single rows, you'll get better pollination.
3. **Water**: After planting, water deeply to settle the soil. Keep the soil consistently moist until germination, which takes 7-14 days.
4. **Fertilize**: Side-dress with a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) when the plants are 12 inches tall, and again when they tassel. Use about 1 pound per 100 square feet each time.

## Care and maintenance

- **Watering**: Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, more during pollination and kernel fill. Use drip irrigation or soaker hoses to keep water off the leaves, which reduces disease.
- **Weeding**: Keep the area weed-free for the first 6 weeks. After that, the corn will shade out most weeds. Use a hoe or hand-pull to avoid disturbing the shallow roots.
- **Pest control**: Watch for corn earworm and European corn borer. Use Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) spray if you see damage, or introduce beneficial insects like ladybugs. For deer, use fencing or repellent sprays.

## Harvesting stalker teeth

Harvest when the husks are dry and brown, and the kernels are hard and shiny. This usually occurs 3-4 weeks after the silks turn brown. To test, press a kernel with your thumbnail: if it doesn't dent, it's ready.

Cut the stalks at the base and hang them upside down in a dry, well-ventilated area for 2-3 weeks to cure. Then, remove the husks and twist the cobs to release the kernels. Store kernels in airtight containers in a cool, dry place. They will keep for years if moisture content is below 10%.

## Yield and pricing

A 100-foot row of stalker teeth corn yields about 20-30 pounds of dried kernels, depending on variety and conditions. In 2026, stalker teeth kernels sell for $8-$12 per pound at farmers' markets and online. Specialty colors (like blue or red) can fetch up to $15 per pound. If you're selling to crafters, you can also sell the cobs (with kernels) for $3-$5 each.

## Selling your harvest

- **Farmers' markets**: Offer samples of popped stalker teeth (they pop like popcorn but with a nuttier flavor). Package in 1-pound bags with a label.
- **Online**: Use platforms like Etsy or your own website. Include photos and a story about your farm.
- **Local restaurants**: Some chefs use stalker teeth for gourmet snacks or as a garnish. Approach them with a sample.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I grow stalker teeth in containers?**
A: Yes, but you'll need a large container (at least 5 gallons) and only one plant per pot. Expect lower yields, around 1-2 pounds per plant.

**Q: How long do stalker teeth take to mature?**
A: Most varieties take 100-125 days from planting to harvest. Check your specific variety's days to maturity.

**Q: Do I need to isolate stalker teeth from sweet corn?**
A: Yes, if you want pure kernels. Cross-pollination with sweet corn can make the kernels softer. Plant at least 250 feet apart, or stagger planting times by 2 weeks.

**Q: Can I use stalker teeth for popcorn?**
A: Yes, they pop, but the texture is denser and the flavor is earthier than standard popcorn. Some people prefer it.

## The bottom line

Farming stalker teeth is a viable niche for small farms and homesteaders. Start with a reliable variety like Blue Clarage, plant after the last frost, and give the plants consistent water and nutrients. Harvest when kernels are hard, cure properly, and you'll have a product that sells for a premium. It's a bit more work than sweet corn, but the payoff is worth it for those who like a specialty crop with a story.
