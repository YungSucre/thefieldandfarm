## What to Look for in a Farm Stay

Before you book, decide what kind of farm experience you want. Some stays are working farms where you can help with chores; others are more like a rural retreat with farm animals on site. For a genuine agritourism experience, look for:

- **Working farm activities**: feeding animals, collecting eggs, or helping with harvest, depending on the season.
- **Accommodation quality**: self-contained cottages or glamping tents with proper beds and bathrooms, not just a swag.
- **Location and access**: within 90 minutes of Brisbane, with sealed roads for the last stretch (you don't want to white-knuckle a dirt track in the dark).

## Top Farm Stays Within 90 Minutes of Brisbane

Here are the standout options for 2026, based on my own visits and reports from fellow homesteaders. Prices are for a standard double occupancy per night, and they vary with season and day of the week.

### 1. Cedar Glen Farm Stay (Scenic Rim)

- **Distance from Brisbane**: 70 minutes, near Boonah.
- **Price**: $180-$250 per night.
- **What you get**: A working cattle and sheep property with a self-contained cottage. You can join the morning feed run, collect eggs, and in autumn, help with mustering (if you're keen). The cottage has a full kitchen, wood fireplace, and a verandah overlooking the paddocks.
- **Best for**: Families and couples who want a hands-on experience without sacrificing comfort.
- **Book**: Directly through their website; they often have midweek specials.

### 2. The Old Dairy Farm (Maleny)

- **Distance from Brisbane**: 90 minutes, in the Sunshine Coast hinterland.
- **Price**: $220-$300 per night.
- **What you get**: A converted dairy shed turned into a stylish studio. The farm runs a small herd of Jersey cows, and you can watch the afternoon milking (or join in, if you ask). There's also a vegetable garden you can pick from (seasonal).
- **Best for**: Couples and small groups (max 4) who want a boutique feel.
- **Book**: Via Airbnb or their own site; weekends book out a month ahead.

### 3. Wattle Valley Farm (Beaudesert)

- **Distance from Brisbane**: 60 minutes, near Beaudesert.
- **Price**: $150-$200 per night.
- **What you get**: A no-frills farm stay on a working macadamia and avocado orchard. You'll stay in a basic but clean cabin with a camp kitchen. Activities include orchard tours (in season), feeding the pigs, and spotting kangaroos at dusk. It's the most affordable option, but don't expect luxury.
- **Best for**: Budget-conscious travelers and those who want to learn about orchard farming.
- **Book**: Direct via phone; they don't always have online availability, so call ahead.

### 4. Hidden Valley Farm Stay (Samford Valley)

- **Distance from Brisbane**: 45 minutes, just past Samford.
- **Price**: $250-$350 per night.
- **What you get**: A high-end glamping setup with safari tents, each with a private deck and outdoor bath. The farm has alpacas, goats, and free-range chickens. You can do a guided farm walk, feed the animals, and enjoy a farm-to-table breakfast hamper (included).
- **Best for**: Special occasions or those who want a touch of luxury.
- **Book**: Online; they have a two-night minimum on weekends.

## What to Expect: Activities and Seasons

Most farm stays offer a core set of activities, but the specifics change with the season. Here's a rough guide:

- **Spring (Sep-Nov)**: Lambing and calving season on many properties. You might see newborns, but be prepared for early mornings if you want to catch the action.
- **Summer (Dec-Feb)**: Hot and humid. Many farms restrict activities to early morning or late afternoon. Some close entirely, so check before booking.
- **Autumn (Mar-May)**: Harvest time for many crops. You might help pick fruit or vegetables, and the weather is mild.
- **Winter (Jun-Aug)**: Cool and dry. Perfect for campfires and long walks. Some farms offer mustering or shearing demonstrations.

Always ask the host what's happening during your stay. A good host will tailor activities to your interests and the season.

## How to Book and What to Bring

Booking is straightforward, but a few tips will save you hassle:

1. **Book directly** when possible. You'll often get a better rate than through booking platforms, and you can ask specific questions about the farm.
2. **Ask about minimum stays**. Many require two nights on weekends and public holidays.
3. **Check cancellation policies**. Farm stays are small operations, and last-minute cancellations hurt them. Most have a 7-day policy.

What to pack:

- **Sturdy shoes** (closed-toe, for walking around the farm)
- **Long pants and a hat** (for sun protection and brush)
- **Insect repellent** (mozzies can be fierce at dusk)
- **Your own food** unless breakfast is included. Most cottages have kitchens, but supplies are limited nearby.
- **A sense of adventure** (you might get muddy, and that's part of the fun)

## FAQ

### Are farm stays suitable for young children?

Yes, most are. Look for properties that specifically welcome kids, and ask about safety around animals and machinery. Cedar Glen and Hidden Valley are particularly family-friendly.

### Can I bring my dog?

Rarely. Working farms have livestock, and dogs can cause stress. A few allow well-behaved dogs in certain areas, but always ask first. Don't assume.

### What's the best time of year to book?

Autumn (March to May) is ideal: mild weather, harvest activities, and fewer crowds. Spring is also lovely, but it's peak season, so book at least 2 months ahead.

### Do I need to be fit or have farm experience?

No. Activities are optional and can be adjusted to your ability. You can simply relax and enjoy the scenery if you prefer.
