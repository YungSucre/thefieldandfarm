Corns are small, thickened areas of skin that develop on your feet from repeated friction or pressure. They are not a medical emergency, but they can be painful and annoying. The good news: you can treat most corns at home with simple tools and habits. This guide walks you through the steps that actually work, based on experience and podiatrist recommendations.

## Understand what you are dealing with

A corn is a concentrated callus, often on the top or side of a toe, with a hard center. It forms when your shoe rubs against a bony area. A soft corn can appear between toes, kept moist by sweat. The key is to remove the pressure source and gently exfoliate the thickened skin. Do not cut corns with a razor or scissors; that risks infection, especially if you have diabetes or poor circulation.

## Step 1: Soak and soften the corn

Soak your feet in warm, soapy water for 10 to 15 minutes. This softens the corn, making it easier to file down. Do this once a day, ideally in the evening. Use a mild soap like Dr. Bronner's Pure-Castile Liquid Soap, about $12 for 16 ounces, or any gentle dish soap. Add a cup of Epsom salt if you like, but it is not necessary.

## Step 2: File the corn gently

After soaking, use a pumice stone or a foot file to gently rub the corn in a circular motion. Do not press hard; you are removing dead skin, not carving a sculpture. A good pumice stone costs $5 to $10. A metal foot file, like the Tweezerman Stainless Steel Callus Shaver, runs about $15 and works faster. Limit filing to 2 to 3 minutes per corn. You should see the corn flatten over several days, not disappear in one session.

## Step 3: Apply moisturizer and protect the area

After filing, pat your feet dry and apply a thick moisturizer containing urea or lactic acid. These ingredients help dissolve keratin, the protein that makes corns hard. CeraVe SA Cream (about $18 for 12 ounces) or AmLactin Foot Cream (about $15 for 7 ounces) are reliable choices. Massage the cream into the corn and surrounding skin. Then, cover the corn with a moleskin pad or a gel toe sleeve to cushion it from friction. Dr. Scholl's Corn Cushions, about $6 for a pack of 6, work well.

## Step 4: Change your footwear and socks

This is the most important step. If you keep wearing the same shoes that caused the corn, it will come back. Look for shoes with a wide toe box, so your toes are not squeezed. Brands like New Balance, Brooks, and Keen offer wide options. Avoid high heels and pointed-toe shoes for daily wear. Wear moisture-wicking socks, such as those made from merino wool or synthetic blends, to reduce friction. A good pair costs $10 to $15.

## Step 5: Use over-the-counter corn removers with caution

Salicylic acid pads or drops (like Compound W, about $8) can speed up removal, but they are not for everyone. They work by chemically dissolving the corn. Apply the pad exactly as directed, usually for 48 hours, then soak and file. If you have diabetes, neuropathy, or any skin condition, skip these products and see a podiatrist instead. Overuse can damage healthy skin.

## When to see a doctor

See a podiatrist if the corn is extremely painful, shows signs of infection (redness, warmth, pus), or if you have diabetes or poor circulation. A doctor can safely trim the corn, prescribe a stronger topical treatment, or fit you for custom orthotics. The average cost of a podiatrist visit is $100 to $200 without insurance.

## How to prevent corns from coming back

Prevention is about reducing pressure. Check your shoes for rough seams or tight spots. Use toe spacers if your toes overlap. Keep your toenails trimmed straight across to avoid pressure on adjacent toes. Moisturize your feet daily to keep skin supple. And rotate your shoes so you are not wearing the same pair every day.

## FAQ

**Can I cut a corn off with a razor?**
No. Cutting increases the risk of infection and can lead to serious complications, especially if you have diabetes. Always file, never cut.

**How long does it take for a corn to go away?**
With daily soaking, filing, and pressure relief, a corn usually shrinks noticeably within 1 to 2 weeks. Complete resolution may take a month, depending on its size and how quickly your skin regenerates.

**Are corn pads with salicylic acid safe?**
They are safe for most healthy people when used as directed. Do not use them if you have diabetes, poor circulation, or broken skin. Always follow the package instructions.

**What is the best shoe for preventing corns?**
A shoe with a wide toe box and a low heel, like a running shoe or a walking shoe from New Balance or Brooks. Avoid pointed toes and high heels for daily wear.

## The bottom line

Treating corns is straightforward: soften, file, moisturize, and change your footwear. Consistency is key. Do the soaking and filing routine daily for two weeks, and you will see real improvement. If the corn does not respond or causes severe pain, see a podiatrist. Your feet carry you every day; give them the care they deserve.
