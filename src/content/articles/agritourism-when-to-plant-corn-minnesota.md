In Minnesota, plant corn after the last frost and when soil temperature reaches 50°F at 2 inches deep. For most of the state, that means late April to mid-May, but check your specific USDA zone: southern zones (4b-5a) can start around April 20, central zones (4a) around April 25, and northern zones (3a-3b) from May 5 to May 15. Planting too early risks rot, too late shortens the growing season, so aim for that window.

## Soil temperature and frost dates
Corn seeds need soil at least 50°F to germinate reliably. Colder soil means slow, uneven emergence and higher rot risk. Use a soil thermometer (about $10 at garden centers) and check at 2 inches deep in the morning. Also, note your average last frost date: southern Minnesota around May 5, central around May 10, northern around May 20. Planting a week before the last frost is okay if soil is warm, but be ready to cover if frost threatens.

## Planting dates by zone
Here are realistic windows for 2026, based on typical conditions:

| USDA Zone | Example areas | Safe planting window | Optimal window |
|-----------|---------------|----------------------|----------------|
| 3a-3b | Roseau, International Falls | May 15 to June 1 | May 20 to May 25 |

These dates assume normal spring weather. If the season is cold and wet, delay a week; if warm and dry, you can start at the early end.

## Choosing varieties
Pick varieties with maturity days that fit your zone. In Minnesota, short-season corns (75-85 days) are safest for northern areas, while mid-season (85-95 days) work in the south. Good choices:

- **Early (75-85 days):** 'Early Sunglow' (sweet corn, 63 days), 'Northern Supersweet' (85 days), 'Jubilee' (85 days, reliable).
- **Mid-season (85-95 days):** 'Golden Bantam' (85 days, heirloom), 'Peaches and Cream' (85 days, bicolor), 'Incredible' (90 days, sweet).
- **For silage or field corn:** 'Pioneer P0157' (85 days), 'Dekalb DKC39-37' (85 days).

Check your seed packet for "days to maturity" and add about 10 days for cooler northern springs.

## How to plant
1. **Prepare the soil:** Work the soil to a depth of 6-8 inches, removing rocks and debris. Corn prefers well-drained loam with pH 6.0-6.8. A soil test costs about $15-20 through the University of Minnesota Extension.
2. **Plant seeds:** Sow seeds 1.5 to 2 inches deep, spacing 9-12 inches apart in rows 30-36 inches apart. For a block, plant at least 4 rows to ensure good pollination. If you plant a single long row, you may get poor ears.
3. **Fertilize:** Apply a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at a rate of 2-3 pounds per 100 square feet, or work in compost. Corn is a heavy feeder; side-dress with nitrogen when plants are 12 inches tall and again when tassels appear.
4. **Water:** Corn needs about 1 inch of water per week, more during tasseling and silking. Use a soaker hose or drip irrigation to avoid wetting foliage.
5. **Weed control:** Hoe or mulch to keep weeds down, especially in the first 6 weeks. Corn roots are shallow, so cultivate carefully.

## Care and troubleshooting
- **Pests:** Watch for corn earworm and European corn borer. Use Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) spray if needed, or plant resistant varieties. Row covers can exclude pests early.
- **Diseases:** Common rust and smut can appear. Choose resistant varieties and rotate crops yearly.
- **Pollination:** Corn is wind-pollinated. Plant in blocks, not single rows, to improve kernel set. If weather is calm, gently shake stalks when tassels appear.
- **Harvest:** Sweet corn is ready about 20 days after silks appear. Kernels should be milky when pierced. Pick in the morning and refrigerate immediately for best flavor.

## Costs and yields
For a home garden, a packet of sweet corn seeds costs $3-5 and covers about 50-100 feet. A 100-foot row yields about 10-15 dozen ears. If you're planting an acre for market, expect seed costs of $40-60 per acre for field corn, and yields of 150-200 bushels per acre for grain, or 15-20 tons per acre for silage, depending on conditions.

## FAQ
**Can I plant corn in Minnesota in June?**
Yes, but only if you choose a very short-season variety (75 days or less) and you live in the south. In northern zones, June planting is risky because fall frost may arrive before maturity. For example, in zone 3b, a June 1 planting of 'Early Sunglow' (63 days) might mature by mid-August, but a cool summer could delay it.

**What if I plant too early?**
Seeds may rot in cold, wet soil. If you must plant early, consider using a plastic mulch to warm the soil, or wait until soil hits 50°F. You can also start seeds indoors in peat pots 3 weeks before planting, but transplanting corn is tricky; handle roots gently.

**How do I know when to harvest sweet corn?**
About 20 days after silks appear, peel back the husk and pierce a kernel with your thumbnail. If the liquid is milky, it's ready. If clear, wait a few days; if doughy, it's overripe. Harvest in the morning and use or refrigerate immediately.

**Do I need to plant different varieties for pollination?**
No, corn is self-pollinating, but planting in blocks helps. If you plant two varieties, they may cross-pollinate, affecting flavor and kernel color. To avoid this, separate different varieties by at least 100 feet or plant them so they tassel at different times.

## The bottom line
Plant corn in Minnesota when the soil is 50°F and after the last frost, typically late April to mid-May depending on your zone. Choose short-season varieties for northern areas, plant in blocks, and keep the soil consistently moist. With proper timing and care, you'll have fresh corn by late summer. For specific local advice, contact your county extension office; they have free bulletins on corn growing in Minnesota.
