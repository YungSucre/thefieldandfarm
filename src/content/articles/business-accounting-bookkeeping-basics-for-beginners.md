If you sell eggs, vegetables, or handmade goods from your homestead, you need basic bookkeeping. It is not about being an accountant; it is about knowing what you spend and earn so you can price correctly and file taxes without panic. Start with a simple system this week: track every dollar in and out, and you will thank yourself at tax time.

## What you need to track

At minimum, track these categories:

- Income: sales of produce, eggs, meat, plants, or value-added products.
- Expenses: seeds, feed, tools, fuel, utilities, repairs, packaging, marketing, and labor.
- Assets: equipment and livestock, for depreciation later.
- Liabilities: loans or credit card debt.

Keep receipts and records for every transaction. Use a separate bank account and credit card for your farm to keep things clean.

## Choose a method: spreadsheet or software

You have two practical options:

1. **Spreadsheet**: Use Excel, Google Sheets, or a free template. Good for very small operations (under $10,000 gross). Cost: $0. You can create columns for date, description, category, income, and expense.
2. **Accounting software**: QuickBooks Online, Wave, or Xero. QuickBooks starts at $30 per month (2026 price). Wave is free for basic invoicing and accounting. Good if you have employees or inventory.

For most homesteaders, a spreadsheet is enough. Start there, and upgrade if you grow.

## Set up your chart of accounts

A chart of accounts is a list of categories. Keep it simple:

- Income: Sales, Other Income
- Expenses: Seeds and Plants, Feed, Fertilizer, Supplies, Repairs, Utilities, Fuel, Marketing, Insurance, Professional Fees, Taxes, Depreciation

You can add subcategories later. The goal is to know where money goes.

## Track income and expenses: step by step

Here is a weekly routine, about 30 minutes:

1. **Record transactions**: Enter every sale and purchase from the week. Use your bank statement and receipts.
2. **Categorize**: Assign each transaction to a category from your chart.
3. **Reconcile**: Compare your records to your bank balance to catch errors.
4. **File receipts**: Keep physical or digital copies. Use a scanner or a folder on your computer.

Do this every week, not monthly. It takes less time and prevents backlog.

## Use a simple cash basis method

Most small farms use cash basis accounting: record income when you receive money, and expenses when you pay. This is simpler than accrual and works for most operations under $25 million gross (IRS limit). It matches your bank account, so it is easy to verify.

## Tools and prices (2026)

- Spreadsheet template: free from Google Sheets or Etsy ($5-$10).
- Receipt scanner app: CamScanner (free) or Adobe Scan (free).
- Mileage tracker: MileIQ ($5.99/month) or a simple notebook.
- Accounting software: Wave (free), QuickBooks Simple Start ($30/month).
- Paper ledger: $10-$15 at office supply stores.

You do not need all of these. Start with a spreadsheet and a receipt folder.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Mixing personal and business money: use separate accounts.
- Forgetting small cash sales: record them immediately.
- Losing receipts: take a photo on your phone and store in a folder.
- Not tracking depreciation: for equipment over $2,500, you can deduct over time.
- Waiting until April: keep up weekly.

## When to hire a professional

If your farm grosses over $50,000, or you have employees, or you are confused about deductions, hire a bookkeeper or CPA. Cost: $200-$500 per month for bookkeeping, or $150-$300 per hour for a CPA. It is worth it to avoid mistakes.

## FAQ

**Do I need a separate bank account?** Yes. It makes bookkeeping and taxes much easier. Open a free business checking account or a separate personal account.

**Can I deduct my tractor?** Yes, if it is used for the farm. You can deduct the full cost under Section 179 (up to $1,220,000 in 2026) or depreciate it over time. Keep records of usage.

**What if I sell at a farmers market for cash?** Record every sale, even small ones. Use a simple sales log or a notebook. At the end of the day, enter the total into your spreadsheet.

**Do I need to pay self-employment tax?** Yes, if your net profit is over $400. You will file Schedule F and Schedule SE. A CPA can help.

## The bottom line

Bookkeeping is not glamorous, but it is essential. Start with a spreadsheet, track weekly, and keep receipts. You will know your true costs, price correctly, and sleep better at tax time. If you stay consistent, you can grow your farm without financial chaos.
