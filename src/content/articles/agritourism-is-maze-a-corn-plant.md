Maze is not a corn plant. A maze is a labyrinth, and a corn maze is a field of corn planted in a pattern that creates paths. The corn itself is usually a field corn variety, not sweet corn. If you're asking because you want to grow a corn maze, you're in the right place. Here's what you need to know to plan, plant, and cut a corn maze for your farm or homestead.

## What is a maze, exactly?

A maze is a puzzle with paths and walls. In a corn maze, the walls are rows of corn. The corn is not a special "maze" variety; it's standard field corn (Zea mays) grown for grain or silage. Field corn grows tall (8 to 10 feet) and stays upright well into fall, making it ideal for cutting paths. Sweet corn, the kind you eat, is shorter and less sturdy, so it's not used for mazes.

## Corn maze basics: what you need to know

Growing a corn maze is a multi-step process. You need a field, a plan, and the right equipment. Here's the timeline for USDA zones 4 to 7, which covers most of the Midwest and Northeast where corn mazes are popular.

### 1. Choose the right corn variety

Select a tall, sturdy field corn. Good choices include:

- **Pioneer P1197** (110-day maturity, tall, strong stalks)
- **Dekalb DKC62-08** (108-day, excellent standability)
- **Channel 210-94** (112-day, very tall)

These are common in the Midwest. For shorter seasons (zones 3 to 4), use a 95 to 100-day variety like **Dekalb DKC45-64**. Check with your local seed dealer for what works in your area.

### 2. Plan your maze design

You can design your own or buy a template. Simple designs (a pumpkin, a star) are easier to cut. Complex designs (logos, detailed scenes) require GPS-guided cutting. For a first maze, start with a simple shape.

Use graph paper or a computer program to map out paths. Paths should be at least 3 feet wide for walking. Wall thickness: 2 to 4 rows of corn, about 30 to 40 inches apart.

### 3. Prepare the field

- **Soil test**: Corn grows best in soil with pH 6.0 to 6.8. Test in fall before planting.
- **Fertilizer**: Apply nitrogen at 150 to 200 pounds per acre, split between pre-plant and side-dress. Phosphorus and potassium based on soil test.
- **Tillage**: Conventional tillage (plow, disk) or no-till. No-till is cheaper and reduces erosion.

### 4. Plant the corn

Plant after the last frost, when soil temperature is 50°F or higher. In zone 5, that's typically late April to mid-May.

- **Seeding rate**: 28,000 to 32,000 seeds per acre. For a maze, you want thick walls, so use the higher rate.
- **Row spacing**: 30 inches is standard. For mazes, you can plant in 15-inch rows for denser walls, but that requires a different planter.
- **Depth**: 1.5 to 2 inches.

### 5. Cut the maze

Wait until the corn is at least 4 feet tall, usually 6 to 8 weeks after planting. Cutting too early means the corn may not recover. Cutting too late (after tasseling) makes it harder to see the pattern.

**Tools**: You can cut with a string trimmer (weed eater) or a riding mower. For large mazes, use a tractor with a mower or a specialized corn maze cutter. A simple method: use a string trimmer with a heavy-duty line. For straight lines, use flags or GPS.

**Step-by-step cutting**:

1. Mark the perimeter of the maze with flags.
2. Cut the main paths first, using a map and compass or GPS.
3. Cut secondary paths, checking your map frequently.
4. Clear all cut stalks from paths so visitors can walk easily.

### 6. Maintenance and opening

- **Water**: Corn needs 1 to 1.5 inches of water per week. If dry, irrigate.
- **Weeds**: Control with pre-emergent herbicides at planting, and cultivate between rows.
- **Pests**: Watch for corn earworm and fall armyworm. Spray if needed.
- **Open for visitors**: Typically mid-September to late October. Charge admission: $8 to $15 per person, depending on your area.

## FAQ

### Can I use sweet corn for a maze?

Technically yes, but sweet corn is shorter (5 to 6 feet) and less sturdy. It may fall over in wind, and the maze won't be as tall. Field corn is better.

### How long does it take for the corn to be tall enough to cut?

About 6 to 8 weeks after planting, when it reaches 4 to 5 feet. By late August, it's usually 8 feet tall.

### Do I need a special permit to open a corn maze to the public?

Check local zoning and business regulations. You'll need liability insurance, and possibly a business license. Contact your county extension office for guidance.

### Can I make a maze without cutting? Like planting in a pattern?

Yes, you can plant in a pattern by skipping rows, but it's harder to control weeds, and the maze is less defined. Cutting is the standard method.

## The bottom line

Maze is not a corn plant; it's a puzzle. But a corn maze is made from field corn, and you can grow one with planning and effort. Start small, choose a simple design, and budget for seed, fertilizer, and insurance. With good marketing, a corn maze can be a profitable agritourism venture. For your first year, focus on learning the process, not on perfection. You'll make mistakes, but that's how you learn. Now, get your soil test done and order seed for next spring.
