## What makes a corn maze great?

A great corn maze is more than just a field with paths. Look for these features:

- **Size and complexity:** Larger mazes (5 to 10 acres) with multiple checkpoints and bridges offer a real challenge. Smaller mazes (1 to 2 acres) are better for young children or quick visits.
- **Theme and design:** The best mazes have a unique shape or story, like a historical figure, a local landmark, or a seasonal motif. Check the farm's website for the 2026 theme before you go.
- **Extras:** Many farms add corn cannons, hayrides, pumpkin patches, and food stands. These turn a maze visit into a full day out.
- **Accessibility:** Some mazes have wheelchair-friendly paths or stroller-friendly routes. Call ahead if you need these.

## How to choose the right maze for your group

- **For a serious challenge:** Pick Connors Farm or Hanson's Farm. These mazes are big, with multiple paths and dead ends. Bring a map or use the farm's app if they have one. Expect to spend 1.5 to 2 hours inside.
- **For young kids (under 6):** Choose a smaller maze like Parlee Farms or Davis Farmland. They have shorter paths and often include activity stations. Kids can get tired quickly, so keep it to 30 to 45 minutes.
- **For a romantic fall date:** Wagon Hill Farm offers a quieter, less crowded experience. The 4-acre maze is manageable, and the farm has scenic views. Go on a weekday afternoon.
- **For a group outing:** Hanson's Farm has group rates and a large picnic area. Call ahead to book a block of tickets.

## What to know before you go

- **Buy tickets online:** Most farms sell timed entry tickets online. This avoids long lines and guarantees entry, especially on weekends. Prices are often $2 to $3 cheaper online.
- **Wear the right shoes:** Corn mazes are dusty and uneven. Wear closed-toe shoes, not sandals. Bring water, especially on warm days.
- **Use the map:** Farms provide paper maps or QR codes for a digital map. If you get lost, stay calm. Most mazes have emergency exits or staff nearby.
- **Check the weather:** Rain can make paths muddy. A light drizzle is fine, but heavy rain may close the maze. Call the farm if in doubt.
- **Bring cash for extras:** While most farms take cards, some smaller stands are cash-only. Have $20 to $40 on hand for snacks, pumpkins, or a corn cannon.

## Realistic costs and time

A visit to a corn maze typically costs $10 to $25 per person, depending on the farm and age. A family of four can expect to spend $40 to $80 on admission alone. Add $10 to $20 for a pumpkin or snacks. Plan for 2 to 3 hours total, including the maze and other activities.

## FAQ

**Q: When is the best time to visit a corn maze in Massachusetts?**
A: Late September to late October is peak season. Weekday mornings are the least crowded. If you want the maze fully grown and not yet trampled, go in the first two weeks of October.

**Q: Are corn mazes wheelchair accessible?**
A: Some are, but not all. Call the farm ahead to ask about accessible paths. Connors Farm and Davis Farmland have stroller-friendly routes, but they may not be fully wheelchair accessible.

**Q: Can I bring my dog?**
A: Most farms do not allow pets, except service animals. Check the farm's policy before you go. Some farms have a designated pet day.

**Q: What happens if it rains?**
A: Light rain is usually fine, but heavy rain can make the maze unsafe. Farms may close or offer rain checks. Check the farm's social media or call before you leave.

A corn maze is a classic fall activity, and with a little planning, you will have a memorable day without the stress.
