Leeks take 10 to 20 minutes to boil, depending on how you cut them and their size. Whole leeks need 15 to 20 minutes, while sliced or halved leeks are done in 8 to 12 minutes. The key is to check with a knife: it should slide in with little resistance, but the leek should still hold its shape. Overcooking turns them to mush, so start checking at the lower end of the range.

## Why boiling time varies

Leeks are alliums, related to onions, but they are milder and more delicate. Their layers hold moisture, so they cook faster than onions. The main variables are:

- **Size**: Thick leeks (1.5 inches across) take longer than thin ones (0.5 inch).
- **Cut**: Whole leeks trap heat in the center, so they need more time. Sliced or halved leeks expose more surface area, so they cook faster.
- **Age**: Fresh, young leeks from your garden are more tender and cook quicker than store-bought ones that may be older and fibrous.
- **Altitude**:

 At high altitudes (above 3,000 feet), water boils at a lower temperature, so add 2 to 3 minutes.

## How to boil leeks perfectly

### Step 1: Clean them thoroughly

Leeks grow in sandy soil, and grit hides between the layers. Slice off the dark green tops (save them for stock) and the root end. Cut the leek in half lengthwise, then rinse under running water, fanning the layers to remove all dirt. If you slice them first, put the slices in a bowl of cold water, swish, and lift out with a slotted spoon.

### Step 2: Cut for your recipe

- **Whole**: For a side dish or to serve cold with vinaigrette, keep the leek whole (after halving lengthwise, tie with kitchen twine to keep layers together).
- **Halved**: For grilling or braising, cut lengthwise into halves.
- **Sliced**: For soups, stews, or sautés, slice crosswise into 1/4-inch rings or half-moons.

### Step 3: Boil in salted water

Fill a pot with enough water to cover the leeks by an inch. Add 1 teaspoon of salt per quart of water. Bring to a rolling boil, then add the leeks. Reduce to a gentle simmer, not a hard boil, to prevent them from breaking apart.

### Step 4: Check for doneness

Start testing at the minimum time:

- **Sliced**: 8 minutes
- **Halved**: 10 minutes
- **Whole**: 15 minutes

Pierce the thickest part with a paring knife. It should go in with slight resistance, like a ripe carrot. If you want them softer for purees, go 2 to 3 minutes longer. Drain immediately and shock in ice water if you are using them cold, or toss with butter and herbs.

## Boiling times by cut and size

| Cut | Thin leek (under 1 inch) | Medium (1 to 1.5 inches) | Thick (over 1.5 inches) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sliced (1/4-inch rings) | 5 to 7 minutes | 8 to 10 minutes | 10 to 12 minutes |
| Halved lengthwise | 8 to 10 minutes | 10 to 12 minutes | 12 to 15 minutes |
| Whole | 12 to 15 minutes | 15 to 18 minutes | 18 to 20 minutes |

These times assume you start with boiling water and simmer gently. If you add leeks to cold water and bring to a boil, add 3 to 4 minutes.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Not cleaning well**: Grit ruins a dish. Always wash after cutting, not before, because cutting opens the layers.
- **Overcrowding the pot**: If you pile in too many leeks, the water temperature drops and they cook unevenly. Use a pot wide enough so they fit in a single layer, or cook in batches.
- **Boiling too hard**: A vigorous boil makes the outer layers split and turn mushy while the center stays raw. Keep it at a gentle simmer.
- **Skipping the salt**: Unsalted water makes leeks taste flat. Salt the water like pasta water.

## Using boiled leeks in recipes

Boiled leeks are versatile. Here are three simple ways to use them this week:

1. **Cold leeks vinaigrette**: Boil whole or halved leeks, drain, and marinate in a mustard vinaigrette. Serve as a salad or side. This is a classic French bistro dish.
2. **Creamed leeks**: Boil sliced leeks, then simmer in cream with a pinch of nutmeg. Great over roasted chicken or mashed potatoes.
3. **Add to soups and stews**: Boiled leeks can be added to potato soup, chicken stew, or a simple broth. They add sweetness and body.

## How to store and reheat

Boiled leeks keep in the fridge for 3 to 4 days in an airtight container. To reheat, warm them gently in a skillet with a little butter or broth, or microwave for 1 to 2 minutes. They do not freeze well after boiling because they become mushy when thawed.

## FAQ

### Can you boil leeks in milk?

Yes, boiling leeks in milk (or half milk, half water) makes them creamier and sweeter. Use the same times as for water, but watch carefully because milk can scorch. This method is common for creamed leek dishes.

### How do you know when leeks are done?

A paring knife should slide into the thickest part with slight resistance, like a ripe carrot. The leek should be tender but not falling apart. Taste a piece: it should be soft and sweet, with no raw crunch.

### Do you need to peel leeks before boiling?

No, you do not peel leeks. Just trim the root and the dark green tops. The outer layer is edible and tender after cooking. If it is tough or damaged, remove it, but otherwise leave it on.

### Can you boil leeks ahead of time?

Yes, you can boil leeks up to 2 days ahead. Drain well, cool, and store in the fridge. Reheat gently or use cold in salads. If you plan to serve them hot, undercook by 1 to 2 minutes so reheating does not make them mushy.

## The bottom line

Boiled leeks take 8 to 20 minutes, depending on how you cut them. For most recipes, sliced leeks in 8 to 10 minutes are your best bet. Always clean them thoroughly, salt the water, and simmer gently. Check early and often, because leeks go from tender to mush quickly. With these times, you will get perfectly cooked leeks every time, whether you are making a simple side or a creamy soup.
