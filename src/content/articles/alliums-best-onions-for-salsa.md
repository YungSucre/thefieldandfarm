If you're growing onions for salsa, you want varieties that are crisp, pungent, and hold their own against tomatoes and chiles. The best choices are white onions like 'White Bermuda' or 'Crystal White Wax', but yellow and red types also work depending on your heat level. Plant them in early spring (zones 3-6) or fall (zones 7-10) for a summer harvest that lines up with tomato season.

## What Makes an Onion Good for Salsa?

Salsa needs onions that add bite without overwhelming. Look for:

- **Crisp texture**: Soft onions turn mushy in salsa. Firm, high-sugar varieties stay crunchy.
- **Pungency**: Mild onions disappear; strong ones dominate. Aim for medium heat.
- **Low water content**: Too much water makes salsa watery. Dry-fleshed onions are better.

White onions are the classic choice because they're sharp and clean. Yellow onions are sweeter and work if you like a milder salsa. Red onions

 add color and a mild bite, but they can bleed into the salsa, turning it pink.

## Planting and Growing Tips

Onions are day-length sensitive. Choose the right type for your zone:

- **Short-day onions** (for zones 7-10): 'White Bermuda', 'Red Burgermaster'. Plant in fall (October-November) for spring harvest.
- **Long-day onions** (for zones 3-6): 'Walla Walla', 'Ailsa Craig'. Plant in early spring (March-April) for summer harvest.
- **Day-neutral onions** (for zones 5-6): 'Candy', 'Super Star'. Plant in early spring.

### Steps to Plant

1. **Start from sets or transplants**: Sets are easier for beginners. Transplants give larger bulbs. Buy from a reputable nursery or online (e.g., Burpee, Johnny's Selected Seeds).
2. **Prepare soil**: Onions need loose, well-drained soil with pH 6.0-6.8. Add compost before planting.
3. **Plant depth**: Set bulbs 1 inch deep, with the tip just below the surface.
4. **Spacing**: Space 4 inches apart in rows 12-18 inches apart. For larger bulbs, space 6 inches.
5. **Water**: Keep soil consistently moist, about 1 inch per week.
6. **Fertilize**: Apply a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at planting, then side-dress with nitrogen when bulbs start to form.

### Harvesting

Onions are ready when the tops flop over and dry. Pull them on a sunny day, let them cure in a warm, dry place for 2-3 weeks, then store in a cool, dry spot. For salsa, use fresh onions within a few weeks; they lose pungency in storage.

## Using Onions in Salsa

For the best flavor, chop onions finely and rinse them in cold water to mellow the bite. This is a trick I use every time. Then mix with tomatoes, chiles, cilantro, lime, and salt.

A simple salsa recipe:

- 4 ripe tomatoes, diced
- 1/2 cup chopped white onion, rinsed
- 1 jalapeño, minced (seeded for less heat)
- 1/4 cup chopped cilantro
- Juice of 1 lime
- Salt to taste

Mix and let sit for 15 minutes. This makes about 3 cups.

## Storage and Preservation

If you have extra onions, you can:

- **Refrigerate**: Fresh chopped onions last 3-5 days in an airtight container.
- **Freeze**: Chop and freeze in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a bag. Use within 6 months.
- **Dehydrate**: Slice and dry at 125°F for 6-8 hours. Store in a jar.

## FAQ

**Can I use red onions in salsa?**
Yes, red onions are great for salsa. They're milder and add color. Just be aware they can tint the salsa pink. 'Red Burgermaster' is a good choice.

**What if I live in a warm climate?**
In zones 8-10, plant short-day varieties in fall. They'll mature in late spring. You can also grow them in winter for a spring harvest.

**How do I make onions milder for salsa?**
Rinse chopped onions in cold water for 30 seconds, then drain. This removes some of the sulfur compounds that cause heat.

**Can I grow onions in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 8 inches deep and 12 inches wide. Plant 3-4 sets per pot. Keep soil moist and fertilize every 2 weeks.

## The Bottom Line

For the best salsa, grow 'White Bermuda' or 'Crystal White Wax' if you're in a warm zone, or 'Ailsa Craig' if you're in a cool zone. Plant at the right time for your area, give them loose soil and consistent water, and harvest when tops flop. Rinse chopped onions to mellow the bite, and use them fresh for peak flavor. With these varieties, your salsa will have the right crunch and kick every time.
