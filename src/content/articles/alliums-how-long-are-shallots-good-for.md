Shallots are good for 1 to 3 months when stored properly in a cool, dark, dry place, and up to 6 months if you cure them correctly after harvest. The exact shelf life depends on how you store them, the variety, and whether they are whole, cut, or cooked. Here is the breakdown you need to keep your shallots fresh and flavorful.

## Storage Life at a Glance

| Storage Method | Shelf Life | Best Conditions |
|----------------|------------|-----------------|
| Pantry (whole, uncut) | 1 to 3 months | 50-65°F, 60-70% humidity, dark, ventilated |
| Refrigerator (whole, uncut) | 1 to 2 months | 35-40°F, in a paper bag or mesh |
| Refrigerator (cut or peeled) | 7 to 10 days | Airtight container |
| Freezer (chopped or whole) | 6 to 8 months | Freezer bag, blanched or raw |
| Dehydrated | 1 to 2 years | Airtight jar, cool, dark |

## How to Store Whole Shallots for Maximum Shelf Life

The key to long-term storage is keeping shallots dry and ventilated. Never store them in plastic bags; they trap moisture and cause rot. Instead, use a mesh bag, a wire basket, or a paper bag with holes. A cool, dark pantry or cellar is ideal. Avoid areas near the stove or dishwasher where heat and humidity fluctuate.

If you buy shallots from a store, they may already be partially cured. For homegrown shallots, curing is essential: after harvest, let them dry in a warm, airy spot for 2 to 3 weeks until the necks are papery and the skins rustle. Then trim the tops to 1 inch and the roots, and store as above.

## How to Tell If Shallots Have Gone Bad

Shallots spoil in three ways: sprouting, softening, and mold. Sprouting is the first sign of age; the shallot is still edible but the flavor turns bitter. Soft spots or a mushy texture indicate rot, and any mold means toss them immediately. A strong, sour smell is another red flag. If only one clove is bad, you can often save the rest, but check the whole bulb for hidden damage.

## Freezing and Dehydrating: Extending Shelf Life

Freezing is the easiest way to keep shallots for months. Peel and chop them, then spread on a baking sheet and freeze for 1 hour before transferring to a freezer bag. This prevents clumping. You can also freeze whole peeled shallots, but they will be softer when thawed, so use them for cooking, not raw.

Dehydrating is another option: slice thinly and dry at 125°F for 6 to 8 hours until brittle. Store in an airtight jar with an oxygen absorber. Rehydrate in water or use directly in soups and stews. Dried shallots lose some pungency, so you may need to use more.

## Best Shallot Varieties for Storage

Some shallots store longer than others. The classic French gray shallot (Allium oschaninii) is prized for flavor but stores only 2 to 3 months. The 'Ambition' variety is a hybrid that stores up to 6 months and resists bolting. 'Conservor' is another long-storing option, with a mild flavor and good disease resistance. For home gardeners in zones 3 to 9, 'Ambition' is a reliable choice for fall planting and spring harvest.

## Common Mistakes That Shorten Shelf Life

- Storing in plastic: traps moisture, causing rot.
- Leaving tops on: they draw moisture from the bulb, speeding spoilage.
- Storing near ripening fruit: ethylene gas from apples and pears triggers sprouting.
- Washing before storage: moisture invites mold. Only wash right before use.
- Storing in the fridge with high humidity: the crisper drawer is too humid for whole shallots; use the main shelf instead.

## FAQ

### Can I store shallots with onions?

Yes, they have similar storage needs. Keep them together in a cool, dry, dark place, but check regularly because one bad onion can spoil the whole batch.

### How long do shallots last in the fridge if they are cut?

Cut or peeled shallots last 7 to 10 days in an airtight container in the fridge. They will lose some flavor over time, so use them within a week for best taste.

### Can I plant sprouted shallots?

Yes, if a shallot has sprouted, you can plant it instead of eating it. Separate the cloves and plant them 6 inches apart, 1 inch deep, in well-drained soil. You will get new shallots in about 90 days.

### What is the best way to store shallots for 6 months?

Cure them properly, then keep them in a mesh bag in a cool (50-60°F), dark, dry place. Check monthly and remove any that show signs of spoilage. For longer storage, freeze or dehydrate.

## The Bottom Line

Shallots are a versatile allium that can last from a week to several months depending on how you handle them. For everyday use, store whole shallots in a cool, dark pantry and use within 1 to 3 months. For long-term storage, cure them well, freeze or dehydrate, and you will have shallots through the winter. Always inspect your stash regularly and remove any that are sprouting or softening to keep the rest fresh.
