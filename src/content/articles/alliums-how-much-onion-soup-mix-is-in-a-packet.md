A standard packet of onion soup mix contains 1 ounce (28 grams) of dry mix, which is about 2 tablespoons. That is the size you will find in most grocery stores, like Lipton's Recipe Secrets Onion Soup Mix. If you are using a generic or store brand, check the label: they are almost always 1 ounce as well, but a few may be 1.5 ounces. Always weigh it if you are scaling a recipe.

The mix is mostly dehydrated onions, salt, cornstarch, and seasonings. When a recipe calls for one packet, it means 2 tablespoons of the dry powder. If you are making your own from scratch, you can replicate the flavor with dried minced onion, onion powder, and a few pantry staples.

## Why the packet size matters

Recipes for dips, pot roasts, or casseroles are built around that 1-ounce packet. If you use a larger or smaller packet, you change the salt and flavor balance. For example, a classic onion dip calls for one packet mixed with 16 ounces of sour cream. If you accidentally use 1.5 ounces, the dip will be too salty and overpowering.

When you grow your own onions and dehydrate them, you will need to know how much dried onion equals that packet. One ounce of commercial mix contains about 1/4 cup of dried minced onion, plus about 1 teaspoon of oni

## How to measure a packet

If you have a packet and need to measure it, here is what to do:

1. Open the packet and pour the contents into a small bowl.
2. Use a spoon to break up any clumps.
3. Measure with a tablespoon: 1 ounce equals 2 level tablespoons.
4. If you need to divide a packet, use a kitchen scale for accuracy. A half packet is 1 tablespoon (14 grams).

For recipes that call for a "packet" but you only have a bulk container of mix, use 2 tablespoons per packet. If the mix is a generic brand, check the nutrition label for serving size: it often says "1/2 packet (about 1 tablespoon)" which confirms the total is 2 tablespoons.

## Homemade substitute for one packet

If you do not want to buy packets, make your own. This mix works in any recipe that calls for one packet. Combine in a small bowl:

- 2 tablespoons dried minced onion
- 1 teaspoon onion powder
- 1 teaspoon salt (use less if you are watching sodium)
- 1/2 teaspoon cornstarch
- 1/2 teaspoon beef bouillon granules (optional, for a deeper flavor)
- 1/4 teaspoon sugar (optional, to balance)

Mix well and store in an airtight jar. This makes the equivalent of one packet. Double or triple the batch and keep it in your pantry for up to 6 months.

If you have home-dehydrated onions, use 2 tablespoons of dried minced onion. You may need to crush them smaller if they are in large pieces. Home-dried onions are often more potent, so start with 1.5 tablespoons and taste.

## Adjusting recipes for fresh onions

If you want to use fresh onions from your garden instead of mix, here is the conversion: one packet of onion soup mix adds about 1/4 cup of dried onion, which rehydrates to roughly 1/2 cup of fresh chopped onion. But the mix also adds salt and seasoning, so you cannot just swap in fresh onion without adjusting.

For a recipe that calls for one packet, use:

- 1/2 cup finely chopped fresh onion, sautéed until soft, plus
- 1/2 teaspoon salt (or to taste), plus
- 1/4 teaspoon onion powder, plus
- 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder (optional)

Sauté the onion in a little oil or butter before adding to the dish. This mimics the flavor of the dehydrated onion and removes the raw bite.

For a pot roast, you can also use 2 packets if you want a stronger flavor, but that doubles the salt. Check the sodium content on the packet: one packet has about 1,800 mg of sodium, so adjust other salt in the recipe.

## Storing leftover mix

If you open a packet and do not use it all, store the leftover in a small airtight container or zip-top bag. It will keep for several months in a cool, dark pantry. Label it with the date and the amount (for example, "1 tablespoon onion soup mix, leftover from 1-oz packet").

Do not refrigerate the dry mix; moisture will cause clumping. If it does clump, you can still use it, just break up the lumps with a fork.

## FAQ

### Can I use onion soup mix past the expiration date?

Yes, if it is dry and has no off smell, it is safe to use. The flavor may fade over time, so add a little extra onion powder if needed. Check for any signs of moisture or mold, and discard if you see any.

### Is onion soup mix gluten-free?

Most brands contain cornstarch, which is gluten-free, but some may contain wheat flour. Check the label. Lipton's is not certified gluten-free, but it does not list wheat as an ingredient. For a gluten-free option, make your own mix with cornstarch.

### How much dried onion is in a packet?

A 1-ounce packet contains about 1/4 cup of dried minced onion, but the total mix includes salt and other seasonings. If you are substituting dried onion, use 2 tablespoons of dried minced onion plus the other seasonings listed above.

### Can I use onion soup mix as a seasoning for vegetables?

Yes, sprinkle 1 teaspoon of mix over roasted vegetables before baking. It adds salt and onion flavor. But remember it is salty, so go easy.

## The bottom line

A standard onion soup mix packet is 1 ounce, or 2 tablespoons. Always measure by weight if you can, because brands vary slightly. For your own garden onions, dehydrate them and make a homemade mix with the recipe above. That way you control the salt and avoid additives. Keep a jar of homemade mix in your pantry, and you will never have to guess again.

When you are cooking, remember that one packet is designed for a specific amount of other ingredients. If you double a recipe, double the mix. If you want less salt, use a homemade mix with reduced salt. And if you are using fresh onions, adjust the liquid in the recipe because fresh onions release water.

Now you know exactly what is in that little envelope, and you can cook with confidence, whether you are using a store-bought packet or your own dried harvest.
