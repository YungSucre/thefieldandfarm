If you are new to canning, the right book can save you from spoiled jars and wasted produce. The best canning books for beginners are clear, safe, and specific. This guide covers the top five books, what each one does well, and how to choose. You can start with one book this week and be canning by the weekend.

## What to Look for in a Beginner Canning Book

A good beginner canning book must follow current USDA guidelines. Safety is not optional. Look for books published after 2015, as recommendations change. The book should cover both water bath and pressure canning, even if you start with water bath. It should have clear step by step instructions, with photos or diagrams for every stage. ## The Top 5 Books, Reviewed

### 1. The All New Ball Blue Book of Preserving

This is the classic, and for good reason. Published by the makers of Ball jars, it is the most trusted reference. The 2024 edition has over 200 recipes, from jams to pickles to meats. It covers both water bath and pressure canning. The instructions are simple and safe. It includes a section on canning basics, equipment, and troubleshooting.

Best for: absolute beginners who want a reliable all purpose guide.

Price: $12 to $18. Available at most hardware stores and online.

### 2. The Complete Book of Home Preserving by Judi Kingry and Lauren Devine

This is a larger book, over 400 pages, with more recipes and detailed instructions. It is published by the same company as the Ball Blue Book, so safety standards are high. It has a chapter on canning basics that is very thorough, including how to choose jars and lids. The recipes are varied, from classic strawberry jam to more adventurous chutneys.

Best for: beginners who want a comprehensive reference and more recipe variety.

Price: $20 to $30. Often available at big box stores.

### 3. Food in Jars by Marisa McClellan

This book focuses on small batch canning. That means you can make a few jars at a time, which is less intimidating and easier for beginners. The recipes are modern and creative, like pickled fennel or bourbon vanilla pear butter. It uses water bath canning mostly, with a few pressure canning recipes. The instructions are friendly and clear.

Best for: beginners who want to start small and try unique flavors.

Price: $18 to $25. Available online and in bookstores.

### 4. The Beginner's Guide to Canning by Diane Devereaux

This is exactly what the title says. It is a short, simple guide with 60 recipes. It covers water bath canning in detail, with step by step photos. It also has a chapter on pressure canning, but it is less thorough. The recipes are basic: jams, pickles, tomatoes. It is a good first book if you are nervous.

Best for: absolute beginners who want the simplest possible introduction.

Price: $14 to $20. Available online.

### 5. Preserving by the Pint by Marisa McClellan

Another book by McClellan, this one is all about small batch canning with even smaller yields. Each recipe makes about 2 to 4 jars. It is perfect for trying new recipes without a huge time commitment. It covers water bath canning only, so you will need another book for pressure canning.

Best for: beginners with limited time or produce.

Price: $15 to $22. Available online.

## How to Choose the Right Book for You

Think about what you want to can. If you have a vegetable garden, you will need pressure canning for low acid foods like green beans and corn. If you mostly make jams and pickles, water bath is enough. The Ball Blue Book covers both, so it is the safest bet. If you want more recipes and are willing to read more, get the Complete Book. If you are short on time, try Food in Jars or Preserving by the Pint.

You can also use online resources like the National Center for Home Food Preservation, but a book is easier to follow in the kitchen.

## Practical Steps to Start This Week

1. Buy one book from the list. The Ball Blue Book is a good default.
2. Read the first two chapters on equipment and basic steps.
3. Gather your equipment: a water bath canner (about $20 to $40), jars (about $10 per dozen), lids, and a jar lifter.
4. Pick a simple recipe, like strawberry jam or pickled cucumbers. Make sure you have all ingredients.
5. Follow the recipe exactly, including processing time and altitude adjustments.
6. Label your jars and store them in a cool, dark place.

## FAQ

### Do I need a pressure canner to start?

No. You can start with water bath canning for high acid foods like fruit, jam, and pickles. You will need a pressure canner for low acid foods like vegetables and meat. Many beginners start with water bath and add a pressure canner later.

### How much does canning equipment cost?

A basic water bath canner costs $20 to $40. A pressure canner costs $70 to $150. Jars cost about $10 per dozen, and lids are about $3 per dozen. You can reuse jars, but not lids. Total start up cost is around $50 to $200 depending on what you need.

### Are online recipes safe?

Some are, but many are not. Stick to recipes from trusted sources like the USDA, Ball, or the books listed here. If a recipe does not include processing times and altitude adjustments, do not use it.

### How long do canned goods last?

Properly canned food is safe for up to a year, but quality is best within 6 to 12 months. Always check seals before storing and discard any jars with bulging lids or off smells.

## The Bottom Line

The best canning book for you depends on your goals. For most beginners, The All New Ball Blue Book is the safest and most complete choice. It covers everything you need to start canning safely. If you want more recipes or a smaller batch approach, try one of the others. Buy one book, read it, and start with a simple recipe. Canning is a skill you build with practice. The first batch is the hardest, but it gets easier. You will save money and enjoy your garden all winter.
