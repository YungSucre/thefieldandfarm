The best garlic for your garden depends on your climate and how you plan to use it. In short: hardneck garlic (like 'Music' or 'German Extra Hardy') thrives in cold zones (3-7) and offers complex flavors, while softneck garlic (like 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red') stores longer and suits mild zones (5-9). Choose based on your USDA zone and whether you want scapes or long storage. Here is how to pick and grow the right one for you.

## Hardneck vs. Softneck: Which Is Better?

Hardneck garlic produces a stiff central stalk, a flower stalk called a scape, and typically 6-12 large cloves per bulb. It has bold, rich flavors and is easier to peel, but it stores only 4-6 months. Softneck garlic lacks a scape, has 10-20 smaller cloves, and stores 6-9 months. It is the type you see braided at markets.

For northern gardeners (zones 3-6), hardneck is the reliable choice because it needs a cold period to form bulbs. For southern gardeners (zones 7-9), softneck performs better because it does not require as much cold. If you live in zone 6 or 7, you can grow both, but hardneck may struggle in hot summers.

## Planting Dates and Spacing

Plant garlic in fall, 4-6 weeks before the ground freezes. This allows root growth before winter. For most zones:

- Zone 3-4: late September to early October
- Zone 5-6: mid-October to early November
- Zone 7-8: late October to mid-November
- Zone 9: November to December (chill cloves in fridge for 6 weeks before planting)

Spacing: plant cloves 4-6 inches apart in rows 12-18 inches apart. Set each clove 2-3 inches deep, pointed end up. Cover with soil and mulch with 4-6 inches of straw or leaves to protect over winter.

## How to Grow Garlic for Maximum Yield

Garlic needs full sun (6+ hours) and well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0. Work in 2-3 inches of compost before planting. In spring, when shoots emerge, side-dress with a balanced fertilizer like 10-10-10 at 1 pound per 100 square feet.

Water 1 inch per week during dry spells, but stop watering 2 weeks before harvest. Remove scapes on hardneck varieties in early summer to direct energy to the bulb. Cut them when they curl, and use them in pesto or sautés.

Weeds compete heavily, so mulch with straw or grass clippings to suppress them. Rotate crops: do not plant garlic where onions or other alliums grew in the past 3 years to prevent disease.

## Harvesting and Curing

Harvest when the lower leaves turn brown but the upper leaves are still green, usually in July for hardneck and June-July for softneck in most zones. Dig gently with a fork, do not pull. Shake off soil, then cure in a dry, shaded, well-ventilated area for 2-4 weeks. Hang in bunches or lay on a screen.

After curing, trim roots and cut stems to 1 inch for storage. Store at 50-60°F with 60-70% humidity. Softneck can be braided; hardneck is best kept in mesh bags.

## Where to Buy Garlic Seed (2026 Prices)

Buy seed garlic from reputable nurseries or online suppliers. Expect to pay $10-$15 per pound for organic seed garlic, which yields about 40-50 cloves (enough for 40-50 plants). Local farm stores may sell individual bulbs for $2-$4 each. Avoid grocery store garlic, as it is often treated to prevent sprouting.

For 2026, popular sources include Burpee, Johnny's Selected Seeds, and local farmers markets. Order early (August-September) because popular varieties sell out.

## FAQ

**Can I grow garlic from grocery store cloves?**

It is possible, but risky. Grocery garlic may be treated with sprout inhibitors or carry diseases. For best results, buy certified seed garlic from a nursery.

**When should I plant garlic in zone 7?**

Plant in late October to mid-November. You can grow both hardneck and softneck, but hardneck may produce smaller bulbs if winters are mild.

**How many garlic bulbs can I expect per pound of seed?**

One pound of seed garlic contains about 40-50 cloves, so you will get 40-50 bulbs, assuming good growing conditions. That is a realistic yield, not a guarantee.

**Do I need to remove scapes?**

Yes, for hardneck varieties. Removing scapes increases bulb size by 10-20%. Use the scapes in cooking or compost them.

## The Bottom Line

The best garlic is the one that fits your zone and storage needs. For cold climates, choose hardneck like 'Music' or 'German Extra Hardy'. For warm climates, choose softneck like 'California Early' or 'Inchelium Red'. Plant in fall, space correctly, and harvest when leaves brown. With proper curing, you will have garlic for months. Start with 1 pound of seed garlic and expand next year based on your success.
