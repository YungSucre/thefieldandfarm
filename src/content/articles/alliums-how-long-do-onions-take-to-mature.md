Onions take 90 to 120 days from planting sets or transplants to full maturity, though seed-grown onions need 100 to 140 days. The exact time depends on your variety, planting method, and day length. In this guide, I'll give you concrete timelines for short-day, intermediate-day, and long-day onions, plus zone-specific planting dates and signs that your onions are ready to pull.

## Understanding onion day-length types

Onions bulb based on day length, not just time. Choose the right type for your latitude or you'll get tops and no bulbs.

- **Short-day onions**: Bulb when days reach 10 to 12 hours. Best for zones 7 and warmer (southern US). Plant in fall, harvest in late spring. Examples: 'Texas SuperSweet', 'Red Creole'.
- **Intermediate-day onions**: Bulb at 12 to 14 hours. Suited for zones 5 to 6 (transitional areas). Plant in early spring, harvest in mid-summer. Examples: 'Candy', 'Red Candy Apple'.
- **Long-day onions**: Bulb when days reach 14 to 16 hours. Best for zones 6 and cooler (northern US). Plant in early spring, harvest in late summer. Examples: 'Yellow Sweet Spanish', 'Walla Walla'.

If you're in zone 6, you can grow long-day onions, but intermediate types also work. Check your zone first.

## Days to maturity by planting method

| Method | Typical days from planting to harvest | Notes |
|--------|----------------------------------------|-------|
| Seeds | 100 to 140 days | Start indoors 8 to 10 weeks before last frost, or direct sow in early spring. |
| Sets (small bulbs) | 90 to 110 days | Plant 1 inch deep, 4 inches apart. Sets are easier but more prone to bolting. |
| Transplants (seedlings) | 90 to 120 days | Buy or grow your own. Transplants give the best bulb size. |

For example, if you plant 'Yellow Sweet Spanish' sets in zone 5 on April 15, expect harvest around July 20 to August 5, about 95 to 110 days later.

## How to tell when onions are ready to harvest

Don't rely solely on the calendar. Look for these signs:

- **Leaf fall**: When 50% to 80% of the tops have flopped over naturally, the bulbs are mature. This usually happens 2 to 3 weeks after the bulb reaches full size.
- **Bulb size**: The bulb should be the expected size for the variety (e.g., 3 to 4 inches for 'Candy').
- **Skin color**: The outer skin should be papery and dry, not green or thick.

To test, gently brush soil away from one bulb. If the skin is tight and the bulb feels firm, it's ready. If the neck is still thick and green, wait a week.

## Steps to harvest and cure onions

1. **Stop watering**: About 2 weeks before harvest, stop watering to help the skins dry and reduce rot.
2. **Loosen soil**: Use a garden fork to gently lift the bulbs, being careful not to bruise them.
3. **Cure**: Lay onions in a single layer in a warm, dry, well-ventilated area (70 to 80°F, 60% humidity). Cure for 2 to 3 weeks until the necks are completely dry and the skins rustle.
4. **Trim**: Cut off the tops to 1 inch and the roots, then store in a cool, dry place (32 to 40°F) in mesh bags or braids.

Curing is essential for long storage. Skip it and your onions may rot in a month.

## Common mistakes that delay maturity

- **Planting too deep**: Sets should be just covered with soil, about 1 inch. Deep planting delays bulbing.
- **Overcrowding**: Space sets 4 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart. Crowded onions compete for nutrients and stay small.
- **Too much nitrogen**: High nitrogen promotes leaf growth, not bulbs. Use a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) at planting, then side-dress with phosphorus and potassium (0-20-20) when bulbing starts.
- **Wrong day length**: This is the biggest mistake. A long-day onion in zone 8 will never bulb properly.

## FAQ

**Can I speed up onion maturity?**
Use transplants instead of seeds or sets. Transplants give you a 4 to 6 week head start. Also, choose a shorter-season variety like 'Red Candy Apple' (95 days) over 'Walla Walla' (110 days).

**What if my onions haven't bulbed by the expected date?**
Check the day length. If you're growing the wrong type for your zone, they may not bulb at all. Also, ensure they're not too crowded or over-fertilized. If the tops haven't fallen by 120 days, lift one to check bulb size; if it's still small, give them another 2 weeks.

**Do onions mature faster from sets or seeds?**
Sets mature about 10 to 20 days faster than seed-grown onions because they're already a year old. However, sets are more likely to bolt (flower) if exposed to cold. For best results, use heat-treated sets or grow from transplants.

**Can I harvest onions early?**
Yes, you can pull onions at any size as green onions or scallions. For full bulbs, wait until the tops fall. If you harvest early, use them fresh; they won't store.

## The bottom line

Onions take 90 to 120 days from planting to harvest, with seed-grown onions needing up to 140 days. Match your variety to your day length, plant at the right time for your zone, and watch for the tops to fall. With proper spacing and curing, you'll have a reliable harvest. Start with sets or transplants for faster results, and keep a garden journal to track your own timeline for next year.
