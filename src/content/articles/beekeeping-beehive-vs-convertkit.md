If you run a homestead, garden, or small farm, you need a way to email your customers and followers. Beehive and ConvertKit are two popular options, but they serve different needs. Beehive is built specifically for newsletter creators who want simplicity and a clean reading experience. ConvertKit (now Kit) is a full marketing platform for creators who need automation, landing pages, and digital product sales. Choose Beehive if you just want to write and send a beautiful newsletter. Choose ConvertKit if you need to grow your audience with lead magnets, sell products, and automate your email sequences.

## What Beehive Does Well

Beehive focuses on one thing: the newsletter. It gives you a clean, ad-free reading experience for your subscribers, which is a big deal if you want your content to feel like a letter from a friend, not a marketing blast.

- **Simple editor**: The writing interface is distraction-free. You write in Markdown or use the visual editor, and Beehive handles the rest.
- **Beautiful emails**: Your newsletter is displayed as a clean, readable page on the web, and the email itself is minimal and elegant.
- **Easy signup forms**: You can embed a signup form on your website or share a link. No complex funnels.
- **No automation**: Beehive does not have sequences or autoresponders. You send one email at a time, or you schedule a broadcast.
- **Pricing**: Beehive charges a flat monthly fee based on subscriber count. As of 2026, the Starter plan is $15/month for up to 1,000 subscribers, and the Creator plan is $35/mon

## What ConvertKit Does Well

ConvertKit, now called Kit, is a creator marketing platform. It is designed for people who sell digital products, courses, or memberships, and it has powerful automation.

- **Visual automation**: You can build sequences that send a series of emails when someone subscribes, buys, or clicks a link. For example, a new subscriber gets a welcome email, then a free guide, then a pitch for your seed catalog.
- **Landing pages and forms**: ConvertKit includes a landing page builder, so you can create a page to collect emails without needing a separate website.
- **Digital product sales**: You can sell ebooks, courses, or even physical products (like honey or seedlings) directly through ConvertKit's commerce features. It handles payment processing and delivery.
- **Subscriber tagging**: You can tag subscribers based on their interests, so you can send a specific email to beekeepers versus vegetable gardeners.
- **Pricing**: ConvertKit has a free plan for up to 10,000 subscribers, but it only allows you to send broadcasts, no automation. The Creator plan starts at $29/month for up to 300 subscribers, and it scales up. As of 2026, the Creator plan for 1,000 subscribers is $49/month, and for 10,000 subscribers it is $119/month. There is also a 10% transaction fee on digital sales unless you are on the higher-tier plan.

## Which One Should You Choose?

If you are a homesteader who sends a monthly update to 500 customers about what is happening on the farm, Beehive is the better choice. It is cheaper, simpler, and your subscribers will actually read your emails because they are not cluttered with ads or calls to action.

If you are a small farmer who sells heirloom seeds online, runs a CSA, or teaches beekeeping courses, ConvertKit is worth the extra cost. The automation and sales features will save you hours, and you can build a funnel that turns a blog reader into a paying customer.

Here is a practical decision guide:

- **Choose Beehive if**: You only need to send a newsletter, you have under 5,000 subscribers, and you do not sell products directly through email.
- **Choose ConvertKit if**: You sell digital products, want to automate your email sequences, or need landing pages to grow your list.
- **Try both**: Both offer free trials (Beehive has 15 days, ConvertKit has 14 days). Sign up, send a test email, and see which interface feels more natural.

## How to Switch (If You Already Have a List)

Switching email providers is easier than you think. Here is a step-by-step plan:

1. **Export your subscribers** from your current platform as a CSV file. Most platforms allow this under Settings or Audience.
2. **Import the CSV into your new platform**. Both Beehive and ConvertKit have import tools that map your columns (name, email, tags) automatically.
3. **Create a welcome email** and send it to your list to let them know about the change.
4. **Update your signup forms** on your website and social media links.
5. **Cancel your old subscription** after you confirm everything is working.

## FAQ

### Can I use Beehive for free?

No, Beehive does not have a free plan. It offers a 15-day free trial, then you pay a flat monthly fee. ConvertKit has a free plan, but it is limited to broadcasts only, no automation.

### Does Beehive support automation?

No, Beehive intentionally does not have automation. If you need to send a sequence of emails automatically, you need ConvertKit or another tool like MailerLite.

### Which is better for selling honey online?

If you are selling physical products like honey, you are better off using a dedicated ecommerce platform like Shopify or WooCommerce, and then use an email tool for marketing. ConvertKit can handle digital sales, but for physical products, you need a shopping cart. You can still use ConvertKit to send emails about your honey, but the actual sale happens on your website.

### How many subscribers do I need before I should worry about this?

If you have under 500 subscribers, either tool will work. But if you plan to grow, think about your long-term needs. If you might sell a course or an ebook in the future, ConvertKit is a safer bet. If you just want to share stories, Beehive is fine.

## The Bottom Line

Beehive is the best choice for a simple, beautiful newsletter with no fuss. ConvertKit is the best choice if you need automation, landing pages, and digital sales. Look at your current setup: if you are only sending occasional updates, save money with Beehive. If you are building a business around your expertise, invest in ConvertKit. Either way, start with a trial, send a few test emails, and pick the one that feels right for your homestead.
