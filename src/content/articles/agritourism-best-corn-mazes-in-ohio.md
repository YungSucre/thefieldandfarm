## The best corn mazes by region

### Central Ohio: The Ohio Maze (Columbus area)

Located in Plain City, about 20 miles northwest of Columbus, The Ohio Maze is a perennial favorite. The maze covers 12 acres, with over 3 miles of paths. They design a new theme each year, and the 2026 theme is "Ohio's Aviation Heritage," featuring a Wright Flyer shape.

- **Ticket prices:** $12 for adults, $10 for kids 4-12, free for 3 and under. Group rates available for 15+.
- **Peak dates:** Open weekends from September 14 through November 2, plus Columbus Day (October 13). Best visited in late September to mid-October when the corn is fully grown.
- **Extras:** Pumpkin patch, corn cannon, and a 5-acre sunflower field that blooms in late August.
- **Why it stands out:** The maze is well-maintained, and they offer a "night maze" on Fridays and Saturdays in October, which adds a spooky twist.

### Northeast Ohio: Maize Valley Farm Market (Hartville)

Maize Valley, about 20 minutes south of Akron, is more than a maze: it is a full agritourism destination with a winery and brewery. The corn maze is 8 acres, with a shorter 1.5-mile path that is great for families with young kids.

- **Ticket prices:** $10 for adults, $8 for kids 4-12, free for 3 and under. Combo tickets with a hayride are $15.
- **Peak dates:** Open daily from September 20 through October 31, 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. The best time is late September to early October for crisp weather and full corn.
- **Extras:** Pumpkin patch, corn maze scavenger hunt, and a giant slide. The winery is a bonus for adults.
- **Why it stands out:** The maze is accessible for strollers, and the farm has a relaxed vibe. It is less crowded than some bigger mazes.

### Northwest Ohio: The Corn Maze at Huffman's (Toledo area)

Huffman's, in Monclova, is a working farm that has been running corn mazes for over 15 years. The maze is 10 acres, with a 2.5-mile path that includes bridges and tunnels.

- **Ticket prices:** $11 for adults, $9 for kids 4-12, free for 3 and under. Family four-pack for $35.
- **Peak dates:** Open weekends from September 13 through November 1, plus Fridays in October. Best visited in October when the corn is at its tallest.
- **Extras:** Corn box (a sandbox filled with corn kernels), pedal carts, and a petting zoo.
- **Why it stands out:** The maze is challenging, and they offer a "flashlight night" on Fridays in October, which is a hit with teens.

### Southwest Ohio: Blooms & Berries Farm Market (Loveland)

Just northeast of Cincinnati, Blooms & Berries is a family-run farm with a 6-acre maze that is perfect for families with kids under 10. The maze is designed with a "storybook" theme, and they change it yearly.

- **Ticket prices:** $9 for adults, $7 for kids 4-12, free for 3 and under. Hayride add-on is $5.
- **Peak dates:** Open daily from September 27 through October 31, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. Late September is ideal for fewer crowds.
- **Extras:** Pumpkin patch, apple cannons, and a corn maze scavenger hunt with prizes.
- **Why it stands out:** It is the most affordable option, and the maze is easy to navigate, so you won't spend hours lost.

### Southeast Ohio: The Great Pumpkin Patch (Jackson)

For a more rural experience, The Great Pumpkin Patch in Jackson is a hidden gem. The maze is 5 acres, but it is part of a larger farm that includes a sunflower maze and a pumpkin patch.

- **Ticket prices:** $8 for adults, $6 for kids 4-12, free for 3 and under.
- **Peak dates:** Open weekends from September 20 through October 26, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Best in early October.
- **Extras:** Hayrides, a corn pit, and a small farm market with local produce.
- **Why it stands out:** It is the most budget-friendly, and the farm is less commercialized, giving you a true harvest feel.

## How to choose the right maze for you

Consider your group's size and ages. For toddlers, pick a smaller maze like Blooms & Berries or Maize Valley. For teens and adults who want a challenge, go with The Ohio Maze or Huffman's. If you want a full day out, Maize Valley has the most extras. Also, check the farm's website for special events like dog days or night mazes, which often require separate tickets.

## Practical tips for your visit

- **Wear comfortable shoes:** You will walk 1 to 3 miles, and the ground can be uneven. Sneakers or boots are best.
- **Bring water:** Many mazes have water stations, but it is safer to carry your own bottle.
- **Use the map:** Most mazes give you a map or a QR code for your phone. Do not rely on memory; it is easy to get turned around.
- **Go early:** Arrive by 10 a.m. on weekends to avoid the biggest crowds, especially in October.
- **Check the weather:** Corn mazes are muddy after rain. If it has rained recently, bring a change of clothes for kids.

## FAQ

### When is the best time to visit a corn maze in Ohio?

Late September to mid-October is ideal. The corn is fully grown, the weather is usually mild, and you avoid the peak Halloween crowds in late October.

### Are corn mazes accessible for strollers or wheelchairs?

Some are, but not all. Maize Valley and Blooms & Berries have wider paths that are stroller-friendly. Call ahead to ask about accessibility, as many mazes have narrow turns.

### Can I bring my dog?

Some farms allow dogs on certain days, like "Dog Days" events. The Ohio Maze and Huffman's have hosted dog days in the past. Check their websites for 2026 dates, and always keep your dog on a leash.

### How long does it take to get through a corn maze?

Plan for 45 minutes to 2 hours, depending on the maze size and your group's pace. Smaller mazes can be done in under an hour, while larger ones like The Ohio Maze can take longer if you get lost.

## The bottom line

Ohio has no shortage of excellent corn mazes, and the best one for you depends on your location and group. For a big, challenging maze with lots of extras, head to The Ohio Maze. For a family-friendly day with a winery, Maize Valley is a solid pick. If you are on a budget, Blooms & Berries or The Great Pumpkin Patch offer great value. Whichever you choose, go early, wear good shoes, and enjoy the fall air. You will not regret a day at any of these farms.
