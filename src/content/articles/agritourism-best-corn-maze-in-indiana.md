If you're looking for the best corn maze in Indiana, start with these proven favorites: Exploration Acres in Lafayette, Tuttle Orchards in Greenwood, and Stuckey Farm in Sheridan. Each offers a unique experience, from massive themed mazes to family-friendly attractions, with prices ranging from $10 to $20 per person. Here's how to choose and what to know before you go.

## Top Corn Mazes in Indiana by Region

### Central Indiana
- **Tuttle Orchards (Greenwood)**: Known for its 10-acre maze with a new design each year. Includes a pumpkin patch, apple picking, and a corn cannon. Open late September through October. Admission: $12-$15.
- **Stuckey Farm (Sheridan)**: Features a 5-acre maze, hayrides, and a petting zoo. Great for families with young kids. Admission: $10-$12.

### Northern Indiana
- **Exploration Acres (Lafayette)**: One of the largest in the state, with a 20-acre maze that often features a famous Indiana landmark or sports theme. Includes a giant jumping pillow and corn pit. Admission: $15-$18.
- **Dull's Tree Farm (Thorntown)**: Offers a 6-acre maze, plus a haunted maze on select nights. Admission: $10-$14.

### Southern Indiana
- **Huber's Farm (Borden)**: Part of a large agritourism operation, with a 10-acre maze, winery, and farm market. Admission: $12-$16.

## What to Expect at a Corn Maze

Most mazes open in late September and run through October, sometimes into early November. You'll typically find:
- **Maze difficulty**: Ranging from easy (30-45 minutes) to challenging (1-2 hours). Check the farm's website for difficulty ratings.
- **Checkpoints**: Many mazes have numbered flags or QR codes to help you navigate and find your way out.
- **Additional attractions**: Hayrides, corn cannons, pumpkin patches, and food stands are common.
- **Weather**: Dress for the weather; mazes are outdoors and can be muddy after rain.

## How to Choose the Best Maze for You

Consider your group's needs:
- **For young children**: Choose a smaller maze (under 5 acres) with wide paths and easy exits, like Stuckey Farm.
- **For thrill-seekers**: Look for haunted mazes or night events, such as Dull's Tree Farm's haunted nights.
- **For large groups**: Bigger mazes like Exploration Acres can handle crowds and offer group rates.
- **For a full day out**: Pick a farm with multiple attractions, like Huber's Farm, which also has a winery.

## Practical Tips for Your Visit

- **Check hours and dates**: Most mazes are open weekends only in September, then daily in October. Confirm on the farm's website or social media.
- **Buy tickets online**: Many farms offer discounts for online purchases. Expect to pay $10-$20 per person, with children under 3 often free.
- **Go early or late**: Avoid peak times (Saturday afternoons) for shorter lines and less crowding.
- **Bring cash**: Some smaller farms are cash-only.
- **Wear comfortable shoes**: You'll be walking on uneven ground for an hour or more.
- **Bring a phone charger**: If you use the farm's app or GPS, you'll want battery life.

## Corn Maze Prices in 2026

Prices have held steady over the past few years. Expect:
- **Adults**: $12-$18
- **Children (3-12)**: $8-$12
- **Seniors (65+)**: $8-$10
- **Group rates (10+ people)**: Often $2-$3 off per ticket

Some farms offer combo tickets with pumpkin patches or hayrides for $20-$25.

## FAQ

### When is the best time to visit a corn maze?

Late September to mid-October is ideal: the corn is tall, the weather is mild, and the crowds are manageable. October weekends are busiest, so go on a weekday if possible.

### Are corn mazes suitable for young children?

Yes, many farms have family-friendly mazes with shorter paths and staff stationed at exits. Look for mazes labeled "easy" or "family-friendly." Always keep an eye on kids, as it's easy to lose sight in tall corn.

### Can I bring my dog?

Most farms do not allow pets, except service animals. Check the farm's policy before bringing your dog.

### What happens if I get lost?

Every maze has exits and staff. If you're truly lost, follow the perimeter fence or look for a checkpoint flag. Many farms have a "break glass" emergency phone or a staff member on a golf cart.
