Painting a pumpkin patch is a fun, profitable addition to any farm stand or U-pick operation. You can turn a $3 pumpkin into a $15 painted product with a few hours of work and basic supplies. Here is exactly how to do it, from choosing the right pumpkins to sealing your finished work.

## Choose the right pumpkins

Not every pumpkin is good for painting. You want a smooth, flat surface with a sturdy stem. Avoid pumpkins with deep ribs, soft spots, or blemishes; they are harder to paint and less appealing to customers.

For painting, the best varieties are:

- **Sugar Pie**: small, smooth, and round, perfect for faces or simple designs. They weigh 2 to 4 pounds.
- **Baby Boo**: white and smooth, great for ghost or snowman designs. They weigh 1 to 2 pounds.
- **Jack-O-Lantern**: larger, but choose ones with shallow ribs. They weigh 10 to 20 pounds and are good for big displays.

Buy pumpkins from a local grower or wholesale market. In 2026, expect to pay $0.50 to $1.50 per pound for bulk pumpkins, or $2 to $5 each for small ones. If you grow your own, factor in seed, water, and labor.

## Gather your supplies

You need:

- **Acrylic craft paint**: $1 to $3 per 2-ounce bottle. Buy a set of basic colors (black, white, red, yellow, blue) plus seasonal shades like orange, green, and purple.
- **Paintbrushes**: a set of foam brushes and fine-tip brushes, $5 to $10.
- **Sponges**: for stippling or base coats, $2 to $4.
- **Painter's tape**: to mask off areas, $5 per roll.
- **Clear acrylic sealer**: spray or brush-on, $8 to $12 per can or bottle. This is essential to protect the paint from moisture and handling.
- **Newspaper or drop cloth**: to protect your work surface.
- **Rubbing alcohol and paper towels**: to clean the pumpkin surface before painting.

Total startup cost: $30 to $50 if you buy a basic kit. You can reuse brushes and tape for many pumpkins.

## Prepare the pumpkin

1. **Clean the pumpkin**: Wipe it with a damp cloth to remove dirt. Then rub the surface with rubbing alcohol to remove any waxy residue. Let it dry completely. This helps the paint adhere.
2. **Dry the stem**: If the stem is wet, let it dry. Paint does not stick to wet stems.
3. **Plan your design**: Sketch it on paper first. For faces, use a stencil or freehand with a chalk pencil. Chalk wipes off easily if you make a mistake.

## Paint the pumpkin

1. **Apply a base coat**: Use a foam brush or sponge to apply a thin, even layer of paint. For solid colors, two coats are usually better. Let each coat dry for 15 to 20 minutes.
2. **Add details**: Use fine-tip brushes for outlines and small details. For letters or numbers, use painter's tape to create straight lines. For polka dots or stars, use a sponge or the end of a brush.
3. **Let it dry completely**: Acrylic paint dries in 20 to 30 minutes, but wait at least an hour before handling or sealing.

## Seal the paint

Sealing is not optional. It protects the paint from scratches, moisture, and the natural decay of the pumpkin. Use a clear acrylic sealer in a well-ventilated area. Spray in light, even coats, holding the can 8 to 10 inches away. Let it dry for 30 minutes. Apply a second coat if you want extra protection.

Sealed pumpkins last 2 to 4 weeks indoors, or 1 to 2 weeks outdoors, depending on weather. Unsealed pumpkins may only last a few days before the paint cracks.

## Display and sell

Display painted pumpkins on a clean, dry table under a canopy or tent. Avoid direct sunlight, which fades paint and speeds up decay. Price them based on size and complexity:

- Small (2 to 4 pounds): $8 to $12
- Medium (5 to 10 pounds): $12 to $18
- Large (10+ pounds): $18 to $25

If you sell at a farm stand, offer a discount for buying three or more. You can also take custom orders: let customers pick a pumpkin and a design, and paint it while they wait. That adds a personal touch and can justify a higher price.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I use spray paint instead of acrylic?**
A: Yes, but spray paint is harder to control and can run. Acrylic is easier for detailed work and is safer for kids if you are doing a workshop.

**Q: How long do painted pumpkins last?**
A: With a sealer, 2 to 4 weeks indoors, 1 to 2 weeks outdoors. Keep them out of direct sun and rain to extend their life.

**Q: What if the pumpkin starts to rot?**
A: Rot usually starts at the bottom or stem. Check daily and remove any that show soft spots. You can also coat the cut stem with petroleum jelly to slow decay.

**Q: Can I paint a pumpkin that has already been carved?**
A: No, carved pumpkins rot quickly and are not suitable for painting. Always use intact pumpkins.

## The bottom line

Painting pumpkins is a low-cost, high-margin product for any fall agritourism business. With $30 in supplies, you can paint dozens of pumpkins and sell them for three to five times your cost. The key is to start with clean, smooth pumpkins, use quality acrylic paint, and always seal your work. Test a few designs before you commit to a full batch, and you will be ready for your first weekend market.

Remember: the pumpkin is a natural product, so it will not last forever. Be upfront with customers about the lifespan, and offer a discount on bulk purchases. With a little practice, you will have a colorful patch that draws in customers and adds to your bottom line.
