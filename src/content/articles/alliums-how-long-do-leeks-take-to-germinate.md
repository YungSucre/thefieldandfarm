Leeks typically germinate in 7 to 14 days when soil temperatures are between 60°F and 70°F. At cooler temperatures (50°F), expect 14 to 21 days; above 75°F, germination may stall. For most gardeners, that means starting seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost date, or direct sowing in early spring as soon as the soil can be worked.

## Why germination speed varies

## Step-by-step for indoor sowing (recommended)

1. **Timing**: Start seeds 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost date. For example, in Zone 5 (last frost around May 15), sow indoors in mid-March. In Zone 8 (last frost around March 15), sow in early January.
2. **Containers**: Use 2-inch cell trays or 4-inch pots with drainage. Fill with a sterile seed-starting mix (like Pro-Mix or Jiffy).
3. **Sowing depth**: Press seeds 1/4 inch deep. Leeks need light to germinate, so do not bury them deeper. Firm the soil gently.
4. **Watering**: Bottom-water or mist gently. Keep the mix moist but not soggy. Cover with a humidity dome or plastic wrap until sprouts appear.
5. **Temperature**: Place on a heat mat set to 68°F to 72°F. A heat mat costs $20 to $30. Without one, a warm room (65°F to 70°F) works, but germination may take a few extra days.
6. **Light**: Once seeds sprout, remove the dome and provide 14 to 16 hours of light. A shop light with two T5 fluorescent tubes (about $40) works well. Keep lights 2 inches above the seedlings.

## Direct sowing outdoors

If you prefer direct sowing, wait until soil temperature reaches at least 50°F, ideally 60°F. In Zone 5, that is usually late April. In Zone 8, you can sow in February or March.

- **Spacing**: Sow seeds 1/4 inch deep, 1 inch apart in rows 12 inches apart. Thin to 4 to 6 inches apart once seedlings are 2 inches tall.
- **Mulch**: A light layer of straw or compost helps retain moisture and keeps soil cool. But do not cover seeds with heavy mulch; they need light.

## Varieties that germinate faster

Some leek varieties are known for quicker germination and earlier maturity. For example:

- **King Richard**: A fast-growing summer leek, germinates in 7 to 10 days under ideal conditions. Harvest in 75 days.
- **American Flag**: A classic, hardy variety, germinates in 10 to 14 days. Good for fall harvest.
- **Giant Musselburgh**: Cold-tolerant, germinates in 10 to 14 days. Overwinters in mild zones.

These are not dramatic differences, but if you are impatient, choose King Richard.

## Common mistakes that slow germination

- **Planting too deep**: Seeds buried more than 1/2 inch may not sprout at all. Always sow at 1/4 inch.
- **Letting soil dry**: Check daily. If the surface looks light brown, mist immediately.
- **Using old seeds**: Leek seeds lose viability after 2 to 3 years. Buy fresh seeds from a reputable source (Johnny's Selected Seeds, Burpee) for 2026. A packet costs $3 to $5 and contains 200 to 300 seeds.
- **Too much heat**: Above 80°F, germination drops. Do not place trays on a sunny windowsill that overheats.

## How to speed up germination

If you are in a hurry, try these two methods:

1. **Pre-soak seeds**: Soak seeds in lukewarm water for 6 to 12 hours before sowing. This softens the seed coat and can shave 2 to 3 days off germination.
2. **Use a heat mat**: A heat mat set to 70°F consistently will give you the fastest, most uniform sprouting. It is worth the $25 investment if you start seeds every year.

## What to do after germination

Once seeds sprout, move them to a bright, cool location (60°F to 65°F) to prevent leggy growth. Water from below or mist gently. When seedlings are 6 inches tall and about pencil-thick, transplant them outdoors after the last frost. Dig a 6-inch deep trench, set the seedlings 6 inches apart, and fill with soil up to the first leaf. This blanches the stems for white shanks.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I plant leek seeds directly in the garden in fall?**
A: Yes, in Zones 7 to 10, you can sow in late summer for a winter harvest. Germination will be slower in cooler soil, but it works. In colder zones, start indoors and transplant.

**Q: Why did my leeks take 3 weeks to germinate?**
A: Likely the soil was below 60°F or the seeds were old. Check your soil temperature with a probe thermometer ($10) and use fresh seeds.

**Q: Do leeks need light to germinate?**
A: Yes, leek seeds need light. Do not cover them with more than 1/4 inch of soil. Press them into the surface or cover lightly with vermiculite.

**Q: How long do leeks take from seed to harvest?**
A: From germination to harvest is typically 100 to 140 days, depending on variety. King Richard can be ready in 75 days; Giant Musselburgh takes 120 days.

## The bottom line

Leeks germinate in 7 to 14 days under ideal conditions: 60°F to 70°F soil, consistent moisture, and light. Start seeds indoors 8 to 10 weeks before your last frost, use fresh seeds, and consider a heat mat for speed. Direct sowing works too, but be patient if soil is cool. With these steps, you will have strong seedlings ready for transplant in no time.
