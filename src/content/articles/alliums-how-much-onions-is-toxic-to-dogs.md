If your dog eats any amount of onion, it's a problem. Even a single slice can cause oxidative damage to red blood cells, leading to anemia. The toxic dose is roughly 0.5% of a dog's body weight in onions, which for a 50-pound dog is about 4 ounces (a medium onion). But clinical signs can appear with less, especially in smaller dogs or with repeated exposure. Don't wait for symptoms; call your vet immediately if your dog has eaten any onion, garlic, or related alliums.

## Why Onions Are Toxic to Dogs

Onions contain N-propyl disulfide and other sulfur compounds. When chewed, these compounds damage hemoglobin in red blood cells, causing them to rupture (hemolytic anemia). The effect is dose-dependent, but even

 small amounts can be dangerous, especially for certain breeds or dogs with underlying health issues.

## Toxic Dose: How Much Is Dangerous?

The general rule: 0.5% of body weight in onions is toxic. That's 5 grams of onion per kilogram of body weight. Here's a quick reference:

| Dog Weight | Toxic Onion Amount (fresh) | Example |
|------------|----------------------------|---------|
| 10 lb (4.5 kg) | 1 ounce (28 g) | About 2 tablespoons chopped |
| 25 lb (11 kg) | 2.5 ounces (70 g) | Half a medium onion |
| 50 lb (23 kg) | 4 ounces (113 g) | One medium onion |
| 75 lb (34 kg) | 6 ounces (170 g) | One large onion |

But these are thresholds for acute toxicity. Even a small bite can cause mild gastrointestinal upset, and repeated small exposures can accumulate. Also, all parts of the onion are toxic: flesh, skin, juice, and even cooked onions. Onion powder is more concentrated; 1 teaspoon of onion powder equals about 1 medium onion.

## Symptoms of Onion Toxicity in Dogs

Symptoms usually appear within 24 to 48 hours, but can be delayed up to 5 days. Watch for:

- Lethargy and weakness
- Pale gums
- Vomiting and diarrhea
- Loss of appetite
- Increased heart rate or rapid breathing
- Dark urine (reddish or brown)
- Collapse in severe cases

If you see any of these after your dog ate onions, get to a vet immediately. The vet may induce vomiting if within a few hours, or give activated charcoal. In severe anemia, a blood transfusion may be needed.

## What to Do If Your Dog Eats Onions

1. **Call your vet or Pet Poison Helpline (855-764-7661) right away.** Have your dog's weight and the amount of onion eaten ready.
2. **Do not induce vomiting unless told to.** Some cases are better managed with supportive care.
3. **Do not wait for symptoms.** Early treatment is more effective and less costly.
4. **Bring a sample of the onion** if possible, to help estimate the dose.

Treatment costs can range from $100 for a mild case to $1,500 or more for severe anemia requiring hospitalization and transfusion.

## Prevention: Keep Onions Away from Dogs

- Store onions in a closed pantry or cupboard, not in open bowls on the counter.
- Compost onion scraps in a dog-proof bin; dogs love the smell of compost.
- Be careful with table scraps: many dishes contain onion powder or cooked onions.
- Teach your dog a solid "leave it" command.
- If you grow onions in your garden, fence off the bed or supervise your dog when outside.

## FAQ

**Q: Can a dog die from eating a small piece of onion?**
A: It's possible, especially for small dogs. Even a small piece can cause oxidative damage, and some dogs are more sensitive. Always treat onion ingestion as an emergency.

**Q: Is garlic as toxic as onions?**
A: Garlic is about 5 times more toxic than onions by weight, but dogs typically eat less of it. The same rule applies: any amount can be harmful, and large amounts are dangerous.

**Q: Are cooked onions safe for dogs?**
A: No. Cooking does not destroy the toxic compounds. In fact, cooked onions can be more concentrated, and onion powder is especially potent.

**Q: How long after eating onions will symptoms appear?**
A: Usually within 24 to 48 hours, but sometimes up to 5 days. If your dog has eaten onions, monitor closely for at least 48 hours and seek vet advice immediately.

## The Bottom Line

No amount of onion is safe for dogs. The toxic dose is roughly 0.5% of body weight, but even a small bite can cause problems, especially in small dogs. If your dog eats any onion, call your vet right away. Prevention is key: store onions securely, keep compost bins dog-proof, and never share onion-containing foods with your dog. Your dog's health is worth the extra caution.
