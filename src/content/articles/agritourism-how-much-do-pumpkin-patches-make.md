A pumpkin patch can bring in $3,000 to $30,000 per acre, but most small operations net far less after costs. The real money comes from agritourism extras like corn mazes, hayrides, and food sales, not just pumpkins. Here is what you can expect in 2026 and how to plan for a profitable season.

## The short answer: what you can realistically earn

For a U-pick patch with 1 to 5 acres, gross revenue typically runs $5,000 to $15,000 per acre. A 1-acre patch with 2,000 to 3,000 pumpkins sold at $0.50 to $1.00 per pound (average 10 to 15 pounds each) can gross $10,000 to $20,000. But subtract seeds, fertilizer, irrigation, labor, and marketing, and net profit often lands between $2,000 and $8,000 per acre. Small farm stand

## Costs to plan for

Before you plant, know your expenses. For a 1-acre patch, realistic 2026 costs are:

- Seeds: $50 to $150 (for 2,000 to 3,000 plants, depending on variety)
- Fertilizer and soil amendments: $200 to $400
- Irrigation (if needed): $300 to $600 for drip tape and fittings
- Pest and weed control: $100 to $300 (organic options cost more)
- Labor: $500 to $2,000 (if you hire help for planting, weeding, or harvest)
- Marketing and signage: $100 to $500
- Liability insurance: $300 to $800 per year (essential for public access)

Total startup or annual operating cost for a small patch: $1,500 to $4,000. If you add a corn maze or hayride, costs jump by $2,000 to $10,000 for construction, maintenance, and permits.

## Pricing strategies that work

Pumpkin pricing varies by region and sales method. In 2026, expect these ranges:

- U-pick: $0.50 to $0.80 per pound, with a minimum charge per pumpkin (e.g., $5 minimum)
- Pre-picked by the pound: $0.40 to $0.60
- Pre-picked by size: $3 to $10 for small, $10 to $20 for medium, $20 to $40 for large decorative pumpkins
- Specialty varieties (white, blue, warty): $1.00 to $1.50 per pound

Set your prices based on local competition. Check nearby farm stands and grocery stores. If you offer a unique experience (hayride, petting zoo), you can charge 20% to 30% more.

## Realistic yield and variety selection

A well-managed patch yields 1,000 to 2,500 marketable pumpkins per acre, depending on variety and weather. Choose varieties that sell well in your region:

- For carving: 'Howden' or 'Connecticut Field' (large, classic shape)
- For painting: 'Baby Boo' (white, small) or 'Jack Be Little' (miniature)
- For pies: 'Sugar Pie' (small, dense flesh)
- For decoration: 'Cinderella' (deep orange, flattened) or 'Jarrahdale' (blue-gray)

Plant after the last frost (after soil reaches 60°F). In USDA zones 3-5, that is late May to early June; zones 6-8, mid-April to mid-May. Space plants 3 to 4 feet apart in rows 6 to 8 feet apart, or use hills with 3 seeds per hill, thinning to 2 plants. Pumpkins need 75 to 120 days to mature, so count backward from your target harvest date.

## The agritourism multiplier

Pumpkins alone rarely make a living. The real profit is in add-ons. A family that comes to pick a $10 pumpkin often spends $40 on cider, donuts, and a hayride. In 2026, typical add-on prices:

- Hayride: $5 to $10 per person
- Corn maze: $8 to $15 per person
- Food and drink: $3 to $8 per item (cider, caramel apples, baked goods)
- Photo props and decorations: $2 to $5 per item

If you can draw 500 visitors over a 4-week season, and each spends $25 total, that is $12,500 in gross revenue. Even with $5,000 in costs, you net $7,500. That is a solid side income for a small farm.

## Steps to start this week

If you are planning for this fall, here is what to do now (assuming you are in the Northern Hemisphere):

1. **Check your soil and order seeds.** Get a soil test ($15 to $30) and adjust pH to 6.0 to 6.8. Order seeds from a reputable supplier like Johnny's Selected Seeds or Burpee. For a 1-acre patch, buy 2 to 3 pounds of seed.
2. **Plan your marketing.** Create a simple website or Facebook page. Post your opening date, hours, and prices. Print a few signs for the road (use 4x4 posts and plywood, cost $50 to $100).
3. **Set your budget.** List all costs and expected revenue. If you need a loan, check with your local FSA office for small farm loans.
4. **Prepare the field.** Till the soil, add compost or fertilizer, and lay drip irrigation if you have it. Pumpkins need consistent moisture, especially during flowering.
5. **Decide on add-ons.** Start small: a hay bale maze and a few photo spots. You can expand next year.

## FAQ

**Q: How much does a pumpkin patch make per acre?**
A: Gross revenue ranges from $5,000 to $15,000 per acre for U-pick, but net profit is typically $2,000 to $8,000 after costs. Small farm stands make less.

**Q: Do I need insurance for a pumpkin patch?**
A: Yes, liability insurance is essential if you invite the public. Costs range from $300 to $800 per year for a small operation. Check with your current farm insurer.

**Q: How long does it take to grow pumpkins?**
A: Most varieties need 75 to 120 days from planting to harvest. Plant after the last frost, and you will be ready for September and October sales.

**Q: Can I make a living from a pumpkin patch?**
A: Unlikely on a small scale. Most growers use pumpkins as a seasonal sideline. To make a full-time income, you need at least 5 to 10 acres, strong agritourism, and a long season with multiple crops.

## The bottom line

A pumpkin patch can be a profitable fall venture, but it is not a get-rich scheme. Expect to net $2,000 to $8,000 per acre with careful planning and good marketing. Focus on the experience, not just the pumpkins, and you will see the best returns. Start small, track your costs, and expand only when you know your numbers. With realistic expectations and hard work, you can turn a patch of dirt into a seasonal income that feels like a win.
