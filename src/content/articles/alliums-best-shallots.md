If you're looking for the best shallots to grow, you want varieties that offer rich flavor, good storage, and reliable yields in your climate. The top choices are 'French Red' (also called 'Griselle' or 'Red Sun'), 'Golden Gourmet', and 'Ambition'. These three stand out for their taste, keeping quality, and adaptability across US zones. Plant them in fall or early spring depending on your zone, and you'll have a harvest that beats anything from the store.

## Why grow shallots?
Shallots are milder and sweeter than onions, and they store well, making them a kitchen staple. They take up little space and can be grown in beds or containers. Plus, each planted bulb multiplies into a cluster of 5 to 10 new bulbs, so a small investment yields a generous crop.

## Planting shallots: timing and spacing
Shallots are planted from sets (small bulbs) or seed. Sets are easier and more reliable for beginners.

**Planting times by zone:**
- **Zones 3-5:** Plant in early spring as soon as soil can be worked (March-April). Fall planting is risky due to harsh winters.
- **Zones 6-7:** Plant in fall (October-November) for an early summer harvest, or in early spring.
- **Zones 8-10:** Plant in fall (October-December) for a spring harvest. In hot zones, choose heat-tolerant varieties like 'Golden Gourmet'.

**Spacing and depth:**
- Space sets 6 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart.
- Plant sets 1 inch deep, with the tip just below the soil surface.
- If planting from seed, sow 1/4 inch deep, thin to 4 inches apart.

**Soil and sun:**
Shallots need full sun (at least 6 hours) and well-drained soil with a pH of 6.0-7.0. Work in compost before planting. Avoid fresh manure, which can cause rot.

## Growing and care
- **Water:** Keep soil evenly moist but not soggy. Water deeply once a week if no rain.
- **Fertilizer:** Side-dress with a balanced organic fertilizer (like 10-10-10) at planting and again when bulbs start to form (about 6 weeks after emergence).
- **Weeding:** Mulch with straw to suppress weeds and retain moisture.
- **Pests:** Onion maggots and thrips can be issues. Use row covers to prevent egg-laying, and rotate crops yearly.

## Harvesting and storing
Harvest when the tops yellow and fall over, usually 90-120 days after planting. In fall-planted shallots, this is typically June-July.

1. Gently lift bulbs with a fork, being careful not to bruise.
2. Cure in a warm, dry, well-ventilated area for 2-3 weeks until necks are dry.
3. Trim tops to 1 inch and roots, then store in a cool, dry place (50-60°F) in mesh bags or crates.

Well-cured shallots can last 4-8 months depending on variety.

## Common mistakes to avoid
- **Planting too deep:** Bulbs may rot. Keep the tip just below the surface.
- **Overwatering:** Shallots are prone to rot in wet soil. Ensure good drainage.
- **Harvesting too early:** Wait until tops die back for maximum size and storage.
- **Skipping crop rotation:** Plant shallots in a different spot each year to prevent disease buildup.

## Where to buy shallot sets
You can find shallot sets at local garden centers or online. Expect to pay $8-$15 per pound of sets, which covers about 50-60 sets. For a 10-foot row, you'll need about 1 pound. Popular online sources include Johnny's Selected Seeds, Burpee, and Dixondale Farms.

## FAQ
**Can I grow shallots in containers?**
Yes, use a pot at least 12 inches deep with drainage holes. Plant sets 4 inches apart, and use a quality potting mix. Water regularly, as containers dry out faster.

**How do I know when shallots are ready to harvest?**
When the leaves turn yellow and flop over, that's the signal. Wait about 2 weeks after that to dig them up, then cure.

**Can I save my own shallot sets for next year?**
Absolutely. After curing, select the best bulbs (firm, no damage) and store them in a cool, dry place. Replant them next season. Over time, you'll develop a strain adapted to your soil.

**What's the difference between shallots and onions?**
Shallots have a milder, sweeter flavor and grow in clusters, while onions form a single bulb. Shallots also store longer and are often preferred for gourmet cooking.

## The bottom line
For the best results, plant 'French Red' or 'Golden Gourmet' shallots in well-drained soil with full sun. Follow the planting dates for your zone, keep the soil moist but not wet, and harvest when the tops die back. With minimal effort, you'll have a flavorful crop that stores for months. Start with sets for ease, and you'll be enjoying homegrown shallots by midsummer.
