Making your own candles is a practical skill that saves money and gives you control over what burns in your home. You need three things: wax, a wick, and a container or mold. This guide walks you through the process from start to finish, with real numbers and tools you can buy today.

![Candle making guide for beginners](/images/articles/candle-making-guide-for-beginners/section-1.jpg)

## Choose your wax

The most common waxes for beginners are paraffin, soy, and beeswax. Paraffin is cheap and easy to work with, but it is petroleum-based. Soy wax is renewable and burns clean, but it can be finicky with fragrance. Beeswax is natural and has a lovely honey scent, but it is the most expensive. For your first batch, use a container soy wax like Golden Brands 464 or NatureWax C-3. These are widely

available online for about $15 to $20 per 5-pound bag. One pound of wax makes about one 8-ounce candle.

## Pick the right wick

The wick size matters more than you think. Too small and the flame drowns; too large and it smokes. For a 3-inch diameter container, use a CD-8 or CD-10 wick. For a 4-inch diameter, go with CD-12 or CD-14. You can buy a wick variety pack for $10 to $15, which is a smart way to test what works. Always use a wick tab (a small metal base) to keep the wick centered and anchored.

## Gather your tools

You do not need a fancy kit. Here is what you need:

- A double boiler or a large pot with a pouring pot (a 2-quart stainless steel pitcher works)
- A kitchen scale (accurate to 1 gram, about $15 to $20)
- A thermometer (candy thermometer or infrared, $10 to $15)
- Containers: mason jars, tin cans, or heat-safe glass, about $1 to $3 each if reused
- Fragrance oils (optional): use candle-safe oils, not essential oils, unless you know they are skin-safe and candle-safe. Start with 1 ounce of fragrance per pound of wax.
- A heat source: a stove or hot plate

## Melt the wax safely

Never melt wax directly on a stove burner. Use a double boiler or a pouring pot set in a pot of simmering water. Cut the wax into small chunks or shavings to speed melting. Heat the wax to 185°F, but do not exceed 200°F. At 185°F, remove from heat and add fragrance oil if using. Stir gently for 2 minutes. Let the wax cool to 135°F to 140°F before pouring. This lower temperature reduces sinkholes and improves adhesion.

## Prepare your containers

Clean your containers with hot soapy water and dry thoroughly. Attach the wick tab to the bottom of the container using a drop of hot glue or a wick sticker. Center the wick and hold it in place with a wick bar or two pencils taped across the top. Make sure the wick is straight and taut.

## Pour and cure

Pour the cooled wax slowly into the container, leaving about 1/2 inch of headspace. Tap the container gently to release air bubbles. Let the candle sit undisturbed for 24 hours. After that, you may notice a small sinkhole near the wick. Fix it by poking a few holes with a skewer and pouring a thin layer of melted wax at 135°F to fill. Wait another 24 hours. Trim the wick to 1/4 inch before the first burn.

## Burn and enjoy

On the first burn, let the candle melt all the way to the edges. This prevents tunneling, where the wax burns down the middle and leaves a thick rim. Burn for 3 to 4 hours at a time, and never leave a candle unattended. Keep the wick trimmed to 1/4 inch each time.

## Troubleshooting common problems

- **Tunneling**: your wick is too small, or you did not do the first full-melt burn. Use a larger wick next time.
- **Sinkholes**: pour at a lower temperature, or do the second pour as described.
- **Poor scent throw**: you added too little fragrance, or the wax was too hot when you added it. Use 1 ounce per pound and add at 185°F.
- **Smoking**: wick is too long or too large. Trim more often, or use a smaller wick.

## FAQ

**Can I use essential oils instead of fragrance oils?**

Yes, but be careful. Essential oils are volatile and may not hold up well in candle wax. Use 1/2 to 1 ounce per pound, and test a small batch first. Some essential oils, like citrus, can cause the wax to separate or the candle to smoke.

**How long do candles last?**

A properly made 8-ounce candle burns for about 40 to 50 hours. Soy wax burns slower than paraffin, so you get more burn time per ounce.

**Why did my candle crack?**

The wax cooled too fast or the container was too cold. Warm your containers to about 100°F before pouring, and keep the room at a steady temperature.

**Can I reuse old candle jars?**

Absolutely. Clean out the old wax by placing the jar in a warm oven (200°F) on a baking sheet, then wipe out the melted wax. Wash with soap and water, and you have a new container.

## The bottom line

Candle making is a straightforward weekend project that pays off in quality and cost. Start with soy wax, a simple container, and a wick variety pack. Follow the temperatures and curing times, and you will have a clean-burning candle in two days. As you gain confidence, experiment with different waxes, wicks, and scents. Keep a notebook of what works, and you will be making gifts and home supplies in no time.
