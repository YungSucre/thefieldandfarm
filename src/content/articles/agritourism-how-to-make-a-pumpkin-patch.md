To make a pumpkin patch, choose a sunny spot with well-drained soil, plant seeds after the last frost, and give each vine plenty of room. Start with a soil test, amend with compost, and select varieties suited to your growing season. This guide covers the steps from site prep to harvest, with real dates and varieties for US zones.

## Site selection and soil preparation

Pumpkins need full sun (at least 6 hours) and fertile, well-drained soil with a pH between 6.0 and 6.8. Avoid low spots where water pools, as wet feet cause rot. If you have heavy clay, consider raised beds or mounds.

Test your soil first: a basic DIY kit costs $10-$20, or send a sample to a lab for $30-$50. Amend based on results: add 2-3 inches of compost or aged manure and work it in 8-10 inches deep. For a 100 square foot patch, mix in 2-3 pounds of a balanced fertilizer (10-10-10) before planting.

## Choosing the right varieties

Pick varieties based on your season length and use. Check days to maturity (DTM) from transplant or seed. For short seasons (under 100 days), choose early types.

| Variety | Type | DTM | Best for |
|---------|------|-----|----------|
| 'Howden' | Classic carving | 110-120 | Jack-o'-lanterns |
| 'Sugar Pie' | Pie pumpkin | 95-100 | Cooking, small size |
| 'Jack Be Little' | Miniature | 90-100 | Decoration |
| 'Connecticut Field' | Heirloom | 110-120 | Large carving, livestock feed |
| 'Cinderella' (Rouge Vif d'Etampes) | Heirloom | 100-110 | Decorative, cooking |

For zone 3-4, choose 'Sugar Pie' or 'Jack Be Little' to ensure maturity. In zones 5-9, you can grow 'Howden' or 'Cinderella'.

## Planting dates and spacing

Plant seeds directly in the ground after the last frost date, when soil temperature is at least 60°F. Use a soil thermometer to check. For a fall harvest, count back from your first frost date: plant 100-120 days before that.

Sow seeds 1 inch deep, 2-3 seeds per hill. Space hills 4-6 feet apart in rows 6-8 feet apart. For vining varieties, thin to the strongest seedling per hill once they have two true leaves. If you use transplants, start seeds indoors 3-4 weeks before planting out, and harden off for a week.

## Caring for the patch

Water deeply once a week, about 1 inch per week, more in hot weather. Use drip irrigation or a soaker hose to keep leaves dry and prevent mildew. Mulch with straw or black plastic to retain moisture and suppress weeds.

Fertilize when vines start to run: side-dress with a nitrogen-rich fertilizer (like blood meal) at a rate of 1 pound per 100 square feet. When flowers appear, switch to a phosphorus-rich fertilizer (like bone meal) to encourage fruit set. Avoid over-fertilizing, which leads to lush vines but few pumpkins.

Pollination: bees do the job, but if you see few bees, hand-pollinate by transferring pollen from male flowers (on long stems) to female flowers (with a small fruit at the base) using a small brush.

## Pest and disease management

Common pests: squash bugs, cucumber beetles, and vine borers. Check undersides of leaves weekly and remove egg masses. Use row covers until flowering, then remove to allow pollination. For vine borers, inject BT (Bacillus thuringiensis) into the stem if you see sawdust-like frass.

Diseases: powdery mildew and downy mildew. Prevent by spacing for air flow, watering at the base, and applying a fungicide (copper or sulfur) at first sign. Remove infected leaves immediately.

## Harvesting and storage

Pumpkins are ready when the skin is hard and the stem has started to dry. Cut with a sharp knife, leaving 3-4 inches of stem. Cure in the sun for 10-14 days to harden the skin, then store in a cool, dry place (50-60°F) for up to 3 months.

Yields: a typical vine produces 2-5 pumpkins, depending on variety and care. For a 100-foot row, expect 50-100 pumpkins.

## FAQ

**When should I plant pumpkins for Halloween?**
Count back 110-120 days from your expected harvest date. For zones 5-6, plant around June 1-15. For zones 7-8, plant in late May to early June. Check your first frost date and adjust.

**Can I grow pumpkins in a small garden?**
Yes, choose bush or semi-bush varieties like 'Sugar Pie' or 'Baby Bear'. Space them 3-4 feet apart, or grow them on a trellis with slings to support the fruit. Miniature varieties need only 2-3 feet.

**How do I know if my soil is good for pumpkins?**
Do a soil test. Pumpkins need well-drained soil with pH 6.0-6.8. If your soil is heavy clay, add compost and consider raised beds. If it's sandy, add organic matter to retain moisture.

**Why are my pumpkins rotting on the vine?**
Rot is often due to excess moisture or poor pollination. Ensure good drainage, water at the base, and place a board or straw under developing fruits to keep them off wet soil. Also, make sure bees are active; hand-pollinate if needed.

## The bottom line

Making a pumpkin patch is straightforward: pick a sunny spot, amend the soil, plant at the right time, and give vines room. Choose varieties that match your season, water consistently, and watch for pests. With a little effort, you'll have pumpkins for carving, cooking, or selling. Start small, learn from your mistakes, and expand next year.
