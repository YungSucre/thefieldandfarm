If you're out of fresh onions, or just want a quicker option, the standard substitution is 1 tablespoon of onion powder for one medium onion. For a more robust flavor, use 1.5 tablespoons. This works for most cooked dishes; for raw uses like salads or salsas, fresh onion is better because powder lacks the crunch and moisture.

## Why the conversion matters

Onion powder is dehydrated and ground, so its flavor is concentrated. One medium onion (about 2.5 to 3 inches in diameter, weighing around 5 to 7 ounces) yields roughly 1/2 to 2/3 cup of chopped onion. When dried, that volume reduces to about 1 to 
![How much onion powder equals 1 medium onion](/images/articles/how-much-onion-powder-equals-1-medium-onion/section-2.jpg)
1.5 tablespoons of powder. This ratio is consistent across brands, though some powders are finer and may pack more densely.

## Exact measurements

| Fresh onion | Onion powder |
|-------------|--------------|
| 1 medium (5-7 oz) | 1 to 1.5 tablespoons |
| 1 small (3-4 oz) | 2 to 3 teaspoons |
| 1 large (8-10 oz) | 2 to 2.5 tablespoons |
| 1 cup chopped | 2 to 3 tablespoons |

For most recipes, start with 1 tablespoon and adjust to taste. If you're cooking a soup or stew, you can add a bit more, as the powder will rehydrate and mellow.

## How to substitute in recipes

When using onion powder in place of fresh, keep these points in mind:

- **Moisture**: Fresh onions add liquid. If your recipe relies on that moisture (like a stir-fry or a sauce), you may need to add a splash of water or broth to compensate.
- **Texture**: Onion powder won't give you the soft, translucent pieces you get from sautéed onions. For texture, consider using onion flakes (which are larger) or rehydrate the powder in a little water before adding.
- **Flavor intensity**: Powder is more pungent than fresh. Start with less, then taste and add more. You can always add, but you can't take out.
- **Raw applications**: For salads, salsas, or garnishes, fresh onion is non-negotiable. Powder won't provide the crisp bite.

## Making your own onion powder

If you grow onions, you can easily make your own powder. Here's how:

1. **Slice** peeled onions thinly (about 1/8 inch). Use a mandoline for even slices.
2. **Dehydrate** at 125°F for 8 to 12 hours, until completely brittle. A food dehydrator (like the Excalibur, around $200) works best, but you can use an oven on the lowest setting with the door ajar.
3. **Grind** the dried slices in a spice grinder or blender until fine. A coffee grinder dedicated to spices (about $20) works well.
4. **Store** in an airtight jar in a cool, dark place. It will keep for up to a year.

One medium onion will yield about 1 to 1.5 tablespoons of powder, so you'll need about 10 medium onions to fill a 4-ounce jar.

## Onion powder vs. onion salt

Don't confuse onion powder with onion salt. Onion salt is a blend of onion powder and salt, usually with an anti-caking agent. If a recipe calls for onion powder, using onion salt will make it too salty. To substitute onion salt for powder, use half the amount and reduce the salt in the recipe accordingly. For example, if you need 1 tablespoon of onion powder, use 1.5 teaspoons of onion salt and cut the recipe's salt by about 1 teaspoon.

## Storage and shelf life

Store onion powder in an airtight container away from heat and light. It will keep for 2 to 3 years, but its potency fades over time. To test freshness, smell it: if it doesn't smell strongly of onion, it's time to replace it. Buy in small quantities, as you'll use it faster and get better flavor.

## FAQ

**Can I use onion powder in place of fresh onion in any recipe?**

Yes, for cooked dishes it works well. For raw preparations like salads or dips, fresh onion is better for texture and bite. If you must use powder, rehydrate it in a little water first and expect a softer texture.

**Is onion powder stronger than fresh onion?**

Yes, it's more concentrated. One tablespoon of powder equals one medium onion in flavor intensity, but because it's dried, it can taste sharper. Start with less and adjust.

**How do I measure onion powder correctly?**

Use a measuring spoon, and level it off. Don't pack it down, as that will give you too much. If you're using a scale, 1 tablespoon of onion powder weighs about 6 to 7 grams.

**Can I substitute onion flakes for powder?**

Yes, but use more. Onion flakes are larger and less dense, so use about 2 tablespoons of flakes for one medium onion. Rehydrate them in warm water for 5 minutes before adding to dishes that are not saucy.

## The bottom line

For most recipes, 1 tablespoon of onion powder replaces one medium onion. If you want a stronger onion flavor, use 1.5 tablespoons. Remember to adjust moisture and texture, and always taste as you go. Onion powder is a pantry staple that saves time, but fresh onions are irreplaceable for raw uses. Keep both on hand, and you'll never be stuck without onion flavor.
