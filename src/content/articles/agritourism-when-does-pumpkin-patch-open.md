Pumpkin patches typically open in late September and run through October 31, with exact dates varying by region and weather. In 2026, most patches in the Midwest and Northeast open the last weekend of September, while southern patches may open by mid-September. If you are planning a visit, call ahead or check the farm's website, as many require reservations on weekends.

## Typical opening dates by region

Pumpkin patch opening dates depend on your USDA hardiness zone and local climate. Here is a general timeline for 2026:

| Region | Typical opening | Peak season | Closing |
|--------|----------------|-------------|---------|
| South (zones 7-9) | Mid-September | Late September to mid-October | October 31 |
| Midwest (zones 5-6) | Last weekend of September | Early to mid-October | October 31 |
| Northeast (zones 5-7) | Last weekend of September | Mid-October | October 31 |
| West (zones 8-10) | Early October | Mid-October | October 31 |

These dates are not set in stone. A hot, dry summer can ripen pumpkins earlier, while a cool, wet one can delay them. In 2025, many patches in the Southeast opened a week early due to an early warm spell. Check local listings in late August for the first announcements.

## How to find your local patch

Start your search in early September. Use these steps:

1. Search online for "pumpkin patch near me" or "u-pick pumpkin farm" plus your county name.
2. Check state agriculture department websites; many list certified farms.
3. Call or email the farm directly. Ask for their opening date and whether they require reservations.
4. Follow farms on social media; they often post opening day announcements first.

Most patches are open daily from 9 AM to 6 PM, but hours shrink on weekdays after Columbus Day. Expect to pay $8 to $15 per pumpkin, or a flat fee of $5 to $10 per person for entry, with pumpkins priced separately. Some high-end patches charge $20 to $30 per person for corn mazes and hayrides, but that is on the pricier side.

## What to expect at a 2026 patch

Pumpkin patches have changed in recent years. Many now offer more than just pumpkins. You will likely find:

- Corn mazes (often $10 to $15 extra)
- Hayrides (included in entry or $5 to $8)
- Petting zoos (free or small fee)
- Food stands with cider and donuts
- Photo ops with themed displays

But the core activity remains picking your own pumpkin. Varieties you will commonly see include:

- 'Howden' for carving, 15 to 25 pounds
- 'Sugar Pie' for baking, 5 to 8 pounds
- 'Jack-O'-Lantern' hybrids, 10 to 20 pounds
- 'Cinderella' for decoration, 20 to 30 pounds

If you want a specific variety, call ahead. Smaller patches may only carry one or two types.

## Planning your visit

To get the best selection, go early in the season. The first two weekends are when the largest pumpkins are available. If you wait until late October, you will find mostly smaller or already picked-over fruit.

Bring your own wagon or wheelbarrow if you have one; many farms provide them, but they run out on busy days. Wear sturdy shoes, as fields can be muddy. Cash is still king at many rural patches, though most now accept cards.

If you are going with kids, set expectations. They will want the first pumpkin they see, but you can guide them to check for soft spots and cuts. A good pumpkin should be firm, with a dry stem, and sound hollow when tapped. Avoid pumpkins with large bruises or mold.

## Starting your own pumpkin patch

If you want to open a patch for the public, start planning a year in advance. Here is a realistic timeline:

- **Fall 2025**: Choose a site with full sun and well-drained soil. Test soil pH; pumpkins prefer 6.0 to 6.8.
- **Winter 2025-2026**: Order seeds from a reputable supplier like Johnny's Selected Seeds or Burpee. Plan for 1,000 to 2,000 plants per acre for a small patch.
- **Spring 2026**: Plant after the last frost, usually mid-May in zones 5-6. Sow seeds 1 inch deep, 18 to 24 inches apart in rows 6 to 8 feet apart. Or use hills with 3 seeds per hill, thinning to 2 plants.
- **Summer 2026**: Water deeply once a week, and watch for squash bugs and powdery mildew. Use row covers early to protect from pests.
- **Late August**: Stop watering to let the vines die back and pumpkins cure on the vine.
- **September**: Harvest when the rind is hard and the stem is dry. Cut with a sharp knife, leaving 3 to 4 inches of stem.

You will need liability insurance, which runs $500 to $1,500 per year for a small farm. Permits vary by county; check with your local planning office. You will also need a parking area and basic signage.

## Costs and revenue for a small patch

Opening a patch is not a get-rich scheme, but it can be a profitable side business. Here are realistic numbers for a 1-acre patch in 2026:

- Seed and soil amendments: $200 to $400
- Water and irrigation: $100 to $300
- Insurance and permits: $600 to $1,800
- Marketing (signs, social media ads): $200 to $500
- Total startup: $1,100 to $3,000

If you sell 3,000 pumpkins at $8 each, that is $24,000 gross. But you will not sell every pumpkin; expect 70 to 80% yield. Net profit after expenses and labor is often $10,000 to $15,000 for a well-run patch. It is a lot of work, but many families do it for the community aspect.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I visit a pumpkin patch in November?**
A: Most patches close by October 31. A few stay open into early November if they have leftover pumpkins, but it is rare. Call ahead if you want a late visit.

**Q: Are pumpkin patches open on Mondays?**
A: Many smaller patches close on Mondays and Tuesdays, especially after the first weekend. Check the farm's schedule before driving out.

**Q: Do I need to buy a pumpkin to enter?**
A: Some patches charge an entry fee, others only charge for pumpkins. It varies. Ask when you call.

**Q: How early should I arrive to avoid crowds?**
A: Go right at opening time on a weekday if possible. Weekends are busiest from 11 AM to 3 PM. Early morning also gives you the best selection.

## The bottom line

Pumpkin patches open in late September and close at the end of October. For 2026, mark the last weekend of September as your starting point, but verify with local farms. If you are starting your own patch, begin planning now, order seeds by winter, and expect to invest $1,000 to $3,000 upfront. Whether you are visiting or growing, the key is to act early for the best pumpkins and the smoothest experience.
